Question: A six-year-old boy develops gross hematuria after taking an antimalarial drug. What is the most probable underlying cause? A. G6PD deficiency B. Hereditary spherocytosis C. Immune hemolysis D. Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria E. Sickle cell disease Correct Option: A Explanation:
● Correct (A): Antimalarial drugs induce oxidative stress that causes acute intravascular hemolysis and dark urine (gross hematuria) in patients lacking glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase.
● Incorrect (B): Hereditary spherocytosis causes extravascular hemolysis driven by membrane instability, not by antimalarial oxidative drug triggers.
● Incorrect (C): Immune hemolysis is mediated by antibodies or complement binding to red cells rather than direct enzymatic oxidative stress from antimalarial ingestion.
● Incorrect (D): Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria is an acquired clonal stem cell defect driven by complement-mediated lysis, not drug-induced oxidative injury.
● Incorrect (E): Sickle cell disease is caused by qualitative hemoglobin polymerization under hypoxic conditions, not oxidative enzyme deficiencies triggered by antimalarial medications.

Question: An elderly patient presents with weakness, numbness in extremities, and elevated unconjugated bilirubin. The most likely diagnosis is: a. alcohol intoxication. b. folate deficiency. c. iron deficiency anemia. d. Vitamin B12 deficiency. e. thalassemia minor. Correct Option: D Explanation:
● Correct (D): Vitamin B12 deficiency causes subacute combined degeneration (numbness) and ineffective erythropoiesis, which releases unconjugated bilirubin from dying marrow precursors.
● Incorrect (A): Alcohol intoxication can cause acute cognitive deficits and neuropathy but does not typically generate a concurrent macrocytic hemolytic pattern.
● Incorrect (B): Folate deficiency shares macrocytic anemia features and elevated bilirubin but strictly lacks the neuro-anatomical tract demyelination that causes extremity numbness.
● Incorrect (C): Iron deficiency anemia causes microcytic change and is completely devoid of neurological tracts degeneration or elevated unconjugated bilirubin production.
● Incorrect (E): Thalassemia minor is a lifelong inherited globin structural trait that does not suddenly manifest as new-onset extremity numbness in old age.

Question: Neurologic symptoms in Vitamin B12 deficiency are produced because of: a. demyelination of the spinal cord. b. elevated homocysteine level. c. elevated methylmalonic acid level. d. impaired DNA synthesis. e. neuronal degeneration. Correct Option: C Explanation:
● Correct (C): Elevated methylmalonic acid level occurs because B12 deficiency disrupts malonyl-CoA metabolism, leading to abnormal fatty acid incorporation and myelin breakdown.
● Incorrect (A): Demyelination of the spinal cord is the resulting structural anatomical lesion, but it is directly produced by the chemical build-up of methylmalonic acid.
● Incorrect (B): Elevated homocysteine level contributes heavily to vascular endothelial injury and atherosclerosis, rather than driving specific myelin sheath damage.
● Incorrect (D): Impaired DNA synthesis leads directly to nuclear-cytoplasmic dyssynchrony and macrocytic megaloblastic changes, but does not cause neurological myelin degradation.
● Incorrect (E): Neuronal degeneration is a late, secondary structural consequence of prolonged myelin sheath loss rather than the primary biochemical mechanism.

Question: A patient presents with low Hb, low MCV, and a strong family history of anemia. The most likely diagnosis is: A. Aplastic anemia. B. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia. C. Beta thalassemia. D. Hereditary spherocytosis. E. Iron deficiency anemia. Correct Option: C
Explanation:
● Correct (C): Beta thalassemia is an inherited autosomal co-dominant disorder of globin chain synthesis that presents as an inherited microcytic anemia, matching the patient's low Hb, low MCV, and strong family history of anemia.
● Incorrect (A): Aplastic anemia is an acquired or rare inherited bone marrow failure condition that characteristically presents as a normocytic or macrocytic anemia, not with low MCV.
● Incorrect (B): Autoimmune hemolytic anemia is an acquired immune-mediated destruction of red blood cells that typically presents as a normocytic or macrocytic anemia due to reticulocytosis, rather than low MCV.
● Incorrect (D): Hereditary spherocytosis is an inherited red cell membrane defect that presents as a normocytic or microcytic anemia with an elevated MCHC, but it is not typically characterized primarily by a profoundly low MCV.
● Incorrect (E): Iron deficiency anemia is predominantly an acquired nutritional or hemorrhagic condition that presents with low Hb and low MCV, but it lacks a genetic etiology explaining a strong family history of anemia.

Question: A 40-year-old male with chronic arthritis presents with pallor. Laboratory findings show ferritin 450 μg/L and TIBC 120 μg/L. What is the most likely diagnosis: A. Acute blood loss anemia. B. Anemia of chronic inflammation. C. Aplastic anemia. D. Iron deficiency anemia. E. Megaloblastic anemia. Correct Option: B
Explanation:
● Correct (B): Anemia of chronic inflammation is triggered by long-standing diseases like chronic arthritis, where inflammatory cytokines elevate ferritin (450 μg/L) as an acute-phase reactant and suppress TIBC (120 μg/L).
● Incorrect (A): Acute blood loss anemia presents with normal baseline iron indices initially, without the elevated ferritin and depressed TIBC seen here.
● Incorrect (C): Aplastic anemia involves pancytopenia from bone marrow failure and does not typically feature the marked reduction in TIBC associated with an inflammatory state.
● Incorrect (D): Iron deficiency anemia is ruled out by these findings because it presents with severely depleted ferritin and an elevated TIBC.
● Incorrect (E): Megaloblastic anemia is a macrocytic disorder arising from impaired DNA synthesis and does not present with an inflammatory pattern of iron parameters.

Explanation:
● Correct (E): Thalassemia minor characteristically presents as a mild microcytic hypochromic red blood cells anemia but maintains a normal serum iron and TIBC because iron stores are entirely unaffected by this globin-chain mutation.
● Incorrect (A): Aplastic anemia typically presents with normocytic or macrocytic red blood cells accompanied by pancytopenia, not microcytic hypochromic red blood cells.
● Incorrect (B): Hemolytic anemia presents with normocytic or macrocytic indices due to reticulocytosis, and would not present with isolated microcytic hypochromic red blood cells and normal iron parameters.
● Incorrect (C): Iron deficiency anemia is directly ruled out because it demands a low normal serum iron and a significantly elevated TIBC alongside microcytic hypochromic red blood cells.
● Incorrect (D): Sideroblastic anemia involves abnormal iron accumulation that typically manifests as increased normal serum iron and saturation levels rather than strictly normal serum iron and TIBC.

C. t(15;17).
D. t(8;14).

Correct Option: A
Explanation:

● Incorrect (B): The t(14;18) translocation is characteristically associated with follicular lymphoma due to the overexpression of the BCL-2 proto-oncogene.

● Incorrect (D): The t(8;14) translocation is the definitive cytogenetic feature of Burkitt lymphoma, leading to c-myc oncogene dysregulation.
● Incorrect (E): The t(9;22) translocation creates the Philadelphia chromosome, which signifies a poor prognosis in pediatric B-ALL.

Question: 9. A 7-year-old boy presents with abrupt onset of fever, pallor, and easy bruising. Labs show pancytopenia and numerous blasts in the peripheral smear. The most likely diagnosis is: A. ALL.
B. AML.
C. CLL.
D. CML.
E. Hairy cell leukemia.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
● Correct (A): The combination of a pediatric presentation (7 years old), acute symptoms of bone marrow failure (fever, pallor, bruising), and peripheral blasts is the classic presentation of ALL.
● Incorrect (B): AML can occur in children but is significantly less common than ALL in a 7-year-old patient.
● Incorrect (C): CLL is an indolent disease exclusively seen in older adults and does not present as an acute blast-driven illness in children.
● Incorrect (D): CML typically presents in adults with a mature leukocytosis and splenomegaly rather than an acute blast-dominant pancytopenia in childhood.
● Incorrect (E): Hairy cell leukemia is a rare mature B-cell chronic lymphoproliferative disorder that predominantly affects older adult males.
