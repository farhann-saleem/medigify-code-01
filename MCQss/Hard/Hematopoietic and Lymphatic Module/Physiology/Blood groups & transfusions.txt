
Question 4
Question: In the Rh blood group system:
A. agglutinins are generally hemolysins.
B. agglutinins are present since birth.
C. anti-Rh antibodies are naturally occurring.
D. Landsteiner’s law is applicable.
E. Landsteiner’s law is not applicable.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Rh agglutinins are generally hemolysins because they activate immune mechanisms that destroy red blood cells.
Incorrect (B): Rh agglutinins appear only after sensitization.
Incorrect (C): Anti-Rh antibodies are acquired rather than naturally occurring.
Incorrect (D): Landsteiner’s law does not apply to the Rh blood group system.
Incorrect (E): Although true conceptually, the key tested property is that Rh agglutinins are generally hemolysins.

Question 7
Question: A baby was born with erythroblastosis fetalis. Her blood group was A⁺. For exchange transfusion, the baby must receive blood with group:
A. A−.
B. A⁺.
C. AB−.
D. B−.
E. O−.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): A− blood is ABO compatible while lacking the Rh antigen responsible for ongoing hemolysis.
Incorrect (B): A⁺ blood still carries the Rh antigen.
Incorrect (C): AB− blood contains B antigen, creating ABO incompatibility.
Incorrect (D): B− blood is ABO incompatible.
Incorrect (E): O− is compatible but the best ABO-matched exchange transfusion is A−.

Question 10
Question: A 30-year-old patient develops hemolytic anemia after a mismatched blood transfusion. The primary immune mechanism responsible is:
A. complement-mediated lysis triggered by IgG or IgM antibodies.
B. IgE-mediated hypersensitivity.
C. immune complex deposition.
D. T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity.
E. Type IV delayed hypersensitivity.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Mismatched blood transfusion activates complement-mediated lysis triggered by IgG or IgM antibodies directed against donor RBC antigens.
Incorrect (B): IgE-mediated hypersensitivity causes allergic reactions rather than hemolytic transfusion reactions.
Incorrect (C): Immune complex deposition is characteristic of Type III hypersensitivity.
Incorrect (D): T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity is not the primary mechanism of acute transfusion hemolysis.
Incorrect (E): Type IV delayed hypersensitivity is mediated by T cells and occurs after 24 to 72 hours.

Question 11

A. Type I hypersensitivity.

Incorrect (A): Type I hypersensitivity is IgE-mediated.

Incorrect (D): Type IV hypersensitivity is T-cell-mediated.

Question 12
Question: A newborn presents with jaundice and anemia shortly after birth. Mother’s blood group is O⁻ and baby’s is A⁺. The underlying cause of erythroblastosis fetalis is:
A. ABO incompatibility mediated by complement only.
B. maternal IgG antibodies crossing the placenta and destroying fetal RBCs.
C. maternal IgM antibodies crossing the placenta.
D. natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity.
E. T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity.

Explanation:
Correct (B): Maternal IgG antibodies cross the placenta and destroy fetal RBCs, causing erythroblastosis fetalis.
Incorrect (A): Complement alone does not explain erythroblastosis fetalis.
Incorrect (C): Maternal IgM antibodies do not cross the placenta.
Incorrect (D): Natural killer cells are not the primary cause.
Incorrect (E): T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity is not responsible.

Question 13

B. IgG-mediated delayed hemolysis.

Explanation:

Incorrect (D): Immune complex deposition is not responsible for acute transfusion reactions.
