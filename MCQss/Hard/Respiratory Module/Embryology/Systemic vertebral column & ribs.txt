A. a cervical (supernumerary) rib
B. a depressed sternal angle
C. a depressed xiphoid process
D. a fractured 1st rib
E. a fractured 10th rib
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
* Correct (A): A cervical (supernumerary) rib arises from the C7 vertebra and directly impinges upon the brachial plexus and subclavian vessels within the superior thoracic aperture.
* Incorrect (B): A depressed sternal angle alters the anterior chest wall geometry but does not compress structures traversing the thoracic inlet.
* Incorrect (C): A depressed xiphoid process sits at the inferior margin of the sternum and cannot impinge upon the superior thoracic aperture.
* Incorrect (D): A fractured 1st rib typically causes acute vascular injury or pneumothorax rather than chronic thoracic outlet syndrome.
* Incorrect (E): A fractured 10th rib involves the lower costal margin and has no anatomical relationship with the superior thoracic aperture.

Question: Which vertebral defect is characterized by hemivertebrae:
A. kyphosis
B. scoliosis
C. spina bifida
D. spina bifida cystica
E. spina bifida occulta
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
* Correct (B): Scoliosis is a lateral curvature of the spine frequently caused by hemivertebrae, where one side of a vertebra fails to form completely.
* Incorrect (A): Kyphosis is an exaggerated anterior-posterior curvature of the thoracic spine, typically resulting from wedge fractures or osteoporosis rather than asymmetric hemivertebrae.
* Incorrect (C): Spina bifida is a broad category of neural tube defects defined by the failure of the vertebral arches to fuse posteriorly.
* Incorrect (D): Spina bifida cystica is a severe neural tube defect marked by a visible fluid-filled sac protruding through the unfused vertebral arches.
* Incorrect (E): Spina bifida occulta is a mild, asymptomatic failure of vertebral arch fusion covered by intact overlying skin.

Question: What is the name of congenital vertebral defect that involves fusion of two or more cervical vertebrae:
A. klippel–Feil syndrome
B. kyphosis
C. lordosis
D. scoliosis
E. spondylolisthesis
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
* Correct (A): Klippel–Feil syndrome is a congenital anomaly characterized by the improper segmentation and subsequent fusion of two or more cervical vertebrae.
* Incorrect (B): Kyphosis refers to an abnormal posterior curvature of the thoracic vertebrae, not a structural fusion defect of the cervical region.
* Incorrect (C): Lordosis describes an exaggerated anterior curvature of the lumbar or cervical spine, unrelated to congenital vertebral fusion.
* Incorrect (D): Scoliosis involves an abnormal lateral curvature of the spinal column rather than localized cervical vertebral fusion.
* Incorrect (E): Spondylolisthesis is the anterior displacement or slipping of a vertebra over the one below it, typically occurring in the lumbar spine.

Question: What is the type of spina bifida characterized by a sac-like protrusion of spinal cord and meninges through an opening in the spine:
A. lipomyelomeningocele
B. meningocele
C. myelomeningocele
D. occulta
E. schizencephaly
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
* Correct (C): Myelomeningocele is a severe form of spina bifida cystica where both the meninges and the spinal cord herniate through the open vertebral defect into a visible external sac.
* Incorrect (A): Lipomyelomeningocele features an abnormal fatty mass attached to the spinal cord, rather than an open fluid-filled sac containing neural tissue.
* Incorrect (B): Meningocele involves the herniation of the meninges alone through the bony defect, leaving the spinal cord intact within the canal.
* Incorrect (D): Occulta is a hidden neural tube defect characterized by an incomplete vertebral arch without any external cystic sac or neural protrusion.
* Incorrect (E): Schizencephaly is a rare developmental disorder marked by abnormal clefts in the cerebral hemispheres, not a spinal column defect.
Tags: Hard, Embryology, Respiratory Module, Systemic: Vertebral Column & Ribs
