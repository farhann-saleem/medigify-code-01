A. congenital neonatal emphysema
B. hyaline membrane disease
C. pulmonary hypoplasia
D. pulmonary sequestration
E. tracheoesophageal fistula
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
* Correct (A): Congenital neonatal emphysema (or congenital lobar emphysema) features a lack of proper bronchial cartilage support, causing structural collapse and air trapping.
* Incorrect (B): Hyaline membrane disease is caused by a surfactant deficiency within the alveoli, not a structural cartilaginous failure of the bronchi.
* Incorrect (C): Pulmonary hypoplasia is a gross reduction in total lung volume and weight, typically caused by oligohydramnios or diaphragmatic hernia.
* Incorrect (D): Pulmonary sequestration consists of a non-functional mass of lung tissue receiving its blood supply from an abnormal systemic artery.
* Incorrect (E): Tracheoesophageal fistula is an abnormal communication channel linking the trachea and esophagus, not an isolated cartilaginous wall defect.

Question: A newborn with collapsed alveoli lined with eosinophilic material has:
A. congenital emphysema
B. congenital lobar cysts
C. cystic fibrosis
D. respiratory distress syndrome
E. tracheoesophageal fistula
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
* Correct (D): Respiratory distress syndrome is characterized by surfactant deficiency leading to alveolar collapse and cellular debris forming glassy eosinophilic hyaline membranes.
* Incorrect (A): Congenital emphysema is characterized by hyperinflation of lung lobes due to cartilage collapse, not diffuse alveolar collapse with eosinophilic membranes.
* Incorrect (B): Congenital lobar cysts present as fluid-filled or air-filled cystic lesions within the lung parenchyma instead of diffuse alveolar collapse.
* Incorrect (C): Cystic fibrosis presents with thick mucus obstructions and chronic infections, which do not produce immediate neonatal alveolar eosinophilic membranes.
* Incorrect (E): Tracheoesophageal fistula clinically presents with immediate choking and regurgitation during feeding rather than isolated alveolar hyaline membranes.

Question: Infant with choking, excessive saliva, pneumonitis, and air in stomach:
A. congenital diaphragmatic hernia
B. congenital lobar emphysema
C. hypertrophic pyloric stenosis
D. respiratory distress syndrome
E. tracheoesophageal fistula
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
* Correct (E): A tracheoesophageal fistula allows swallowed saliva to overflow into the lungs (pneumonitis) and permits inspired air to pass through the fistula into the stomach.
* Incorrect (A): Congenital diaphragmatic hernia presents primarily with severe respiratory distress and a scaphoid abdomen due to displaced bowel loops.
* Incorrect (B): Congenital lobar emphysema involves localized air-trapping within hyperinflated lung fields without causing excessive salivation or direct air entry into the stomach.
* Incorrect (C): Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis presents with non-bilious projectile vomiting in older infants, without causing direct respiratory choking or pneumonitis.
* Incorrect (D): Respiratory distress syndrome features primary surfactant lack and expiratory grunting, lacking the excessive salivation and air-distended stomach typical of a fistula.
Tags: Hard, Embryology, Respiratory Module, Systemic: Trachea & Lungs

Question: An infant presents with choking and coughing during feeding, excessive salivation, and abdominal distension. What is the most likely diagnosis:
A. congenital diaphragmatic hernia
B. esophageal hiatal hernia
C. physiological umbilical herniation
D. respiratory distress syndrome
E. tracheoesophageal fistula
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
* Correct (E): Tracheoesophageal fistula causes choking as fluid enters the lungs, while air moving through the fistula into the gastrointestinal tract produces abdominal distension.
* Incorrect (A): Congenital diaphragmatic hernia presents with a scaphoid abdomen and severe respiratory distress, not excessive salivation or abdominal distension.
* Incorrect (B): Esophageal hiatal hernia presents with mild gastroesophageal reflux and does not cause acute neonatal choking, cough, or prominent abdominal distension.
* Incorrect (C): Physiological umbilical herniation is a normal midgut developmental phase outside the abdomen that produces no respiratory or salivary symptoms.
* Incorrect (D): Respiratory distress syndrome causes tachypnea, cyanosis, and grunting from surfactant deficiency, without excessive salivation or feeding-induced choking.
Tags: Hard, Embryology, Respiratory Module, Systemic: Trachea & Lungs
