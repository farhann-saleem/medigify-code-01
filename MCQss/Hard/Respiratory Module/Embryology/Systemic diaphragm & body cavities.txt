A. dorsal mesentery of the esophagus to develop
B. lateral and dorsal body walls to contribute muscle
C. pleuropericardial membrane to develop completely
D. pleuroperitoneal membranes to fuse in a normal fashion
E. septum transversum to develop
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
* Correct (D): Failure of the pleuroperitoneal membranes to fuse in a normal fashion with the septum transversum and dorsal mesentery leaves the posterolateral pleuroperitoneal canal open.
* Incorrect (A): Defective development of the dorsal mesentery of the esophagus leads to hiatal or congenital esophageal hernias, not classical Bochdalek diaphragmatic hernias.
* Incorrect (B): Failure of the lateral and dorsal body walls to contribute muscle leads to localized thinning or eventration of the diaphragm, not a true herniation defect.
* Incorrect (C): Incomplete development of the pleuropericardial membrane affects pericardial cavity separation and does not lead to an abdominal-thoracic diaphragmatic herniation.
* Incorrect (E): Complete failure of the septum transversum to develop results in large anterior midline defects or total diaphragmatic agenesis, which is extremely rare compared to fusion failure.

Question: A Congenital diaphragmatic hernia is usually life threatening because it is associated with:
A. heart hypoplasia
B. liver agenesis
C. liver hypoplasia
D. physiological umbilical hernia
E. pulmonary hypoplasia
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
* Correct (E): The herniated abdominal organs occupy the thoracic cavity during critical fetal stages, causing fatal pulmonary hypoplasia due to mechanical compression of the lung parenchyma.
* Incorrect (A): While the heart may be displaced laterally (dextroposition), primary heart hypoplasia is not the chief cause of mortality.
* Incorrect (B): Liver agenesis is a severe homeobox gene mutation defect and is not a structural consequence or component of a diaphragmatic hernia.
* Incorrect (C): The liver may herniate or become malrotated, but primary liver hypoplasia does not dictate the life-threatening respiratory prognosis.
* Incorrect (D): Physiological umbilical hernia is a normal stage of midgut development between weeks 6 and 10 and is unrelated to the lethal intrathoracic compression.

Question: An 8-day-old boy presents with episodes of cyanosis and improved breathing in upright position, with absent breath sounds on the left side. What is the diagnosis:
A. congenital diaphragmatic hernia
B. esophageal hiatal hernia
C. laryngeal web obstruction
D. physiological umbilical herniation
E. tetralogy of Fallot
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
* Correct (A): A congenital diaphragmatic hernia presents shortly after birth with respiratory distress, cyanosis, and absent breath sounds on the left side due to the presence of abdominal loops in the thorax.
* Incorrect (B): An esophageal hiatal hernia typically presents with postprandial vomiting, feeding difficulties, or reflux rather than acute neonatal cyanosis and completely absent unilateral breath sounds.
* Incorrect (C): A laryngeal web obstruction presents with stridor, aphonia, or uniform bilateral respiratory distress, not localized unilateral loss of breath sounds.
* Incorrect (D): Physiological umbilical herniation is an asymptomatic, normal embryological phase of the midgut that resolves before birth.
* Incorrect (E): Tetralogy of Fallot presents with cyanosis and a harsh systolic ejection murmur, but breath sounds remain bilaterally equal and clear.
Tags: Hard, Embryology, Respiratory Module, Systemic: Diaphragm & Body Cavities
