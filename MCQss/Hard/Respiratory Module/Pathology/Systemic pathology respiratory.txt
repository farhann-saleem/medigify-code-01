 Question: A 55-year-old heavy smoker presents with severe dyspnea, dry cough, barrel chest, prolonged expiration, and decreased FEV1. What is the principal pathogenetic mechanism:
  A. production of autoantibodies against alveolar basement membrane.
  B. protease production by neutrophils and macrophages.
  C. squamous metaplasia of bronchial epithelium.
  D. type III hypersensitivity reaction.
  E. type IV hypersensitivity reaction.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Smoking recruits neutrophils and macrophages whose proteases destroy alveolar walls, producing emphysema.
 •	Incorrect (A): Autoantibodies against alveolar basement membrane occur in Goodpasture syndrome.
 •	Incorrect (C): Squamous metaplasia is associated with smoking-related epithelial change, not emphysema pathogenesis.
 •	Incorrect (D): Type III hypersensitivity is immune-complex mediated.
 •	Incorrect (E): Type IV hypersensitivity is T-cell mediated and not the principal mechanism here.

 Question: A 4-year-old child has seasonal breathing difficulty with eosinophilia and Charcot-Leyden crystals in sputum. Which mediator is responsible for bronchospasm:
  A. histamine.
  B. interleukins.
  C. leukotrienes.
  D. prostaglandins.
  E. tumor necrosis factor-α.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Leukotrienes are potent bronchoconstrictors responsible for sustained bronchospasm in allergic asthma.
 •	Incorrect (A): Histamine contributes early but is less important for prolonged bronchospasm.
 •	Incorrect (B): Interleukins regulate inflammation rather than directly causing major bronchospasm.
 •	Incorrect (D): Prostaglandins have varied effects and are not the principal bronchospastic mediators.
 •	Incorrect (E): Tumor necrosis factor-α promotes inflammation but does not primarily mediate bronchospasm.

 Question: A 28-year-old man presents with infertility, bronchiectasis, and dextrocardia. What is the diagnosis:
  A. Caplan syndrome.
  B. idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.
  C. Kartagener syndrome.
  D. primary tuberculosis.
  E. situs inversus.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Kartagener syndrome is primary ciliary dyskinesia characterized by bronchiectasis, infertility, and dextrocardia/situs inversus.
 •	Incorrect (A): Caplan syndrome combines rheumatoid arthritis with pneumoconiosis.
 •	Incorrect (B): Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis does not explain dextrocardia and infertility.
 •	Incorrect (D): Primary tuberculosis lacks the characteristic triad.
 •	Incorrect (E): Situs inversus alone does not account for bronchiectasis and infertility.

 Question: A 22-year-old female develops sudden dyspnea with wheezing. Sputum shows Curschmann spirals and Charcot-Leyden crystals with eosinophils. What is the diagnosis:
  A. atelectasis.
  B. bronchial asthma.
  C. bronchiectasis.
  D. chronic bronchitis.
  E. pulmonary edema.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Wheezing with eosinophils, Curschmann spirals, and Charcot-Leyden crystals is classic for bronchial asthma.
 •	Incorrect (A): Atelectasis involves lung collapse without these sputum findings.
 •	Incorrect (C): Bronchiectasis produces purulent sputum and recurrent infections.
 •	Incorrect (D): Chronic bronchitis is characterized by chronic productive cough rather than eosinophilic sputum.
 •	Incorrect (E): Pulmonary edema produces frothy fluid rather than asthma-specific sputum features.

 Question: A 50-year-old man with progressive dyspnea shows restrictive pattern on pulmonary function tests and interstitial linear opacities on X-ray. Which pathology is most likely:
  A. chronic inflammation with eosinophils in bronchi.
  B. honeycomb lung with alveolar fibrosis.
  C. hyperplasia of mucous glands in bronchi.
  D. uniform dilation of air spaces distal to bronchioles.
  E. vascular congestion with alveolar edema.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Restrictive physiology with interstitial opacities strongly suggests pulmonary fibrosis producing honeycomb lung with alveolar fibrosis.
 •	Incorrect (A): Eosinophilic bronchial inflammation is characteristic of asthma.
 •	Incorrect (C): Hyperplasia of mucous glands in bronchi is typical of chronic bronchitis.
 •	Incorrect (D): Uniform dilation of air spaces distal to bronchioles describes emphysema, an obstructive disease.
 •	Incorrect (E): Vascular congestion with alveolar edema is characteristic of pulmonary edema rather than chronic interstitial fibrosis.

 Question: A 60-year-old man presents with chronic productive cough and copious purulent sputum. CT shows bronchial dilatation and wall thickening. What is the diagnosis?
  A. Asthma.
  B. Bronchiectasis.
  C. Chronic bronchitis.
  D. Emphysema.
  E. Pneumonia.
  Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Bronchiectasis is characterized by permanent bronchial dilatation with chronic productive cough, copious purulent sputum, and CT evidence of bronchial wall thickening.
 •	Incorrect (A): Asthma causes reversible airway obstruction and wheezing, not permanent bronchial dilatation with purulent sputum.
 •	Incorrect (C): Chronic bronchitis causes chronic cough with sputum production but lacks the marked bronchial dilatation seen on CT.
 •	Incorrect (D): Emphysema involves destruction of alveolar walls and air-space enlargement rather than bronchial wall thickening.
 •	Incorrect (E): Pneumonia is an acute infectious consolidation and does not typically cause permanent bronchial dilatation.

 Question: A 61-year-old man presents with shortness of breath and chest X-ray shows diffuse pulmonary infiltrates with patchy interstitial fibrosis. What is the diagnosis?
  A. Bronchiectasis.
  B. Chronic bronchitis.
  C. Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.
  D. Lobar pneumonia.
  E. Pulmonary emphysema.
  Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis produces progressive dyspnea with diffuse pulmonary infiltrates and patchy interstitial fibrosis due to chronic fibrosing interstitial lung disease.
 •	Incorrect (A): Bronchiectasis primarily affects the bronchi with dilatation and recurrent infections rather than diffuse interstitial fibrosis.
 •	Incorrect (B): Chronic bronchitis causes mucus hypersecretion and airway inflammation without patchy interstitial fibrosis.
 •	Incorrect (D): Lobar pneumonia typically presents with localized lobar consolidation rather than diffuse fibrotic infiltrates.
 •	Incorrect (E): Pulmonary emphysema causes alveolar destruction and hyperinflation, not interstitial fibrosis.

 Question: A newborn of a diabetic mother presents with respiratory distress and ground glass opacity on chest X-ray. What is the diagnosis?
  A. Asthma.
  B. Bronchiolitis.
  C. Bronchitis.
  D. Respiratory distress syndrome.
  E. Transient tachypnea of the newborn.
  Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (D): Respiratory distress syndrome results from surfactant deficiency and classically presents in newborns with respiratory distress and ground glass opacity on chest X-ray.
 •	Incorrect (A): Asthma is a chronic airway disease and is not a cause of neonatal respiratory distress.
 •	Incorrect (B): Bronchiolitis is a viral lower respiratory tract infection occurring mainly in infants after the neonatal period.
 •	Incorrect (C): Bronchitis is uncommon in newborns and does not typically produce diffuse ground glass opacity.
 •	Incorrect (E): Transient tachypnea of the newborn causes mild respiratory distress due to delayed lung fluid clearance but is not primarily associated with surfactant deficiency.
 Tags: Hard, Pathology, Respiratory Module, Systemic Pathology: Respiratory
