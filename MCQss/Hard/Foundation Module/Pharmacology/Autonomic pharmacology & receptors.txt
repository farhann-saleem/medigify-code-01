Question: A study was carried out in isolated skeletal muscle preparations to determine the action of a new drug “succinylcholine,” which in separate studies is bound to the same receptors as acetylcholine. In the absence of succinylcholine, acetylcholine caused contraction of the muscle & in presence of succinylcholine, acetylcholine did not cause contraction. But when the concentration of acetylcholine was increased, muscle contraction occurred. Which of the following best describes succinylcholine?
 A. Chemical antagonist
 B. Competitive antagonist
 C. Irreversible antagonist
 D. Noncompetitive antagonist
 E. Physiologic antagonist
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
​Correct (B): Succinylcholine acts as a competitive antagonist under these experimental conditions because it binds reversibly to the same orthosteric site as acetylcholine, allowing an increased concentration of the native agonist to fully overcome the blockade.
​Incorrect (A): A chemical antagonist suppresses signaling by binding directly to the agonist molecule in solution to neutralize it, rather than interacting with the tissue receptor site.
​Incorrect (C): An irreversible antagonist binds covalently or with extremely high affinity to the receptor, preventing an increased concentration of agonist from restoring the maximal tissue response.
​Incorrect (D): A noncompetitive antagonist binds to an allosteric site or permanently deactivates the receptor downstream, meaning an increased concentration of agonist cannot overcome the block.
​Incorrect (E): A physiologic antagonist acts on a completely independent receptor system to trigger an opposing biological pathway, rather than competing for the primary receptor site.
