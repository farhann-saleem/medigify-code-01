Question: An 11-month-old girl is referred to a pediatric gastroenterology clinic due to a history of generalized weakness, slow growth, and refractory diarrhea. For the past month she has been hospitalized regularly to receive parenteral nutrition. Examination of the epithelium lining her small intestine confirms that the failure to absorb nutrients is most likely due to a significant decrease in which of the following:
A. Cell layers
B. Cilia
C. Desmosomes
D. Gap junctions
E. Microvilli
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The inner absorptive surface area of enterocytes relies directly on microvilli; a significant decrease in these structures dramatically reduces nutrient absorption, driving refractory diarrhea and growth failure.
Incorrect (A): A decrease in cell layers is not the primary cause of absorption failure, as the intestinal mucosa is structurally a simple (single-layer) epithelium by default.
Incorrect (B): Cilia are specialized for transporting lumenal debris or gametes via coordinated beating and do not participate in intestinal nutrient uptake.
Incorrect (C): Desmosomes provide structural adhesion to prevent mechanical shearing between adjacent cells but do not form the surface area needed for absorption.
Incorrect (D): Gap junctions allow direct electrical and chemical communication between adjacent epithelial cell cytoplasms rather than facilitating lumenal nutrient extraction.

Question 16
With a 5–year history of chronic respiratory infections, a 23-year-old, non-smoking man is referred to an otolaryngologist. A bronchial biopsy indicates altered structures in the epithelial cells. Which of the following, if altered to reduce function, is most likely involved in this patient's condition?
A. Basolateral cell membrane folds
B. Cilia
C. Hemidesmosomes
D. Microvilli
E. Stereocilia
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Motile cilia line the respiratory tract epithelium and drive the mucociliary escalator; their structural or functional alteration impairs mucus clearance, leading to chronic respiratory infections.
Incorrect (A): Basolateral cell membrane folds function primarily to expand surface area for fluid and ion transport, unrelated to clearing respiratory mucus.
Incorrect (C): Hemidesmosomes anchor cells to the basement membrane, and their dysfunction causes epithelial blistering or detachment rather than selective mucociliary clearance failure.
Incorrect (D): Microvilli are non-motile, actin-rich projections designed to maximize surface area for absorption, not to move luminal contents along the airway.
Incorrect (E): Stereocilia are elongated, non-motile specialized microvilli found in the epididymis and inner ear, and are absent from the respiratory epithelium.
