### Question 1
A neuron is perfused with a solution in which the extracellular K+ concentration is increased from 4 mM to 8 mM while other ions remain constant. According to membrane physiology described in Guyton & Hall, what is the most immediate effect on the resting membrane potential?
A. Depolarization due to reduced K+ diffusion gradient
B. Depolarization due to reduced Na+ diffusion gradient
C. Hyperpolarization due to increased K+ efflux
D. Hyperpolarization due to increased Na+ efflux
E. No change because Na+ permeability dominates RMP
**Correct Option: A**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (A)**: Increasing extracellular K+ concentration decreases the chemical concentration gradient driving K+ efflux out of the cell, which reduces the outward diffusion of positive charges and causes depolarization due to reduced K+ diffusion gradient.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: This immediate shift in electrical potential is driven purely by changes in potassium dynamics, not a depolarization due to reduced Na+ diffusion gradient.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Raising external potassium concentration diminishes the chemical gradient, preventing hyperpolarization due to increased K+ efflux.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: Sodium ion dynamics remain unaffected by this external potassium shift, ruling out a hyperpolarization due to increased Na+ efflux.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: At rest, potassium leak channels are highly active, meaning there is definitely a significant potential shift rather than no change because Na+ permeability dominates RMP.

### Question 4
A patient presents with severe hypokalemia. Which of the following changes in neuronal physiology is most likely according to Guyton & Hall?
A. Increased Na+ permeability causes spontaneous depolarization
B. Resting membrane potential becomes less negative, decreasing excitability
C. Resting membrane potential becomes more negative, decreasing excitability
D. Threshold potential becomes less negative, increasing excitability
E. Threshold potential becomes more negative, increasing excitability
**Correct Option: C**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (C)**: Lowering extracellular potassium steepens the concentration gradient out of the cell, driving greater potassium efflux until the resting membrane potential becomes more negative, decreasing excitability as it moves farther from the activation threshold.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: Extracellular potassium depletion does not alter initial resting leak kinetics to produce an increased Na+ permeability causes spontaneous depolarization.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Enhanced chemical gradients force more positive potassium charges out of the cell, preventing the outcome where the resting membrane potential becomes less negative, decreasing excitability.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: The structural activation voltage of voltage-gated sodium channels is not directly shifted, meaning the threshold potential becomes less negative, increasing excitability is false.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Gating threshold parameters remain independent of this extracellular fluid potassium imbalance, so the threshold potential becomes more negative, increasing excitability is incorrect.

### Question 7
A researcher artificially blocks K+ leak channels in a resting neuron while keeping Na+ permeability unchanged. What will most likely occur to the resting membrane potential?
A. It will equal the Cl- equilibrium potential
B. It will equal the K+ equilibrium potential
C. It will move toward the Ca2+ equilibrium potential
D. It will move toward the Na+ equilibrium potential
E. It will remain completely unchanged at the baseline
**Correct Option: D**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (D)**: Eliminating the high resting potassium conductance allows the minor, remaining background sodium influx to dominate the membrane's electrical profile, so it will move toward the Na+ equilibrium potential (+61\text{ mV}).
 * **Incorrect (A)**: Chloride forces do not automatically dictate resting voltages when potassium pathways are shut down, so it will equal the Cl- equilibrium potential is incorrect.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Blocking the main efflux pathway prevents the membrane from reaching maximum hyperpolarization, so it will equal the K+ equilibrium potential is false.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Intracellular calcium currents do not regulate resting baseline potential shifts, meaning it will move toward the Ca2+ equilibrium potential is incorrect.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Severing the primary ion pathway that maintains resting negativity guarantees a significant shift, meaning it will remain completely unchanged at the baseline is false.

### Question 9
A nerve fiber is experimentally cooled to a very low temperature, slowing Na+-K+ pump activity significantly. Initially, what happens to the resting membrane potential?
A. Immediate depolarization due to absolute pump failure
B. Immediate depolarization due to K+ channel closure
C. Immediate hyperpolarization due to reduced Na+ permeability
D. Immediate hyperpolarization due to reduced K+ permeability
E. No immediate change because RMP is mainly determined by diffusion potentials
**Correct Option: E**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (E)**: The bulk of resting voltage is generated by passive ion diffusion through leak channels down established gradients; thus, halting the pump only removes its small active contribution, resulting in no immediate change because RMP is mainly determined by diffusion potentials.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: Because the metabolic pump directly contributes only about -4\text{ mV} to the resting potential, its arrest does not cause an immediate depolarization due to absolute pump failure.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Thermal slowing of the active transporter does not cause immediate structural closure of passive leak pathways, ruling out an immediate depolarization due to K+ channel closure.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Passive leak pathways remain open immediately after cooling, so an immediate hyperpolarization due to reduced Na+ permeability does not occur.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: Potassium leak currents are driven by diffusion gradients that persist initially, ruling out an immediate hyperpolarization due to reduced K+ permeability.

### Question 16
In a neuron where Na+ permeability suddenly becomes equal to K+ permeability, the membrane potential would move closest to:
A. average of Na+ and K+ equilibrium potentials.
B. maximum value of Ca2+ equilibrium potential.
C. the baseline K+ equilibrium potential.
D. the baseline Na+ equilibrium potential.
E. minimum value of Cl- equilibrium potential.
**Correct Option: A**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (A)**: According to the Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz equation, if the permeabilities of two ions become equal, the membrane potential will settle near the mathematical average of Na+ and K+ equilibrium potentials.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Calcium conductance parameters are not involved in this permeability shift, so the potential does not move toward the maximum value of Ca2+ equilibrium potential.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Increased sodium influx depolarizes the cell, pulling the membrane potential away from the baseline K+ equilibrium potential.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: Because potassium permeability remains high and counteracts the sodium influx, the membrane potential cannot reach the baseline Na+ equilibrium potential.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: This equal-permeability state is driven entirely by sodium and potassium dynamics, making the minimum value of Cl- equilibrium potential irrelevant.

### Question 17
A patient with severe hypocalcemia presents with muscle spasms and tetany. Which physiological mechanism explains the increased neuronal excitability?
A. Decreased threshold potential due to increased K+ channel permeability
B. Decreased threshold potential due to increased Na+ channel permeability
C. Increased threshold potential due to decreased K+ channel permeability
D. Increased threshold potential due to decreased Na+ channel permeability
E. Increased threshold potential due to increased Cl- channel permeability
**Correct Option: B**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (B)**: Extracellular calcium ions normally bind to and stabilize the exterior of voltage-gated sodium channels; a drop in calcium lowers the voltage required for activation, leading to a decreased threshold potential due to increased Na+ channel permeability.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: This hypocalcemia-induced hyperexcitability is caused by alterations in sodium channel gating, not a decreased threshold potential due to increased K+ channel permeability.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Lowering extracellular calcium increases excitability by making the threshold more negative, contradicting an increased threshold potential due to decreased K+ channel permeability.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: Hypocalcemia causes the activation threshold to shift to a less positive (more negative) voltage, making an increased threshold potential due to decreased Na+ channel permeability incorrect.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Chloride channel mechanics are not involved in hypocalcemic tetany, ruling out an increased threshold potential due to increased Cl- channel permeability.

Question 25: During the relative refractory period of an action potential, which ionic condition is primarily responsible for reduced excitability:
A. complete Na+ channel inactivation
B. closure of K+ channels
C. increased K+ permeability causing hyperpolarization
D. persistent Na+ channel opening
E. reduced Cl– permeability causing depolarization
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
 * Correct (C): Excitability is reduced during this period because increased K+ permeability causing hyperpolarization moves the membrane potential further below the resting level, requiring a stronger stimulus to hit threshold.
 * Incorrect (A): A complete Na+ channel inactivation defines the absolute refractory period, rendering the nerve completely unexcitable rather than relatively unexcitable.
 * Incorrect (B): The closure of K+ channels would decrease potassium efflux, making the cell easier to depolarize rather than reducing its excitability.
 * Incorrect (D): A persistent Na+ channel opening would maintain depolarization and prevent the cell from entering a state where a stronger stimulus is needed to re-fire.
 * Incorrect (E): A reduced Cl– permeability causing depolarization would make the cell more excitable by bringing the membrane potential closer to threshold.

Question 33: Tetanization does not occur in cardiac muscle because:
A. due to decreased refractory period
B. due to gap junctions
C. due to increased Ca2+ influx
D. due to plateau phase
E. due to prolonged refractory period
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): Tetanization is prevented because, due to prolonged refractory period, the cardiac muscle cannot be re-excited until mechanical relaxation has already begun, preventing wave summation.
 * Incorrect (A): If the heart suffered a decreased refractory period, it would allow premature electrical stimulation and increase the risk of tetanic contraction.
 * Incorrect (B): While gap junctions allow the myocardium to function as a functional syncytium, they do not govern the electrical refractoriness that prevents tetanus.
 * Incorrect (C): An increased Ca2+ influx acts as the ion source for contraction and the plateau, but it is the duration of the electrical refractoriness itself that directly stops tetanization.
 * Incorrect (D): The plateau phase is the underlying cause of the long refractory period, but the question asks why tetanization is prevented, which is due to prolonged refractory period itself.

Question 36: A demyelinated nerve fiber in Multiple sclerosis most commonly shows:
A. failure of impulse propagation
B. increased conduction velocity
C. increased muscle contractility
D. increased neurotransmitter release
E. reduced absolute refractory period
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): Without myelin insulation, the longitudinal current leaks across the bare axonal membrane, dropping the potential below threshold and causing a failure of impulse propagation.
 * Incorrect (B): Stripping myelin exposes leaky membrane patches, which drastically decreases rather than causing increased conduction velocity.
 * Incorrect (C): The central loss of signal propagation leads to muscle weakness and paresis rather than increased muscle contractility.
 * Incorrect (D): The breakdown of axonal conduction leads to fewer action potentials reaching the nerve terminal, resulting in lower, not increased neurotransmitter release.
 * Incorrect (E): Demyelination typically prolongs or disrupts channel recovery states, making a reduced absolute refractory period biochemically incorrect.

Question 37: In Multiple sclerosis, symptoms often worsen with increased body temperature because:
A. heat further impairs conduction in demyelinated axons
B. heat increases muscle metabolism
C. heat reduces acetylcholine release
D. heat stimulates antibody production
E. heat suppresses Na+–K+ pump activity
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): This phenomenon (Uhthoff's symptom) occurs because heat further impairs conduction in demyelinated axons by altering the gating kinetics of leaky voltage-gated Na+ channels, worsening the conduction block.
 * Incorrect (B): While heat increases muscle metabolism slightly, this systemic metabolic shift does not cause the transient, focal neurological deficits seen in MS patients.
 * Incorrect (C): The clinical worsening seen with Uhthoff's phenomenon is an axonal conduction failure, not because heat reduces acetylcholine release at the peripheral synapse.
 * Incorrect (D): Acute temperature-induced symptom spikes happen within minutes, which is far too rapid to be driven by a mechanism where heat stimulates antibody production.
 * Incorrect (E): The transient worsening is caused by altered sodium channel inactivation kinetics in bare axons, not because heat suppresses Na+–K+ pump activity.
