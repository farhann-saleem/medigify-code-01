
Question: All the following statements are true except:
A. basilic vein continues into the axilla as the axillary vein.
B. cephalic vein arises from the lateral side of the venous arch.
C. deep veins have more numerous valves than superficial veins.
D. dorsal metacarpal veins unite to form the dorsal venous arch.
E. medial cubital vein shunts blood from basilic to cephalic vein.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): medial cubital vein shunts blood from basilic to cephalic vein. is the false statement because blood flow through the median (medial) cubital vein runs obliquely upward from the *cephalic* vein to the *basilic* vein.
 * Incorrect (A): basilic vein continues into the axilla as the axillary vein. is a true anatomical fact occurring at the inferior margin of the teres major muscle.
 * Incorrect (B): cephalic vein arises from the lateral side of the venous arch. is anatomically true as it originates from the radial side of the dorsal venous network.
 * Incorrect (C): deep veins have more numerous valves than superficial veins. is a true vascular physiology principle designed to counter higher hydrostatic pressures deep in musculature.
 * Incorrect (D): dorsal metacarpal veins unite to form the dorsal venous arch. is a true anatomical statement regarding the formation of the superficial venous system on the dorsum of the hand.

Question: In the upper limb:
A. lower trunk lesions of brachial plexus will affect abduction of fingers
B. proximal humeral epiphyses fuses at 12 years of age
C. proximal radioulnar joint does not communicate with elbow joint
D. radial artery is the main arterial supply of the forearm
E. section of radial nerve in radial groove leads to weakness of extension
**Correct Option: A**
**Explanation:**
 * **Correct (A):** A lower trunk lesion (C_8, T_1) damages the axons destined for the ulnar nerve, paralyzing all interossei muscles which leads to a loss of abduction of fingers.
 * **Incorrect (B):** The proximal humeral epiphyses fuses significantly later, typically between 18 to 21 years of age, not at 12 years.
 * **Incorrect (C):** The proximal radioulnar joint shares its synovial cavity directly and communicates freely with the elbow joint.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The ulnar artery, through its larger caliber and common interosseous branch, is typically considered the main arterial supply of the forearm.
 * **Incorrect (E):** Sectioning the radial nerve in the radial groove leaves the long head of triceps intact but causes a total loss (paralysis) of wrist and finger extension, rather than mere "weakness" of extension.

Question:  All the following statements are true except:
A. basilic vein continues into the axilla as the axillary vein
B. cephalic vein arises from the lateral side of the dorsal venous arch
C. deep veins have more numerous valves as compared to the superficial veins
D. dorsal metacarpal veins form the dorsal venous arch
E. medial cubital vein shunts blood from the basilic vein into the cephalic vein
**Correct Option: E**
**Explanation:**
 * **Correct (E):** The median cubital vein shunts blood in the opposite direction, running obliquely upward from the lateral *cephalic vein* into the medial *basilic vein* within the cubital fossa.
 * **Incorrect (A):** The basilic vein passes deep near the lower border of the teres major muscle to directly continue into the axilla as the axillary vein.
 * **Incorrect (B):** The cephalic vein reliably originates anatomically from the lateral side of the dorsal venous network of the hand.
 * **Incorrect (C):** Deep veins of the upper limb run against gravity accompanied by muscular action, thereby possessing more numerous valves than superficial veins.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The dorsal metacarpal veins merge proximally to form the clinical dorsal venous arch on the dorsum of the hand.

Question: The forearm remains in supination because of loss of function of this nerve:
A. axillary nerve
B. median nerve
C. musculocutaneous nerve
D. radial nerve
E. ulnar nerve
**Correct Option: B**
**Explanation:**
 * **Correct (B):** The median nerve innervates both the pronator teres and pronator quadratus muscles; losing its function leaves the supinator muscles unopposed, keeping the forearm in supination.
 * **Incorrect (A):** The axillary nerve supplies the deltoid and teres minor muscles and exerts no motor control over forearm rotation.
 * **Incorrect (C):** The musculocutaneous nerve supplies the biceps brachii (a powerful supinator); its loss would favor pronation rather than leaving the arm stuck in supination.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The radial nerve supplies the supinator muscle; an isolated loss would impair supination, leaving the forearm prone to unresisted pronation.
 * **Incorrect (E):** The ulnar nerve controls intrinsic hand muscles and specific wrist flexors but plays no mechanical role in forearm pronation or supination.

Question: Regarding the venous drainage of the upper limb, which of the following statements is incorrect?
A. The basilic vein continues superiorly into the axilla as the axillary vein.
B. The cephalic vein arises anatomically from the lateral side of the dorsal venous arch.
C. The deep veins of the upper limb possess fewer valves than the corresponding superficial veins.
D. The dorsal metacarpal veins coalesce superiorly to form the formal dorsal venous arch.
E. The median cubital vein shunts blood directly from the cephalic vein into the basilic vein.
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
 * **Correct (C):** The statement is incorrect because deep veins (venae comitantes) of limbs actually possess *more* valves than superficial veins to effectively counteract gravity via the skeletal muscle pump.
 * **Incorrect (A):** This statement is correct; the basilic vein merges with the brachial veins at the lower border of the teres major to form the axillary vein.
 * **Incorrect (B):** This statement is correct; the cephalic vein originates from the lateral side of the dorsal venous network of the hand.
 * **Incorrect (D):** This statement is correct; the dorsal metacarpal veins drain upward from the fingers to establish the formal dorsal venous arch.
 * **Incorrect (E):** This statement is correct; the median cubital vein runs obliquely in the cubital fossa, shunting blood from the lateral cephalic vein to the medial basilic vein.

A. Basilic vein continues into the axilla as the axillary vein
B. Cephalic vein arises from the dorsal venous arch
C. Deep veins have more numerous valves as compared to the superficial veins
D. Dorsal metacarpal veins form the dorsal venous arch
E. Medial cubital vein shunts blood from the basilic vein into the cephalic vein
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * **Correct (E):** The median cubital vein shunts blood in the opposite direction, diverting blood from the cephalic vein into the basilic vein within the cubital fossa.
 * **Incorrect (A):** The basilic vein runs deeply at the lower border of the teres major to directly continue into the axilla as the axillary vein.
 * **Incorrect (B):** The cephalic vein originates laterally from the dorsal venous arch of the hand before ascending the arm.
 * **Incorrect (C):** To counter high muscular pressures, deep veins have more numerous valves as compared to the superficial veins.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The dorsal metacarpal veins drain the digits and coalesce proximally to form the dorsal venous arch on the back of the hand.
