 
Question: A patient falls on a flexed hip and experiences posterior dislocation. On examination, adduction and internal rotation of the thigh are observed. Which ligament is most likely ruptured?
A. Iliofemoral ligament
B. Ischiofemoral ligament
C. Ligamentum teres
D. Pubofemoral ligament
E. Zona orbicularis
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The ischiofemoral ligament reinforces the hip joint capsule posteriorly and is structurally ruptured or severely torn when a posterior dislocation occurs.
Incorrect (A): The iliofemoral ligament is located anteriorly and resists hyperextension, remaining intact during standard posterior dislocation mechanics.
Incorrect (C): The ligamentum teres is an intracapsular conduit for a small artery to the head of the femur; while it may tear, it is not a primary capsular reinforcing ligament stabilizing posterior displacement.
Incorrect (D): The pubofemoral ligament is positioned anteroinferiorly on the joint capsule, preventing excessive abduction rather than posterior displacement.
Incorrect (E): The zona orbicularis forms a circular collar around the femoral neck but is not a directional reinforcing ligament stabilizing against posterior luxation.
