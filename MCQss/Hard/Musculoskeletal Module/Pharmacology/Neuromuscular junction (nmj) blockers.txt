
Question: Myalgias are a common postoperative complaint of patients who receive large doses of succinylcholine, possibly the result of muscle fasciculations caused by depolarization. Which drug administered in the operating room can be used to prevent postoperative pain caused by succinylcholine:
A. atracurium
B. baclofen
C. dantrolene
D. diazepam
E. lidocaine
Correct Option: E
Explanation:

* ​Correct (E): Intravenous lidocaine stabilizes membrane potentials and reduces the severity of succinylcholine-induced muscle fasciculations, preventing postoperative myalgias.
* ​Incorrect (A): Atracurium is a non-depolarizing NMJ blocker; while pre-curarization with low-dose non-depolarizing blockers can reduce fasciculations, it requires careful titration to avoid premature paralysis.
* ​Incorrect (B): Baclofen is a centrally acting GABA-B receptor agonist used for chronic spasticity, not for acute intraoperative prevention of succinylcholine-induced myalgias.
* ​Incorrect (C): Dantrolene targets ryanodine receptors to treat malignant hyperthermia but is not indicated for the routine prevention of benign postoperative myalgias.
* ​Incorrect (D): Diazepam is a benzodiazepine that acts centrally via GABA-A receptors, lacking a direct effect on the peripheral muscle fasciculations caused by succinylcholine at the endplate.

Question: Which drug is most likely to cause hyperkalemia leading to cardiac arrest in patients with spinal cord injuries?
A. Atracurium
B. Cisatracurium
C. Pancuronium
D. Succinylcholine
E. Vecuronium
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

* ​Correct (D): Succinylcholine is a depolarizing neuromuscular blocker that causes sustained opening of nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, leading to an efflux of intracellular potassium that can trigger severe hyperkalemia and cardiac arrest in patients with up-regulated receptors from spinal cord injuries.
* ​Incorrect (A): Atracurium is a non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocker that prevents receptor activation and does not cause intracellular potassium efflux or hyperkalemia.
* ​Incorrect (B): Cisatracurium is an isomer of atracurium that operates as a non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocker, meaning it does not alter cellular potassium gradients.
* ​Incorrect (C): Pancuronium is a long-acting non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocker that does not depolarize the motor end plate or cause significant hyperkalemia.
* ​Incorrect (E): Vecuronium is an intermediate-acting non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocker that blocks acetylcholine competitively without causing potassium release.
