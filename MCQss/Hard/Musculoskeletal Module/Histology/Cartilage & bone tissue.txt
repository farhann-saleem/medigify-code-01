Question: The periosteum is firmly attached to compact bone by:
A. canalicular processes from endosteal osteocytes
B. circumferential lamellae of the outer cortex
C. dense collagenous fibers from Volkmann's canals
D. perforating bundles of Sharpey's fibers
E. thin cytoplasmic processes of osteoblasts
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Perforating bundles of Sharpey's fibers are thick bundles of type I collagen that extend from the periosteum into the outer circumferential lamellae to securely anchor the periosteum to bone.
Incorrect (A): Canalicular processes from endosteal osteocytes are thin cell processes localized within the canaliculi inside the bone matrix and do not bridge the periosteum.
Incorrect (B): Circumferential lamellae of the outer cortex form the linear bony layers beneath the periosteum but do not themselves constitute an active anchoring mechanism.
Incorrect (C): Dense collagenous fibers from Volkmann's canals run through vascular channels perpendicular to the osteon and do not act as the primary external anchoring fibers.
Incorrect (E): Thin cytoplasmic processes of osteoblasts are short cellular structures involved in appositional growth and cell communication, not large structural anchoring bundles.

Question: Variety of bone cells most commonly involved in primary carcinoma of bone "Osteosarcoma":
A. chondrocytes
B. osteoblasts
C. osteoclasts
D. osteocytes
E. osteoprogenitor cells
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Osteosarcoma is a malignant bone tumor characterized histologically by the uncontrolled proliferation of cancerous osteoblasts that produce neoplastic, unmineralized osteoid.
Incorrect (A): Chondrocytes are cartilage-producing cells involved in chondrosarcomas, a distinct class of malignant skeletal tumors.
Incorrect (C): Osteoclasts are multinucleated bone-resorbing cells derived from the monocyte-macrophage lineage, not the mesenchymal origin cell of an osteosarcoma.
Incorrect (D): Osteocytes are terminally differentiated, quiescent bone cells trapped in lacunae that do not undergo rapid neoplastic mitotic division.
Incorrect (E): Osteoprogenitor cells are stem cells that can give rise to osteosarcomas, but the tumor cells themselves display an active, bone-matrix-producing osteoblast phenotype.

Question: In "Rickets & Osteomalacia" there is osteoid formation but there is no mineralization of osteoid. Keeping in view the functions of bone cells, which cells are actually pathologic:
A. chondrocytes
B. osteoblasts
C. osteoclasts
D. osteocytes
E. osteoprogenitor cells
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The osteoblasts are the pathologic cells because, due to a lack of calcium or vitamin D, they fail to properly mineralize the organic osteoid matrix they have synthesized.
Incorrect (A): Chondrocytes are cartilage matrix-producing cells found in growth plates and joints, not the primary cells responsible for the defective mineralization of bone osteoid.
Incorrect (C): Osteoclasts are bone-resorbing cells whose degradative or remodeling functions are not the underlying cause of this failure to lay down mineral minerals.
Incorrect (D): Osteocytes are mature cells encased within the matrix that monitor strain, rather than the active secretory cells failing to execute matrix calcification.
Incorrect (E): Osteoprogenitor cells are quiescent stem cells whose primary function is recruitment and differentiation, not the active synthesis or mineralization of osteoid.
