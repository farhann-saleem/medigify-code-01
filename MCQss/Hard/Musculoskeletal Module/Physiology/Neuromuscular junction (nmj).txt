​Question: A patient presents with muscle rigidity and painful spasms after a contaminated wound. The toxin responsible blocks release of which inhibitory neurotransmitter in the spinal cord:
A. acetylcholine
B. dopamine
C. glutamate
D. glycine
E. serotonin
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (D): Glycine is an essential inhibitory neurotransmitter in the spinal cord whose release from Renshaw cells is blocked by tetanospasmin, causing unchecked motor neuron firing.
 * ​Incorrect (A): acetylcholine is incorrect because acetylcholine is an excitatory neurotransmitter at the neuromuscular junction, and blocking it would yield flaccid paralysis instead of rigidity.
 * ​Incorrect (B): dopamine is incorrect because dopamine functions primarily within central motor pathways like the substantia nigra, not as the primary spinal reflex inhibitor.
 * ​Incorrect (C): glutamate is incorrect because glutamate is the primary central excitatory neurotransmitter; blocking it would reduce excitation and muscle tone.
 * ​Incorrect (E): serotonin is incorrect because serotonin modulates mood, pain, and sleep pathways and is not the targeted spinal inhibitory neurotransmitter underlying tetanus spasms.

​Question: Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome causes muscle weakness primarily due to:
A. autoimmune destruction of postsynaptic acetylcholine receptors
B. defective troponin–tropomyosin conformational complex function
C. increased acetylcholine breakdown within the synaptic cleft
D. reduced Ca²⁺ entry into presynaptic nerve terminals
E. reduced Na⁺ entry through voltage-gated sarcolemmal channels
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (D): Reduced Ca²⁺ entry into presynaptic nerve terminals occurs because autoantibodies target and destroy voltage-gated calcium channels, impairing downstream neurotransmitter exocytosis.
 * ​Incorrect (A): autoimmune destruction of postsynaptic acetylcholine receptors is incorrect because this describes the pathophysiological mechanism of myasthenia gravis, not Lambert-Eaton syndrome.
 * ​Incorrect (B): defective troponin–tropomyosin conformational complex function is incorrect because the intracellular contractile apparatus remains entirely functional in this disease.
 * ​Incorrect (C): increased acetylcholine breakdown within the synaptic cleft is incorrect because acetylcholinesterase levels and activity remain normal within the junctional cleft.
 * ​Incorrect (E): reduced Na⁺ entry through voltage-gated sarcolemmal channels is incorrect because muscle action potentials are not impaired by antibodies targeting voltage-gated sodium channels.

​Question: Succinylcholine causes muscle paralysis mainly by:

B. inhibiting acetylcholine release from presynaptic vesicles
C. preventing Ca²⁺ binding to troponin regulatory complexes
D. producing persistent depolarization of the motor end plate
E. stimulating rapid clearance of calcium into sarcoplasmic reticulum
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (D): Producing persistent depolarization of the motor end plate renders adjacent voltage-gated sodium channels inactivated, preventing the propagation of new action potentials.
 * ​Incorrect (A): blocking acetylcholine receptors competitively without activation is incorrect because this describes non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockers such as vecuronium or curare.
 * ​Incorrect (B): inhibiting acetylcholine release from presynaptic vesicles is incorrect because succinylcholine acts exclusively as a postsynaptic agonist and does not affect vesicular release.
 * ​Incorrect (C): preventing Ca²⁺ binding to troponin regulatory complexes is incorrect because succinylcholine does not interact with intracellular myofilament proteins.
 * ​Incorrect (E): stimulating rapid clearance of calcium into sarcoplasmic reticulum is incorrect because it does not modulate SERCA pumps or intracellular calcium sequestration rates.

 * ​Incorrect (A): blockade of voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in presynaptic nerve terminals is incorrect because organophosphates do not alter calcium entry kinetics at the nerve terminal.

 * ​Incorrect (D): reduced acetylcholine synthesis in presynaptic motor nerve terminals is incorrect because synthesis pathways remain active; the pathology is entirely driven by accumulation in the synapse.

B. failure of Ca²⁺ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum
C. rapid depletion of adenosine triphosphate stores
D. reduced Na⁺ permeability in skeletal muscle fibers
E. slowed clearance of acetylcholine from synaptic clefts
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): Decreased number of functional acetylcholine receptors causes the muscle to fail to reach threshold as normal presynaptic rundown occurs with repetitive stimulation.
 * ​Incorrect (B): failure of Ca²⁺ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum is incorrect because the sarcoplasmic intracellular machinery is capable of working normally if an action potential can be successfully generated.
 * ​Incorrect (C): rapid depletion of adenosine triphosphate stores is incorrect because the weakness is a failure of electrical transmission, not metabolic exhaustion or depletion of high-energy phosphates.
 * ​Incorrect (D): reduced Na⁺ permeability in skeletal muscle fibers is incorrect because voltage-gated sodium channels themselves are structurally intact and open normally once threshold is met.
 * ​Incorrect (E): slowed clearance of acetylcholine from synaptic clefts is incorrect because clearance is not slowed; rather, the standard rate of clearance cannot compensate for the lack of functional target receptors.

Correct Option: D
Explanation:

 * Incorrect (A): The autoimmune pathology is localized strictly to the postsynaptic muscle membrane, meaning action potentials fail in motor neurons is incorrect as nerve conduction remains entirely normal.
 * Incorrect (B): Cellular metabolic pathways remain intact, so adenosine triphosphate production decreases in muscle cells does not occur.
 * Incorrect (C): Intracellular excitation-contraction coupling machinery is unimpeded, meaning calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum is blocked is false.
 * Incorrect (E): Postsynaptic voltage-gated sodium channels function normally but are physically missing or disorganized due to simplified junctional folds, rather than being permanently inactivated.

Question: In Myasthenia gravis, the extraocular muscles are commonly affected first mainly because they:
A. contain mostly smooth muscle fibers.
B. have fewer neuromuscular junctions.
C. lack functional troponin complexes.
D. rely solely on anaerobic glycolysis.
E. require high-frequency neuromuscular transmission.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): The continuous, rapid movements of the eyes require high-frequency neuromuscular transmission, making these muscles highly susceptible to even a minor drop in the neuromuscular safety factor.
 * Incorrect (A): Extraocular muscles are highly specialized skeletal muscles, meaning the claim that they contain mostly smooth muscle fibers is anatomically incorrect.
 * Incorrect (B): These muscles feature exceptionally small motor units and dense innervation, meaning they have a higher density rather than fewer neuromuscular junctions.
 * Incorrect (C): As skeletal muscle tissue, they possess fully functional troponin complexes to regulate actin-myosin cross-bridge cycling.
 * Incorrect (D): Extraocular muscles are packed with mitochondria and possess a rich blood supply to support aerobic metabolism, meaning they do not rely solely on anaerobic glycolysis.
