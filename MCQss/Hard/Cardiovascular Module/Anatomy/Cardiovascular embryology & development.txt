Question: A child born with craniofacial defects and transposition of the great vessels most likely has abnormal development of:
A. Ectoderm
B. Endoderm
C. Hypoblast
D. Mesoderm
E. Neural crest cells
Correct Option: E
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (E): Neural crest cells migrate extensively from the dorsal neural tube to form both the viscerocranium skeletal structures and the spiral aorticopulmonary septum; thus, their disruption causes paired craniofacial and outflow tract anomalies.
Incorrect (A): Standard surface ectoderm forms the epidermis and central nervous nervous tissue elements, but does not migrate to form the cardiac septa or facial bones.
Incorrect (B): The endoderm lines the epithelial tracts of the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts, but does not differentiate into facial bones or outflow structures.
Incorrect (C): The hypoblast is an early blastocyst layer involved in establishing embryonic polarity and forming the yolk sac membrane, having no direct role in late organogenesis.
Incorrect (D): General mesoderm forms the myocardium and skeletal muscles, but defects strictly confined to the combination of facial bones and truncal septation point selectively to neural crest cell failure.

Question: The ductus arteriosus persists after birth in part due to failure of oxygen-induced smooth muscle constriction. This smooth muscle originates embryologically from:
A. Endocardial cushion tissue
B. Mesothelium of the pericardial cavity
C. Neural crest–derived mesenchyme of the sixth arch
D. Somatic mesoderm of lateral plate
E. Vitelline arterial mesenchyme
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
 
Correct (C): The vascular smooth muscle layer of the fourth and sixth aortic arches is uniquely composed of Neural crest–derived mesenchyme of the sixth arch, which mediates the critical postnatal contractile response to oxygen.
Incorrect (A): Endocardial cushion tissue is localized to the atrioventricular and conotruncal boundaries, contributing to valves and septa, not the great vessel media.
Incorrect (B): The mesothelium of the pericardial cavity forms the epicardium and visceral pericardium layers, not the media of the branchial arch arteries.
Incorrect (D): Somatic mesoderm of lateral plate forms the body wall structures and skeletal components, not the branchial arch vascular media.
Incorrect (E): Vitelline arterial mesenchyme forms the vascular wall components of abdominal vessels supplying the midgut loop.

Question: A double aortic arch that forms a vascular ring around the trachea and esophagus results from persistence of:
A. both right and left fourth arch–dorsal aorta segments
B. left dorsal aorta between arches III and IV
C. right dorsal aorta distal to seventh intersegmental artery
D. right sixth arch distal segment
E. ventral aortic sac
Correct Option: A
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (A): A double aortic arch occurs when both right and left fourth arch–dorsal aorta segments persist, failure of the normal regression of the distal right dorsal aorta leads to a vascular ring surrounding the trachea and esophagus.
Incorrect (B): Abnormalities of the left dorsal aorta between arches III and IV typically disrupt the normal development of the common carotid and internal carotid system rather than forming a vascular ring.
Incorrect (C): Persistence or regression of the right dorsal aorta distal to seventh intersegmental artery is associated with an interrupted aortic arch or an aberrant right subclavian artery, not a double arch.
Incorrect (D): Persistence of the right sixth arch distal segment results in a right-sided ductus arteriosus, which does not independently encircle the trachea and esophagus as a double arch.
Incorrect (E): The ventral aortic sac contributes to the proximal ascending aorta and brachiocephalic trunk, and its abnormal partitioning does not cause the formation of a double aortic arch vascular ring.
