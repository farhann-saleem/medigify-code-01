Question: Blood flow velocity in a narrowed femoral artery is measured. The internal diameter is 0.4 cm, the average blood velocity is 80 cm/sec, the blood density is 1.05 g/cm³, and the viscosity is 0.03 g/cm·sec. Calculate the Reynolds number (Re) and determine its physiological consequence.
A. Re = 560; laminar flow with no disturbance
B. Re = 1120; stable laminar flow without bruit formation
C. Re = 2240; transitional flow with possible turbulence and bruit
D. Re = 4480; fully laminar silent flow despite high velocity
E. Re = 3200; complete flow cessation due to resistance
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Incorrect (A): Too low for given velocity and diameter.
Incorrect (B): Still laminar threshold underestimated.
Incorrect (D): Laminar cannot persist at high Reynolds number.
Incorrect (E): Flow does not cease at high Re.

Question: A child is diagnosed with Tetralogy of Fallot, a congenital heart defect. What four anatomical abnormalities characterize this condition, and what is its primary physiological consequence?
A. Atrial septal defect, mitral stenosis, left ventricular hypertrophy, low pulmonary pressure; causing pulmonary overcirculation.
B. Patent ductus arteriosus, coarctation of the aorta, left axis deviation, heart block; causing systemic hypoxemia.
C. Tricuspid atresia, coronary aneurysm, high cardiac output, polyuria; causing volume overload.
D. Ventricular septal defect, pulmonary artery stenosis, overriding aorta, right ventricular hypertrophy; causing a right-to-left shunt and cyanosis.
E. Ventricular septal defect, tricuspid regurgitation, left ventricular hypertrophy, aortic stenosis; causing left-to-right shunting.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Tetralogy of Fallot consists of ventricular septal defect, pulmonary artery stenosis, overriding aorta, and right ventricular hypertrophy, producing a right-to-left shunt and cyanosis.
Incorrect (A): These abnormalities do not comprise Tetralogy of Fallot.
Incorrect (B): Patent ductus arteriosus and coarctation of the aorta are separate congenital defects.
Incorrect (C): Tricuspid atresia and coronary aneurysm are not components of Tetralogy of Fallot.
Incorrect (E): Tetralogy of Fallot does not include tricuspid regurgitation or left ventricular hypertrophy.

Question: According to the Law of Laplace (Tension = Pressure × Radius / Wall Thickness), why
does a dilated, failing heart require more oxygen for the same amount of work?
A. Because aortic pressure is always markedly higher in heart failure
B. Because heart rate is always lower in heart failure
C. Because increased radius requires greater wall tension to generate the same pressure
D. Because wall thickness increases substantially during ventricular dilation
E. Because wall tension decreases as ventricular radius increases
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): An increased ventricular radius requires greater wall tension to generate the same
pressure, increasing myocardial oxygen demand.
Incorrect (A): Elevated aortic pressure is not required to explain increased oxygen consumption
in dilation.
Incorrect (B): Heart rate may vary and is not the primary Laplace-based mechanism.
Incorrect (D): Ventricular dilation typically increases radius more than wall thickness.
Incorrect (E): Wall tension increases, not decreases, as ventricular radius increases.
