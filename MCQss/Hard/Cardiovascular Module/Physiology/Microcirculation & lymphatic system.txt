Question: Trace the physiological steps that lead to cardiac dyspnea and acute pulmonary edema in left ventricular myocardial infarction.
 A. Right ventricular failure causes systemic venous collapse
 B. SA node degeneration leads to conduction block
 C. Left ventricular failure causes pulmonary venous pressure rise and alveolar edema
 D. Aortic valve closure failure causes systemic hypotension only
 E. Coronary artery spasm reduces pulmonary vascular resistance
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 • Correct (C): LV failure elevates left atrial and pulmonary venous pressures, driving fluid into alveoli and causing dyspnea.
 • Incorrect (A): RV failure causes systemic congestion, not pulmonary edema.
 • Incorrect (B): SA node degeneration is a conduction disorder, not pulmonary edema mechanism.
 • Incorrect (D): Aortic valve issues do not directly explain alveolar fluid accumulation.
 • Incorrect (E): Coronary spasm affects myocardial perfusion, not pulmonary vascular resistance.

Question: A 22-year-old female presents with severe dyspnea, hemoptysis, and a low-pitched, rumbling diastolic murmur heard best at the apex with the patient in the left lateral decubitus position. Explain the pathophysiology connecting her valvular lesion to her pulmonary symptoms.


C. Pulmonary valve stenosis causes left ventricular volume overload and pulmonary edema.
D. Tricuspid regurgitation induces severe left atrial atrophy and alveolar hemorrhage.
E. Ventricular septal defect lowers pulmonary capillary pressure and causes hemoptysis.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Mitral stenosis elevates left atrial and pulmonary capillary pressures, causing pulmonary edema and leakage of erythrocytes into alveoli, leading to hemoptysis.
Incorrect (B): Aortic regurgitation does not produce pulmonary symptoms through suppression of erythrocyte destruction.
Incorrect (C): Pulmonary valve stenosis does not directly cause left ventricular volume overload.
Incorrect (D): Tricuspid regurgitation does not cause severe left atrial atrophy as the mechanism of hemoptysis.
Incorrect (E): Ventricular septal defect generally increases, not lowers, pulmonary blood flow and pressure.
