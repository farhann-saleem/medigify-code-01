 A. Reduced heart rate prolongs diastole
 B. Increased stroke volume with rapid diastolic backflow
 C. Increased total peripheral resistance in systole
 D. Complete loss of arterial compliance
 E. Equalization of pulmonary and systemic pressures
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:

Question: A patient is brought to the emergency department with a severe spinal cord injury from a motorcycle accident. Their blood pressure is 75/40 mmHg, but their heart rate is slow at 50 beats/min and their skin is warm and dry. What type of shock is this, and what is the mechanism?
A. Cardiogenic shock due to direct heart trauma

D. Septic shock from an infected wound

Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Neurogenic shock results from loss of sympathetic vasomotor tone after spinal cord injury, causing vasodilation, hypotension, and bradycardia with warm dry skin.
Incorrect (A): Cardiogenic shock would present with tachycardia and cold clammy skin due to pump failure.
Incorrect (C): Hypovolemic shock causes tachycardia and cool clammy skin, not bradycardia and warmth.
Incorrect (D): Septic shock is associated with infection-driven systemic inflammation rather than isolated spinal injury.
Incorrect (E): Obstructive shock (e.g., pulmonary embolism) causes tachycardia and hypoxia, not isolated bradycardia with warm skin.
