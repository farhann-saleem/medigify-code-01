Question 4
A patient's ECG reveals a mean electrical axis of +140 degrees. Which underlying physical or pathological condition best explains this finding?
A. Deep expiration while lying down
B. Left ventricular hypertrophy
C. Pulmonary hypertension with left atrial enlargement
D. Right ventricular hypertrophy due to pulmonary valve stenosis
E. Systemic arterial hypertension
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Right ventricular hypertrophy due to pulmonary valve stenosis increases right ventricular electrical forces, causing marked right axis deviation toward +140 degrees.
Incorrect (A): Deep expiration while lying down may shift the axis leftward rather than markedly rightward.
Incorrect (B): Left ventricular hypertrophy produces left axis deviation.
Incorrect (C): Left atrial enlargement does not produce severe rightward ventricular axis deviation.
Incorrect (E): Systemic arterial hypertension commonly causes left ventricular hypertrophy and leftward axis shift.

Question 6
Why does an acute, transmural myocardial infarction of the anterior left ventricular wall cause ST-segment elevation in the precordial leads V1-V4?
a. purkinje conduction slows down exclusively in the posterior wall
b. the baseline (T-P segment) is shifted downward due to an injury current pointing away from the chest electrodes during diastole
c. the injured muscle generates a positive voltage during the plateau phase of the normal cells
d. the SA node increases its voltage output toward the anterior chest
e. ventricular repolarization becomes synchronized with atrial depolarization
Correct Option: b
Explanation:
Correct (b): An injury current pointing away from the chest electrodes during diastole depresses the baseline (T-P segment), making the ST segment appear elevated.
Incorrect (a): Posterior wall Purkinje slowing does not explain anterior ST-segment elevation.
Incorrect (c): Injured myocardium does not generate a positive plateau-phase voltage responsible for this ECG change.
Incorrect (d): SA node voltage output does not determine ST-segment elevation patterns.
Incorrect (e): Synchronization of ventricular repolarization with atrial depolarization does not cause infarction-related ST elevation.

Question 7
Vectorial analysis shows that during a left bundle branch block (LBBB), the QRS complex becomes significantly prolonged to 0.14 seconds, and the mean electrical axis shifts heavily to the left. What explains the prolonged duration and axis shift?
a. depolarization originates from an ectopic atrial focus and bypasses the AV node
b. left ventricular depolarization must occur via slow muscular conduction rather than the rapid Purkinje system, delaying its activation vector
c. the absolute refractory period of the entire myocardium is reduced
d. the right ventricle depolarizes much slower than the left ventricle
e. the SA node bypasses the AV node via the bundle of Bachmann
Correct Option: b
Explanation:
Correct (b): In LBBB, left ventricular depolarization must occur via slow muscular conduction rather than the rapid Purkinje system, prolonging QRS duration and shifting the activation vector leftward.
Incorrect (a): An ectopic atrial focus does not account for bundle branch block physiology.
Incorrect (c): Reduced refractory period does not explain delayed ventricular depolarization.
Incorrect (d): The left ventricle, not the right ventricle, is delayed in LBBB.
Incorrect (e): The bundle of Bachmann conducts between atria and does not bypass the AV node to cause LBBB.

Question 13

Question 16
A patient suffers a myocardial infarction that destroys the upper portion of the interventricular septum, causing a Third-degree (Complete) AV Block. The atria continue to beat at 75 beats/min via the SA node. The ventricles establish an independent escape rhythm. What are the expected ECG characteristics of this ventricular escape rhythm?
a. absent QRS complexes with high-amplitude sinusoidal waves
b. normal QRS complexes at a rate of 100 beats/min, locked in a 1:1 ratio with P waves
c. PR interval prolongation alternating with dropped QRS complexes
d. wide, bizarre QRS complexes at a rate of 15 to 40 beats/min, completely dissociated from P waves
e. wide, bizarre QRS complexes at a rate of 60 to 80 beats/min, synchronized with P waves
Correct Option: d
Explanation:
Correct (d): Ventricular escape rhythm in Third-degree (Complete) AV Block produces wide, bizarre QRS complexes at a rate of 15 to 40 beats/min, completely dissociated from P waves.
Incorrect (a): Ventricular escape rhythms generate QRS complexes rather than their absence.
Incorrect (b): Complete AV block does not maintain a 1:1 P-wave to QRS relationship.
Incorrect (c): This pattern describes second-degree AV block rather than complete block.
Incorrect (e): Ventricular escape rhythms are slower and not synchronized with atrial activity.
