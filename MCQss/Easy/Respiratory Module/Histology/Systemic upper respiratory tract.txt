Question 8: The brush cells in the respiratory epithelium are characterized by the presence of?
A. Cilia.
B. Flagella.
C. Microvilli.
D. Stereocilia.
E. Transport vesicles.
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Brush cells are specialized chemosensory cells defined morphologically by a dense apical tuft of short, blunt microvilli that contact afferent nerve endings.
Incorrect (A): Cilia are motile projections characteristic of ciliated columnar cells that drive mucus clearance, not the sensory projections of brush cells.
Incorrect (B): Flagella are long, whip-like locomotory organelles found on spermatozoa and are not present on any cells of the respiratory epithelium.
Incorrect (D): Stereocilia are elongated, non-motile actin-rich structures found in the epididymis and inner ear, distinct from the short microvilli of brush cells.
Incorrect (E): Transport vesicles are ubiquitous intracellular membrane-bound structures found in all secretory cells, not a distinguishing surface feature of brush cells.

Question 10: The lining epithelium of true vocal cords:
A. pseudostratified ciliated columnar.
B. simple ciliated columnar.
C. simple cuboidal.
D. simple squamous.
E. stratified squamous.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The true vocal cords are lined by stratified squamous epithelium to protect the mucosa against mechanical wear, abrasion, and shear stress caused by high-velocity phonation.
Incorrect (A): Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium lines most of the respiratory tract but cannot withstand the high mechanical friction experienced by the true vocal cords.
Incorrect (B): Simple ciliated columnar epithelium is found in smaller intrapulmonary bronchioles and lacks the multi-layered thickness required for mechanical protection.
Incorrect (C): Simple cuboidal epithelium lines transitional airways like respiratory bronchioles and offers no protection against severe physical abrasion.
Incorrect (D): Simple squamous epithelium is structurally optimized for gas diffusion in the alveoli rather than enduring mechanical frictional forces.
