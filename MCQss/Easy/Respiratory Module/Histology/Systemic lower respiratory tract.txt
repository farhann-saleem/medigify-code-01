Question 1: Which of the following type of cells is a component of Blood-Air barrier?
A. Clara cells
B. Dust cells
C. Goblet cells
D. Type I pneumocytes
E. Type II pneumocytes
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Type I pneumocytes are attenuated squamous cells that comprise over 95% of the alveolar surface area and form the thin epithelial component of the Blood-Air barrier for gas exchange.
Incorrect (A): Clara cells (exocrine bronchiolar cells) are located in bronchioles, not the alveolar wall, and secrete protective proteins.
Incorrect (B): Dust cells are mobile alveolar macrophages that phagocytose debris within the alveolar spaces but do not form the structural Blood-Air barrier.
Incorrect (C): Goblet cells are mucus-secreting cells restricted to upper conducting airways and are completely absent from the alveolar gas-exchange regions.
Incorrect (E): Type II pneumocytes are cuboidal cells that produce surfactant and act as progenitors, but their shape renders them too thick to serve as efficient structural components of the Blood-Air barrier.

Question 4: Surfactant is secreted by which cells?
A. Clara cells
B. Dust cells
C. Goblet cells
D. Type I pneumocytes
E. Type II pneumocytes
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Type II pneumocytes synthesize, store, and secrete pulmonary surfactant from specialized intracellular organelles known as lamellar bodies to reduce alveolar surface tension.
Incorrect (A): Clara cells secrete uteroglobin and surfactant-like defense proteins into bronchioles, but do not produce true alveolar pulmonary surfactant.
Incorrect (B): Dust cells are alveolar macrophages responsible for clearing foreign particulate matter via phagocytosis, not for synthesis or secretion of surfactant.
Incorrect (C): Goblet cells secrete high-molecular-weight mucins that form respiratory mucus, not amphipathic surface-active phospholipids.
Incorrect (D): Type I pneumocytes are highly thin squamous cells structurally optimized for gas diffusion and lack the metabolic machinery to secrete surfactant.

Question 5: Which of the following structure is not a part of conducting zone of respiratory system?
A. Alveoli
B. Bronchi
C. Larynx
D. Nose
E. Trachea
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Alveoli serve as the functional sites of external respiration and gas exchange, placing them strictly in the respiratory zone rather than the conducting zone.
Incorrect (B): Bronchi serve exclusively to channel, warm, and humidify inspired air, making them part of the conducting zone.
Incorrect (C): Larynx acts as a cartilaginous passageway for air and a mechanism for phonation, making it part of the conducting zone.
Incorrect (D): Nose acts as the primary entrance that filters, warms, and humidifies air, classifying it as part of the conducting zone.
Incorrect (E): Trachea is a large cartilaginous tube conducting air from the larynx toward the primary bronchi, classifying it within the conducting zone.

Question 6: Which of the following is a Cartilage containing structure in respiratory system?
A. Alveoli
B. Alveolar ducts
C. Respiratory bronchioles
D. Terminal bronchioles
E. Trachea
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Trachea contains distinctive C-shaped rings of hyaline cartilage within its adventitia to maintain airway patency during inspiration.
Incorrect (A): Alveoli are thin-walled sacs optimized for gas diffusion and completely lack structural support from cartilage.
Incorrect (B): Alveolar ducts are linear arrangements of alveoli lined by a delicate framework of smooth muscle and connective tissue, without cartilage.
Incorrect (C): Respiratory bronchioles represent the transition to the gas exchange zone and completely lack any cartilaginous support.
Incorrect (D): Terminal bronchioles are the smallest segments of the conducting zone and are characterized by a complete absence of cartilage.

Question 11: Epithelial lining of respiratory bronchioles:
A. pseudostratified ciliated columnar.
B. simple ciliated columnar.
C. simple cuboidal.
D. simple squamous.
E. stratified squamous.
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Respiratory bronchioles are primarily lined by simple cuboidal epithelium containing a mix of ciliated cells and exocrine bronchiolar cells as the airway transitions toward gas exchange surfaces.
Incorrect (A): Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium is restricted to larger conducting airways like the trachea and bronchi, disappearing before the bronchiolar level.
Incorrect (B): Simple ciliated columnar epithelium lines the larger terminal bronchioles but thins down to a cuboidal morphology in the respiratory bronchioles.
Incorrect (D): Simple squamous epithelium lines the alveoli proper for gas exchange but does not form the continuous lining of the respiratory bronchiolar walls.
Incorrect (E): Stratified squamous epithelium protects high-friction environments like the pharynx and true vocal cords, and is not found in the delicate lower respiratory tree.

Question 12: Most of the lung surface is covered with which cells?
A. Alveolar brush cells
B. Alveolar macrophages
C. Exocrine bronchiolar cells
D. Type I pneumocytes
E. Type II pneumocytes
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Type I pneumocytes are highly flattened cells that occupy roughly 95% of the internal alveolar surface area, maximizing the structural zone available for passive gas diffusion.
Incorrect (A): Alveolar brush cells are sparse chemosensory cells that comprise a negligible fraction of the total internal alveolar surface.
Incorrect (B): Alveolar macrophages are dynamic, unattached immune cells that scavenge lumens rather than forming a structural covering over the lung surface.
Incorrect (C): Exocrine bronchiolar cells are located in the conducting and transitional bronchioles and do not extend into the alveolar surface environment.
Incorrect (E): Type II pneumocytes are more numerous numerically than Type I cells but, due to their cuboidal shape, cover only about 5% of the alveolar surface.

Question 13: Which cells serve as stem cells to regenerate alveolar cells?
A. Alveolar brush cells
B. Alveolar macrophages
C. Endothelial cells
D. Type I pneumocytes
E. Type II pneumocytes
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Type II pneumocytes act as the resident stem cells of the alveolar epithelium, retaining mitotic capacity to divide and differentiate into both Type I and Type II pneumocytes following tissue injury.
Incorrect (A): Alveolar brush cells are terminally differentiated chemosensory cells and do not possess progenitor or regenerative capabilities.
Incorrect (B): Alveolar macrophages are marrow-derived immune cells that function in phagocytosis and lack the capacity to differentiate into epithelial lineages.
Incorrect (C): Endothelial cells form the inner lining of pulmonary capillaries and can only regenerate vascular tissue, not alveolar epithelial cells.
Incorrect (D): Type I pneumocytes are highly differentiated, terminally specialized cells that cannot undergo cell division or regenerate the alveolar lining.

Question 16: Which structural feature distinguishes between terminal and respiratory bronchioles?
A. Alveoli
B. Cilia
C. Elastic fibers
D. Exocrine bronchiolar cells
E. Smooth muscle
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Respiratory bronchioles are defined by the presence of individual alveoli outpouching directly from their walls, which interrupts the continuous epithelial lining seen in terminal bronchioles.
Incorrect (B): Cilia are present in the epithelia of both terminal and respiratory bronchioles, meaning their presence cannot be used as a defining boundary marker.
Incorrect (C): Elastic fibers are structurally abundant in the walls of both types of bronchioles to maintain radial traction and patency.
Incorrect (D): Exocrine bronchiolar cells (Clara cells) reside prominently within both terminal and respiratory bronchioles, making them non-discriminative.
Incorrect (E): Smooth muscle forms a prominent layer in both terminal and respiratory bronchiolar walls, regulating luminal resistance across both segments.
