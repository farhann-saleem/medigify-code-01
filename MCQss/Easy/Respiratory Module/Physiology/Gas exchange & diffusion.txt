Question 1: The rate of diffusion of gases through the respiratory membrane is inversely proportional to:
A. Diffusion capacity of the respiratory membrane for the gas
B. Pressure gradient across the respiratory membrane
C. Solubility of the gas in the respiratory membrane
D. Surface area of the respiratory membrane
E. Thickness of the respiratory membrane
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): According to Fick's law of diffusion, the rate of gas transfer across a membrane is inversely proportional to the thickness of the respiratory membrane.
 * Incorrect (A): Diffusion capacity of the respiratory membrane for the gas is directly proportional to the rate of gas movement, not inversely.
 * Incorrect (B): A greater pressure gradient across the respiratory membrane increases the driving force, which directly increases the rate of diffusion.
 * Incorrect (C): Higher solubility of the gas in the respiratory membrane enhances its movement, making the diffusion rate directly proportional to solubility.
 * Incorrect (D): A larger surface area of the respiratory membrane provides more space for gas exchange, directly increasing the rate of diffusion.

Question 3: Diffusing capacity of respiratory membrane for oxygen:
A. 10 ml/min/mmHg
B. 21 ml/min/mmHg
C. 50 ml/min/mmHg
D. 120 ml/min/mmHg
E. 400 ml/min/mmHg
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): Under resting conditions, the mean diffusing capacity of respiratory membrane for oxygen in a normal young adult is exactly 21 ml/min/mmHg.
 * Incorrect (A): The value 10 ml/min/mmHg is too low and does not represent the baseline physiological standard for oxygen diffusion.
 * Incorrect (C): The value 50 ml/min/mmHg is typical for carbon dioxide diffusing capacity during rest, not oxygen.
 * Incorrect (D): The value 120 ml/min/mmHg is an incorrect baseline and does not match standard physiological metrics for resting gas exchange.
 * Incorrect (E): The value 400 ml/min/mmHg represents a hyperdynamic state or maximum capacity for highly soluble gases during heavy exercise, not resting oxygen.
