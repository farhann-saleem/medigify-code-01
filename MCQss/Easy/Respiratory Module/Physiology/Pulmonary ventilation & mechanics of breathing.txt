

Question: The principal muscle used during normal quiet respiration is:
A. external intercostal muscles
B. internal intercostal muscles
C. rectus abdominis muscle
D. the diaphragm
E. transversus abdominis muscle
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

Correct (D): The diaphragm is the primary and most essential muscle responsible for driving
the volume changes during normal quiet respiration.

Incorrect (A): The external intercostal muscles assist in quiet inspiration by elevating the ribs,
but they play a secondary role compared to the diaphragm.

Incorrect (B): The internal intercostal muscles are active during forced, active expiration rather
than normal quiet respiration.

Incorrect (C): The rectus abdominis muscle is an accessory muscle of forced expiration and
remains inactive during quiet respiration.

Incorrect (E): The transversus abdominis muscle acts to increase intra-abdominal pressure
during forced expiration, not quiet respiration.

Question: During inspiration, the vertical dimension of the thoracic cavity is increased due to
contraction of:
A. diaphragm
B. external intercostals
C. internal intercostals
D. scalene
E. sternocleidomastoid
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

Correct (A): Contraction of the diaphragm flattens its domes downward, which directly
elongates and increases the vertical dimension of the thoracic cavity.

Incorrect (B): Contraction of the external intercostals elevates the ribs to increase the
anteroposterior and transverse diameters, not the vertical dimension.

Incorrect (C): Contraction of the internal intercostals depresses the ribs during forced
expiration, reducing thoracic volume.

Incorrect (D): The scalene muscles elevate the upper ribs during forced inspiration to increase
the upper anteroposterior diameter, not the vertical dimension.

Incorrect (E): The sternocleidomastoid muscle lifts the sternum during forced, accessory
inspiration rather than driving changes in the vertical dimension.

Question: In bucket-handle movement of ribs, which diameter increases?
A. Anteroposterior
B. Longitudinal
C. Oblique
D. Transverse
E. Vertical
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

Correct (D): The bucket-handle movement of ribs involves the lateral elevation of the lower
shafts, which directly expands the transverse diameter of the thoracic cage.

Incorrect (A): The anteroposterior diameter is increased primarily by the pump-handle
movement of the upper ribs and sternum.

Incorrect (B): The longitudinal diameter is not a standard anatomical term used to describe
the primary dimensions altered by rib cage articulation during breathing.

Incorrect (C): An oblique diameter does not correspond to the specific lateral expansion
driven by the bucket-handle mechanics.

Incorrect (E): The vertical diameter is altered by the downward excursion of the diaphragm
rather than rib movement.

Question: Which of the following statements best explains intrapulmonary pressure?
A. Difference between atmospheric pressure and respiratory pressure
B. Negative pressure in the intrapleural space
C. Pressure within the alveoli of the lungs
D. Pressure within the intercostal muscles
E. Pressure within the pleural cavity
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Correct (C): Intrapulmonary pressure is explicitly defined as the pressure within the alveoli of
the lungs, which fluctuates above and below atmospheric pressure during the respiratory cycle.

Incorrect (A): The difference between atmospheric pressure and respiratory pressure does not
define the localized absolute pressure value within the lungs.

Incorrect (B): The negative pressure in the intrapleural space describes intrapleural pressure,
not intrapulmonary pressure.

Incorrect (D): Pressure within the intercostal muscles refers to interstitial intramuscular
tension, which is completely separate from airway pressures.

Incorrect (E): Pressure within the pleural cavity is the definition of intrapleural pressure, not
intrapulmonary pressure.

Question: Pulmonary compliance is increased by:
A. fibrosis of lungs
B. hering-Breuer reflex
C. pulmonary edema
D. pulmonary surfactant
E. vagotomy
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

Correct (D): Pulmonary surfactant lowers alveolar surface tension, preventing alveolar
collapse and significantly increasing overall pulmonary compliance.

Incorrect (A): Fibrosis of lungs deposits stiff collagen fibers that severely reduce, rather than
increase, lung compliance.

Incorrect (B): The hering-Breuer reflex is a neural mechanism regulating inflation depth and
rate, exerting no direct biophysical effect on compliance.

Incorrect (C): Pulmonary edema floods alveoli and interstitial spaces, increasing stiffness and
drastically lowering compliance.

Incorrect (E): A vagotomy alters the baseline neural airway caliber and breathing rhythm but
does not increase the structural compliance of the lung tissue.

Question: Major component of pulmonary surfactant is:
A. carbohydrates
B. lecithin
C. protein
D. sphingomyelin
E. triglycerides
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

Correct (B): Lecithin (specifically dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine) is the predominant
phospholipid component responsible for the surface tension-reducing properties of pulmonary
surfactant.

Incorrect (A): Carbohydrates are present only in trace amounts within surfactant glycoproteins
and do not comprise a major component.

Incorrect (C): Surfactant proteins (SP-A, B, C, D) are crucial for function but constitute only
about 10% of the total surfactant composition.

Incorrect (D): Sphingomyelin is a minor membrane lipid present in surfactant but its ratio
relative to lecithin is low in functional mature surfactant.

Incorrect (E): Triglycerides are neutral lipids found in minor quantities within the surfactant
mixture, not forming a major component.

Question: Forceful expiration does not involve contraction of:
A. external intercostals
B. external oblique
C. internal intercostals
D. rectus abdominis
E. transversus abdominis
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

Correct (A): The external intercostals function to elevate the ribs during inspiration; therefore,
forceful expiration does not involve contraction of these muscles.

Incorrect (B): The external oblique is an abdominal wall muscle that actively contracts to drive
forced expiration.

Incorrect (C): The internal intercostals contract forcefully to depress the ribs and decrease
thoracic volume during active expiration.

Incorrect (D): The rectus abdominis contracts strongly to push the abdominal contents upward
against the diaphragm during forced expiration.

Incorrect (E): The transversus abdominis is recruited during forceful expiration to compress
the abdominal cavity and maximize expiratory pressure.

Question: In athletes, muscles of active expiration include:
A. diaphragm and scalene
B. diaphragm and sternocleidomastoid
C. external intercostals and scalene
D. external intercostals and sternocleidomastoid
E. rectus abdominis and internal intercostals
Correct Option: E
Explanation:

Correct (E): During heavy exercise in athletes, active expiration is driven by the coordinated
contraction of abdominal muscles like the rectus abdominis and thoracic depressors like the
internal intercostals.

Incorrect (A): The diaphragm and scalene are muscles primarily dedicated to driving
inspiration, not active expiration.

Incorrect (B): The diaphragm and sternocleidomastoid function exclusively during quiet and
forced inspiration respectively.

Incorrect (C): The external intercostals and scalene both act to expand thoracic volume during
inspiration, making them incorrect for expiration.

Incorrect (D): The external intercostals and sternocleidomastoid serve to elevate the rib cage
and sternum during active, forced inspiration.
