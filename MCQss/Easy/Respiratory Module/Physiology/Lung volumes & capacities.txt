Question: Vital capacity:
A. equals inspiratory reserve volume plus residual volume
B. is decreased in pulmonary tuberculosis
C. is increased in pulmonary fibrosis
D. is not affected in obstructive lung diseases
E. is normally 4600 ml
Correct Option: E
Explanation:

Correct (E): The vital capacity is normally 4600 ml in a healthy young adult male.

Incorrect (A): Vital capacity equals inspiratory reserve volume plus tidal volume plus
expiratory reserve volume, not residual volume.

Incorrect (B): Vital capacity is decreased in pulmonary tuberculosis due to structural
parenchymal damage and restrictive changes, rendering this option incorrect as it describes a
correct physiological trend rather than the absolute true definition or standard baseline value
given in E.

Incorrect (C): Vital capacity is increased in pulmonary fibrosis is incorrect because fibrosis
causes restrictive lung disease, which severely reduces vital capacity.

Incorrect (D): Vital capacity is not affected in obstructive lung diseases is false because air
trapping leads to an increased residual volume, which can secondarily reduce vital capacity.

Question: The difference between total lung capacity and vital capacity is:
A. expiratory reserve volume
B. functional residual capacity
C. inspiratory capacity
D. inspiratory reserve volume
E. residual volume
Correct Option: E
Explanation:

Correct (E): The residual volume is the volume of air remaining in the lungs after a maximal
exhalation, representing the exact mathematical difference between total lung capacity and vital
capacity.

Incorrect (A): The expiratory reserve volume is already included within the vital capacity
component.

Incorrect (B): The functional residual capacity includes both expiratory reserve volume and
residual volume, making it larger than the difference between total lung capacity and vital
capacity.

Incorrect (C): The inspiratory capacity is a component of vital capacity, representing tidal
volume plus inspiratory reserve volume.

Incorrect (D): The inspiratory reserve volume is the extra volume of air that can be inspired
over and above the normal tidal volume, which is already accounted for within vital capacity.

Question: The volume of air present in the lungs after normal tidal expiration is:
A. expiratory reserve volume
B. functional residual capacity
C. inspiratory capacity
D. ispiratory reserve volume
E. residual volume
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

Correct (B): The functional residual capacity is the equilibrium volume of air remaining in the
lungs at the end of a normal tidal expiration.

Incorrect (A): The expiratory reserve volume is only the additional volume that can be
forcefully exhaled after a normal tidal expiration, omitting the residual volume.

Incorrect (B): The spelling error in "ispiratory reserve volume" does not affect the fact that
inspiratory reserve volume represents air inspired above tidal volume, not air remaining after
expiration.

Incorrect (D): The inspiratory reserve volume refers to the maximal volume of air that can be
inhaled after a normal inspiration.

Incorrect (E): The residual volume is the volume of air remaining only after a maximal, forceful
expiration, not a normal tidal expiration.

Question: Helium dilution method is used to measure:
A. expiratory reserve volume
B. inspiratory capacity
C. residual volume
D. tidal volume
E. vital capacity
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Correct (C): The residual volume cannot be measured by direct spirometry and requires
indirect volume calculation techniques such as the helium dilution method.

Incorrect (A): The expiratory reserve volume can be directly measured via standard
spirometry.

Incorrect (B): The inspiratory capacity is easily quantified using standard direct spirometry.

Incorrect (D): The tidal volume is the volume of air inspired or expired with each normal breath
and is measured directly via spirometry.

Incorrect (E): The vital capacity can be fully exhaled and registered directly on a standard
spirometer.
