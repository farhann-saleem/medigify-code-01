Question: Which route of administration is commonly used for insulin:
 A. Intramuscular
 B. Intravenous
 C. Oral
 D. Subcutaneous
 E. Topical
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
​Correct (D): Subcutaneous injection provides slow, consistent, and predictable absorption through the vascular connective tissue underlying the skin, making it ideal for routine home-based glycemic control.
​Incorrect (A): Intramuscular administration is avoided for routine insulin delivery because it leads to overly rapid absorption and an increased risk of severe, unpredictable hypoglycemia.
​Incorrect (B): Intravenous delivery is reserved exclusively for acute, emergency settings like diabetic ketoacidosis, as it triggers immediate systemic distribution and clearance.
​Incorrect (C): Oral administration is ineffective because insulin is a protein molecule that is rapidly degraded by proteolytic enzymes and gastric acid in the gastrointestinal tract.
​Incorrect (E): Topical delivery cannot be used because insulin is a large hydrophilic macromolecule that is completely unable to penetrate the thick, lipophilic stratum corneum of intact skin.

Question: A molecule that interacts as an activator or inhibitor and brings about a change in biological function through its chemical actions is known as a:
 A. drug.
 B. hormone.
 C. neurotransmitter.
 D. pheromone.
 E. protein.
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
​Correct (A): A drug is defined broadly in pharmacology as any substance or molecule introduced from an exogenous source that modifies cellular function via chemical interactions as an activator or inhibitor.
​Incorrect (B): A hormone is restricted specifically to endogenous chemical messengers synthesized by endocrine glands and transported through the bloodstream to regulate distant target organs.
​Incorrect (C): A neurotransmitter is limited exclusively to endogenous signaling molecules released by neurons into a synaptic cleft to transmit electrical impulses between cells.
​Incorrect (D): A pheromone refers strictly to volatile ectohormones secreted outside the body to trigger social, behavioral, or physiological responses in other individuals of the same species.
​Incorrect (E): A protein is a macromolecular structural category composed of amino acids; while many drugs or endogenous targets are proteins, not all chemical modulators belong to this molecular class.

Question: IV administration of drugs:
 A. achieves 100% bioavailability.
 B. avoids systemic distribution.
 C. is slowly absorbed.
 D. undergoes the first-pass metabolism.
 E. rapidly excretes by renal paths.
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
​Correct (A): Intravenous (IV) administration introduces the entire drug dose directly into the venous circulation, bypassing all absorptive barriers to achieve a baseline bioavailability of exactly 100%.
​Incorrect (B): This route delivers the drug directly into the systemic blood flow, ensuring immediate and widespread systemic distribution to target tissues throughout the body.
​Incorrect (C): It bypasses the absorption phase entirely, resulting in immediate bioavailability rather than a slow, rate-limited uptake process.
​Incorrect (D): Because the drug bypasses the portal vein and enters the systemic circulation directly, it avoids hepatic first-pass metabolism during its initial circuit.
​Incorrect (E): Elimination kinetics depend strictly on the chemical properties of the molecule and hepatic clearance, meaning IV administration does not fundamentally alter or speed up renal excretion rates.

Question: Which route of administration is commonly used for vaccines:
 A. Intradermal
 B. Intramuscular
 C. Intravenous
 D. Oral
 E. Subcutaneous
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
​Correct (B): Intramuscular injection is commonly used for vaccines because the high vascularity of skeletal muscle ensures efficient antigen presentation while minimizing localized tissue irritation.
​Incorrect (A): Intradermal administration is highly specialized and reserved for select immunizations like BCG, rather than serving as the standard route for routine vaccine schedules.
​Incorrect (C): Intravenous injection is never used for vaccines because introducing an immunogenic antigen directly into the bloodstream can trigger life-threatening systemic anaphylaxis.
​Incorrect (D): Oral administration is limited to specific mucosal pathogens, such as rotavirus, because gastric acid and digestive enzymes break down most standard vaccine antigens.
​Incorrect (E): Subcutaneous injection is used only for specific live-attenuated formulations, as it offers slower absorption and can cause more localized tissue reactivity than intramuscular delivery.

Question: Which route of administration is used for drugs that require steady, continuous release, such as nitroglycerin:
 A. Intramuscular
 B. Intravenous
 C. Oral
 D. Subcutaneous
 E. Topical
 Correct Option: E
 Explanation:
​Correct (E): Topical application (via transdermal patches or ointments) is used for nitroglycerin because absorption through the skin bypasses hepatic first-pass metabolism and ensures steady, continuous release over an extended period.
​Incorrect (A): Intramuscular delivery causes a rapid spike in drug levels followed by a sharp decline, making it unsuitable for maintaining a steady, continuous therapeutic window.
​Incorrect (B): Intravenous administration triggers immediate systemic distribution but requires a continuous IV pump infrastructure, making it impractical for long-term, steady outpatient management.
​Incorrect (C): Oral dosing subjects nitroglycerin to heavy hepatic first-pass metabolism, which rapidly degrades the active compound before it can achieve steady systemic distribution.
​Incorrect (D): Subcutaneous injection provides relatively slow absorption but still causes a distinct peak-and-trough plasma profile, failing to deliver a truly continuous release.

​Incorrect (B): Intravenous delivery is a specialized parenteral route reserved for rapid systemic distribution or precise dosing, rather than a standard solution for managing a bad taste.
​Incorrect (C): Oral administration exposes the drug directly to gustatory receptors on the tongue, causing severe palatability issues and poor patient compliance if the compound has a bitter taste.
