Question: For a drug given orally, the principal site of drug absorption is the:
 A. colon.
 B. large intestine.
 C. oesophagus.
 D. small intestine.
 E. stomach.
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
​Correct (D): The small intestine serves as the principal site of drug absorption due to its massive surface area, created by specialized villi and microvilli, combined with high perfusion and prolonged transit time.
​Incorrect (A): The colon has a minimal surface area and lacks the specialized absorptive architecture needed for highly efficient nutrient or xenobiotic uptake, limiting its role to water reabsorption.
​Incorrect (B): The large intestine lacks microscopic villi, resulting in a drastically restricted surface area that prevents it from acting as the primary portal for systemic drug absorption.
​Incorrect (C): The oesophagus is a thick, muscular transport tube lined with non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium designed for bolus movement with virtually no absorptive capacity.
​Incorrect (E): The stomach lacks an expansive microvillar surface area and is coated in a thick, protective mucus layer, rendering it a secondary absorptive site despite its acidic environment.

Question: The action of the drug on the body is known as:
 A. pharmacodynamics.
 B. pharmacokinetics.
 C. pharmacology.
 D. pharmacotherapeutics.
 E. pharmacovigilance.
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
​Correct (A): Pharmacodynamics deals specifically with the biochemical and physiological action of the drug on the body, focusing on receptor interactions, signaling mechanisms, and dose-response relationships.
​Incorrect (B): Pharmacokinetics describes what the body does to the drug, tracking the quantitative pathways of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion over time.
​Incorrect (C): Pharmacology is the broad, overarching scientific discipline that encompasses the study of drug origins, chemical properties, biological effects, and therapeutic uses.
​Incorrect (D): Pharmacotherapeutics focuses on the clinical application of drugs to diagnose, prevent, or treat specific medical diseases and pathological conditions.
​Incorrect (E): Pharmacovigilance is the specialized epidemiological system dedicated to monitoring, detecting, assessing, and preventing adverse effects or safety issues linked to post-market pharmaceutical products.

Question: Which route of administration is commonly used for insulin:
A. Intramuscular
B. Intravenous
C. Oral
D. Subcutaneous
E. Topical
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
* ​Correct (D): Subcutaneous injection provides slow, consistent, and predictable absorption through the vascular connective tissue underlying the skin, making it ideal for routine home-based glycemic control.
* ​Incorrect (A): Intramuscular administration is avoided for routine insulin delivery because it leads to overly rapid absorption and an increased risk of severe, unpredictable hypoglycemia.
* ​Incorrect (B): Intravenous delivery is reserved exclusively for acute, emergency settings like diabetic ketoacidosis, as it triggers immediate systemic distribution and clearance.
* ​Incorrect (C): Oral administration is ineffective because insulin is a protein molecule that is rapidly degraded by proteolytic enzymes and gastric acid in the gastrointestinal tract.
* ​Incorrect (E): Topical delivery cannot be used because insulin is a large hydrophilic macromolecule that is completely unable to penetrate the thick, lipophilic stratum corneum of intact skin.

Question: A molecule that interacts as an activator or inhibitor and brings about a change in biological function through its chemical actions is known as a:
A. drug.
B. hormone.
C. neurotransmitter.
D. pheromone.
E. protein.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
* ​Correct (A): A drug is defined broadly in pharmacology as any substance or molecule introduced from an exogenous source that modifies cellular function via chemical interactions as an activator or inhibitor.
* ​Incorrect (B): A hormone is restricted specifically to endogenous chemical messengers synthesized by endocrine glands and transported through the bloodstream to regulate distant target organs.
* ​Incorrect (C): A neurotransmitter is limited exclusively to endogenous signaling molecules released by neurons into a synaptic cleft to transmit electrical impulses between cells.
* ​Incorrect (D): A pheromone refers strictly to volatile ectohormones secreted outside the body to trigger social, behavioral, or physiological responses in other individuals of the same species.
* ​Incorrect (E): A protein is a macromolecular structural category composed of amino acids; while many drugs or endogenous targets are proteins, not all chemical modulators belong to this molecular class.

Question: Which type of transmembrane signaling receptors are used by Vitamin D:
A. Enzyme-linked
B. G-protein coupled
C. Intracellular
D. JAK-STAT
E. Membrane spanning
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
* ​Correct (C): Intracellular receptors bind highly lipophilic ligands like Vitamin D, which freely cross the plasma membrane to form hormone-receptor complexes that alter gene transcription directly within the nucleus.
* ​Incorrect (A): Enzyme-linked receptors are surface-bound molecules that trigger an intrinsic or tightly coupled cytosolic enzymatic cascade, typically used by hydrophilic peptide growth factors rather than lipophilic vitamins.
* ​Incorrect (B): G-protein coupled receptors utilize a seven-transmembrane architecture to activate heterotrimeric G-proteins at the plasma membrane, serving hydrophilic ligands like catecholamines.
* ​Incorrect (D): JAK-STAT signaling pathways are membrane-bound cytokine receptor networks that recruit cytosolic Janus kinases to phosphorylate transcription factors, bypassing intracellular direct ligand-binding.
* ​Incorrect (E): Membrane spanning structures are localized strictly at the cell surface interface, whereas the functional signaling complex for Vitamin D resides deep within the cytosol or nucleus.

Question: Routes of drug excretion from the body include the:
 A. biliary tract.
 B. intestines.
 C. kidneys.
 D. lungs.
 E. skin.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
​Correct (C): The kidneys serve as the primary and most efficient route of drug excretion from the body, utilizing glomerular filtration and active tubular secretion to clear polar compounds into urine.
​Incorrect (A): The biliary tract is an important secondary clearance pathway for large, lipophilic metabolites, but it is not the primary route for systemic drug elimination.
​Incorrect (B): The intestines contribute to elimination by passing unabsorbed or biliary-secreted waste, but they do not serve as the primary organ for systemic drug excretion.
​Incorrect (D): The lungs are highly specialized for eliminating volatile anesthetics and gases via exhalation, making them less relevant for clearing standard solid or liquid pharmaceutical agents.
​Incorrect (E): The skin excretes trace amounts of certain drugs through sweat and sebum, but its total capacity is negligible compared to major systemic clearance routes.
