Question: Which type of transmembrane signaling receptors are used by Vitamin D:
 A. Enzyme-linked
 B. G-protein coupled
 C. Intracellular
 D. JAK-STAT
 E. Membrane spanning
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
​Correct (C): Intracellular receptors bind highly lipophilic ligands like Vitamin D, which freely cross the plasma membrane to form hormone-receptor complexes that alter gene transcription directly within the nucleus.
​Incorrect (A): Enzyme-linked receptors are surface-bound molecules that trigger an intrinsic or tightly coupled cytosolic enzymatic cascade, typically used by hydrophilic peptide growth factors rather than lipophilic vitamins.
​Incorrect (B): G-protein coupled receptors utilize a seven-transmembrane architecture to activate heterotrimeric G-proteins at the plasma membrane, serving hydrophilic ligands like catecholamines.
​Incorrect (D): JAK-STAT signaling pathways are membrane-bound cytokine receptor networks that recruit cytosolic Janus kinases to phosphorylate transcription factors, bypassing intracellular direct ligand-binding.
​Incorrect (E): Membrane spanning structures are localized strictly at the cell surface interface, whereas the functional signaling complex for Vitamin D resides deep within the cytosol or nucleus.
