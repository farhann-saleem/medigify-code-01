Question: Local anesthetic act by: 
Options: a) Blocking sodium channel 
b) Blocking sodium/potassium ATPase pump 
c) Blocking NMJ 
d) Blocking calcium channel 
Correct Option: Ans. Blocking sodium channel
 Explanation: Local anesthetics function by blocking voltage-dependent sodium (Na+) channels on the inner surface of nerve membranes, which prevents the influx of ions necessary for action potential conduction. They exhibit state-dependent blockade, meaning they bind preferentially to channels in their open (activated) or inactivated states, rather than resting channels. Blocking the sodium/potassium pump or calcium channels would not produce the rapid, reversible loss of sensation characteristic of these drugs.

Question: Which of the following local anesthetic drugs has vasoconstrictive properties: 
Options: a) Cocaine 
b) Lidocaine 
c) Bupivacaine 
d) Succinylcholine 
Correct Option: Ans. Cocaine 
Explanation: Cocaine is unique among local anesthetics because it possesses intrinsic vasoconstrictive properties due to its ability to inhibit the reuptake of norepinephrine; in contrast, agents like lidocaine and bupivacaine typically cause vasodilation. Question: Bupivacaine is used for surgical procedures as a/an: 
Options: 
a) Stimulant 
b) Antiemetic 
c) General anesthetic 
d) Local anesthetic 
Correct Option: Ans. Local anesthetic 
Explanation: Bupivacaine acts as a local anesthetic, working to block voltage-gated sodium channels, thereby preventing their influx.
