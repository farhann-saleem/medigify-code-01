Question: Which structure lies in both the posterior and superior mediastinum? 
A. Azygos vein 
B. Esophagus 
C. Heart 
D. Internal thoracic vein 
E. Superior vena cava 
Correct Option: B 
Explanation:
* Correct (B): The esophagus descends through the superior mediastinum (behind the trachea) and continues into the posterior mediastinum to the esophageal hiatus, making it the only structure that spans both compartments.
* Incorrect (A): The azygos vein lies in the posterior mediastinum; its arch passes into the superior mediastinum briefly, but the vein is not classified as a primary content of both compartments.
* Incorrect (C): The heart lies in the middle mediastinum within the pericardial sac and does not occupy the posterior or superior mediastinum.
* Incorrect (D): The internal thoracic vein runs along the anterior chest wall in the anterior mediastinum and does not occupy the posterior or superior mediastinum.
* Incorrect (E): The superior vena cava lies in the superior mediastinum only; it terminates at the right atrium and does not extend into the posterior mediastinum.

Question: Which of the following is a content of the anterior mediastinum? 
A. Arch of the aorta 
B. Esophagus 
C. Phrenic nerve 
D. Thoracic duct 
E. Thymus gland 
Correct Option: E 
Explanation:
* Correct (E): The thymus gland (or its remnant) is the principal content of the anterior mediastinum, lying anterior to the pericardium between the sternum and great vessels.
* Incorrect (A): The arch of the aorta is a major content of the superior mediastinum, not the anterior mediastinum.
* Incorrect (B): The esophagus passes through the superior and posterior mediastinum and does not occupy the anterior mediastinum.
* Incorrect (C): The phrenic nerve runs through the superior and middle mediastinum (alongside the pericardium) but is not a primary content of the anterior mediastinum.
* Incorrect (D): The thoracic duct ascends through the posterior mediastinum and has no course through the anterior mediastinum.

Question: The anterior boundary of the posterior mediastinum is formed by the: 
A. esophagus 
B. pericardium 
C. sternum 
D. T5–T12 vertebrae 
E. thymus gland 
Correct Option: B 
Explanation:
* Correct (B): The pericardium (along with the posterior surface of the heart) forms the anterior boundary of the posterior mediastinum, separating it from the middle mediastinum.
* Incorrect (A): The esophagus is a content of the posterior mediastinum, not a boundary.
* Incorrect (C): The sternum forms the anterior boundary of the anterior mediastinum, not the posterior.
* Incorrect (D): The T5–T12 vertebrae form the posterior boundary of the posterior mediastinum.
* Incorrect (E): The thymus gland is located in the anterior/superior mediastinum and plays no role in forming posterior mediastinal boundaries.

﻿Question: AV shunt is present in: 
A. deltoid muscle 
B. fingertips 
C. liver parenchyma 
D. spleen pulp 
E. thyroid gland 
Correct Option: B 
Explanation:
* Correct (B): Arteriovenous (AV) shunts are highly abundant in the cutaneous tissue of the fingertips, where they play a critical role in thermoregulation by bypassing capillary beds.
* Incorrect (A): The deltoid muscle contains continuous capillaries rather than specialized arteriovenous shunts to maximize metabolic exchange during exercise.
* Incorrect (C): The liver parenchyma features specialized, wide discontinuous capillaries known as sinusoids to facilitate hepatic filtration.
* Incorrect (D): The spleen pulp contains a unique open and closed circulatory system characterized by splenic sinusoids, not AV shunts.
* Incorrect (E): The thyroid gland is highly vascularized with fenestrated capillaries to allow rapid hormone secretion into the bloodstream.
