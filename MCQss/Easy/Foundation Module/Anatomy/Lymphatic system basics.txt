A. absent in the dermal layers 
B. blind ended vascular structures 
C. composed of thick muscular walls 
D. smaller in caliber than blood capillaries 
E. unable to form interconnected networks Correct Option: B 
Explanation:
* Correct (B): Lymph capillaries are uniquely structured as blind ended vascular structures that serve as the starting point of the lymphatic drainage system.
* Incorrect (A): Lymph capillaries are highly abundant within the dermal layers of the skin to drain excess interstitial fluid.
* Incorrect (C): Lymph capillaries consist of a single layer of endothelial cells and completely lack thick muscular walls.
* Incorrect (D): Lymph capillaries are structurally larger in caliber than blood capillaries to accommodate large proteins and interstitial debris.
* Incorrect (E): Lymph capillaries are highly capable of forming extensive, interconnected networks throughout the interstitial spaces of vascular tissues.

Question: The lymph from abdomen enters in thoracic cavity by: 
A. bronchomediastinal trunk 
B. cisterna chyli 
C. left subclavian vein 
D. right lymphatic duct 
E. superior vena cava 
Correct Option: B 
Explanation:
* Correct (B): The cisterna chyli is a dilated lymphatic sac located at the lower end of the thoracic duct that collects all lymph from the abdomen before it ascends into the thoracic cavity.
* Incorrect (A): The bronchomediastinal trunk drains lymph specifically from the thorax, including the lungs, bronchi, and mediastinal structures.
* Incorrect (C): The left subclavian vein is the final vascular destination where the thoracic duct empties its contents, not the conduit through which lymph enters the thorax.
* Incorrect (D): The right lymphatic duct drains lymph exclusively from the upper right quadrant of the body into the right venous angle.
* Incorrect (E): The superior vena cava receives systemic venous blood and does not directly transport lymph out of the abdominal region.

Question: Lymph is a transit fluid between: 
A. blood and interstitial fluid 
B. blood and intracellular fluid 
C. blood and plasma 
D. blood and serum 
E. plasma and serum 
Correct Option: A 
Explanation:
* Correct (A): Lymph is formed when interstitial fluid enters lymphatic capillaries, serving as a dynamic intermediary fluid system that returns fluid and proteins from the interstitial fluid back into the blood.
* Incorrect (B): Intracellular fluid is contained entirely within cell membranes and does not directly exchange components to form lymph.
* Incorrect (C): Plasma is the liquid component of blood itself, rather than a separate compartment separated from blood by lymph.
* Incorrect (D): Serum is the fluid remaining after blood clotting factors are removed from plasma, representing an in vitro or post-clotting state rather than a distinct anatomical compartment.
* Incorrect (E): Both plasma and serum represent the acellular fluid portions of blood (before and after clotting, respectively) and do not form the boundary compartments between which lymph functions as a transit fluid.

Question: Valves are found in the: 
A. arteries and capillaries 
B. arteries only 
C. capillaries only 
D. lymphatic vessels and veins 
E. lymphatic vessels only 
Correct Option: D 
Explanation:
* Correct (D): Lymphatic vessels and veins both operate under low-pressure conditions and contain structural endothelial folds forming valves to prevent retrograde flow and ensure unidirectional movement toward the heart.
* Incorrect (A): Arteries carry blood under high pressure and do not require valves, while capillaries consist of a single layer of endothelial cells completely devoid of valves.
* Incorrect (B): Arteries utilize high hydrostatic pressure and elastic recoil to propel blood forward, making valves structurally unnecessary.
* Incorrect (C): Capillaries are microscopic vessels optimized solely for nutrient and gas exchange, completely lacking a muscular wall or internal valves.
* Incorrect (E): While lymphatic vessels contain numerous valves, this option is incomplete because veins also rely on a valvular mechanism to facilitate venous return against gravity.
