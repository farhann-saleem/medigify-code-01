Question 1. Which of the following belongs to the diarthrosis variety of joints?
 
A. Gomphosis
B. Schindylesis
C. Synchondrosis
D. Symphysis
E. Synovial
 
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 
Correct (E): Synovial joints are freely movable joints (diarthroses) characterized by a synovial cavity and high mobility.
Incorrect (A): Gomphosis is a fibrous peg-and-socket joint with immobility.
Incorrect (B): Schindylesis is a fibrous suture-type joint with no synovial cavity.
Incorrect (C): Synchondrosis is a primary cartilaginous joint with limited movement.
Incorrect (D): Symphysis is a secondary cartilaginous joint allowing limited movement.

Question 3. The following are ball-and-socket joints?

B. Elbow joint and wrist joint
C. Hip joint and shoulder joint
D. Interphalangeal joint and metacarpophalangeal joint
E. Temporomandibular joint and knee joint

Explanation:

Incorrect (A): Ankle is hinge; knee is modified hinge.

Question 1
 
Meniscus is a wedge-shaped fibrocartilage found in:
A. hip joint.
B. elbow joint.
C. shoulder joint.
D. knee joint.
E. ankle joint.
Correct Option: D
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (D): The knee joint features medial and lateral crescentic, wedge-shaped fibrocartilage structures called menisci that deepen the articular surfaces of the tibia to shock-absorb and stabilize the joint.
Incorrect (A): The hip joint contains a fibrocartilaginous acetabular labrum rather than a wedge-shaped meniscus to deepen its socket.
Incorrect (B): The elbow joint is a pure hinge synovial joint completely devoid of intra-articular fibrocartilaginous menisci.
Incorrect (C): The shoulder joint utilizes a glenoid labrum made of fibrocartilage to expand its socket cavity, not a meniscus.
Incorrect (E):The ankle joint relies purely on bony mortise stability and collateral ligaments without any intra-articular meniscus structures.

Question 4
 
Which of the following is not a plane joint:
A. between articular processes of vertebra.
B. between carpals.
C. between tarsals.
D. knee joint.
E. acromioclavicular joint.
Correct Option: D
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (D): The knee joint is not a plane joint; it is structurally classified as a modified hinge (bicondylar) joint allowing flexion, extension, and limited rotation.
Incorrect (A): The joints between articular processes of vertebra (zygapophysial joints) are true plane joints that permit gliding movements.
Incorrect (B): The joints between carpals (intercarpal joints) are classic plane joints where flat bony surfaces slide past one another.
Incorrect (C): The joints between tarsals (intertarsal joints) are structurally plane joints designed for limited gliding motions during foot inversion and eversion.
Incorrect (E): The acromioclavicular joint is anatomically classified as a plane synovial joint allowing gliding movements between the acromion and clavicle.

Question 5
 
Select incorrect pair:
A. hinge joint (knee joint).
B. pivot joint (atlantoaxial joint).
C. ellipsoid joint (wrist joint).
D. sellar joint (wrist joint).
E. ball and socket joint (shoulder joint).
Correct Option: D
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (D): Sellar joint (wrist joint) is an incorrect pair because the wrist joint is actually an ellipsoid (condylar) joint, while a sellar (saddle) joint describes the 1st carpometacarpal joint.
Incorrect (A): Hinge joint (knee joint) is considered a correct traditional pairing as the knee functions primarily as a hinge joint.
Incorrect (B): Pivot joint (atlantoaxial joint) is a correct pairing because the median atlantoaxial joint allows rotational movement around a central bony pivot.
Incorrect (C): Ellipsoid joint (wrist joint) is a correct pairing because the radiocarpal articulation features an oval condyle fitting into an elliptical cavity.
Incorrect (E): Ball and socket joint (shoulder joint) is a correct pairing because the spherical head of the humerus articulates with the shallow glenoid cavity.
