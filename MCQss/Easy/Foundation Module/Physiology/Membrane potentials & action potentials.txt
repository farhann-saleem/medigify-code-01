### Question 3
In a nerve fiber where Na+ permeability suddenly increases while K+ permeability remains unchanged, which principle explains the resulting shift in membrane potential?
A. Donnan equilibrium principle
B. Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz principle

D. Laplace law principle
E. Nernst equilibrium principle
**Correct Option: B**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (B)**: When multiple ions are permeable across a membrane, the net potential is determined by their relative concentrations and individual permeabilities simultaneously, which is mathematically quantified by the Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz principle.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: The accumulation of non-diffusible intracellular proteins drawing small counter-ions across a semipermeable barrier is defined by the Donnan equilibrium principle.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Buffer chemistry and acid-base blood bicarbonate balances are calculated exclusively via the Henderson-Hasselbalch principle.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: Wall tension and internal pressures of hollow spherical or cylindrical biological chambers are governed by the Laplace law principle.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Systems restricted to a single, completely isolated ion species moving purely across a theoretical membrane utilize the Nernst equilibrium principle.

 * Incorrect (B): Adaptation is the property where a receptor or nerve fiber decreases its firing frequency over time in response to a continuous, constant stimulus.
 * Incorrect (D): Facilitation involves an increase in responsiveness or excitability to subsequent stimuli due to subthreshold accumulation of neurotransmitter or calcium.

 * Incorrect (B): The latent period is the time delay between the delivery of a stimulus and the actual onset of the biological response.
 * Incorrect (D): The utilization time is the time required for a stimulus of exactly rheobase strength to successfully stimulate the tissue.
