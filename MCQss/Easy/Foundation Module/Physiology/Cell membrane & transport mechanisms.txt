Question 1
Question: In primary active transport, energy is derived from:
A. ATP hydrolysis
B. Electrochemical gradient
C. Glucose cotransport
D. Ionic concentration gradient
E. Proton gradient
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Primary active transport directly uses energy released from ATP hydrolysis to move substances against their electrochemical gradients.
Incorrect (B): An electrochemical gradient provides energy for secondary active transport, not primary active transport.
Incorrect (C): Glucose cotransport is a form of secondary active transport and does not directly supply energy.
Incorrect (D): An ionic concentration gradient is an energy source for secondary transport, not primary active transport.
Incorrect (E): A proton gradient powers certain secondary transport systems rather than primary active transport.

### Question 10
Which structural feature of neurons is primarily responsible for rapid intracellular transport of neurotransmitter vesicles from soma to axon terminal?
A. Actin microfilaments
B. Microglial processes
C. Microtubules
D. Neurofilaments
E. Schwann cell cytoplasm
**Correct Option: C**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (C)**: Microtubules form the long, rigid intracellular tracks along which kinesin motor proteins walk to move neurotransmitter vesicles from the cell body to the nerve terminal.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: Short-range cortical structural networks and localized membrane movements are mediated by actin microfilaments, not long-distance axonal transport.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Phagocytic immune responses within central nervous tissues are carried out by microglial processes, which are separate cellular extensions.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: General structural stability and structural caliber preservation inside the axon are provided by neurofilaments, not active transport tracking.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Peripheral myelin insulation wraps around the outside of axons via Schwann cell cytoplasm, which plays no role in internal vesicle transport.

### Question 12

B. Fast anterograde transport via dynein motor proteins
C. Passive diffusion through the continuous axoplasm
D. Retrograde transport via dynein motor proteins
E. Slow anterograde transport via kinesin motor proteins
**Correct Option: D**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (D)**: Retrograde transport via dynein motor proteins moves worn-out organelles and endocytosed materials backward along microtubules from the axon terminal toward the cell body for degradation.

 * **Incorrect (B)**: Anterograde transport is driven by kinesin motors, not by dynein, making fast anterograde transport via dynein motor proteins mechanistically incorrect.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Structural clearance mechanisms require active, ATP-driven molecular motors rather than passive diffusion through the continuous axoplasm.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Structural cytoskeletal components like neurofilaments move outward from the soma via slow anterograde transport via kinesin motor proteins.

Question 26: In unmyelinated nerve fibers:
A. impulse travels slowly along the axon
B. refractory period is very short
C. Schwann cells are absent
D. spinal cord is the major site of their location
E. synaptic transmission is completely blocked
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): Because they lack an insulating myelin sheath, the impulse travels slowly along the axon via continuous cable conduction rather than rapid saltatory conduction.
 * Incorrect (B): The refractory period is determined by voltage-gated channel kinetics, not by the absence of myelin, meaning it is not inherently very short.
 * Incorrect (C): Schwann cells are absent is incorrect because unmyelinated peripheral fibers are still structurally engulfed and supported by Schwann cell cytoplasm without being wrapped in layers of myelin.
 * Incorrect (D): The spinal cord is the major site of their location is false, as unmyelinated fibers (such as postganglionic autonomic and slow pain C-fibers) are widely distributed in the periphery.
 * Incorrect (E): The synaptic transmission is completely blocked statement is false, as unmyelinated fibers successfully release neurotransmitters to propagate signals across synapses.
   Tags: Easy, Physiology, Foundation, Cell Membrane & Transport Mechanisms

Question 28: Nodes of Ranvier are:
A. having Nissl granules in them
B. highly permeable to ions
C. present on nerve fibers at irregular intervals
D. present on unmyelinated nerve fibers
E. sites of high lipid content
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): Nodes of Ranvier are bare patches of axonal membrane containing a high density of voltage-gated channels, making them highly permeable to ions during depolarization.
 * Incorrect (A): The property of having Nissl granules in them is incorrect because these protein-synthesizing structures are confined to the neuronal soma and dendrites, not axon nodes.
 * Incorrect (C): The statement present on nerve fibers at irregular intervals is factually incorrect; these nodes occur at highly regular, precise intervals along myelinated axons.
 * Incorrect (D): The statement present on unmyelinated nerve fibers is false, as these nodes are defined as the structural gaps between adjacent myelin segments.
 * Incorrect (E): Being sites of high lipid content describes the myelin sheath segments themselves, whereas the nodes represent an absence of lipid-rich myelin.

 * Incorrect (E): The disease causes the destruction and loss of myelin rather than the hypertrophy of peripheral myelin sheaths, which are made by unaffected Schwann cells.
