Question: Lymphatic nodules are found in each of the following except the:
A. appendix.
B. lymph node.
C. spleen.
D. thymus.
E. tonsil.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The thymus is a primary lymphoid organ dedicated strictly to T-cell maturation and lacks B-cell-containing lymphatic nodules.
Incorrect (A): The lamina propria and submucosa of the appendix feature a continuous layer of prominent lymphatic nodules.
Incorrect (B): The outer cortex of a lymph node is highly organized into spherical primary and secondary lymphatic nodules.
Incorrect (C): The white pulp of the spleen contains classic lymphatic nodules clustered around central arterioles.
Incorrect (E): The palatine, lingual, and pharyngeal tonsils all contain numerous subepithelial lymphatic nodules.

Question: Reticular cells of epithelial origin are found in:
A. lymph nodes.
B. Peyer patches.
C. splenic cords.
D. the thymus.
E. tonsillar crypts.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The thymus is derived from endoderm and uniquely relies on structural epithelioreticular cells of epithelial origin to support T-cell development.
Incorrect (A): The structural scaffolding here is composed of mesenchymal-derived fibroblastic reticular cells and type III collagen.
Incorrect (B): These mucosal aggregates use standard mesenchymal fibroblastic reticular cells to form their structural meshwork.
Incorrect (C): The cords of Billroth within the red pulp rely on mesenchymal reticular cells and reticular fibers to filter blood elements.
Incorrect (E): This area consists of invaginated stratified squamous epithelium and underlying lymphoid stroma, but lacks the internal epithelial cytoreticulum seen in primary lymphoid organs.

Question: Paracortex of lymph nodes contains:
A. erythrocytes.
B. lymphocytes.
C. macrophages.
D. megakaryocytes.
E. plasma cells.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The paracortex is the distinct deep cortical zone of lymph nodes populated almost exclusively by T lymphocytes.
Incorrect (A): These oxygen-carrying cells remain confined within functional blood vessels unless severe structural trauma occurs.
Incorrect (C): While present to process antigens, they are localized primarily within the sinus systems and medullary regions rather than characterizing the paracortex.
Incorrect (D): These massive, platelet-producing cells reside exclusively within the red bone marrow hematopoiesis lines.
Incorrect (E): These terminal B-cell derivatives migrate down to secrete antibodies within the marrow-like medullary cords.

﻿Question 1
The subcapsular (marginal) sinus of lymph nodes would normally contain:
A. B cells
B. Dendritic cells
C. Erythrocytes
D. Plasma cells
E. Reticular cells
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Incorrect (A): B cells are characteristically localized and organized within the outer cortex and lymphoid nodules, rather than passing freely through the subcapsular (marginal) sinus.
Incorrect (B): Dendritic cells reside primarily within the paracortex and deep cortical regions of the lymph node to present antigens to T cells.
Incorrect (C): Plasma cells are predominantly located within the medullary cords of the lymph node where they actively secrete antibodies into the efferent lymph.
Incorrect (E): Reticular cells form the structural, stationary meshwork framework spanning the entire parenchyma of the lymph node rather than flowing through the fluid sinuses.

Incorrect (A): lymph nodes contain numerous prominent lymphatic nodules located within their outer cortical region.

Incorrect (C): spleen contains abundant lymphatic nodules which collectively comprise the periarterial white pulp.
Incorrect (E): tonsils contain highly developed lymphatic nodules beneath their epithelial lining to monitor ingested and inhaled antigens.

Question 5

A. cortical lymphoid nodules of lymph nodes

D. lymphoid nodules of the tonsils
E. paracortex region of lymph nodes
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): paracortex region of lymph nodes serves as the specific, T-cell dependent zone where mature T cells home and interact with antigen-presenting cells.
Incorrect (A): cortical lymphoid nodules of lymph nodes represent the B-cell dependent zones of the node, not the homing site for T cells.
Incorrect (B): deep medullary cords of lymph nodes are populated primarily by plasma cells, macrophages, and B cells producing antibodies.
Incorrect (C): hilus region of lymph nodes is the structural exit and entry point for major blood vessels and efferent lymphatics, not a lymphoid homing zone.
Incorrect (D): lymphoid nodules of the tonsils are specialized B-cell territories organized for somatic hypermutation and antibody production.

Question 8
Paracortex of lymph nodes contains:
A. erythrocytes
B. lymphocytes
C. macrophages
D. neutrophils
E. plasma cells
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): lymphocytes—specifically T helper and cytotoxic T cells—predominantly pack this region, making the paracortex the dedicated T-cell zone of the lymph node.
Incorrect (A): erythrocytes are confined to the vascular lumen or the sinuses during filtration and do not populate the paracortex parenchyma.
Incorrect (C): macrophages are primarily localized in the subcapsular and medullary sinuses to clear cellular debris and pathogens.
Incorrect (D): neutrophils are absent from the healthy paracortex, only migrating there during acute, suppurative lymphadenitis.
Incorrect (E): plasma cells reside preferentially within the medullary cords where they secrete immunoglobulins into the lymphatic circulation.
