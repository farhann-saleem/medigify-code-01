Question 1
Which of the following cell components is not surrounded by a membrane?
A. Lysosomes
B. Mitochondria
C. Nucleolus
D. Nucleus
E. Peroxisomes
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The nucleolus is a dense, non-membrane-bound subnuclear structure where ribosomal RNA transcription and ribosome assembly take place.
Incorrect (A): Lysosomes are membrane-bound organelles containing hydrolytic enzymes.
Incorrect (B): Mitochondria are surrounded by a double-membrane system.
Incorrect (D): The nucleus is enclosed by a double-layered nuclear envelope.
Incorrect (E): Peroxisomes are single membrane-bound organelles involved in oxidative reactions.

Question 2
Golgi apparatus is concerned with:
A. concentration & packing of secretory products
B. energy production in the matrix
C. synthesis of fatty acids
D. synthesis of proteins
E. synthesis of secretory products
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): The primary function of the Golgi apparatus is the post-translational modification, concentration & packing of secretory products into vesicles.
Incorrect (B): Energy production in the matrix is the primary metabolic role of the mitochondria.
Incorrect (C): Synthesis of fatty acids occurs primarily in the cytosol and smooth endoplasmic reticulum, not the Golgi apparatus.
Incorrect (D): Synthesis of proteins is performed by ribosomes and the rough endoplasmic reticulum.
Incorrect (E): Synthesis of secretory products like proteins or lipids happens in the endoplasmic reticulum before transport to the Golgi.

Question 7
Which organelle is the only double layered organelle in eukaryotic cell?
A. Lysosomes
B. Microbodies
C. Mitochondria
D. Nucleolus
E. Peroxisomes
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Among the cytoplasmic options listed, mitochondria are bound by a distinct outer and an inner mitochondrial membrane, forming a double layered organelle.
Incorrect (A): Lysosomes are enclosed by a single lipid bilayer membrane.
Incorrect (B): Microbodies are small, single-membrane-bound cytoplasmic vesicles.
Incorrect (D): The nucleolus is a non-membrane-bound subnuclear region, not a membrane-enclosed cytoplasmic organelle.
Incorrect (E): Peroxisomes are single membrane-bound structures containing oxidative enzymes.

Question 8
Which part of mitochondria shows cristae?
A. External surface of outer membrane
B. Inner surface of outer membrane
C. Intermembranous space
D. Matrix space
E. Inner membrane
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The inner membrane of mitochondria folds inward to form shelf-like or tubular projections called cristae, increasing surface area for the electron transport chain.
Incorrect (A): The external surface of outer membrane is smooth and interacts directly with the cytoplasm, lacking folds.
Incorrect (B): The inner surface of outer membrane lines the intermembrane space and does not form cristae.
Incorrect (C): The intermembranous space is the fluid-filled compartment between the outer and inner membranes, not a structural component that folds.
Incorrect (D): The matrix space is the central fluid-filled compartment enclosed by the inner membrane, containing mitochondrial DNA and enzymes.

Question 9
Which of the following organelles is basophilic?
A. Golgi apparatus
B. Lysosomes
C. Mitochondria
D. Rough endoplasmic reticulum
E. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The rough endoplasmic reticulum contains a high concentration of ribosomal RNA, which binds basic dyes like hematoxylin, making the organelle basophilic.
Incorrect (A): The Golgi apparatus does not contain significant amounts of nucleic acids and appears as a clear, non-basophilic zone on standard staining.
Incorrect (B): Lysosomes contain proteins and hydrolytic enzymes that exhibit acidophilic (eosinophilic) rather than basophilic properties.
Incorrect (C): Mitochondria are rich in proteins and membrane lipids, rendering them eosinophilic under standard histological staining.
Incorrect (E): The smooth endoplasmic reticulum is involved in lipid metabolism and lacks the acidic ribosomes required to demonstrate cytological basophilia.
Tags: Easy, Histology, Foundation, Microscopy, Staining & Cytology

Question 13
Which intermediate filament is found in skin?
A. Actin
B. Desmin
C. Keratin
D. Lamin
E. Vimentin
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Keratin is the signature intermediate filament protein found in epithelial cells, providing structural mechanical integrity to the epidermis of the skin.
Incorrect (A): Actin is a component of microfilaments (thin filaments), not an intermediate filament.
Incorrect (B): Desmin is an intermediate filament protein found specifically in muscle cells, not in epithelial skin structures.
Incorrect (D): Lamin is a structural intermediate filament protein confined to the nuclear lamina inside the nucleus, rather than the cytoplasm of skin cells.
Incorrect (E): Vimentin is an intermediate filament protein characteristic of cells derived from mesenchyme, such as fibroblasts and endothelial cells.

Question 15
An intermediate filament protein found in cytoplasm of most epithelial cells is which of the following?
A. Actin
B. Desmin
C. Keratin
D. Laminin
E. Vimentin
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Keratin is the class of intermediate filament proteins uniquely expressed within the cytoplasm of most epithelial cells to offer structural protection against mechanical stress.
Incorrect (A): Actin forms microfilaments, which are 7 nm thin filaments distinct from the 10 nm intermediate filament family.
Incorrect (B): Desmin is an intermediate filament found exclusively in skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle tissue, not epithelial cells.
Incorrect (D): Laminin is a major structural glycoprotein found within the extracellular basement membrane, not an intracellular intermediate filament protein.
Incorrect (E): Vimentin is the intermediate filament marker characteristically expressed within mesenchymal cells rather than epithelial sheets.

Question 17
A medical student is observing cell division under a specialized microscope. He can visualize the chromatids being pulled apart at the centromere and moving toward the poles. These cells are exhibiting:
A. anaphase
B. cytokinesis
C. karyokinesis
D. metaphase
E. telophase
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Anaphase is precisely defined by the synchronous separation of sister chromatids at their centromeres and their subsequent migration toward opposite spindle poles.
Incorrect (B): Cytokinesis refers to the physical division of the cytoplasm into two daughter cells, which succeeds the mechanical migration of chromosomes.
Incorrect (C): Karyokinesis is a broad term denoting the entire process of nuclear division, whereas chromatid separation represents a specific phase within it.
Incorrect (D): Metaphase involves the stationary alignment of chromosomes along the central equatorial plate prior to any poleward separation.
Incorrect (E): Telophase is characterized by the uncoiling of separated chromosomes and the reconstruction of nuclear envelopes around the newly formed daughter nuclei.
