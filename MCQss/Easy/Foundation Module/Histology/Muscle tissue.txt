Question: Cardiac and skeletal muscle are different in that:
A. only cardiac muscle has inherent rhythmicity.
B. only cardiac muscle has peripheral nuclei.
C. only skeletal muscle has intercalated discs.
D. only skeletal muscle has rapid calcium influx.
E. only skeletal muscle has transverse striations.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Cardiac muscle possesses specialized pacemaker cells that allow it to exhibit inherent rhythmicity without external nervous stimulation.
Incorrect (B): Only skeletal muscle has peripheral nuclei, whereas cardiac muscle typically features central nuclei.
Incorrect (C): Only cardiac muscle has intercalated discs, which structurally and electrically couple adjacent cardiomyocytes.
Incorrect (D): Both cardiac and skeletal muscle utilize rapid calcium influx during excitation-contraction coupling.
Incorrect (E): Both skeletal and cardiac muscle possess a sarcomeric arrangement that produces visible transverse striations.

Question: The ability of skeletal muscle fibers to regenerate following injury is best described as:
A. Completely nonexistent.
B. Extensive and rapid.

Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Regeneration is quite limited because it relies on a small pool of resident satellite cells, often resulting in fibrosis rather than full tissue restoration.
Incorrect (A): Regeneration is not completely nonexistent, as satellite cells can proliferate and fuse to repair minor sarcolemmal damage.
Incorrect (B): Regeneration is not extensive and rapid, as large-scale injuries cannot be completely restored by the limited satellite cell population.
Incorrect (C): Regeneration is not moderately well developed compared to tissues like liver or bone, which possess robust regenerative cascades.
Incorrect (D): Regeneration is not overwhelmingly continuous, as skeletal muscle cells remain quiescent unless explicitly triggered by mechanical trauma.

Question: Thin filaments have all of the following components except:

B. Myosin.

D. Troponin I.

Explanation:
Correct (B): Myosin is the major component of thick filaments, not thin filaments.
Incorrect (A): F-actin forms the double-helical structural backbone of the thin filaments.
Incorrect (C): Troponin C is a component of the thin filament complex responsible for binding calcium ions to initiate cross-bridge cycling.
Incorrect (D): Troponin I is a component of the thin filament complex that inhibits the binding of myosin heads to actin in the resting state.
Incorrect (E): Troponin T is a component of the thin filament complex that anchors the troponin trimers to the tropomyosin strand.

Question: The contraction of the myofibril in skeletal muscle takes place:
A. at the H band.
B. at the I band.
C. at the M band.
D. between two Z bands.
E. within the T tubule.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Contraction occurs within the sarcomere, which is the functional contractile unit defined as the region between two Z bands.
Incorrect (A): The H band is merely a sub-region of the sarcomere that contains only thick filaments and shortens during contraction, but does not encompass the full contractile apparatus.
Incorrect (B): The I band is the light band containing only thin filaments that narrows during contraction, rather than serving as the full zone of contraction.
Incorrect (C): The M band is the narrow structural anchoring line in the exact center of the thick filaments.
Incorrect (E): The T tubule is an invagination of the sarcolemma that transmits action potentials but contains no contractile machinery.

A. cell shape and intercalated discs.
B. cell shape and nuclear position.
C. cell volume and mitochondrial size.
D. fiber branch angle and diameter.
E. striations and intercalated discs.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:

Incorrect (A): Cell shape and intercalated discs are insufficient, as cell shapes can overlap or distort depending on the plane of a histological section.
Incorrect (B): Cell shape and nuclear position fail to cleanly isolate cardiac from smooth muscle, as both can exhibit central nuclei and elongated shapes.
Incorrect (C): Cell volume and mitochondrial size are highly variable quantitative parameters that cannot be reliably used for qualitative histological triage.
Incorrect (D): Fiber branch angle and diameter are inconsistent, altered heavily by tissue preparation, and do not provide definitive criteria for all three types.

Question: What changes occur during muscle contraction:
A. A-band shortens.
B. distance between Z lines increase.
C. H-zone expands completely.
D. I-band shortens.
E. sarcomere doesn't change.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The I-band shortens during contraction as actin filaments are pulled progressively further toward the center of the sarcomere.
Incorrect (A): The A-band length corresponds to the fixed length of the myosin thick filaments, so it never shortens during contraction.
Incorrect (B): The distance between Z lines decreases as the sarcomere contracts and shortens.
Incorrect (C): The H-zone narrows or disappears entirely as actin filaments slide into it, rather than expanding completely.
Incorrect (E): The overall length of the sarcomere shortens significantly during contraction, making the claim that it doesn't change false.
