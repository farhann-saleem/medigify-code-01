Question: The hydrogen ion concentration of pure water is equal to:
A. 1 × 10^-14 M.
B. 1 × 10^-7 M.
C. 1 × 10^-8 M.
D. 1 × 10^7 M.
E. 1 × 10^14 M.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Pure water at 25°C has a hydrogen ion concentration of 1 × 10^-7 M, corresponding to pH 7.
Incorrect (A): 1 × 10^-14 M represents the ionic product of water, not the hydrogen ion concentration alone.
Incorrect (C): 1 × 10^-8 M corresponds to a slightly alkaline solution, not pure water.
Incorrect (D): 1 × 10^7 M is physiologically impossible and mathematically incorrect for hydrogen ion concentration.
Incorrect (E): 1 × 10^14 M exceeds the concentration possible in aqueous solutions.

Question: The pressure that stops the osmosis of pure solvent into solution through a semipermeable membrane is known as:
A. dialysis.
B. diffusion.
C. oncotic pressure.
D. osmotic pressure.
E. vapor pressure.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Osmotic pressure is the hydrostatic pressure required to prevent net solvent movement across a semipermeable membrane.
Incorrect (A): Dialysis refers to the separation of solutes by selective diffusion through a membrane.
Incorrect (B): Diffusion is the passive movement of molecules down a concentration gradient.
Incorrect (C): Oncotic pressure specifically refers to osmotic pressure generated by plasma proteins.
Incorrect (E): Vapor pressure relates to the evaporation tendency of liquids, not osmosis.
