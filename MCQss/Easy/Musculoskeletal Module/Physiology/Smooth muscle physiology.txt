Question: Action potential with plateau is seen in?
A. Intestinal smooth muscle fibers
B. SA node fibers
C. Skeletal muscle fibers
D. Ureteric smooth muscle fibers
E. Uterine smooth muscles
Correct Option: E
Explanation:

Correct (E): Uterine smooth muscles exhibit action potentials with a distinct plateau phase, which allows for prolonged periods of contraction during labor.

Incorrect (A): Intestinal smooth muscle fibers typically display spikes or slow-wave potential changes rather than true plateau action potentials.

Incorrect (B): SA node fibers generate pacemaker potentials characterized by a slow prepotential and spontaneous depolarization, lacking a plateau phase.

Incorrect (C): Skeletal muscle fibers exhibit rapid, spiked action potentials lasting only a few milliseconds without any plateau phase.

Incorrect (D): Ureteric smooth muscle fibers generate spike-like action potentials or plateau potentials depending on species, but "Uterine smooth muscles" are the definitive classic example of plateau dynamics in this specific comparison.

Question: During smooth muscle contraction, the main source of Ca²⁺ is?
A. Extracellular fluid
B. Mitochondria
C. Sarcoplasmic reticulum
D. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum
E. Terminal cisternae
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

Correct (A): Extracellular fluid serves as the main source of Ca²⁺ for smooth muscle contraction, entering via voltage-gated or ligand-gated calcium channels.

Incorrect (B): Mitochondria function primarily in metabolic ATP production and do not serve as a primary source of contractile calcium.

Incorrect (C): The sarcoplasmic reticulum is poorly developed in smooth muscle and contributes significantly less calcium than the extracellular pool.

Incorrect (D): Smooth endoplasmic reticulum serves general biosynthetic roles and is not the primary immediate source of calcium ions during excitation.

Incorrect (E): Terminal cisternae are specialized structures of the sarcoplasmic reticulum found in skeletal muscle, not smooth muscle.

Question: During smooth muscle contraction, Ca²⁺ primarily binds to?
A. Actin
B. Calmodulin
C. Myosin
D. Troponin
E. Tropomyosin
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

Correct (B): Calmodulin is the primary regulatory binding partner for calcium ions in smooth muscle, initiating downstream kinase activation.

Incorrect (A): Actin is a thin filament structural component that binds to myosin heads, not directly to calcium ions.

Incorrect (C): Myosin functions as the thick filament motor protein that undergoes phosphorylation, rather than binding directly to initiating calcium ions.

Incorrect (D): Troponin is entirely absent in smooth muscle tissue, making calcium binding to it impossible in this context.

Incorrect (E): Tropomyosin binds along the groove of actin filaments but does not possess calcium-binding properties.
