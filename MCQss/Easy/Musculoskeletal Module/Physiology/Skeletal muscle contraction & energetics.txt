Question: In a muscle fiber, light bands:
A. are anisotropic to polarized light.
B. are produced due to Z disks.
C. contain myosin filaments.
D. show interaction among actin & myosin filaments.
E. show interaction among nebulin & titin filaments.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): The light bands (I bands) are bisected and anchorally anchored at their midpoints by dense protein structures known as Z disks.
 * Incorrect (A): Light bands are isotropic to polarized light, whereas dark bands are anisotropic.
 * Incorrect (C): Light bands contain only actin filaments, whereas myosin filaments are restricted to the dark (A) bands.
 * Incorrect (D): Interaction among actin & myosin filaments occurs exclusively within the overlap zone of the dark A band, not the light band.
 * Incorrect (E): Light bands contain actin, troponin, tropomyosin, and nebulin, but they do not show functional interaction among nebulin & titin filaments to form the band itself.

Question: The portion of a myofibril between two successive Z lines is:
A. A band.
B. H zone.
C. I band.
D. Sarcoplasm.
E. Sarcomere.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): The sarcomere is structurally defined as the functional contractile unit of a myofibril lying between two successive Z lines.
 * Incorrect (A): The A band is the fixed dark region containing thick filaments and is only a subcomponent within a single sarcomere.
 * Incorrect (B): The H zone is the central pale region of the A band containing only thick filaments, not the entire distance between Z lines.
 * Incorrect (C): The I band is the light region containing thin filaments that spans across two adjacent sarcomeres, bisected by a single Z line.
 * Incorrect (D): The Sarcoplasm is the intracellular fluid cytoplasm of the muscle fiber rather than a structural segment of the myofibril.

Question: Which of the following covers the active site of actin?
A. Nebulin
B. Spectrin
C. Titin
D. Tropomyosin
E. Troponin
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * Correct (D): In a resting muscle, the elongated protein strands of tropomyosin physically wrap around and cover the active site of actin to prevent myosin binding.
 * Incorrect (A): Nebulin acts as an internal scaffold to regulate the precise length of the actin thin filaments.
 * Incorrect (B): Spectrin is a cytoskeletal protein associated with the cell membrane matrix, not the actin thin filament active sites.
 * Incorrect (C): Titin is a massive elastic protein that scaffolds and anchors the thick myosin filaments to the Z line.
 * Incorrect (E): Troponin is a regulatory complex that senses calcium ions and shifts tropomyosin away from, rather than directly covering, the active site.

B. binding to troponin.
C. Na+-Ca2+ exchanger.
D. passive diffusion into mitochondria.

Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): Muscle relaxation requires active sequestration of calcium, which is achieved primarily by the SERCA-type Ca2+ ATPase pump of sarcoplasmic reticulum.
 * Incorrect (B): The binding to troponin activates muscle contraction; relaxation requires calcium to unbind from this complex.
 * Incorrect (C): The Na+-Ca2+ exchanger plays an important role in cardiac muscle calcium efflux but handles a negligible fraction of calcium clearance in skeletal muscle fibers.
 * Incorrect (D): There is a minimal, secondary slow sequestration of calcium via mitochondrial transporters, not rapid passive diffusion into mitochondria for relaxation.
 * Incorrect (E): The plasma membrane Ca2+ ATPase pumps calcium into the extracellular fluid, which is minor compared to the internal recycling performed by the sarcoplasmic reticulum.

Question: During shortening of a sarcomere, there is no change in length of:
A. A band.

D. M line.
E. Z disc.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): The A band corresponds to the full structural length of the thick myosin filaments, which do not change in length during sarcomere shortening.
 * Incorrect (B): The H zone narrows or disappears completely as thin actin filaments slide into the center of the sarcomere.
 * Incorrect (C): The I band shortens substantially as thin filaments are drawn inward toward the M line, increasing the zone of filament overlap.
 * Incorrect (D): The M line is a narrow structural anchoring anchoring center line rather than a dynamic horizontal band segment that scales down.
 * Incorrect (E): The Z disc marks the boundary walls of the individual sarcomere units and moves inward toward the center, narrowing the absolute distance between them.

​Question: Calsequestrin is found in:
A. cytoplasm
B. Golgi apparatus
C. nucleus
D. sarcoplasmic reticulum
E. T-tubules
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (D): Sarcoplasmic reticulum contains calsequestrin to bind and sequester large quantities of calcium ions at low free concentration, optimizing calcium storage within the terminal cisternae.
 * ​Incorrect (A): cytoplasm is incorrect because calsequestrin is localized internally within the lumen of the reticulum, not free within the cell cytoplasm.
 * ​Incorrect (B): Golgi apparatus is incorrect because this organelle is responsible for protein sorting and modification, not intracellular calcium storage during muscle rest.
 * ​Incorrect (C): nucleus is incorrect because genomic storage and transcription take place here, and it lacks the high-capacity calcium-binding protein machinery.
 * ​Incorrect (E): T-tubules is incorrect because T-tubules are continuations of the extracellular space and do not house internal calcium-binding proteins like calsequestrin.

Question: The protein involved in skeletal muscle contraction but not in smooth muscle contraction is?
A. Caldesmon
B. Calmodulin
C. Myosin light chain kinase
D. Troponin-C
E. Tropomyosin
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

Correct (D): Troponin-C is a calcium-binding protein strictly utilized in striated skeletal and cardiac muscle regulation and is entirely absent in smooth muscle.

Incorrect (A): Caldesmon is a regulatory protein specific to smooth muscle contraction that inhibits actin-myosin interaction.

Incorrect (B): Calmodulin is the primary calcium-binding protein utilized in smooth muscle contraction to initiate the activation cascade.

Incorrect (C): Myosin light chain kinase is a key regulatory enzyme specific to smooth muscle contraction activation via filament phosphorylation.

Incorrect (E): Tropomyosin is present in both skeletal and smooth muscle thin filaments, though its regulatory role differs between them.
