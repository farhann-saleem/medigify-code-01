​Question
A researcher measures equilibrium potentials in a neuron. If intracellular K+ is 140 mM and extracellular K+ is 4 mM at 37°C, which statement best explains the significance of the calculated Nernst potential for K+?
A. It defines the threshold voltage required to trigger an action potential for K+
B. It indicates the specific voltage at which voltage-gated Na+ channels will open
C. It is the potential at which electrical and chemical gradients for K+ are opposite
D. It represents the maximum depolarization achieved during a typical action potential
E. It represents the membrane potential when Na+ and K+ permeabilities become equal
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The Nernst equation identifies the equilibrium point where the inward electrical force exactly matches and balances the outward chemical concentration force for a single permeable ion.
Incorrect (A): The phrase "it defines the threshold voltage required to trigger an action potential for K+" is incorrect because the threshold voltage is determined by the opening characteristics of voltage-gated sodium channels, not individual ion equilibrium potentials.
Incorrect (B): The phrase "it indicates the specific voltage at which voltage-gated Na+ channels will open" is incorrect because the potassium equilibrium potential does not dictate the gating voltage of sodium channels.
Incorrect (D): The phrase "it represents the maximum depolarization achieved during a typical action potential" is incorrect because the peak of an action potential approaches the equilibrium potential of sodium, not potassium.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "it represents the membrane potential when Na+ and K+ permeabilities become equal" is incorrect because matching permeabilities yield a voltage intermediate between their respective equilibrium potentials.

A. Donnan equilibrium principle
B. Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz principle
C. Henderson-Hasselbalch principle
D. Michaelis-Menten principle
E. Nernst equilibrium principle
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

Incorrect (A): The phrase "Donnan equilibrium principle" is incorrect because it describes the behavior of charged particles near a semipermeable membrane containing non-diffusible proteins rather than dynamic changes in gated channel permeabilities.
Incorrect (C): The phrase "Henderson-Hasselbalch principle" is incorrect because this equation relates pH, bicarbonate, and partial pressure of carbon dioxide in acid-base physiology.
Incorrect (D): The phrase "Michaelis-Menten principle" is incorrect because it models the kinetic rate of enzyme-catalyzed reactions as a function of substrate concentration.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "Nernst equilibrium principle" is incorrect because it is restricted to situations where the membrane is selectively permeable to only one individual ion species.

​Question: Which property of action potential ensures that once threshold is reached, the response occurs with fixed amplitude regardless of stimulus strength?
A. Accommodation
B. Adaptation
C. All-or-none principle
D. Facilitation
E. Summation
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): All-or-none principle states that if a stimulus is strong enough to reach threshold, an action potential of maximal and constant amplitude is triggered automatically.
Incorrect (A): Accommodation refers to a rise in threshold level when a nerve is stimulated slowly or continuously with a subthreshold current.
Incorrect (B): Adaptation is a gradual decrease in firing frequency seen in some sensory receptors during a prolonged, constant stimulus.
Incorrect (D): Facilitation describes an increase in postsynaptic excitability caused by repeated subthreshold stimuli or previous subthreshold depolarization.
Incorrect (E): Summation involves the algebraic adding together of individual post-synaptic potentials to reach threshold, rather than determining the fixed amplitude of the resulting action potential.

​Question: A nerve fiber is stimulated repeatedly at very high frequency but fails to generate another action potential immediately after the first. Which physiological period explains this?
A. Absolute refractory period
B. Accommodation period
C. Latent period
D. Relative refractory period
E. Supranormal period
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Absolute refractory period is the timeframe immediately following an action potential during which voltage-gated sodium channels are completely inactivated and no stimulus, regardless of strength, can initiate another impulse.
Incorrect (B): Accommodation period is not a standard physiological time limit, but rather describes the process where a slow-rising stimulus fails to trigger a response due to gradual inactivation of sodium channels.
Incorrect (C): Latent period is the brief delay between the application of a stimulus and the first visible sign of the resulting response.
Incorrect (D): Relative refractory period allows a second action potential to be generated if the stimulus applied is exceptionally strong, rather than causing an absolute failure to fire.
Incorrect (E): Supranormal period is a transient phase near the end of repolarization where nerve excitability is temporarily increased, not abolished.
