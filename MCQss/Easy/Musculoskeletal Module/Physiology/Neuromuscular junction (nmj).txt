Question: Ach receptors in muscle fiber membrane are:
A. Acetylcholine-gated ion channels
B. Carbohydrate-based receptor structures
C. Enzymes causing destruction of acetylcholinesterase
D. G-protein coupled metabotropic receptors
E. Voltage-gated slow calcium channels
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): Acetylcholine-gated ion channels open directly upon the binding of acetylcholine to allow the influx of sodium ions, initiating depolarization of the muscle fiber.
 * ​Incorrect (B): Carbohydrate-based receptor structures is incorrect because these receptors are complex glycoproteins made predominantly of transmembrane protein subunits.
 * ​Incorrect (C): Enzymes causing destruction of acetylcholinesterase is incorrect because they are ion channels that act as postsynaptic receptors, not metabolic enzymes that degrade acetylcholinesterase.
 * ​Incorrect (D): G-protein coupled metabotropic receptors is incorrect because skeletal muscle nicotinic receptors are ionotropic ligand-gated channels, whereas metabotropic receptors are found in cardiac and smooth muscle.
 * ​Incorrect (E): Voltage-gated slow calcium channels is incorrect because these receptors are gated by ligand binding (acetylcholine) rather than changes in membrane voltage.

​Question: Acetylcholine binds to which subunits of Ach receptors:
A. both alpha and beta subunits
B. one alpha subunit exclusively
C. one beta subunit exclusively
D. two alpha subunits concurrently
E. two beta subunits concurrently
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (D): Two alpha subunits concurrently must bind two molecules of acetylcholine to induce the conformational change required to open the channel pore.
 * ​Incorrect (A): both alpha and beta subunits is incorrect because the binding sites are located specifically on the alpha subunits, not on the beta subunits.
 * ​Incorrect (B): one alpha subunit exclusively is incorrect because binding to only one alpha subunit is insufficient to open the channel.
 * ​Incorrect (C): one beta subunit exclusively is incorrect because beta subunits lack the structural binding domains for acetylcholine.
 * ​Incorrect (E): two beta subunits concurrently is incorrect because the binding pockets reside entirely on the alpha subunits of the pentameric receptor structure.

​Question: During neuromuscular transmission, release of acetylcholine from vesicles in the motor nerve terminal occurs due to:
A. Ca²⁺ influx
B. chloride influx
C. H⁺ efflux
D. K⁺ influx
E. Na⁺ influx
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): Ca²⁺ influx through voltage-gated calcium channels triggers vesicle fusion with the presynaptic membrane via structural interactions with SNARE proteins.
 * ​Incorrect (B): chloride influx is incorrect because chloride entry is an inhibitory event that hyperpolarizes membranes, which would prevent neurotransmitter exocytosis.
 * ​Incorrect (C): H⁺ efflux is incorrect because proton movement across the membrane sets up intravesicular gradients but does not drive terminal vesicular docking or fusion.
 * ​Incorrect (D): K⁺ influx is incorrect because potassium ions typically flow outward to repolarize cells, and influx does not trigger neurotransmitter release.
 * ​Incorrect (E): Na⁺ influx is incorrect because sodium influx is responsible for propagating the presynaptic action potential down the axon, but does not directly mediate the exocytosis machinery.

​Question: Curare produces skeletal muscle paralysis primarily by:
A. blocking acetylcholine receptors at the motor end plate
B. decreasing acetylcholinesterase breakdown activity
C. increasing acetylcholine breakdown at the synaptic cleft
D. inhibiting acetylcholine synthesis in the presynaptic terminal
E. preventing Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): Blocking acetylcholine receptors at the motor end plate competitively prevents acetylcholine from binding, thereby preventing the generation of an end-plate potential.
 * ​Incorrect (B): decreasing acetylcholinesterase breakdown activity is incorrect because reducing breakdown is the mechanism of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, which increase acetylcholine action.
 * ​Incorrect (C): increasing acetylcholine breakdown at the synaptic cleft is incorrect because curare does not alter or interact with the enzymatic degradation kinetics of acetylcholinesterase.
 * ​Incorrect (D): inhibiting acetylcholine synthesis in the presynaptic terminal is incorrect because curare has no effect on choline acetyltransferase or presynaptic synthesis pathways.
 * ​Incorrect (E): preventing Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum is incorrect because curare works strictly at the extracellular postsynaptic membrane, not on intracellular calcium channels.

​Question: In myasthenia gravis, muscle weakness occurs mainly because:
A. autoimmune destruction of postsynaptic acetylcholine receptors
B. excess acetylcholine release from presynaptic motor nerve terminals
C. increased Ca²⁺ influx into postsynaptic skeletal muscle fibers
D. inhibition of acetylcholinesterase in the neuromuscular junction
E. mutation in ryanodine receptors of sarcoplasmic reticulum
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): Autoimmune destruction of postsynaptic acetylcholine receptors reduces the number of functional nicotinic receptors, making it difficult to generate an action potential threshold.
 * ​Incorrect (B): excess acetylcholine release from presynaptic motor nerve terminals is incorrect because presynaptic acetylcholine storage, synthesis, and quantitative release remain completely normal.
 * ​Incorrect (C): increased Ca²⁺ influx into postsynaptic skeletal muscle fibers is incorrect because calcium influx is not elevated postsynaptically; fewer functional ion channels lead to reduced cellular activation.
 * ​Incorrect (D): inhibition of acetylcholinesterase in the neuromuscular junction is incorrect because acetylcholinesterase functions normally, and its inhibition is actually the intended therapeutic remedy.
 * ​Incorrect (E): mutation in ryanodine receptors of sarcoplasmic reticulum is incorrect because the intracellular components of excitation-contraction coupling are genetically intact.
