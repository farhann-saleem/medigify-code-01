 
Question: A surgeon examining the popliteal fossa during a procedure wants to identify the most superficial structure within the fossa. Which of the following structures lies most superficially:
A. popliteal artery.
B. popliteal vein.
C. posterior tibial artery.
D. small saphenous vein.
E. tibial nerve.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): From superficial to deep within the central popliteal fossa, the major neurovascular structures are arranged as the tibial nerve, popliteal vein, and popliteal artery.
Incorrect (A): The popliteal artery is the deepest major structure in the fossa, lying directly against the popliteal surface of the femur.
Incorrect (B): The popliteal vein occupies an intermediate position, sandwiched between the more superficial nerve and the deeper artery.
Incorrect (C): The posterior tibial artery is a terminal branch that originates at the distal border of the popliteal fossa and does not represent the most superficial structure within the fossa itself.
Incorrect (D): The small saphenous vein pierces the deep fascia at the lower part of the fossa to terminate in the popliteal vein, but it is a superficial tributary rather than the principal deep neurovascular structure encountered centrally.
