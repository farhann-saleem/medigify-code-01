 
Question: During anatomical dissection of the popliteal region, a student is asked to identify structures forming the boundaries of the popliteal fossa. Which of the following structures does not contribute to its boundaries?
A) Biceps femoris
B) Lateral head of gastrocnemius
C) Medial head of gastrocnemius
D) Semimembranosus
E) Soleus
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The soleus muscle arises inferior to the popliteal fossa from the posterior aspects of the tibia and fibula, meaning it does not contribute to its boundaries.
Incorrect (A): The biceps femoris forms the superolateral boundary of the popliteal fossa.
Incorrect (B): The lateral head of gastrocnemius forms the inferolateral boundary of the popliteal fossa.
Incorrect (C): The medial head of gastrocnemius forms the inferomedial boundary of the popliteal fossa.
Incorrect (D): The semimembranosus forms the superomedial boundary of the popliteal fossa.

Question: A sports injury results in inability to initiate flexion of the knee joint due to failure of the mechanism that unlocks the knee from full extension. Which muscle normally performs this action?
A) Gastrocnemius
B) Plantaris
C) Popliteus
D) Semitendinosus
E) Vastus medialis
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The popliteus muscle contracts to laterally rotate the femur on the tibia (or medially rotate the tibia when open-chain), which unlocks the knee from full extension and allows flexion to begin.
Incorrect (A): The gastrocnemius is a powerful plantar flexor of the ankle and assists with knee flexion, but it lacks the transverse rotational torque to unlock the knee joint.
Incorrect (B): The plantaris is a vestigial muscle with a minor role in weak plantarflexion and knee flexion, but it is entirely incapable of performing the unlocking mechanism.
Incorrect (D): The semitendinosus flexes and medially rotates the leg once flexion has commenced, but it cannot initiate the primary unlocking phase from complete weight-bearing extension.
Incorrect (E): The vastus medialis is a component of the quadriceps femoris that functions exclusively to extend the knee joint and stabilize the patella.

Question: During anatomical study of the popliteal fossa, a student is asked to identify the most anterior structure within the fossa. Which of the following structures lies deepest (most anterior)?
 
A. Common fibular nerve
B. Popliteal artery
C. Popliteal vein
D. Sciatic nerve
E. Tibial nerve
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The Popliteal artery lies closest to the popliteal surface of the femur, making it the deepest and most anterior structure within the popliteal fossa.
Incorrect (A): Common fibular nerve runs superficially along the lateral boundary of the fossa, far posterior to the vascular plane.
Incorrect (C): Popliteal vein occupies an intermediate position, lying superficial (posterior) to the popliteal artery and deep to the tibial nerve.
Incorrect (D): Sciatic nerve typically terminates at the superior apex of the fossa into its component branches, remaining superficial to the vascular structures.
Incorrect (E): Tibial nerve is the most superficial (posterior) of the major central structures crossing the popliteal fossa.

Question: While examining a patient with instability of the knee joint, the physician considers the ligaments preventing abnormal movements of the tibia. Which ligament prevents abduction of the tibia at the knee joint?
 
A. Anterior cruciate ligament
B. Lateral collateral ligament
C. Medial collateral ligament
D. Posterior cruciate ligament
E. Transverse ligament
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The Medial collateral ligament spans the medial aspect of the knee joint and firmly resists valgus stress, which prevents abnormal abduction of the tibia relative to the femur.
Incorrect (A): Anterior cruciate ligament primarily resists anterior translation of the tibia relative to the femur, rather than resisting lateral abduction.
Incorrect (B): Lateral collateral ligament resists varus stress, meaning it prevents abnormal adduction (not abduction) of the tibia.
Incorrect (D): Posterior cruciate ligament primarily resists posterior translation of the tibia relative to the femur, rather than resisting lateral abduction.
Incorrect (E): Transverse ligament connects the anterior horns of the medial and lateral menisci and does not prevent gross abduction of the tibia.

Question: During a knee injury evaluation, a student is asked which ligament is attached to the head of the fibula. Which of the following ligaments has this attachment?
 
A. Anterior cruciate ligament
B. Lateral collateral ligament
C. Medial collateral ligament
D. Posterior cruciate ligament
E. Tendon of popliteus
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The Lateral collateral ligament (fibular collateral ligament) passes extracapsularly from the lateral femoral epicondyle to insert directly onto the lateral surface of the head of the fibula.
Incorrect (A): Anterior cruciate ligament is an intracapsular structure attaching to the anterior intercondylar area of the tibia, completely separate from the fibula.
Incorrect (C): Medial collateral ligament is located on the opposite side of the joint and attaches to the medial condyle and shaft of the tibia.
Incorrect (D): Posterior cruciate ligament is an intracapsular structure attaching to the posterior intercondylar area of the tibia, completely separate from the fibula.
Incorrect (E): Tendon of popliteus originates from the lateral femoral condyle and inserts onto the posterior tibia, passing deep to the lateral collateral ligament without attaching to the fibular head.

Question: A patient sustains a severe knee injury during a football match involving simultaneous damage to the medial collateral ligament, medial meniscus, and anterior cruciate ligament. This injury pattern is commonly known as:
A. Fibular collateral ligament, lateral meniscus, and anterior cruciate ligament injury.
B. Fibular collateral ligament, medial meniscus, and posterior cruciate ligament injury.
C. Tibial collateral ligament, lateral meniscus, and anterior cruciate ligament injury.
D. Tibial collateral ligament, lateral meniscus, and posterior cruciate ligament injury.
E. Tibial collateral ligament, medial meniscus, and anterior cruciate ligament injury.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): O'Donoghue's "unhappy triad" is classically defined as the simultaneous tearing of the tibial collateral ligament, medial meniscus, and anterior cruciate ligament.
Incorrect (A): This option incorrectly substitutes the fibular collateral ligament and lateral meniscus for the medial structures involved.
Incorrect (B): This option incorrectly pairs the lateral fibular collateral ligament and the posterior cruciate ligament with the medial meniscus.
Incorrect (C): This choice incorrectly identifies the lateral meniscus instead of the medial meniscus, which is attached to the collateral ligament.
Incorrect (D): This option inaccurately incorporates both the lateral meniscus and the posterior cruciate ligament into the classic triad definition.
