 
Question: During anatomical study of the posterior aspect of the hip joint, a group of small muscles including obturator internus, obturator externus, gemelli, and quadratus femoris are identified. What is their principal function:
A. some act as medial rotators of the thigh.
B. their actions depend on degree of hip extension.
C. their actions depend on degree of hip flexion.
D. they are powerful abductors of the thigh.
E. they are powerful lateral rotators of the thigh.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): This functional group forms the deep gluteal muscles whose primary mechanical alignment allows them to act as they are powerful lateral rotators of the thigh at the hip joint.
Incorrect (A): These specific deep muscles do not possess orientations that allow them to function as some act as medial rotators of the thigh.
Incorrect (B): While positional changes can minorly alter vectors, their structural layout means their actions depend on degree of hip extension is incorrect as their primary action is not inverted by extension.
Incorrect (C): It is incorrect to state their actions depend on degree of hip flexion as their defining principal function, which remains lateral rotation in anatomical position.
Incorrect (D): The deep rotators are too small and horizontally oriented to ensure they are powerful abductors of the thigh, a role reserved for gluteus medius and minimus.

Question: A 22-year-old athlete presents with weakness of thigh extension and difficulty rising from a sitting position. Which of the following muscles is the strongest extensor of the thigh and plays an important role in this movement:
A. gluteus maximus.
B. gluteus minimus.
C. iliopsoas.
D. quadratus femoris.
E. tensor fasciae latae.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): The gluteus maximus is the largest muscle mass of the gluteal region and serves as the most powerful extensor of the hip joint when force is required.
Incorrect (B): The gluteus minimus acts primarily to abduct and medially rotate the hip, offering no contribution to hip extension.
Incorrect (C): The iliopsoas is a direct antagonist to extension, serving as the strongest flexor of the thigh at the hip joint.
Incorrect (D): The quadratus femoris functions purely as a short lateral rotator of the thigh and cannot generate hip extension.
Incorrect (E): The tensor fasciae latae acts to flex and abduct the thigh, which opposes the extension movement required to stand up.

Question: During dissection of the femur in an anatomy laboratory, a student identifies the insertion of the tendon of piriformis muscle. This tendon attaches to which of the following parts of the femur:
A. femoral head.
B. femoral shaft.
C. greater trochanter.
D. lesser trochanter.
E. popliteal surface.
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The piriformis muscle exits the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen and inserts directly onto the superior border of the greater trochanter of the femur.
Incorrect (A): The femoral head is completely intra-articular and articulates with the acetabulum; it does not receive any muscular insertions.
Incorrect (B): The femoral shaft serves as the attachment site for larger thigh muscles like the vasti and adductors, not short gluteal rotators.
Incorrect (D): The lesser trochanter serves as the specific insertion site for the tendon of the iliopsoas muscle.
Incorrect (E): The popliteal surface is located on the posterior distal aspect of the femur and forms the floor of the popliteal fossa.

A) Gluteus maximus

Correct (B): The gluteus minimus (alongside the gluteus medius) is anatomically responsible for both abducting and medially rotating the thigh; hence, its paralysis matches these dual findings perfectly.
Incorrect (A): The gluteus maximus is a powerful extensor and lateral rotator of the thigh, functioning in complete opposition to medial rotation.
Incorrect (C): The obturator internus is a short lateral rotator of the thigh, which does not abduct or medially rotate the limb.
Incorrect (D): The piriformis serves as a lateral rotator of the extended thigh and an abductor during flexion, but it cannot perform medial rotation.
Incorrect (E): The quadratus femoris is a dedicated lateral rotator and adductor of the thigh, directly contradicting the presentation of abductor/medial rotator weakness.

Question: During a gluteal intramuscular injection, the physician carefully selects a site to avoid injury to the sciatic nerve. The safest site for injection is which of the following areas?
A) Lower lateral quadrant of the gluteal region
B) Lower medial quadrant of the gluteal region
C) Midpoint of gemelli muscles of the gluteal region
D) Over the sacrospinous ligament of the gluteal region
E) Upper lateral quadrant of the gluteal region
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The upper lateral quadrant of the gluteal region contains the thick muscle belly of the gluteus medius and is completely clear of the pathway of the sciatic nerve, making it the safest site for injection.
Incorrect (A): The lower lateral quadrant of the gluteal region places the injection too close to the emergence paths of regional pelvic nerves and the deep structures of the hip joint.
Incorrect (B): The lower medial quadrant of the gluteal region lies directly over the anatomic course of the thick sciatic nerve, carrying an extreme risk of nerve laceration or chemical injury.
Incorrect (C): The midpoint of gemelli muscles of the gluteal region is situated deep in the infrapiriform space, right where the sciatic nerve runs down into the thigh.
Incorrect (D): The region over the sacrospinous ligament of the gluteal region houses the internal pudendal vessels and the pudendal nerve, making it highly hazardous for needle placement.

Question: A patient receiving an intramuscular injection in the gluteal region suddenly develops severe radiating pain down the posterior aspect of the thigh and leg. Which nerve is most likely injured due to incorrect placement of the injection?
 
A. Common fibular nerve
B. Saphenous nerve
C. Sciatic nerve
D. Superior gluteal nerve
E. Sural nerve
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The Sciatic nerve exits the pelvis inferior to the piriformis muscle; improper injections given in the lower or inner quadrants of the gluteal region can directly injure it, causing pain down the posterior thigh and leg.
Incorrect (A): Common fibular nerve arises as a terminal branch of the sciatic nerve near the popliteal fossa, far distal to the gluteal injection site.
Incorrect (B): Saphenous nerve is a branch of the femoral nerve supplying the anteromedial leg and foot, entirely unrelated to the gluteal region.
Incorrect (D): Superior gluteal nerve exits superior to the piriformis to supply the gluteus medius and minimus, and its injury causes Trendelenburg sign rather than posterior radiating pain.
Incorrect (E): Sural nerve is a sensory nerve formed in the posterior calf, far removed from the gluteal region.

A. Extension and lateral rotation

Explanation:
Correct (A): The gluteus maximus is the primary extensor and a powerful lateral rotation engine of the thigh at the hip joint.

Incorrect (D): The muscle opposes both flexion and medial rotation, making this functional pairing completely incorrect.
Incorrect (E): Hyperabduction and circumduction are primarily driven by the abductors (gluteus medius/minimus) and multi-muscle coordination, not the gluteus maximus.

Question: A patient presents with inability to stabilize the pelvis while walking on one leg. Which muscle is paralyzed, and which nerve is involved?

B. Gluteus maximus, inferior gluteal nerve
C. Gluteus medius, superior gluteal nerve
D. Iliopsoas, femoral nerve
E. Tensor fascia lata, femoral nerve
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The gluteus medius is the primary abductor of the hip innervated by the superior gluteal nerve, and its paralysis prevents pelvis stabilization when standing on one leg.
Incorrect (A): The adductor longus is innervated by the obturator nerve and adducts the thigh; it does not prevent contralateral pelvic drop during gait.
Incorrect (B): The gluteus maximus is supplied by the inferior gluteal nerve and extends the hip, rather than stabilizing the horizontal plane of the pelvis.
Incorrect (D): The iliopsoas is a major hip flexor supplied by the femoral nerve and lumbar ventral rami, playing no direct role in lateral pelvic stabilization.
Incorrect (E): The tensor fascia lata is innervated by the superior gluteal nerve, not the femoral nerve, making this anatomical pairing incorrect.
