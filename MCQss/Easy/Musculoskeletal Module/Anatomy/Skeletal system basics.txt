
Question: Regarding the clavicle, all of the following statements are correct EXCEPT:
A. It has no medullary cavity
B. It is an example of the short bones
C. It is the commonly fractured bone
D. It is the first bone to ossify in the fetal life
E. It ossifies in membrane
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * **Correct (B):** The clavicle is classified morphologically as a modified long bone because it is elongated and placed horizontally, making it incorrect to call it an example of the short bones.
 * **Incorrect (A):** The clavicle is atypical for a long bone as it has no medullary cavity.
 * **Incorrect (C):** The clavicle is highly vulnerable to indirect forces transmitted from the arm, making it the commonly fractured bone.
 * **Incorrect (D):** It is embryologically unique as it is the first bone to ossify in the fetal life.
 * **Incorrect (E):** Unlike most typical long bones, it ossifies in membrane through intramembranous ossification.

Question: A physician examines the anatomy of the hip joint and reviews the ligaments responsible for stabilizing it. Which ligament strongly resists hyperextension of the hip joint?
 
A. Iliofemoral ligament
B. Ischiofemoral ligament
C. Ligament of head of femur
D. Pubofemoral ligament
E. Transverse acetabular ligament
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): The Iliofemoral ligament (Y-ligament of Bigelow) is the strongest ligament in the body and tightens significantly during extension, strongly resisting hyperextension of the hip joint.
Incorrect (B): Ischiofemoral ligament limits hyperextension and medial rotation but is much weaker and acts secondary to the iliofemoral ligament.
Incorrect (C): Ligament of head of femur (ligamentum teres) conducts a small artery to the femoral head and primarily tightens during adduction rather than resisting hyperextension.
Incorrect (D): Pubofemoral ligament checks excessive abduction and lateral rotation of the hip joint rather than primarily resisting hyperextension.
Incorrect (E): Transverse acetabular ligament bridges the acetabular notch to convert it into a foramen and does not restrict joint motion or resist hyperextension.

Question: A patient presents with inability to laterally rotate the thigh and external rotation at hip joint after a posterior dislocation of the hip. Which nerve is most likely injured?
A. Femoral nerve
B. Inferior gluteal nerve
C. Obturator nerve
D. Sciatic nerve
E. Superior gluteal nerve
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The sciatic nerve runs directly posterior to the hip joint capsule and is the most common nerve injured during a posterior dislocation of the hip, disrupting regional motor function.
Incorrect (A): The femoral nerve lies anterior to the hip joint and is unaffected by standard posterior dislocation displacement.
Incorrect (B): The inferior gluteal nerve exits superior/posteriorly to supply the gluteus maximus but is not trapped or disrupted as consistently as the thick main trunk of the sciatic nerve.
Incorrect (C): The obturator nerve passes through the obturator foramen anteromedially, insulated from posterior hip joint disruptions.
Incorrect (E): The superior gluteal nerve runs superiorly to the piriformis muscle, escaping direct impingement from a lower posterior femoral head displacement.

Question: During a posterior approach to the hip, a surgeon accidentally transects the sciatic nerve before it bifurcates. Which of the following functions will be lost first?
A. Hip abduction
B. Hip adduction
C. Hip extension
D. Hip lateral rotation
E. Knee flexion and foot dorsiflexion
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Complete transection of the main trunk of the sciatic nerve proximal to its bifurcation immediately halts all motor signaling to the hamstring muscles and all muscles below the knee, eliminating knee flexion and foot dorsiflexion.
Incorrect (A): Hip abduction is preserved because the gluteus medius and minimus are supplied by the superior gluteal nerve, which arises independently from the sacral plexus.
Incorrect (B): Hip adduction is unaffected because the medial thigh muscles are innervated by the obturator nerve, a branch of the lumbar plexus.
Incorrect (C): Hip extension is largely preserved because its primary driver, the gluteus maximus, is innervated independently by the inferior gluteal nerve.
Incorrect (D): Hip lateral rotation is maintained because the deep lateral rotators are supplied by distinct, short muscular branches of the sacral plexus that exit proximal to the sciatic nerve.
