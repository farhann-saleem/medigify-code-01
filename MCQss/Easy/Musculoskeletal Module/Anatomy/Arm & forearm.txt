
Question: The muscle attached to the coracoid process of scapula is:
A. brachialis.
B. deltoid.
C. long head of biceps brachii.
D. long head of triceps brachii.
E. short head of biceps brachii.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): short head of biceps brachii. originates directly from the apex of the coracoid process of the scapula alongside the coracobrachialis muscle.
 * Incorrect (A): brachialis. originates from the anterior distal half of the humerus and inserts onto the ulnar tuberosity.
 * Incorrect (B): deltoid. attaches to the spine of the scapula, acromion, and lateral third of the clavicle, not the coracoid process.
 * Incorrect (C): long head of biceps brachii. originates from the supraglenoid tubercle of the scapula, routing intracapsularly over the humeral head.
 * Incorrect (D): long head of triceps brachii. originates from the infraglenoid tubercle of the scapula.

Question: The structure that holds the head of radius against the ulna is the:
A. annular ligament.
B. interosseous membrane.
C. lateral collateral ligament.

Explanation:

 * Incorrect (B): interosseous membrane. is a flat sheet of connective tissue connecting the shafts of the radius and ulna, not localized to the radial head.
 * Incorrect (C): lateral collateral ligament. reinforces the lateral side of the elbow joint capsule but does not encircle the radial head.
 * Incorrect (D): medial collateral ligament. stabilizes the medial side of the elbow joint, checking valgus stress.
 * Incorrect (E): ulnar collateral ligament. is synonymous with the medial collateral ligament, providing zero structural support to the proximal radioulnar articulation.

 * **Incorrect (B):** The median nerve travels anteromedially down the arm and is typically injured in supracondylar fractures of the distal humerus.
 * **Incorrect (C):** The musculocutaneous nerve courses deeply through the coracobrachialis muscle and is rarely damaged by closed mid-shaft humeral fractures.
 * **Incorrect (E):** The ulnar nerve passes behind the medial epicondyle distally and is not endangered at the mid-shaft level.
