Question: A medical student dissecting the gluteal region of a cadaver observes the iliotibial tract and notes two muscles inserting into it. Which of the following muscles are attached to the iliotibial tract?
A. Gluteus maximus & Gluteus medius
B. Gluteus maximus & Gluteus minimus
C. Gluteus maximus & Tensor fascia lata
D. Piriformis & Tensor fascia lata
E. Tensor fascia lata & Gluteus minimus
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The iliotibial tract serves as a shared insertion point for the superficial and larger portions of the Gluteus maximus & Tensor fascia lata, stabilizing the knee and hip joints during locomotion.
Incorrect (A): The Gluteus medius does not attach to this tract; it inserts directly onto the lateral surface of the greater trochanter of the femur.
Incorrect (B): The Gluteus minimus does not attach to this tract; it inserts onto the anterior aspect of the greater trochanter of the femur.
Incorrect (D): The Piriformis muscle is a deep lateral rotator that inserts onto the superior border of the greater trochanter and has no structural association with the iliotibial tract.
Incorrect (E): The Gluteus minimus is incorrectly included here because it inserts onto the greater trochanter, not into the fascia.

C. the femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve lies within the lateral compartment.

Incorrect (B): It is false because the femoral artery occupies the intermediate compartment; the artery actually occupies the lateral compartment of the sheath.
Incorrect (C): The statement is incorrect because the femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve lies within the lateral compartment, piercing the anterior wall rather than traveling as a long-term resident inside it.
Incorrect (E): The statement is false because the femoral nerve occupies the intermediate compartment; the femoral nerve runs completely outside and lateral to the femoral sheath.

Incorrect (C): The pectineus is a flat, quadrangular muscle forming part of the medial floor of the femoral triangle.

Incorrect (C): The statement is anatomically reversed because the lateral compartment contains the femoral vein; the lateral compartment encloses the femoral artery.
Incorrect (E): The statement is false because the posterior wall is formed by the fascia lata; the posterior wall is created by the fascia iliaca and pectineus fascia.

Incorrect (C): The statement is anatomically true because the medial circumflex femoral artery arises from profunda femoris artery in the vast majority of individuals.
Incorrect (D): The statement is true because the profunda femoris artery is a large branch of femoral artery given off within the femoral triangle to supply the deep thigh.
Incorrect (E): The statement is true because the superficial epigastric artery arises from femoral artery near the inguinal ligament as a superficial branch.

Incorrect (B): The inferior gluteal artery supplies the gluteus maximus and adjacent pelvic structures, playing no major role in adult femoral head nutrition.
Incorrect (D): The obturator artery gives off a branch via the ligamentum teres, but this vessel is typically small or obliterated in adults and does not provide the chief blood supply.
Incorrect (E): The superficial circumflex iliac artery is a superficial branch of the femoral artery supplying skin and fascia near the inguinal region.
