
Question: The following proximal row of carpal bones except:
A. lunate
B. pisiform
C. scaphoid
D. triquetral
E. trapezium
**Correct Option: E**
**Explanation:**
 * **Correct (E):** The trapezium is a carpal bone belonging exclusively to the distal row, where it articulates with the first metacarpal bone.
 * **Incorrect (A):** The lunate is a central bone situated firmly within the proximal row of carpal bones.
 * **Incorrect (B):** The pisiform is a small, sesamoid bone located within the proximal row of carpal bones, sitting on the triquetrum.
 * **Incorrect (C):** The scaphoid is the most lateral bone of the proximal row of carpal bones.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The triquetral bone is positioned on the medial aspect of the proximal row of carpal bones.

Question: The medial wall of the anatomical snuff box consists of the tendon from:
A. abductor pollicis longus
B. extensor pollicis brevis
C. extensor pollicis longus
D. flexor carpi radialis

**Correct Option: C**
**Explanation:**
 * **Correct (C):** The medial (posterior) boundary of the anatomical snuff box is formed exclusively by the tendon of the extensor pollicis longus muscle.
 * **Incorrect (A):** The abductor pollicis longus tendon forms part of the lateral (anterior) boundary, not the medial wall.
 * **Incorrect (B):** The extensor pollicis brevis tendon travels alongside the abductor pollicis longus to form the lateral boundary.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The flexor carpi radialis lies on the volar aspect of the wrist and is completely excluded from the dorsal snuff box boundaries.
 * **Incorrect (E):** The flexor pollicis longus is a deep anterior compartment muscle that does not participate in forming this dorsal space.

Question: Injury to ulnar nerve results in absence of sensation in which part of the hand?
A. Index finger
B. Little finger
C. Middle finger
D. Ring finger
E. Thumb
**Correct Option: B**
**Explanation:**
 * **Correct (B):** The ulnar nerve provides exclusive cutaneous sensory innervation to the palmar and dorsal aspects of the little finger (pinky).
 * **Incorrect (A):** The index finger receives its sensory innervation strictly via the digital branches of the median nerve.
 * **Incorrect (C):** The middle finger is innervated cutaneously on its palmar aspect entirely by the median nerve.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The ring finger has split innervation (median lateral, ulnar medial), making it an imprecise selection compared to the absolute loss in the little finger.
 * **Incorrect (E):** The thumb's cutaneous sensation is governed by the median nerve (palmar) and radial nerve (dorsal).

A. deep branch of the radial nerve.

 * **Incorrect (A):** The deep branch of the radial nerve travels in the posterior compartment of the forearm and does not enter the carpal tunnel.
 * **Incorrect (B):** The deep branch of the ulnar nerve enters the hand through Guyon's canal, running completely outside the carpal tunnel.
 * **Incorrect (D):** The superficial branch of the radial nerve courses superficially over the anatomical snuffbox and bypasses the palmar wrist compartments entirely.
 * **Incorrect (E):** The superficial branch of the ulnar nerve travels superficially to the flexor retinaculum within Guyon's canal and is uninvolved in carpal tunnel syndrome.

Question: Injury of which nerve can lead to claw hand:
A. axillary nerve
B. median nerve
C. musculocutaneous nerve
D. radial nerve
E. ulnar nerve
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): Chronic injury to the ulnar nerve paralyzes the medial lumbricals and interossei, leading to the characteristic hyperextension at MCP and flexion at IP joints termed claw hand.
 * Incorrect (A): Axillary nerve damage paralyzes the deltoid muscle, which leads to loss of shoulder abduction without impacting hand posture.
 * Incorrect (B): Median nerve injury typically causes "ape hand" deformity due to loss of thenar opposition, affecting the lateral fingers rather than classic claw hand.
 * Incorrect (C): Musculocutaneous nerve injury causes loss of elbow flexion and forearm supination without directly affecting intrinsic hand mechanics.
 * Incorrect (D): Radial nerve injury paralyzes the forearm extensors, causing "wrist drop" rather than structural clawing of the digits.
   Tags: Easy, Anatomy, Musculo skeletal module (MSK), Hand

 * Incorrect (C): The scaphoid bone belongs to the proximal carpal row, rendering this sequence anatomically incorrect for the distal segment.

 * Incorrect (C): The tendon of palmaris longus travels superficially in the forearm and inserts directly onto the anterior surface of the flexor retinaculum.
 * Incorrect (D): The ulnar nerve courses superficial to the flexor retinaculum through Guyon's canal on the medial side of the wrist.
 * Incorrect (E): The ulnar vessels travel alongside the ulnar nerve superficially through Guyon's canal, well outside the deep boundaries of the carpal tunnel.

Question: Wasting of thenar eminence may be caused by damage of:
A. anterior interosseous nerve.
B. median nerve.
C. palmar branches of musculocutaneous nerve.
D. radial nerve.
E. ulnar nerve.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): The recurrent branch of the median nerve innervates the thenar muscles (abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, and opponens pollicis); denervation yields muscle atrophy or wasting of thenar eminence.
 * Incorrect (A): The anterior interosseous nerve is a pure motor branch of the median nerve supplying deep forearm flexors, but it terminates before entering the hand.
 * Incorrect (C): The musculocutaneous nerve terminates as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm, giving sensation to the lateral forearm skin with no motor branches to the thenar muscles.
 * Incorrect (D): The radial nerve supplies motor control to the extensor compartments of the arm and forearm, supplying only sensation to parts of the dorsal hand.
 * Incorrect (E): The ulnar nerve supplies most intrinsic hand muscles, including the hypothenar eminence, but spares the bulk of the thenar eminence muscles.

A. anterior interosseous nerve.
B. median nerve.
C. nerve to anconeus.
D. nerve supplying the supinator.

Explanation:
 * Correct (B): The median nerve travels through the restricted fibro-osseous space of the carpal tunnel beneath the flexor retinaculum, making it the targeted structure compressed in carpal tunnel syndrome.
 * Incorrect (A): The anterior interosseous nerve branches off in the proximal forearm to supply deep muscles and does not pass through the carpal tunnel.
 * Incorrect (C): The nerve to anconeus is a branch of the radial nerve given off in the arm, terminating at the elbow joint.
 * Incorrect (D): The nerve supplying the supinator is the deep branch of the radial nerve (posterior interosseous nerve), which stays in the posterior compartment of the forearm.
 * Incorrect (E): The ulnar nerve enters the hand superficially to the flexor retinaculum via Guyon's canal, running completely outside the carpal tunnel.
