Question: What is the primary component of yellow marrow?
A. Dense fibrous tissue
B. Hematopoietic tissue
C. Hyaline cartilage
D. Reticular connective tissue
E. Unilocular fat
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Yellow marrow is a non-hematopoietic stromal tissue predominantly composed of unilocular fat (adipocytes) which stores energy within the medullary cavity of adult long bones.
Incorrect (A): Dense fibrous tissue is found in ligaments, tendons, and the outer layer of the periosteum, not inside the central bone marrow space.
Incorrect (B): Hematopoietic tissue is the active, blood-cell-producing element that defines red marrow, which is replaced by yellow marrow as individuals age.
Incorrect (C): Hyaline cartilage is an avascular skeletal tissue found on articular surfaces and epiphyseal plates, never a component of bone marrow.
Incorrect (D): Reticular connective tissue forms the structural cellular framework of both red and yellow marrow but is not the primary volume component of yellow marrow.
