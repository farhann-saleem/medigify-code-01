Question: What is the space that an osteocyte rests in?
A. Canaliculi
B. Haversian canal
C. Howship's lacuna
D. Lacuna
E. Volkmann's canal
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): A lacuna is the specific, fluid-filled micro-cavity within the mineralized bone matrix that houses the cell body of a mature osteocyte.
Incorrect (A): Canaliculi are the tiny tunnel-like channels containing cytoplasmic processes, not the primary space where the main cell body of the osteocyte rests.
Incorrect (B): Haversian canal is a central macroscopic channel running longitudinally through an osteon to carry blood vessels and nerves.
Incorrect (C): Howship's lacuna is a transient, enzymatically carved resorption depression on a bone surface that houses active osteoclasts, not resting osteocytes.
Incorrect (E): Volkmann's canal is a transverse channel housing blood vessels that runs perpendicular to osteons to connect Haversian canals to each other and the periosteum.

Question: Which of the following descriptions is a characteristic of cancellous bone:
A. composed of a dense network of parallel columns
B. composed of a thin shell of flat plates
C. composed of an interconnected network of trabeculae
D. composed of parallel arrays of osteons
E. composed of solid layers of circumferential lamellae
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Cancellous bone is defined macro- and micro-architecturally by being composed of an interconnected network of trabeculae (bony spicules) that enclose large marrow spaces.
Incorrect (A): Composed of a dense network of parallel columns describes the structural arrangement of osteons found exclusively within compact bone.
Incorrect (B): Composed of a thin shell of flat plates describes the flat bones of the skull or the outer tables of compact bone rather than the inner spongy architecture.
Incorrect (D): Composed of parallel arrays of osteons is the signature microanatomical characteristic of compact bone, not cancellous bone.
Incorrect (E): Composed of solid layers of circumferential lamellae describes the outer and inner boundaries of compact bone cortices.

Question: Which cell is the mature bone cell?
A. Chondroblast
B. Chondrocyte
C. Osteblast
D. Osteoclast
E. Osteocyte
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The osteocyte is the mature bone cell that becomes completely entrapped within a mineralized matrix lacuna to maintain bone homeostasis and detect mechanical strain.
Incorrect (A): Chondroblast is an immature cartilage-forming cell that secretes the extracellular matrix of cartilage.
Incorrect (B): Chondrocyte is a mature cartilage cell residing within cartilage lacunae, completely distinct from bone tissue cells.
Incorrect (C): Osteblast is an active, immature bone-forming cell responsible for synthesizing and secreting unmineralized osteoid matrix.
Incorrect (D): Osteoclast is a large, multinucleated macrophage-derived cell specialized for resorbing bone matrix during remodeling.

Question: The two most abundant inorganic components of bone matrix are:
A. calcium carbonate and sodium citrate
B. calcium phosphate and calcium carbonate
C. fluoride crystals and magnesium carbonate
D. magnesium sulfate and calcium sulfate
E. potassium chloride and sodium phosphate
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Calcium phosphate (which precipitates as hydroxyapatite crystals) and calcium carbonate represent the vast majority of the inorganic minerals that give bone its compressive strength.
Incorrect (A): Calcium carbonate and sodium citrate is incorrect because sodium citrate is only present in trace quantities within bone matrix.
Incorrect (C): Fluoride crystals and magnesium carbonate are trace or minor constituent elements that modify bone crystal structure but are not highly abundant.
Incorrect (D): Magnesium sulfate and calcium sulfate are sulfate salts not typically utilized or concentrated to a significant degree within mineralized bone matrix.
Incorrect (E): Potassium chloride and sodium phosphate are soluble salts predominantly localized to fluid compartments rather than forming solid bone mineral crystals.

Question: Haversian canals are linked by:
A. canaliculi
B. lacunae
C. osteons
D. periosteum
E. Volkmann's canals
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Volkmann's canals are vascular channels that run transversely or obliquely through compact bone to directly link adjacent longitudinal Haversian canals to one another.
Incorrect (A): Canaliculi link adjacent lacunae to each other and to Haversian canals for nutrient micro-diffusion, but do not link the large Haversian canals directly.
Incorrect (B): Lacunae are isolated cellular fluid spaces housing osteocyte cell bodies and do not serve as structural linking conduits for macroscopic canals.
Incorrect (C): Osteons are the complete concentric cylindrical functional units of compact bone rather than the individual interconnecting cross-channels.
Incorrect (D): Periosteum is the external bilayered dense connective tissue capsule that covers the outside of a bone, not an internal connecting channel.

Incorrect (E): Specialized transport proteins along the articular surface do not constitute the primary or widespread mechanism for deeper parenchymal chondrocyte nutrition.

Question: Which type of cartilage is found in intervertebral disc:
A. articular cartilage
B. costal cartilage
C. elastic cartilage
D. fibrous cartilage
E. hyaline cartilage
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Fibrous cartilage (fibrocartilage) combines dense regular connective tissue arrays with cartilage features to provide extreme tensile strength within the annulus fibrosus of the intervertebral disc.
Incorrect (A): Articular cartilage forms the protective, low-friction capping layer on the bony ends of diarthrodial joints rather than making up the body of the intervertebral disc.
Incorrect (B): Costal cartilage forms the specific anatomical bridges that securely connect the anterior ends of the ribs to the sternum.
Incorrect (C): Elastic cartilage contains highly flexible branching resilient networks that are restricted to sites like the external pinna, epiglottis, and auditory tubes.
Incorrect (E): Hyaline cartilage forms a homogenous matrix that lacks the dense bundles of heavy type I collagen fibers structurally required within intervertebral discs.

Question: What is the primary component of fibrocartilage:
A. collagen fibers
B. elastic fibers
C. ground substance
D. keratin fibers
E. reticular fibers
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Dense bundles of thick collagen fibers (specifically Type I alongside Type II) give fibrocartilage its hallmark structural resistance to severe shear and tensile forces.
Incorrect (B): Elastic fibers impart shape-rebound flexibility and are found in elastic cartilage rather than the dense, tough matrix of fibrocartilage.
Incorrect (C): Ground substance exists within fibrocartilage but is sparse compared to the overwhelming dense networks of fibrillar proteins that dominate the tissue volume.
Incorrect (D): Keratin fibers are intracellular intermediate filaments found in epithelial keratinocytes and do not occur as an extracellular component of cartilage matrices.
Incorrect (E): Reticular fibers form delicate supporting networks for cellular organs and do not provide the heavy tensile strength required in fibrocartilage matrices.

Question: What is the term for the spaces in which chondrocytes are located in fibrocartilage:
A. canaliculi
B. haversian canals
C. lacunae
D. perichondrial clefts
E. Volkmann's canals
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Lacunae are the small, fluid-filled structural spaces or cavities within the rigid extracellular matrix that isolate and house individual or groups of chondrocytes.
Incorrect (A): Canaliculi are the microscopic radiating tunnels that span the calcified matrix of bone to interconnect adjacent living osteocytes.
Incorrect (B): Haversian canals are major longitudinal macro-channels that carry neurovascular bundles through the center of osteons in compact bone tissue.
Incorrect (D): Perichondrial clefts is an unscientific term that does not describe the microenvironment enclosing mature, fully embedded fibrocartilage cells.
Incorrect (E): Volkmann's canals are transverse bony channels that transport blood vessels from the periosteum inward to interconnect individual longitudinal Haversian systems.
