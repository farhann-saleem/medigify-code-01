
Question: Local anesthetic act by:
A. blocking calcium channel
B. blocking NMJ
C. blocking potassium channel
D. blocking sodium channel
E. blocking sodium/potassium ATPase pump
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

* ​Correct (D): Local anesthetics directly exert their effect by blocking sodium channel proteins along the neuronal axon, preventing the influx of sodium ions necessary for action potential generation and propagation.
* ​Incorrect (A): Blocking calcium channel primarily impacts cardiac pacemakers or smooth muscle contraction rather than preventing axonal nociceptive propagation.
* ​Incorrect (B): Blocking NMJ disrupts somatic motor efferent transmission but does not describe the foundational mechanism of peripheral sensory local anesthesia.
* ​Incorrect (C): Blocking potassium channel delays cell repolarization instead of blocking the depolarization threshold required to trigger sensory nerve conduction.
* ​Incorrect (E): Blocking sodium/potassium ATPase pump disrupts long-term resting membrane potential homeostatic gradients but does not block acute action potential propagation.

Question: Which of the following local anesthetic drugs has vasoconstrictive properties:
A. bupivacaine
B. cocaine
C. lidocaine
D. mepivacaine
E. procaine
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

* ​Correct (B): Cocaine stands out because it blocks the presynaptic reuptake of norepinephrine, leading to localized sympathetic accumulation and intrinsic vasoconstrictive properties.
* ​Incorrect (A): Bupivacaine exerts direct smooth muscle relaxation properties that result in significant clinical vasodilation at the site of administration.
* ​Incorrect (C): Lidocaine acts as a classic vasodilatory agent that increases regional blood flow, requiring an added vasoconstrictor to extend its clinical longevity.
* ​Incorrect (D): Mepivacaine displays minimal but distinct vasodilation in most clinical settings, lacking any intrinsic vasoconstrictive properties.
* ​Incorrect (E): Procaine is an ester anesthetic with strong vasodilatory actions that promote rapid systemic absorption away from the target injection site.

Question: Bupivacaine is used for surgical procedures as a/an:
A. antiemetic agent
B. general anesthetic
C. local anesthetic
D. muscle relaxant
E. stimulant agent
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

* ​Correct (C): Bupivacaine is an amide local anesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels along nerve fibers, preventing the conduction of local pain and sensory signals during regional or infiltration anesthesia.
* ​Incorrect (A): antiemetic agent medications prevent vomiting by acting on central or peripheral emetic receptors, a property bupivacaine lacks.
* ​Incorrect (B): general anesthetic agents induce a reversible loss of consciousness and central nervous system depression, whereas bupivacaine provides localized, regional nociceptive blockade.
* ​Incorrect (D): muscle relaxant drugs act at the neuromuscular junction or within the central reflex arches, while bupivacaine functions non-specifically on axonal sodium conduction.
* ​Incorrect (E): stimulant agent drugs increase physiological or nervous activity in the body, which directly contradicts the nerve-blocking properties of bupivacaine.
