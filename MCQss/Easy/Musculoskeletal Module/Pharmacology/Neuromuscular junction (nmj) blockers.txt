
Question: Neuromuscular blockers work by:
A. binding to muscarinic receptors at NMJ
B. binding to nicotinic receptors at NMJ
C. destroying NMJ
D. enhancing access of receptor of NMJ to epinephrine
E. inhibiting release of calcium at NMJ
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

* ​Correct (B): Neuromuscular blockers target somatic motor transmission by binding to nicotinic receptors at NMJ, preventing endogenous acetylcholine from safely depolarizing the motor endplate.
* ​Incorrect (A): Binding to muscarinic receptors at NMJ is incorrect because muscarinic receptors govern autonomic postganglionic parasympathetic sites, not skeletal muscle endplates.
* ​Incorrect (C): Destroying NMJ implies irreversible structural or cellular tissue necrosis, whereas neuromuscular blockade represents a temporary, fully reversible pharmacological state.
* ​Incorrect (D): Enhancing access of receptor of NMJ to epinephrine describes an adrenergic mechanism, whereas the neuromuscular junction is strictly driven by cholinergic signaling.
* ​Incorrect (E): Inhibiting release of calcium at NMJ describes a pre-synaptic or intracellular defect, whereas clinical neuromuscular blockers target surface membrane endplate receptors.

Question: Which of the following drugs is a non-depolarizing blocker of NMJ:
A. atracurium
B. cisatracurium
C. pancuronium
D. rocuronium
E. tubocurarine
Correct Option: E
Explanation:

* ​Correct (E): Tubocurarine serves as the foundational, classic non-depolarizing blocker of NMJ, acting as a competitive antagonist against acetylcholine at the postsynaptic motor endplate.
* ​Incorrect (A): Atracurium is a competitive, non-depolarizing agent that breaks down via Hofmann elimination, but it is a modern synthetic drug rather than the classic alkaloid index drug.
* ​Incorrect (B): Cisatracurium is an isomer of atracurium used for its clean organ-independent elimination profile, not the primary historical reference non-depolarizing blocker.
* ​Incorrect (C): Pancuronium is an aminosteroid non-depolarizing blocker known for vagolytic side effects, distinct from the benchmark benzylisoquinoline tubocurarine.
* ​Incorrect (D): Rocuronium is an intermediate-acting aminosteroid non-depolarizing neuromuscular agent commonly chosen for rapid sequence intubation.

Question: Neuromuscular paralysis that results from pharmacologic antagonism at the acetylcholine receptor of the end plate (e.g., by tubocurarine) is called:
A. depolarizing blockade
B. desensitization
C. non-depolarizing blockade
D. phase II blockade
E. stabilizing blockade
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

* ​Correct (C): Competitive inhibition without activating the postsynaptic membrane prevents endplate potential development, an effect formally classified as a non-depolarizing blockade.
* ​Incorrect (A): Depolarizing blockade involves persistent, long-term stimulation and initial activation of the endplate, which is opposite to the silent receptor antagonism of tubocurarine.
* ​Incorrect (B): Desensitization is a prolonged state of unresponsiveness induced by persistent agonist exposure, distinct from competitive receptor blockade.
* ​Incorrect (D): Phase II blockade occurs when a depolarizing agent like succinylcholine undergoes prolonged exposure, causing the endplate to respond like a non-depolarizing block.
* ​Incorrect (E): Stabilizing blockade is an archaic historical synonym that does not match standard modern pharmacological classifications for competitive endplate antagonism.

Question: Which drug acts like a nicotinic agonist and depolarizes the neuromuscular end plate:
A. edrophonium
B. neostigmine
C. pyridostigmine
D. succinylcholine
E. tubocurarine
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

* ​Correct (D): Succinylcholine consists of two joined acetylcholine molecules, allowing it to act as a resilient nicotinic agonist that depolarizes the neuromuscular end plate.
* ​Incorrect (A): Edrophonium is a short-acting, reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor used to assess myasthenic crises, rather than a direct nicotinic receptor agonist.
* ​Incorrect (B): Neostigmine is a reversible carbamate acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that raises endogenous acetylcholine levels instead of directly binding nicotinic sites as an agonist.
* ​Incorrect (C): Pyridostigmine is an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor utilized for the long-term maintenance of myasthenia gravis, lacking direct depolarizing agonist properties.
* ​Incorrect (E): Tubocurarine acts as a competitive antagonist that prevents endplate depolarization, which is the exact opposite of a depolarizing nicotinic agonist.

Question: Tubocurarine acts on:
A. ganglionic nicotinic receptors
B. muscarinic receptors
C. nerve axons
D. neuromuscular end plates
E. receptor at adrenergic junctions
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

* ​Correct (D): Tubocurarine acts on neuromuscular end plates by serving as a competitive antagonist at somatic nicotinic receptors (N_M), blocking the action of acetylcholine.
* ​Incorrect (A): ganglionic nicotinic receptors (N_N) are molecularly distinct and are not the primary physiological site of action for tubocurarine at normal paralytic doses.
* ​Incorrect (B): muscarinic receptors are targets for postganglionic parasympathetic signaling and are not structurally or functionally blocked by tubocurarine.
* ​Incorrect (C): nerve axons rely on voltage-gated sodium and potassium channels to conduct electrical signals, which are unaffected by the receptor-blocking action of tubocurarine.
* ​Incorrect (E): receptor at adrenergic junctions respond to catecholamines within the sympathetic nervous system and do not bind tubocurarine.
