Question: Vitamin K is required by liver for normal formation of:
A. albumin.
B. fibrinogen.
C. globulin.
D. plasminogen.
E. prothrombin.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): prothrombin requires vitamin K as an essential cofactor for the post-translational gamma-carboxylation of its glutamic acid residues, enabling calcium binding and subsequent clotting activation.
Incorrect (A): albumin is a major plasma transport protein synthesized by the liver independently of vitamin K pathways.
Incorrect (B): fibrinogen is a soluble plasma protein converted to fibrin during coagulation, but its hepatic synthesis does not require vitamin K.
Incorrect (C): globulin represents a diverse group of proteins synthesized by both the liver and the immune system without requiring vitamin K functionality.
Incorrect (D): plasminogen is the inactive zymogen of the primary fibrinolytic enzyme plasmin and does not rely on vitamin K for its hepatic production.

​Question: After tissue injury, the extrinsic pathway of blood coagulation is initiated by:
A. factor VII.
B. factor IX.
C. factor X.
D. factor XII.
E. tissue factor.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): tissue factor is exposed on the subendothelial membrane following vascular trauma, binding plasma factor VIIa to directly initiate the extrinsic pathway.
Incorrect (A): factor VII circulates in plasma and must bind to the exposed tissue factor to form the active extrinsic tenase complex.
Incorrect (B): factor IX belongs exclusively to the intrinsic pathway component of the coagulation cascade.
Incorrect (C): factor X marks the beginning of the common pathway where the intrinsic and extrinsic pathways converge.
Incorrect (D): factor XII is the contact factor that initiates the intrinsic pathway upon exposure to negatively charged surfaces, not the extrinsic pathway.

​Question: Blood clot is dissolved by:
A. antithrombin.
B. fibrinolysin.
C. plasminogen.
D. protein C.
E. thrombin.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): fibrinolysin (also known as plasmin) actively cleaves fibrin polymers within a blood clot into soluble degradation products to dissolve the thrombus.
Incorrect (A): antithrombin is a circulating protease inhibitor that inactivates thrombin and factor Xa to prevent new clot formation rather than dissolving existing clots.
Incorrect (C): plasminogen is the inactive proenzyme that must first be cleaved by tissue plasminogen activator to become active fibrinolysin before it can dissolve a clot.
Incorrect (D): protein C is an anticoagulant that degrades activated factors V and VIII to limit clot propagation, but it lacks direct fibrinolytic activity to dissolve a clot.
Incorrect (E): thrombin is a procoagulant enzyme that converts fibrinogen to fibrin to form a blood clot, opposing the process of dissolution.
