
Question: In heme catabolism, the first bile pigment formed is:
A. bilirubin.
B. biliverdin.
C. mesobilirubin.
D. stercobilin.
E. urobilin.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): biliverdin is the first bile pigment formed when heme oxygenase opens the porphyrin ring.
Incorrect (A): bilirubin is formed later by reduction of biliverdin.
Incorrect (C): mesobilirubin is not the first bile pigment in heme catabolism.
Incorrect (D): stercobilin is a later intestinal pigment derived from bilirubin metabolism.
Incorrect (E): urobilin is a later urinary pigment derived from intestinal bilirubin metabolism.

Question: In heme catabolism, the first bile pigment formed is:
A. bilirubin
B. biliverdin
C. cholic acid
D. deoxycholic acid
E. lithocholic acid
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): Biliverdin is the immediate, green-pigmented cleavage product generated when the heme ring is opened at the alpha-methene bridge by microsomal heme oxygenase.
 * Incorrect (A): Bilirubin is a secondary bile pigment generated only after the primary cleavage product undergoes downstream reduction by cytosolic biliverdin reductase.
 * Incorrect (C): Cholic acid is a primary bile acid synthesized de novo from cholesterol in hepatocytes, completely independent of the oxidative degradation pathway of heme.
 * Incorrect (D): Deoxycholic acid is a secondary bile acid produced exclusively by microbial biotransformation of primary bile acids within the intestinal lumen.
 * Incorrect (E): Lithocholic acid is a toxic, monohydroxy secondary bile acid generated by bacterial 7-alpha-dehydroxylation of chenodeoxycholic acid in the distal gut.

Question: The conjugation of bilirubin occurs in liver cells. Which of the enzymes catalyzes this reaction:
A. bilirubin reductase
B. dismutase
C. serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase
D. UDP-glucose dehydrogenase
E. UDP-glucuronyl transferase
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): UDP-glucuronyl transferase catalyzes the transfer of glucuronic acid molecules from UDP-glucuronic acid to hydrophobic bilirubin, converting it into water-soluble bilirubin diglucuronide.
 * Incorrect (A): Bilirubin reductase is a non-physiological term; the enzyme that processes the bile pigment pathway prior to conjugation is biliverdin reductase, which synthesizes bilirubin.
 * Incorrect (B): Dismutase refers to enzymes like superoxide dismutase that neutralize reactive oxygen species and do not participate in organic anion conjugation reactions.
 * Incorrect (C): Serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase is a cytosolic and mitochondrial enzyme tracking hepatocellular injury that transfers amino groups rather than glucuronosyl moieties.
 * Incorrect (D): UDP-glucose dehydrogenase generates the UDP-glucuronic acid cofactor in the cytosol but does not directly catalyze the actual conjugation attachment to the bilirubin molecule.

Question: Biliverdin is converted into bilirubin by the action of enzyme:
A. bilirubin oxidase
B. bilirubin reductase
C. biliverdin oxidase
D. biliverdin reductase
E. heme oxygenase
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * Correct (D): Biliverdin reductase utilizes NADPH as a reducing equivalent donor to specifically reduce the central methene bridge of biliverdin, converting it into unconjugated bilirubin.
 * Incorrect (A): Bilirubin oxidase is an enzyme found in non-human systems like fungi that breaks down bilirubin, rather than synthesizing it from precursors within human heme catabolism.
 * Incorrect (B): Bilirubin reductase is a misnomer that inverts the physiological direction and substrate specificity of the cytosolic reduction step in bile pigment production.
 * Incorrect (C): Biliverdin oxidase does not exist in human metabolic pathways; further catabolism requires the reduction of biliverdin rather than its oxidation.
 * Incorrect (E): Heme oxygenase is the upstream enzyme that generates biliverdin by cleaving heme, rather than the enzyme responsible for converting the green pigment to bilirubin.
