Question: In sickle cell haemoglobin, glutamic acid in 6th position of both β chains is replaced by:
A. glycine
B. leucine
C. lysine
D. tyrosine
E. valine
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): Valine substitution creates a pathological hydrophobic patch on the exterior surface of the beta chain, driving deoxyhemoglobin polymerization and erythrocyte sickling.
 * Incorrect (A): Glycine replacement at this position does not occur in human hemoglobinopathies and would alter structural flexibility without driving the specific hydrophobic aggregation seen in sickle cell disease.
 * Incorrect (B): Leucine is a branched-chain hydrophobic amino acid that is not coded by the point mutation responsible for sickle cell hemoglobin.
 * Incorrect (C): Lysine replacement at the 6th position of the beta chain yields HbC, a distinct structural hemoglobinopathy characterized by target cells rather than sickling.
 * Incorrect (D): Tyrosine is a bulky, polar aromatic amino acid that is not involved in the genetic etiology or molecular pathogenesis of sickle cell hemoglobin.

Question: In HbS, the amino acid at position 6 in β chain is substituted by:
A. arginine
B. histidine
C. lysine
D. proline
E. valine
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): Valine replaces glutamic acid at position 6, substituting a charged, hydrophilic residue with a hydrophobic one that promotes pathological polymerization under deoxygenated conditions.
 * Incorrect (A): Arginine substitution at this position does not occur clinically and would preserve a charged, hydrophilic surface environment that prevents the polymerization characteristic of HbS.
 * Incorrect (B): Histidine is a basic amino acid involved in the Bohr effect and iron coordination but is not the substituting residue that defines the structural abnormality of HbS.
 * Incorrect (C): Lysine substitution at the 6th position of the beta chain changes the local charge profile to produce HbC, causing a milder phenotype distinct from HbS.
 * Incorrect (D): Proline is a helix-breaking amino acid whose insertion at this site would disrupt the secondary structure of the A-helix rather than inducing the specific polymerizing hydrophobic patch of HbS.

Question: In variegate porphyria the following enzyme is deficient:
A. coproporphyrinogen oxidase
B. ferrochelatase
C. porphobilinogen deaminase
D. protoporphyrinogen oxidase
E. uroporphyrinogen III synthase
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * Correct (D): Variegate porphyria is an autosomal dominant hepatic porphyria caused by a deficiency in protoporphyrinogen oxidase, leading to the accumulation of protoporphyrinogen IX.
 * Incorrect (A): A deficiency in coproporphyrinogen oxidase results in hereditary coproporphyria, not variegate porphyria.
 * Incorrect (B): A deficiency in ferrochelatase causes erythropoietic protoporphyria, not variegate porphyria.
 * Incorrect (C): A deficiency in porphobilinogen deaminase results in acute intermittent porphyria, not variegate porphyria.
 * Incorrect (E): A deficiency in uroporphyrinogen III synthase causes congenital erythropoietic porphyria, not variegate porphyria.

Question: In hereditary protoporphyria, enzyme deficient is:
A. coproporphyrinogen oxidase

 * Incorrect (C): A deficiency in porphobilinogen deaminase underlies acute intermittent porphyria, not hereditary protoporphyria.
 * Incorrect (D): A deficiency in protoporphyrinogen oxidase results in variegate porphyria, not hereditary protoporphyria.

Question: In Crigler–Najjar syndrome there is:
A. conjugated hyperbilirubinemia
B. decreased erythrocyte survival
C. increased hemolytic activity
D. increased transferase activity
E. unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): Because hepatic conjugation is non-functional or severely impaired in Crigler–Najjar syndrome, bilirubin cannot be converted, leading to profound unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia.
 * Incorrect (A): Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia requires intact intrahepatic conjugation, which is absent or severely impaired in Crigler–Najjar syndrome.
 * Incorrect (B): Erythrocyte life span is entirely unaffected in this disorder, meaning there is no decreased erythrocyte survival driving the jaundice.
 * Incorrect (C): The jaundice is purely metabolic and hepatic in origin, showing no evidence of increased hemolytic activity or premature red blood cell destruction.
 * Incorrect (D): Crigler–Najjar syndrome is caused by a structural defect or complete deletion of the enzyme gene, resulting in a loss rather than increased transferase activity.

Question: Hb S is formed by substitution of an amino acid at position 6 in:
A. alpha chain
B. beta chain
C. delta chain
D. epsilon chain
E. gamma chain
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): Hb S is caused by a point mutation in the gene encoding the beta chain, leading to the substitution of hydrophilic glutamic acid with hydrophobic valine at position 6.
 * Incorrect (A): The alpha chain sequence is entirely normal in patients with sickle cell hemoglobin (Hb S).
 * Incorrect (C): The delta chain combines with alpha chains to form adult Hemoglobin A2, and does not contain the mutation responsible for Hb S.
 * Incorrect (D): The epsilon chain is an embryonic globin chain that plays no role in the structural pathology of mutant adult Hb S.
 * Incorrect (E): The gamma chain is a structural component of fetal hemoglobin (Hb F) and is unaffected by the point mutation that forms Hb S.
