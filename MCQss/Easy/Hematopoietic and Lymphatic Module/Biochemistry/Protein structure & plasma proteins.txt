Question: Which of the following form of haemoglobin provides useful information for the management of diabetes mellitus:
A. HbA1
B. HbA1c
C. HbA2
D. HbC
E. HbS
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): HbA1c is a non-enzymatically glycosylated form of hemoglobin whose stable plasma concentration reflects the average ambient blood glucose levels over the preceding 120 days.
 * Incorrect (A): HbA1 represents the broad, total fraction of minor adult glycated hemoglobins, making it too non-specific compared to its precise subfraction for clinical diabetic monitoring.
 * Incorrect (C): HbA2 is a normal minor adult variant composed of two alpha and two delta chains that increases in beta-thalassemia trait but has no clinical utility in diabetes mellitus.
 * Incorrect (D): HbC is a structural hemoglobin variant caused by a lysine substitution that results in mild hemolytic anemia and is unrelated to glycemic status or diabetic management.
 * Incorrect (E): HbS is the pathological variant underlying sickle cell disease, caused by a valine substitution, and carries no prognostic or diagnostic value for diabetes mellitus.

 * Incorrect (E): Transferrin is the specialized plasma transport protein dedicated entirely to moving ferric iron ions between tissues, completely lacking the capacity to bind intact hemoglobin molecules.

Question: Normal haemoglobin is composed of 4 polypeptide chains:
A. 2 alpha and 2 beta
B. 2 alpha and 2 delta
C. 2 alpha and 2 epsilon
D. 2 alpha and 2 gamma
E. 2 alpha and 2 zeta
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): 2 alpha and 2 beta chains organize into a soluble \alpha_2\beta_2 tetramer that constitutes HbA, representing greater than 95% of normal adult human hemoglobin.
 * Incorrect (B): 2 alpha and 2 delta chains form HbA2, which is a minor, normal adult variant that accounts for less than 3.5% of total circulating hemoglobin.
 * Incorrect (C): 2 alpha and 2 epsilon chains do not represent a stable physiological pairing, as epsilon chains combine exclusively with zeta chains to form embryonic Gower-1 or alpha chains to form transient Gower-2.
 * Incorrect (D): 2 alpha and 2 gamma chains constitute HbF (fetal hemoglobin), which is the predominant functional tetramer during gestation but drops to trace levels under normal adult conditions.
 * Incorrect (E): 2 alpha and 2 zeta chains do not pair physiologically because zeta is an embryonic alpha-like globin gene that acts as a structural alternative to the alpha chain itself.
