Question: Which of the following organs is least likely to be affected by ischemic infarct:
 A. heart.
 B. kidney.
 C. liver.
 D. spleen.
 E. lung.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Liver has dual blood supply from the hepatic artery and portal vein, making ischemic infarction uncommon.
 •	Incorrect (A): Heart depends on coronary arteries and is highly susceptible to ischemic infarction.
 •	Incorrect (B): Kidney has end-arterial circulation with limited collateral supply.
 •	Incorrect (D): Spleen has end arteries and commonly develops pale infarcts.
 •	Incorrect (E): Lung can infarct despite dual supply, particularly with compromised bronchial circulation.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Hemodynamic Disorders, Thrombosis & Shock

Question: Which of the following is associated with fat embolism:
 A. fracture of long bones.
 B. hypercholesterolemia.
 C. hypertriglyceridemia.
 D. megaloblastic anemia.
 E. sickle cell anemia.
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (A): Fat embolism most commonly follows fractures of long bones that release marrow fat into circulation.
 •	Incorrect (B): Hypercholesterolemia promotes atherosclerosis, not fat embolism.
 •	Incorrect (C): Hypertriglyceridemia does not produce embolic fat globules.
 •	Incorrect (D): Megaloblastic anemia has no association with fat embolism.
 •	Incorrect (E): Sickle cell anemia causes vaso-occlusion by sickled erythrocytes, not fat emboli.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Hemodynamic Disorders, Thrombosis & Shock

Question: What is the usual site of lodging of venous emboli:
 A. bones.
 B. brain.
 C. liver.
 D. lungs.
 E. spleen.
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (D): Venous emboli typically travel through the right heart and lodge in pulmonary arteries.
 •	Incorrect (A): Bones are not the first capillary bed encountered by venous emboli.
 •	Incorrect (B): Brain embolization usually arises from arterial or paradoxical emboli.
 •	Incorrect (C): Liver is not a common destination for systemic venous emboli.
 •	Incorrect (E): Spleen receives arterial rather than venous emboli.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Hemodynamic Disorders, Thrombosis & Shock

Question: Which one among the following is a component of Virchow’s triad:
 A. endothelial hypertrophy.
 B. endothelial injury.
 C. increased factor II levels.
 D. thrombocytopenia.
 E. vascular calcification.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Endothelial injury is one of the three major factors predisposing to thrombosis.
 •	Incorrect (A): Endothelial hypertrophy is not a recognized component of Virchow’s triad.
 •	Incorrect (C): Increased factor II levels may promote thrombosis but are not a triad component.
 •	Incorrect (D): Thrombocytopenia generally predisposes to bleeding rather than thrombosis.
 •	Incorrect (E): Vascular calcification is not included in Virchow’s triad.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Hemodynamic Disorders, Thrombosis & Shock

Question: Which of the following does not predispose to thrombosis:
 A. endothelial damage
 B. hypercoagulability
 C. platelet aggregates
 D. thrombocytopenia
 E. vascular stasis
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (D): Thrombocytopenia reduces platelet availability, decreasing thrombosis risk.
 •	Incorrect (A): Endothelial damage promotes clot formation.
 •	Incorrect (B): Hypercoagulability increases thrombotic tendency.
 •	Incorrect (C): Platelet aggregation is central to thrombus formation.
 •	Incorrect (E): Vascular stasis promotes coagulation activation.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Hemodynamic Disorders, Thrombosis & Shock

Question: Paradoxical emboli are:
 A. caused by atmospheric pressure changes
 B. caused by myocardial infarction
 C. due to interventricular or interatrial septal defect
 D. fat embolism phenomenon
 E. pulmonary emboli in lungs
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Paradoxical emboli pass through septal defects from right to left circulation.
 •	Incorrect (A): Atmospheric pressure changes relate to decompression sickness.
 •	Incorrect (B): Myocardial infarction does not cause paradoxical embolism.
 •	Incorrect (D): Fat embolism arises from long bone fractures.
 •	Incorrect (E): Pulmonary emboli remain within pulmonary circulation.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Hemodynamic Disorders, Thrombosis & Shock

Question: The following predispose to deep vein thrombosis except:
 A. abdominal surgery
 B. factor VIII deficiency
 C. immobilization
 D. malnutrition
 E. pregnancy
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Factor VIII deficiency causes bleeding tendency, not thrombosis.
 •	Incorrect (A): Surgery induces stasis and endothelial injury.
 •	Incorrect (C): Immobilization leads to venous stasis.
 •	Incorrect (D): Malnutrition may contribute to hypercoagulability states indirectly.
 •	Incorrect (E): Pregnancy increases hypercoagulability.

Question: At autopsy, the pathologist identifies “heart failure cells.” This finding results from:
  A. activation of coagulation cascade
  B. chronic passive congestion of the lungs
  C. hypoxic myocardial injury
  D. myocardial hyperemia
  E. pulmonary edema
  Correct Option: B
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Heart failure cells are hemosiderin-laden macrophages from chronic passive pulmonary congestion.
 •	Incorrect (A): Coagulation cascade activation is unrelated to these cells.
 •	Incorrect (C): Myocardial hypoxia affects heart muscle, not alveolar macrophages.
 •	Incorrect (D): Hyperemia is acute increased blood flow, not chronic congestion.
 •	Incorrect (E): Pulmonary edema is a consequence, not the cellular cause.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Hemodynamic Disorders, Thrombosis & Shock

Question: Most frequent site of venous thrombosis:
  A. cerebral venous sinuses
  B. hepatic vein
  C. lower extremity veins
  D. portal vein
  E. pulmonary vein
  Correct Option: C
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Lower extremity deep veins are the most common site of venous thrombosis.
 •	Incorrect (A): Cerebral venous thrombosis is rare.
 •	Incorrect (B): Hepatic vein thrombosis is uncommon (Budd-Chiari syndrome).
 •	Incorrect (D): Portal vein thrombosis is less common.
 •	Incorrect (E): Pulmonary veins are not typical sites of thrombosis.
