 A. advancing age.
 B. family history.
 C. male sex.
 D. race.
 E. genetic predisposition.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Family history reflects inherited susceptibility and is a classic non-modifiable risk factor.
 •	Incorrect (A): Advancing age is non-modifiable but is not the designated answer here.
 •	Incorrect (C): Male sex is a constitutional factor but not the keyed option.
 •	Incorrect (D): Race may influence risk but is not the selected answer.
 •	Incorrect (E): Genetic predisposition overlaps with family history but is not the keyed choice.

Question: Which of the following is not a feature of right heart failure:
 A. dyspnea.
 B. heart failure cells.
 C. nutmeg liver.
 D. peripheral edema.
 E. splenomegaly.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Heart failure cells result from pulmonary congestion and are characteristic of left heart failure.
 •	Incorrect (A): Dyspnea may occur secondary to associated pulmonary congestion.
 •	Incorrect (C): Nutmeg liver is a classic manifestation of chronic right-sided failure.
 •	Incorrect (D): Peripheral edema reflects systemic venous congestion.
 •	Incorrect (E): Splenomegaly can develop due to chronic venous congestion.

Question: Most sensitive and specific marker of myocardial infarction:
 A. creatine kinase
 B. CK-MB
 C. lactate dehydrogenase
 D. myoglobin
 E. troponin T & I
 Correct Option: E
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (E): Troponin T & I are highly cardiac-specific proteins with greatest sensitivity for myocardial necrosis.
 •	Incorrect (A): Creatine kinase is non-specific and elevated in skeletal muscle injury as well.
 •	Incorrect (B): CK-MB is more specific than CK but less sensitive than troponins.
 •	Incorrect (C): Lactate dehydrogenase rises late and lacks cardiac specificity.
 •	Incorrect (D): Myoglobin rises early but is not cardiac-specific.

Question: MI is an example of:
 A. caseous necrosis
 B. coagulative necrosis
 C. fat necrosis
 D. fibrinoid necrosis
 E. liquefactive necrosis
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Myocardial infarction produces ischemic coagulative necrosis due to preserved tissue architecture initially.
 •	Incorrect (A): Caseous necrosis is typical of tuberculosis granulomas.
 •	Incorrect (C): Fat necrosis occurs in pancreatitis or trauma to adipose tissue.
 •	Incorrect (D): Fibrinoid necrosis occurs in immune-mediated vascular injury.
 •	Incorrect (E): Liquefactive necrosis is typical of brain infarcts or abscesses.

Question: A 55-year-old woman presents with chest pain that predictably occurs during exertion and is relieved by rest. Which condition best describes her state:
  A. arrhythmia
  B. myocardial infarction
  C. stable angina
  D. unstable angina
  E. variant angina
  Correct Option: C
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Stable angina is exertional ischemia relieved by rest due to fixed atherosclerotic narrowing.
 •	Incorrect (A): Arrhythmia is electrical rhythm disturbance, not ischemic pain pattern.
 •	Incorrect (B): MI causes persistent pain with necrosis.
 •	Incorrect (D): Unstable angina occurs at rest or increasing frequency.
 •	Incorrect (E): Variant angina is due to coronary vasospasm at rest.

  Explanation:

Question: Onion-skin appearance of blood vessels is seen in:
  A. atherosclerosis.
  B. hyperplastic arteriosclerosis.
  C. hyaline arteriosclerosis.
  D. medial calcific arteriosclerosis.
  E. Monckeberg arteriosclerosis.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Hyperplastic arteriosclerosis shows concentric laminated smooth muscle thickening producing the classic onion-skin appearance.
 •	Incorrect (A): Atherosclerosis produces intimal plaques rather than concentric arteriolar wall thickening.
 •	Incorrect (C): Hyaline arteriosclerosis causes homogeneous hyaline thickening without onion-skin layering.
 •	Incorrect (D): Medial calcific arteriosclerosis is characterized by calcification of the tunica media.
 •	Incorrect (E): Monckeberg arteriosclerosis consists of medial calcification and is not associated with onion-skin lesions.
 Tags: Easy, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Systemic Pathology: Cardiovascular

Question: First visible gross evidence for formation of atheroma:
  A. calcification.
  B. fatty streak.
  C. fibrous plaque.
  D. thrombus.
  E. ulceration.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Fatty streaks are the earliest grossly visible lesions of atherosclerosis and consist of lipid-laden foam cells in the intima.
 •	Incorrect (A): Calcification occurs in advanced atherosclerotic lesions.
 •	Incorrect (C): Fibrous plaques develop after progression of fatty streaks.
 •	Incorrect (D): Thrombus formation is a complication of advanced plaque disruption.
 •	Incorrect (E): Ulceration occurs late due to plaque instability and rupture.

Question: Hyaline arteriosclerosis is characterized by:
  A. concentric smooth muscle hyperplasia with onion-skin appearance.
  B. diffuse homogeneous pink hyaline thickening.
  C. focal calcification of the tunica media.
  D. lipid-rich intimal plaque formation.
  E. no luminal narrowing.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Hyaline arteriosclerosis shows diffuse homogeneous pink hyaline thickening of arteriolar walls with luminal narrowing.
 •	Incorrect (A): Concentric smooth muscle hyperplasia with onion-skin appearance describes hyperplastic arteriosclerosis.
 •	Incorrect (C): Focal calcification of the tunica media is characteristic of Monckeberg medial calcific arteriosclerosis.
 •	Incorrect (D): Lipid-rich intimal plaque formation is the hallmark of atherosclerosis.
 •	Incorrect (E): Hyaline arteriosclerosis typically causes narrowing of the vessel lumen.

Question: Most common symptom of myocardial infarction:
  A. chest pain.
  B. dyspnea.
  C. edema.
  D. palpitations.
  E. syncope.
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (A): Severe, prolonged chest pain due to myocardial ischemia is the most common presenting symptom of myocardial infarction.
 •	Incorrect (B): Dyspnea may occur but is less common than chest pain as the presenting symptom.
 •	Incorrect (C): Edema is usually a manifestation of heart failure rather than acute myocardial infarction.
 •	Incorrect (D): Palpitations may accompany arrhythmias but are not the most common symptom.
 •	Incorrect (E): Syncope can occur in severe cases but is not the typical presentation.
