Question: The proximal part of the right subclavian artery is derived from the:
A. Aortic sac
B. First aortic arch
C. Fourth aortic arch
D. Second aortic arch
E. Third aortic arch
Correct Option: C
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (C): The fourth aortic arch develops asymmetrical derivatives, specifically forming the proximal part of the right subclavian artery on the right side and a segment of the arch of aorta on the left side.
Incorrect (A): The aortic sac contributes to the formation of the brachiocephalic artery and the proximal portion of the arch of aorta, not the right subclavian artery.
Incorrect (B): The first aortic arch largely regresses, with its remnants contributing minorly to the formation of the maxillary artery.
Incorrect (D): The second aortic arch mostly obliterates, leaving behind small portions that form the stapedial and hyoid arteries.
Incorrect (E): The third aortic arch gives rise to the common carotid artery and the proximal segment of the internal carotid artery.

Question: The left horn of the sinus venosus forms the:
A. Bulbus cordis
B. Coronary sinus
C. Crista terminalis
D. Left atrial appendage
E. Sinus venarum
Correct Option: B
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (B): Due to extensive left-to-right shunting of embryonic blood, the left horn of the sinus venosus loses its prominent venous connections and regresses to form the coronary sinus and the small oblique vein of the left atrium.
Incorrect (A): The bulbus cordis is a primary segment of the main heart tube that gives rise to ventricular components, entirely separate from the inflow sinus venosus.
Incorrect (C): The crista terminalis is a muscular ridge in the right atrium marking the boundary where the right horn of the sinus venosus incorporated into the primitive atrium.
Incorrect (D): The left atrial appendage develops from the muscular wall of the primitive atrium itself, characterized by internal pectinate bands.
Incorrect (E): The sinus venarum is the smooth-walled part of the adult right atrium that is formed by the incorporation of the right horn of the sinus venosus.

Question: Ebstein anomaly is strongly associated with maternal ingestion of:
A. Alcohol
B. Lithium
C. Phenytoin
D. Retinoic acid
E. Warfarin
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 
Correct (B): Maternal exposure to Lithium during the first trimester is a classically established teratogenic risk factor strongly associated with Ebstein anomaly, characterized by apical displacement of the tricuspid valve leaflets.
Incorrect (A): Alcohol ingestion during pregnancy is tied to fetal alcohol syndrome, which typically presents with ASDs or VSDs rather than Ebstein anomaly.
Incorrect (C): Phenytoin usage during pregnancy causes fetal hydantoin syndrome, frequently manifesting with cleft lip/palate and generic congenital heart defects.
Incorrect (D): Retinoic acid embryopathy produces severe craniofacial anomalies and outflow tract defects like transposition or truncus arteriosus.
Incorrect (E): Warfarin administration causes fetal warfarin syndrome, resulting in nasal hypoplasia and skeletal abnormalities rather than specific tricuspid anomalies.

Question: The spiral orientation of the aorticopulmonary septum is functionally important because it ensures that the:
A. aorta communicates with the left ventricle only
B. aorta communicates with the left ventricle and pulmonary trunk communicates with the right ventricle
C. coronary arteries arise correctly from the aortic sinuses
D. outflow tracts cross each other without dividing the lumen
E. pulmonary trunk communicates with the right ventricle only
Correct Option: B
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (B): The 180-degree spiral orientation of the aorticopulmonary septum is functionally important because it ensures that the aorta communicates with the left ventricle and pulmonary trunk communicates with the right ventricle, preventing transposition.
Incorrect (A): While true, this statement is incomplete; the spiral division must simultaneously align both reciprocal connections, ensuring proper ventriculoarterial concordance for both the systemic and pulmonary circuits.
Incorrect (C): The origin of the coronary arteries from the aortic sinuses depends on localized endothelial sprouting and signaling at the root of the aorta, not on the macroscopic spiral rotation of the septum.
Incorrect (D): The aorticopulmonary septum completely divides the single truncus lumen into two distinct, anatomically separated vessel channels, rather than crossing without luminal division.
Incorrect (E): This option is incomplete as it focuses entirely on the right side of the heart, omitting the essential reciprocal alignment of the aorta with the left ventricle achieved by the spiral.
