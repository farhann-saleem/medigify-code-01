Question: The fibrous pericardium is derived from the:
A. Pericardioperitoneal canal
B. Pleuroperitoneal folds
C. Pleuroperitoneal membrane
D. Septum transversum
E. Somatic mesoderm
Correct Option: D
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (D): The septum transversum is a mass of splanchnic mesoderm that gives rise to the central tendon of the diaphragm and the fibrous pericardium enclosing the heart.
Incorrect (A): The pericardioperitoneal canal is the primitive body cavity path that temporarily connects the pericardial and peritoneal cavities before closure.
Incorrect (B): The pleuroperitoneal folds are structural ridges that grow into the pericardioperitoneal canals to help partition the thoracic and abdominal cavities.
Incorrect (C): The pleuroperitoneal membrane is the fused connective tissue structure that contributes to the posterolateral portions of the definitive diaphragm.
Incorrect (E): The somatic mesoderm lines the body wall and gives rise to the parietal layers of the serous membranes, but not the heavy fibrous pericardium.
