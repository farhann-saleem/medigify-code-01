Question 1: A 32-year-old pilot complains of seasonal allergies. Which medication is most appropriate for management of his allergy symptoms:
  A. cetirizine
  B. cyclizine
  C. diphenhydramine
  D. fexofenadine
  E. promethazine
  Correct Option: D
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (D): fexofenadine is a second-generation H1 antihistamine with minimal sedation, making it ideal for pilots requiring alertness.
 •	Incorrect (A): cetirizine may still cause mild sedation compared to fexofenadine.
 •	Incorrect (B): cyclizine is primarily an antiemetic for motion sickness, not allergic rhinitis.
 •	Incorrect (C): diphenhydramine is a first-generation antihistamine causing significant sedation.
 •	Incorrect (E): promethazine is highly sedating and unsuitable for tasks requiring wakefulness.
 Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Respiratory Pathology, Anti-histamines

Question 3: Which of the following drugs is the most effective physiologic antagonist of histamine in smooth muscle:
  A. albuterol
  B. cetirizine
  C. epinephrine
  D. ranitidine
  E. sumatriptan
  Correct Option: C
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): epinephrine physiologically reverses histamine effects via α- and β-adrenergic stimulation in smooth muscle.
 •	Incorrect (A): albuterol is a β2 agonist but not a full physiologic antagonist of histamine systemically.
 •	Incorrect (B): cetirizine blocks H1 receptors but does not physiologically oppose histamine effects broadly.
 •	Incorrect (D): ranitidine blocks H2 receptors, affecting gastric acid, not smooth muscle histamine effects.
 •	Incorrect (E): sumatriptan is a 5-HT1 agonist used in migraine, unrelated to histamine antagonism.
 Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Respiratory Pathology, Anti-histamines

Question 5: A patient suffers from troublesome allergic rhinitis due to pollen, and you want to prescribe a drug that is least likely to cause sedation. What is your best choice:
  A. betamethasone
  B. cimetidine
  C. fexofenadine
  D. hydroxyzine
  E. loratadine
  Correct Option: E
  Explanation:

 •	Incorrect (A): betamethasone is a corticosteroid, not a primary antihistamine for acute rhinitis control.
 •	Incorrect (B): cimetidine is an H2 blocker and unrelated to allergic rhinitis treatment.
 •	Incorrect (C): fexofenadine is non-sedating but not the keyed answer in this item.
 •	Incorrect (D): hydroxyzine is a sedating first-generation antihistamine.

Question 12: Which of the following will result from blockade of H2 receptors:
  A. decreased cAMP in gastric mucosa
  B. decreased channel opening in enteric nerves
  C. increased cAMP in cardiac muscle
  D. increased gastric acid secretion
  E. increased IP3 in platelets
  Correct Option: A
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (A): H2 receptor blockade reduces adenylate cyclase activity, lowering cAMP and gastric acid secretion.
 •	Incorrect (B): enteric nerve channel effects are not primary H2-mediated actions.
 •	Incorrect (C): cardiac cAMP is not directly decreased by H2 blockade in this context.
 •	Incorrect (D): H2 blockade reduces, not increases, gastric acid secretion.
 •	Incorrect (E): IP3 pathways are not central to H2 receptor signaling.

Question 16: A 30-year-old woman who is a beekeeper always gets sting bites by bees and develops rash. Which drug should she always keep with her:
  A. antihistamine
  B. beta blockers
  C. epinephrine
  D. montelukast
  E. steroid
  Correct Option: C
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): epinephrine is the first-line emergency treatment for anaphylaxis from bee stings.
 •	Incorrect (A): antihistamines are adjuncts, not life-saving in anaphylaxis.
 •	Incorrect (B): beta blockers can worsen anaphylaxis and reduce epinephrine efficacy.
 •	Incorrect (D): montelukast is not useful in acute allergic emergencies.
 •	Incorrect (E): steroids have delayed onset and are not immediate rescue therapy.
