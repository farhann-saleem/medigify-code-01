A. diaphragm
B. fibrous pericardium
C. parietal pleura
D. parietal peritoneum
E. visceral pleura
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
* Correct (B): The pleuropericardial membranes contain the phrenic nerves and expand medially to fuse and form the adult fibrous pericardium.
* Incorrect (A): The diaphragm develops from the septum transversum, pleuroperitoneal membranes, dorsal mesentery of esophagus, and body wall musculature, not the pleuropericardial membranes.
* Incorrect (C): The parietal pleura is derived from the somatopleuric lateral plate mesoderm lining the thoracic body wall.
* Incorrect (D): The parietal peritoneum develops from the somatopleuric lateral plate mesoderm lining the abdominal cavity.
* Incorrect (E): The visceral pleura develops from the splanchnopleuric lateral plate mesoderm intimately investing the lung buds.

Question: A Congenital diaphragmatic hernia most commonly occurs:
A. on the left anteromedial side
B. on the left posterolateral side
C. on the right anteromedial side
D. on the right posterolateral side
E. within the central tendon midline
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
* Correct (B): A posterolateral defect (Bochdalek hernia) occurs predominantly on the left posterolateral side because the left pericardioperitoneal canal closes later than the right.
* Incorrect (A): Anteromedial defects (Morgagni hernias) occur through the sternocostalis hiatus and are much less common than posterolateral hernias.
* Incorrect (C): Right anteromedial hernias are rare parasternal defects protected by the adjacent mediastinal structures.
* Incorrect (D): Right posterolateral side hernias are less common because the large developing mass of the embryonic liver physically blocks abdominal herniation on that side.
* Incorrect (E): Central tendon midline defects involve failure of septum transversum central development and are structurally distinct from the classic common posterolateral herniation.
