
4. Question: When does surfactant production start?
 A. 21st week
 B. 24th week
 C. 28th week
 D. 32nd week
 E. 36th week
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 • Correct (B): Surfactant production begins around the 24th week as type II pneumocytes mature.
 • Incorrect (A): 21st week is too early for functional surfactant synthesis.
 • Incorrect (C): 28th week marks increased production, not initiation.
 • Incorrect (D): 32nd week shows near adequate surfactant levels but not onset.
 • Incorrect (E): 36th week is when surfactant reaches maturity, not start.

Question: Pulmonary hypoplasia is commonly associated with which condition:
A. congenital bronchial cysts
B. diaphragmatic hernia
C. hyaline membrane disease
D. laryngeal atresia
E. tracheoesophageal fistula
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
* Correct (B): A diaphragmatic hernia allows abdominal viscera to compress the developing lungs, mechanically restricting thoracic space and triggering severe pulmonary hypoplasia.
* Incorrect (A): Congenital bronchial cysts are usually localized fluid-filled anomalies that cause focal atelectasis or air trapping rather than global pulmonary hypoplasia.
* Incorrect (C): Hyaline membrane disease is caused by surfactant deficiency in premature infants, affecting lung compliance rather than primary lung tissue mass development.
* Incorrect (D): Laryngeal atresia causes distal fluid retention and lung overgrowth (hyperplasia) rather than hypoplasia.
* Incorrect (E): Tracheoesophageal fistula typically presents with feeding and respiratory difficulties due to aspiration or air entry into the stomach, not primary pulmonary hypoplasia.

Question: Which of the following correctly describes the development of the lungs, in order:
A. alveolar period, pseudoglandular period, canalicular period, terminal sac period
B. canalicular period, pseudoglandular period, terminal sac period, alveolar period
C. pseudoglandular period, canalicular period, embryonic period, alveolar period
D. pseudoglandular period, canalicular period, terminal sac period, alveolar period
E. pseudoglandular period, terminal sac period, alveolar period, canalicular period Correct Option: D
Explanation:
* Correct (D): Histological lung maturation strictly progresses through the pseudoglandular period (weeks 5–16), canalicular period (weeks 16–26), terminal sac period (weeks 26–birth), and finally the alveolar period (eight months to childhood).
* Incorrect (A): This sequence completely reverses the early developmental stages with late stages.
* Incorrect (B): This order incorrectly places the canalicular period before the pseudoglandular period.
* Incorrect (C): This list incorrectly incorporates the broad embryonic period as a late intra-uterine histological stage between the canalicular and alveolar periods.
* Incorrect (E): This sequence incorrectly places the canalicular period at the very end of lung maturation.

Question: During early development, which structure partitions the laryngotracheal tube from the foregut:
A. laryngotracheal groove
B. laryngotracheal septum
C. tracheoesophageal folds
D. tracheoesophageal fistula
E. tracheoesophageal septum
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
* Correct (E): Fusion of the longitudinal tracheoesophageal folds forms the tracheoesophageal septum, which partitions the laryngotracheal tube from the dorsal foregut.
* Incorrect (A): The laryngotracheal groove is the initial invagination on the ventral foregut wall rather than the partitioning structure.
* Incorrect (B): The term laryngotracheal septum is an inaccurate anatomical designation for the partition between these two tubes.
* Incorrect (C): The tracheoesophageal folds are the precursor structures that must fuse to create the definitive partitioning septum.
* Incorrect (D): A tracheoesophageal fistula is a pathological congenital anomaly resulting from incomplete separation, not the normal partitioning structure itself.

Question: In which stage of lung maturation is the blood-air barrier beginning to be established:
A. alveolar period
B. canalicular period
C. embryonic period
D. pseudoglandular period
E. terminal sac period
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
* Correct (E): During the terminal sac period, primordial alveoli thin out and capillaries establish intimate contact with the epithelium to form the functional blood-air barrier.
* Incorrect (A): The alveolar period matures and expands an already established blood-air barrier rather than initiating its structure.
* Incorrect (B): The canalicular period marks the formation of respiratory bronchioles and vascular proliferation, but terminal thin-walled sacs are not yet established for the barrier.
* Incorrect (C): The embryonic period covers early organogenesis and initial budding before any respiratory spaces or barriers develop.
* Incorrect (D): The pseudoglandular period resembles an exocrine gland with no thin respiratory surfaces or blood-air barrier components.

Question: Which component would be directly affected by failure of foregut endoderm differentiation:
A. alveoli
B. bronchial cartilages
C. intrinsic laryngeal muscles
D. laryngeal cartilages
E. nasal epithelium
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
* Correct (A): The epithelial lining of the entire lower respiratory tract, including the specialized cells of the alveoli, is derived directly from the foregut endoderm.
* Incorrect (B): The bronchial cartilages are derived from splanchnic mesoderm, rendering them safe from primary endodermal differentiation failure.
* Incorrect (C): The intrinsic laryngeal muscles develop from the mesoderm of the fourth and sixth pharyngeal arches.
* Incorrect (D): The laryngeal cartilages arise from neural crest tissue within the pharyngeal arches rather than foregut endoderm.
* Incorrect (E): The nasal epithelium is derived from ectodermal nasal placodes, not from the endoderm of the foregut.

Question: Components of the blood-air barrier are derived from:
A. ectoderm only
B. endoderm and ectoderm
C. endoderm only
D. visceral mesoderm and endoderm
E. visceral mesoderm only
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
* Correct (D): The functional blood-air barrier is composed of Type I pneumonocytes derived from endoderm and capillary endothelial cells derived from visceral mesoderm.
* Incorrect (A): Ectoderm plays zero part in the development of the vascular or alveolar structures that comprise the barrier.
* Incorrect (B): This option incorrectly includes ectoderm and completely omits the critical role played by the visceral mesoderm.
* Incorrect (C): Endoderm only supplies the alveolar epithelial side, ignoring the endothelial and connective tissues needed for the barrier.
* Incorrect (E): Visceral mesoderm only forms the vascular endothelium, omitting the endoderm-derived epithelial lining of the barrier.

Question: Surfactant production starts in:
A. 16th week
B. 20th week
C. 24th week
D. 28th week
E. 32nd week
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
* Correct (C): Type II pneumonocytes differentiate and begin the initial production of pulmonary surfactant around the 24th week of gestation.
* Incorrect (A): The 16th week marks the end of the pseudoglandular period, where no terminal air spaces or Type II pneumonocytes have developed.
* Incorrect (B): The 20th week lies within the canalicular period before Type II pneumonocytes initiate functional surfactant production.
* Incorrect (D): By the 28th week, surfactant production has increased sufficiently to allow premature infant survival, but this is well after its initial onset.
* Incorrect (E): The 32nd week represents an advanced period of surfactant accumulation in the amniotic fluid, not its initial startup period.
Tags: Moderate, Embryology, Respiratory Module, Systemic: Trachea & Lungs
