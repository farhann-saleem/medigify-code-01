Question 2: Regulation of the size of the lumen of bronchioles is controlled by:
A. elastic fibers and cartilage.
B. hyaline cartilage plates.
C. smooth muscle and collagen.
D. smooth muscle and elastic fibers.
E. smooth muscle and hyaline cartilage.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Bronchioles lack structural cartilage, meaning dynamic luminal diameter regulation relies entirely on the constriction/relaxation of smooth muscle countered by the radial traction of elastic fibers.
Incorrect (A): Cartilage is structurally absent in bronchioles, meaning elastic fibers alone cannot actively regulate or contract the lumen.
Incorrect (B): Hyaline cartilage plates are structural features characteristic of bronchi and are entirely lost by the time the airway transitions into a bronchiole.
Incorrect (C): Collagen provides passive structural tensile strength to the airway wall but lacks the high elasticity required to structurally balance active smooth muscle luminal regulation.
Incorrect (E): Hyaline cartilage is not found within bronchiolar walls, rendering options reliant on cartilage mechanics invalid for this airway segment.

Question 3: A medical student while examining the slide of a terminal bronchiole found a dome-shaped cell with extensive RER, well developed Golgi complex, abundant mitochondria and some profiles of SER. The cell is?
A. Basal cell
B. Brush cell
C. Clara cell
D. Goblet cell
E. Kulchitsky cell
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Clara cell (exocrine bronchiolar cell) is a non-ciliated, dome-shaped cell residing in bronchioles, ultrastructurally optimized with extensive RER, well developed Golgi complex, and SER profiles to secrete surfactant-like proteins and detoxify xenobiotics.
Incorrect (A): Basal cell is a short, triangular stem cell resting on the basement membrane of larger conducting airways, lacking a dome-shaped apical profile.
Incorrect (B): Brush cell is characterized by specialized apical microvilli and a chemosensory role rather than a dense secretory ultrastructure.
Incorrect (D): Goblet cell is a mucus-secreting cell containing large, pale apical mucin granules instead of a dome-shaped profile packed with diverse metabolic organelles.
Incorrect (E): Kulchitsky cell is an enteroendocrine cell filled with small dense-core granules near its basal aspect to secrete amine hormones into the lamina propria.

Question 9: Multi-lamellar bodies are present in?
A. Alveolar macrophages.
B. Bronchiolar brush cells.
C. Exocrine bronchiolar cells.
D. Type I pneumocytes.
E. Type II pneumocytes.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Type II pneumocytes contain multi-lamellar bodies, which are specialized, membrane-bound intracellular storage granules packed with concentric whorls of newly synthesized surfactant phospholipids.
Incorrect (A): Alveolar macrophages contain heterophagic lysosomes and phagosomes containing engulfed debris, not secretory multi-lamellar bodies.
Incorrect (B): Bronchiolar brush cells contain chemosensory elements and glycogen stores, but lack surfactant-storing multi-lamellar bodies.
Incorrect (C): Exocrine bronchiolar cells (Clara cells) contain dense, electron-opaque apical secretory granules, not multi-lamellar structures.
Incorrect (D): Type I pneumocytes possess minimal organelles within their thin cytoplasm and do not synthesize or store material in multi-lamellar bodies.

Question 14: Which type of junctions present between type I pneumocytes and type II pneumocytes?
A. Anchoring junctions
B. Communicating junctions
C. Desmosomal junctions
D. Gap junctions
E. Occluding junctions
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Occluding junctions (tight junctions) seal the intercellular spaces between adjacent Type I and Type II pneumocytes, preventing the unregulated paracellular leakage of interstitial fluid into the alveolar air spaces.
Incorrect (A): Anchoring junctions mechanically attach cells to the matrix or cytoskeleton but do not form a tight physical seal against fluid transudation.
Incorrect (B): Communicating junctions allow direct cytoplasmic transfer of small signaling ions between adjacent cells but do not seal extracellular spaces.
Incorrect (C): Desmosomal junctions provide strong localized spot adhesion to resist mechanical shearing forces but do not form a continuous occluding barrier.
Incorrect (D): Gap junctions allow metabolic and electrical coupling via connexons but are structural variants of communicating junctions, not sealing junctions.

Question 15: Which of the following components increase(s) as a proportion of the respiratory tract wall from trachea to alveoli?
A. Cilia
B. Elastic fibers
C. Hyaline cartilage
D. Seromucous glands
E. Smooth muscle
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Elastic fibers increase in relative proportion as the airway descends, forming a vital continuous structural network that drives passive elastic recoil during expiration.
Incorrect (A): Cilia decrease in density and height from the trachea downward, disappearing completely before the air reaches the alveoli.
Incorrect (D): Seromucous glands are confined to the submucosa of the trachea and bronchi, disappearing completely at the bronchiolar level.
Incorrect (C): Hyaline cartilage decreases progressively down the bronchial tree and is entirely absent past the small bronchi, failing to reach the alveoli.
Incorrect (E): Smooth muscle increases proportionally through the bronchioles but drops off rapidly, becoming highly sparse and discontinuous in alveolar walls.

Question 17: Which of the following features distinguishes a bronchus within a lung from the primary bronchus?
A. Glands in the submucosa
B. Irregular plates of cartilage
C. Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium
D. Smooth muscle in the wall
E. Thick connective tissue adventitia
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Intrapulmonary bronchi contain irregular plates of hyaline cartilage, whereas the extrapulmonary primary bronchus retains the continuous C-shaped rings characteristic of the trachea.
Incorrect (A): Glands in the submucosa are found in both primary and intrapulmonary bronchi, so they cannot serve as a distinguishing feature between them.
Incorrect (C): Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium lines the lumina of both primary and larger intrapulmonary bronchi, making it non-diagnostic.
Incorrect (D): Smooth muscle in the wall is organized into a prominent interlacing spiral layer in both structures to regulate airway diameter.
Incorrect (E): Thick connective tissue adventitia blends into surrounding structures in both bronchial regions, failing to serve as a reliable microscopic differentiator.
