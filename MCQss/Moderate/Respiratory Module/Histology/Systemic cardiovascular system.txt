Question: Fenestrated capillaries have:
A. a discontinuous basal lamina.
B. a thickened endothelium containing large gaps.
C. an altered basement membrane with broad clefts.
D. extremely attenuated areas of endothelium with pores.
E. pores that are evenly spaced over the entire surface.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Fenestrated capillaries are characterized by extremely attenuated areas of endothelium with pores that span the thin cytoplasm of the endothelial cells.
Incorrect (A): A discontinuous basal lamina is a defining structural feature of sinusoidal capillaries, not fenestrated capillaries.
Incorrect (B): The endothelium of fenestrated capillaries is highly thinned and attenuated, not a thickened endothelium containing large gaps.
Incorrect (C): They possess a continuous and structurally intact basement membrane rather than an altered basement membrane with broad clefts.
Incorrect (E): The pores are organized in clustered patches or fenestrated fields rather than being pores that are evenly spaced over the entire surface.

Question: When compared to arteries, veins have each of the following characteristics except:
A. high amount of elastic fibers.
B. larger caliber and thinner walls.
C. less developed muscle in tunica media.
D. less developed elastic tissue in tunica media.
E. the adventitia is often the thickest layer.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): This statement is false because veins operate under low-pressure conditions and contain a noticeably low amount of elastic fibers compared to high-pressure arteries.
Incorrect (B): It is true that veins display a larger caliber and thinner walls than their companion arteries due to lower structural resistance demands.
Incorrect (C): It is true that veins have a less developed muscle in tunica media, exhibiting fewer and more loosely organized smooth muscle strands.
Incorrect (D): It is true that veins feature less developed elastic tissue in tunica media, lacking distinct, highly consolidated elastic lamellae.
Incorrect (E): It is true that the adventitia is often the thickest layer in venous walls, composed primarily of collagenous connective tissue.

Question: The vessels whose adventitia is described as thick longitudinal bundles of collagen and elastic fibers and isolated longitudinal bundles of smooth muscle are:
A. arterioles
B. capillaries
C. elastic arteries
D. muscular arteries
E. venules
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Large venules and medium-to-large veins characteristically display a thick tunica adventitia containing longitudinal bundles of collagen, elastic fibers, and isolated longitudinal bundles of smooth muscle.
Incorrect (A): Arterioles are small, regulatory resistance vessels that completely lack longitudinal bundles of smooth muscle within their thin outer adventitial layer.
Incorrect (B): Capillaries are minimal microscopic exchange channels composed solely of endothelium and a basal lamina, entirely lacking an adventitia.
Incorrect (C): Elastic arteries possess a thin tunica adventitia composed of loose connective tissue, with a tunica media dominated by concentric, circular elastic lamellae.
Incorrect (D): Muscular arteries feature an adventitia composed of dense collagen and elastic fibers, completely lacking isolated longitudinal bundles of smooth muscle cells.

Question: Which of the following statements about healthy, intact capillaries is true:
A. They control systemic blood pressure.
B. They inhibit intravascular clot formation.
C. They are lined by a simple columnar epithelium.
D. They have a continuous smooth muscle coat.
E. They possess a thick tunica adventitia.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Healthy, intact capillaries secrete antithrombotic factors like prostacyclin and nitric oxide to inhibit intravascular clot formation along their luminal surface.
Incorrect (A): Systemic blood pressure is controlled and adjusted by the tone of muscular arterioles rather than by individual capillaries.
Incorrect (C): Capillaries are lined by a simple squamous epithelium (endothelium), not by a simple columnar epithelium.
Incorrect (D): Capillaries are completely devoid of smooth muscle, lacking a continuous smooth muscle coat entirely within their thin exchange walls.
Incorrect (E): Capillaries consist only of an endothelial tube and a basal lamina, meaning they completely lack a thick tunica adventitia.

Question: Diameter of Sinusoidal Capillaries:
A. 10–20 microns
B. 30–40 microns
C. 50–60 microns
D. 60–80 microns
E. 90–100 microns
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The diameter of Sinusoidal Capillaries characteristically ranges between 30–40 microns, providing an enlarged, slow-flowing luminal pathway that optimizes cellular and molecular exchange.
Incorrect (A): A diameter of 10–20 microns corresponds to the luminal size of standard continuous capillaries, fenestrated capillaries, or small postcapillary venules.
Incorrect (C): A span of 50–60 microns exceeds the normal physiological diameter of sinusoidal channels in lymphoid, hepatic, and hematopoietic organs.
Incorrect (D): A range of 60–80 microns represents larger venular structures or small collecting veins rather than individual sinusoidal capillary beds.
Incorrect (E): A diameter of 90–100 microns defines macroscopic venous channels and small veins, far exceeding the scale of microvascular sinusoidal networks.
