Question 2: The diffusion of CO2 across the alveolar membrane is about 20 times faster than O2 because:
A. CO2 is actively transported
B. CO2 is more soluble in water
C. Alveolar area of CO2 diffusion is larger
D. CO2 molecular weight is significantly lower
E. CO2 pressure gradient is larger
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): While CO2 is slightly heavier than O2, CO2 is more soluble in water (and body fluids) by about 24-fold, making its overall diffusion coefficient 20 times higher.
 * Incorrect (A): Carbon dioxide crosses the membrane entirely via passive simple diffusion, so it is incorrect that CO2 is actively transported.
 * Incorrect (B): The alveolar area of CO2 diffusion is larger is false because both respiratory gases share the exact same physical alveolar-capillary surface area.
 * Incorrect (D): The molecular weight of CO2 (44) is actually higher than O2 (32), meaning CO2 molecular weight is significantly lower is factually incorrect.
 * Incorrect (E): The CO2 pressure gradient is larger is false; the normal gradient for CO2 is only 6 mmHg, while O2 has a much larger gradient of about 64 mmHg.

Question 1:
 A patient with hypoxia has arterial PO₂ of 60 mmHg and hemoglobin concentration 15 g/dl. He is most likely suffering from?
 A. Anemic hypoxia
 B. Carbon monoxide hypoxia
 C. Histotoxic hypoxia
 D. Hypoxic hypoxia
 E. Ischemic hypoxia
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 • Correct (D): Hypoxic hypoxia occurs when arterial PO₂ is reduced despite normal hemoglobin concentration.
 • Incorrect (A): Anemic hypoxia requires decreased hemoglobin concentration, which is normal here.
 • Incorrect (B): Carbon monoxide hypoxia involves Hb binding interference, not low PO₂.
 • Incorrect (C): Histotoxic hypoxia is due to cellular inability to use oxygen, not reduced arterial PO₂.
 • Incorrect (E): Ischemic hypoxia results from impaired blood flow, not low arterial PO₂.
