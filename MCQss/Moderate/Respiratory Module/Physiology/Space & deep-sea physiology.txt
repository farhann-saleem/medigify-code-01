
Question 4:
 If a person goes to high altitude, it will lead to?
 A. Metabolic acidosis
 B. Metabolic alkalosis
 C. Respiratory acidosis
 D. Respiratory alkalosis
 E. Mixed acid-base disorder
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 • Correct (D): Respiratory alkalosis occurs due to hyperventilation lowering arterial CO₂.
 • Incorrect (A): Metabolic acidosis is not triggered by altitude exposure.
 • Incorrect (B): Metabolic alkalosis is not the primary response; it may occur later as compensation.
 • Incorrect (C): Respiratory acidosis results from hypoventilation, opposite of altitude physiology.
 • Incorrect (E): Mixed acid-base disorder is not typical in isolated altitude exposure.

Question 5:
 During acclimatization at high altitude, the change not likely to occur is?
 A. Accelerated erythropoiesis
 B. Increased hemoglobin concentration
 C. Increased rate of pulmonary ventilation
 D. Respiratory acidosis
 E. Right ventricular hypertrophy
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 • Correct (D): Respiratory acidosis does not occur; hyperventilation causes alkalosis instead.
 • Incorrect (A): Accelerated erythropoiesis is a hallmark adaptation to hypoxia.
 • Incorrect (B): Increased hemoglobin concentration develops to improve oxygen carriage.
 • Incorrect (C): Increased rate of pulmonary ventilation is an immediate compensatory response.
 • Incorrect (E): Right ventricular hypertrophy can occur due to pulmonary hypertension at altitude.

Question 2
 A navy worker remained in deep sea for about 8 hours and ascended rapidly to the surface. His symptoms are most likely due to:
 A. exposure to atmospheric oxygen after surfacing.
 B. formation of nitrogen bubbles in his body.
 C. inhalation of a helium-rich breathing mixture.
 D. low atmospheric pressure at the surface.
 E. prolonged exposure to cold seawater.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 • Correct (B): Rapid ascent lowers ambient pressure, causing dissolved nitrogen to form bubbles in tissues and blood, producing decompression sickness.
 • Incorrect (A): Exposure to atmospheric oxygen after surfacing does not cause decompression sickness.
 • Incorrect (C): Inhalation of a helium-rich breathing mixture reduces nitrogen-related decompression problems rather than causing them.
 • Incorrect (D): Low atmospheric pressure alone is not the direct mechanism; nitrogen bubble formation is responsible for symptoms.
 • Incorrect (E): Prolonged exposure to cold seawater may cause hypothermia but does not produce decompression sickness.
 Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Respiratory, Aviation, Space & Deep-Sea Physiology

Question 5
 Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is most effective in:
 A. anemic hypoxia.
 B. carbon monoxide poisoning.
 C. chronic osteomyelitis.
 D. decompression sickness.
 E. gas gangrene.
 Correct Option: E
 Explanation:
 • Correct (E): Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is particularly effective in gas gangrene because high oxygen tensions inhibit anaerobic bacterial growth and improve tissue oxygenation.
 • Incorrect (A): Anemic hypoxia is primarily due to reduced oxygen-carrying capacity and is not most effectively treated with hyperbaric oxygen therapy.
 • Incorrect (B): Carbon monoxide poisoning benefits from hyperbaric oxygen therapy, but gas gangrene is the condition in which it is considered most effective.
 • Incorrect (C): Chronic osteomyelitis may be treated adjunctively with hyperbaric oxygen therapy, but it is not the most effective indication.
 • Incorrect (D): Decompression sickness responds well to hyperbaric oxygen therapy, but gas gangrene represents a more definitive therapeutic indication.
 Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Respiratory, Aviation, Space & Deep-Sea Physiology
