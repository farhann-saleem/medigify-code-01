
Question 6:
 Pulmonary compliance increases secondary to?
 A. Bronchial asthma
 B. Emphysema
 C. Old age
 D. Pulmonary fibrosis
 E. Surfactant deficiency
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 • Correct (B): Emphysema destroys alveolar walls, increasing lung compliance.
 • Incorrect (A): Bronchial asthma causes airway obstruction, not increased compliance.
 • Incorrect (C): Old age reduces elastic recoil, not markedly increasing compliance.
 • Incorrect (D): Pulmonary fibrosis stiffens lungs, decreasing compliance.
 • Incorrect (E): Surfactant deficiency increases surface tension, reducing compliance.

Question: Negative intrapleural pressure is created by:
A. the action of abdominal muscles
B. the contractile properties of respiratory muscles
C. the elastic properties of the thoracic cage and lungs
D. the surface tension of alveolar fluid
E. the total volume of anatomical dead space
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Correct (C): The negative intrapleural pressure is established by the opposing elastic forces
pulling away from each other: the elastic properties of the thoracic cage recoiling outward and
the lungs recoiling inward.

Incorrect (A): The action of abdominal muscles is utilized during active exhalation and does
not generate the resting negative intrapleural pressure.

Incorrect (B): The contractile properties of respiratory muscles drive active volume changes
but do not inherently create the baseline resting negative intrapleural pressure.

Incorrect (D): The surface tension of alveolar fluid contributes to lung recoil inward but is only
one component of the opposing elastic mechanical pull

Incorrect (E): The total volume of anatomical dead space plays a role in ventilation distribution
but has no mechanical influence on creating intrapleural pressure.

Question: At rest, work of breathing is carried out mainly to overcome:
A. elastic recoil tendency of lungs
B. large airway resistance
C. small airway resistance
D. thoracic wall inertia
E. tissue resistance
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

Correct (A): During normal quiet breathing at rest, the majority of compliance work is
expended to overcome the elastic recoil tendency of lungs and chest wall structures.

Incorrect (B): Large airway resistance accounts for a portion of airway resistance but does not
represent the main component of total work at rest.

Incorrect (C): Small airway resistance is normally very low due to the massive total
cross-sectional area of parallel airways, minimizing its contribution to baseline work.

Incorrect (D): Thoracic wall inertia is a negligible dynamic force that does not constitute the
primary resistive workload during resting respiration.

Incorrect (E): Tissue resistance is a minor fraction of non-elastic resistance compared to the
overarching elastic forces.

Question: Which of the following factors determines lung compliance:
A. airway caliber
B. alveolar surface tension
C. flexibility of the thoracic cage
D. muscles of inspiration
E. pulmonary dead space
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

Correct (B): The internal elastic forces of the lung tissue along with the alveolar surface
tension are the primary determinants of internal lung compliance.

Incorrect (A): Airway caliber determines airway resistance to airflow, which is a dynamic
kinetic property separate from static lung compliance.

Incorrect (C): Flexibility of the thoracic cage affects total respiratory system compliance, but
lung compliance itself is dictated by intrinsic parenchymal and alveolar forces.

Incorrect (D): Muscles of inspiration generate the pressure gradients necessary to move air
but do not structurally dictate or determine tissue compliance properties.

Incorrect (E): Pulmonary dead space reflects regional ventilation-perfusion geography and
does not influence the mechanical dispensability of lung parenchyma.

Question: Surfactant deficiency is not likely to produce:
A. collapse of alveoli
B. increased compliance of lungs
C. increased surface tension of fluid lining alveoli
D. microvascular hemorrhage
E. pulmonary edema
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

Correct (B): Surfactant deficiency causes a severe drop in compliance, meaning it is not likely
to produce an increased compliance of lungs.

Incorrect (A): Collapse of alveoli is a direct hallmark consequence of high surface tension in
the absence of surfactant.

Incorrect (C): An increased surface tension of fluid lining alveoli occurs automatically when
the active surfactant molecules are deficient.

Incorrect (D): Microvascular hemorrhage occurs due to the massive negative hydrostatic
pressures generated across damaged, collapsing alveolar-capillary membranes.

Incorrect (E): Pulmonary edema is promoted as high alveolar surface tension draws
transudative fluid out of capillaries into the alveolar spaces.
