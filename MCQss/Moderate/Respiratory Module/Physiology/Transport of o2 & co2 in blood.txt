Question: Hemoglobin is 89% saturated at:
A. 21 mmHg
B. 28 mmHg
C. 40 mmHg
D. 60 mmHg
E. 68 mmHg
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * Correct (D): At a partial pressure of 60 mmHg, the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve flattens out, ensuring hemoglobin remains approximately 89% saturated.
 * Incorrect (A): At 21 mmHg, hemoglobin is highly unsaturated and falls well below its 50% saturation threshold.
 * Incorrect (B): At 28 mmHg, hemoglobin is only slightly above its P_{50} value and is roughly 55% saturated.
 * Incorrect (C): At 40 mmHg, which is typical of resting mixed venous blood, hemoglobin is approximately 75% saturated with oxygen.
 * Incorrect (E): At 68 mmHg, hemoglobin enters the upper plateau region of the curve where its saturation exceeds 92%.

Question: Dissociation of oxygen from oxyhemoglobin is facilitated by:
A. decreased 2,3-diphosphoglycerate concentration
B. decrease in H+ concentration
C. decreased temperature
D. exercise
E. rest
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * Correct (D): Exercise increases metabolic rate, which raises tissue temperature, PCO_2, and H^+ concentration, shifting the curve to the right to accelerate oxygen dissociation.
 * Incorrect (A): Decreased 2,3-diphosphoglycerate concentration shifts the curve to the left, which increases hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen and inhibits dissociation.
 * Incorrect (B): A decrease in H+ concentration raises pH and stabilizes the relaxed state of hemoglobin, reducing oxygen release.
 * Incorrect (C): Decreased temperature slows down molecular motion and stabilizes oxyhemoglobin, shifting the curve leftward to suppress dissociation.
 * Incorrect (E): Rest decreases metabolic demands and lowers heat and acid production, stabilizing oxygen binding rather than facilitating release.

Question: Oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve is shifted to the left by:
A. fall in pH
B. fetal hemoglobin
C. increased 2,3-diphosphoglycerate
D. increased PCO2
E. increased temperature
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): Fetal hemoglobin lacks the ability to bind 2,3-BPG effectively, giving it a higher oxygen affinity that shifts the dissociation curve to the left.
 * Incorrect (A): A fall in pH increases H^+ binding to hemoglobin, stabilizing the tense state and shifting the curve to the right.
 * Incorrect (C): Increased 2,3-diphosphoglycerate binds preferentially to deoxygenated hemoglobin, forcing a rightward shift to promote oxygen unloading.
 * Incorrect (D): Increased PCO2 enhances carbamino compound formation and hydrogen ion generation, driving a rightward shift via the Bohr effect.
 * Incorrect (E): Increased temperature weakens the iron-oxygen bonds, shifting the curve to the right to favor oxygen release to metabolically active tissues.

Question: What is the cause of left shift of oxygen-hemoglobin curve?
A. Carbon monoxide poisoning
B. Exercise
C. Increased 2,3-BPG
D. Increased PCO2
E. Increased temperature
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): Carbon monoxide poisoning locks the remaining iron sites into a high-affinity state, preventing normal oxygen release and causing a structural left shift.
 * Incorrect (B): Exercise generates heat, lactic acid, and carbon dioxide, which structurally shifts the curve to the right to meet tissue metabolic demands.
 * Incorrect (C): Increased 2,3-BPG stabilizes the deoxygenated, tense conformation of hemoglobin, causing a distinct rightward shift.
 * Incorrect (D): Increased PCO2 enhances the Bohr effect by lowering intracellular pH, which induces a right shift in the dissociation curve.
 * Incorrect (E): Increased temperature breaks down the secondary bonds holding oxygen to heme, directly forcing a right shift.

Question: Increased oxygen displacing carbon dioxide from hemoglobin is known as the:
A. Bohr effect
B. chloride shift
C. double Bohr effect
D. Haldane effect
E. Hamburger phenomenon
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * Correct (D): The Haldane effect dictates that oxygen binding to hemoglobin reduces its affinity for carbon dioxide and protons, promoting CO_2 unloading in the lungs.
 * Incorrect (A): The Bohr effect describes how increased carbon dioxide and hydrogen ions lower hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen at the tissue level.
 * Incorrect (B): The chloride shift is the reciprocal exchange of bicarbonate and chloride ions across the red blood cell membrane during gas transport.
 * Incorrect (C): The double Bohr effect is a specialized placental mechanism where reciprocal pH changes across maternal and fetal blood optimize oxygen transfer.
 * Incorrect (E): The Hamburger phenomenon is simply another historical clinical term for the chloride shift and does not describe oxygen-driven CO_2 displacement.
