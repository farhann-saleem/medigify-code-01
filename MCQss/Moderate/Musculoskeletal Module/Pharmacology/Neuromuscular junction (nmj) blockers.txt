
Question: Succinylcholine causes depolarization by:
A. acting like ACh to stimulate nicotinic receptor
B. activating calcium channel on presynaptic terminal
C. activating sodium channel on postsynaptic terminal
D. blocking acetylcholinesterase
E. inhibiting potassium efflux
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

* ​Correct (A): Succinylcholine works by acting like ACh to stimulate nicotinic receptor sites on the endplate, generating persistent, un-repolarized depolarization that leaves the muscle refractory to further stimulation.
* ​Incorrect (B): Activating calcium channel on presynaptic terminal would boost acetylcholine exocytosis, increasing muscle contractions instead of producing a flaccid depolarizing block.
* ​Incorrect (C): Activating sodium channel on postsynaptic terminal describes a downstream voltage-gated ionic event rather than succinylcholine’s primary receptor-binding step.
* ​Incorrect (D): Blocking acetylcholinesterase is the mechanism of indirect cholinomimetics like neostigmine, whereas succinylcholine is a direct-acting receptor agonist.
* ​Incorrect (E): Inhibiting potassium efflux would prolong an active cardiac action potential but does not describe how succinylcholine opens endplate channels to initiate depolarizing paralysis.

Question: Which of the following drugs is the most effective in emergency management of malignant hyperthermia:
A. atropine
B. dantrolene
C. edrophonium
D. pyridostigmine
E. succinylcholine
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

* ​Correct (B): Dantrolene is the drug of choice because it binds to ryanodine receptors (RyR1), blocking calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum to stop the hypermetabolic crisis of malignant hyperthermia.
* ​Incorrect (A): Atropine blocks muscarinic receptors to treat bradycardia but cannot stop the intracellular skeletal muscle calcium surge driving malignant hyperthermia.
* ​Incorrect (C): Edrophonium increases acetylcholine levels at the neuromuscular junction, which has no effect on abnormal intracellular calcium release channels.
* ​Incorrect (D): Pyridostigmine is a long-acting cholinesterase inhibitor used for myasthenia gravis and does not counteract intracellular hypermetabolic cascades.
* ​Incorrect (E): Succinylcholine is a classic triggering agent that causes malignant hyperthermia in susceptible individuals, making its use absolutely contraindicated.

Question: Curare has been given as a paralyzing agent in patients undergoing surgical procedures. Its mode of action is best described by inhibiting the action of which of the following?
A. Alpha1-adrenergic receptors
B. Beta1-adrenergic receptors
C. Muscarinic receptors
D. Nicotinic receptors
E. Presynaptic auto-receptors
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

* ​Correct (D): Curare acts as a competitive antagonist at Nicotinic receptors located at the neuromuscular junction, blocking endogenous acetylcholine binding to induce flaccid muscle paralysis.
* ​Incorrect (A): Alpha1-adrenergic receptors are G-protein coupled receptors found on vascular smooth muscle and are completely unaffected by curare.
* ​Incorrect (B): Beta1-adrenergic receptors are primary G-protein coupled receptors located in cardiac tissue and are not targeted by curare.
* ​Incorrect (C): Muscarinic receptors are postganglionic parasympathetic and sweat gland receptors that are not inhibited by the skeletal muscle-paralyzing actions of curare.
* ​Incorrect (E): Presynaptic auto-receptors control neurotransmitter release via feedback inhibition and are not the primary, paralyzing site of action for curare.

Question: Neuromuscular paralysis produced by Rocuronium is blocked by:
A. atracurium
B. dantrolene
C. lidocaine
D. neostigmine
E. succinylcholine
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

* ​Correct (D): neostigmine inhibits acetylcholinesterase, which increases synaptic levels of acetylcholine to outcompete and reverse the competitive blockade established by the non-depolarizing agent rocuronium.
* ​Incorrect (A): atracurium is another non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocker and would compound, rather than block, rocuronium-induced paralysis.
* ​Incorrect (B): dantrolene prevents calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum by targeting ryanodine receptors and acts as a muscle relaxant rather than a reversal agent.
* ​Incorrect (C): lidocaine is a local anesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels and would potentially potentiate neuromuscular blockade instead of blocking it.
* ​Incorrect (E): succinylcholine is a depolarizing muscle relaxant that would further alter motor endplate polarization and would not act as an antidote to reverse rocuronium.

Question: Which muscle is usually the last to be paralyzed and the first to recover from neuromuscular blockade?
A. Abdominal muscle
B. Diaphragm
C. Foot muscle
D. Hand muscle
E. Laryngeal muscle
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

* ​Correct (B): Diaphragm tissue has high vascularity and a greater density of nicotinic receptors, making it highly resistant to paralysis during onset and the first muscle to regain function during recovery.
* ​Incorrect (A): Abdominal muscle is part of the large trunk musculature that is paralyzed prior to the respiratory muscles and recovers after them.
* ​Incorrect (B): Foot muscle belongs to the peripheral small muscle groups that are paralyzed early in the sequence and recover late.
* ​Incorrect (D): Hand muscle contains small, highly sensitive motor units that undergo neuromuscular blockade rapidly before the central respiratory muscles.
* ​Incorrect (E): Laryngeal muscle is a specialized upper airway muscle that is paralyzed relatively quickly to facilitate intubation, unlike the highly resistant diaphragm.
    Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Musculoskeletal, Neuromuscular Junction (NMJ) Blockers
