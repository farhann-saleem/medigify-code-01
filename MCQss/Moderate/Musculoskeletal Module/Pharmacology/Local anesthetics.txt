Question: Local anesthetics cause vasodilation. Their rate and duration of action can be increased & toxicity can be decreased by:
A. administering another vasodilator anesthetic
B. administering vasoconstrictor like epinephrine
C. decreasing dose of that vasodilating anesthetic
D. increasing dose of local anesthetic
E. infusing a large volume of isotonic saline
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

* ​Correct (B): Co-administering vasoconstrictor like epinephrine localized the anesthetic at the injection site by reducing regional blood flow, which retards systemic absorption and minimizes systemic toxicity.
* ​Incorrect (A): Administering another vasodilator anesthetic accelerates systemic clearance, shortening its clinical duration and increasing the risk of systemic toxicity.
* ​Incorrect (C): Decreasing dose of that vasodilating anesthetic lowers systemic toxicity but fails to increase the duration of action or individual drug potency.
* ​Incorrect (D): Increasing dose of local anesthetic risks systemic toxicity and cardiovascular collapse without correcting the underlying rate of vascular clearance.
* ​Incorrect (E): Infusing a large volume of isotonic saline dilutes the local drug concentration within the tissue, speeding up systemic clearance and lessening clinical efficacy.

Question: In general, local anesthetics block which fibers more rapidly:
A. intermediate & unmyelinated
B. larger & myelinated
C. larger & unmyelinated
D. smaller & myelinated
E. smaller & unmyelinated
Correct Option: D
Explanation:

* ​Correct (D): Smaller & myelinated axons (such as B and A-delta fibers) are blocked more rapidly because the anatomical distance between their nodes of Ranvier is short, allowing the anesthetic to rapidly reach blocking concentrations across successive nodes.
* ​Incorrect (A): Intermediate & unmyelinated fibers do not exist as a primary high-susceptibility anatomical subclass and lack the nodal architecture that accelerates differential blockade.
* ​Incorrect (B): Larger & myelinated fibers require much greater spatial lengths of anesthetic exposure because of their wide inter-nodal distance, making them more resistant to rapid blocks.
* ​Incorrect (C): Larger & unmyelinated fibers present a substantial physical diffusion barrier for the drug and lack nodes of Ranvier, rendering them slower to block.
* ​Incorrect (E): Smaller & unmyelinated C-fibers, while small, lack the concentrated nodal targets of myelinated axons, meaning they are often blocked after smaller & myelinated fibers.

Question: Local anesthetics are of two types: esters & amides. If a person is allergic to one ester local anesthetic, what should be done for local anesthesia:
A. enhance dose of ester type local anesthetic
B. give amide type local anesthetic
C. give general sedative instead of local anesthetic
D. reduce dose of ester type local anesthetic
E. try another ester type local anesthetic
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

* ​Correct (B): Cross-reactivity exists among esters due to their common para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) metabolite, so one must give amide type local anesthetic because it has a distinct metabolic pathway with no allergen cross-reactivity.
* ​Incorrect (A): Enhance dose of ester type local anesthetic increases the allergen payload, which can trigger a severe, life-threatening systemic anaphylactic reaction.
* ​Incorrect (C): Give general sedative instead of local anesthetic induces central depression without blocking peripheral nociception, failing to provide localized procedural analgesia.
* ​Incorrect (D): Reduce dose of ester type local anesthetic fails to clear allergen exposure, as even micro-doses of allergen can precipitate an immune hypersensitivity cascade.
* ​Incorrect (E): Try another ester type local anesthetic carries a high risk of cross-hypersensitivity because virtually all ester agents break down into allergic PABA derivatives.
    Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Musculoskeletal, Local Anesthetics

Question: Systemic side effect of local anesthetic (Lidocaine) is:
A. cardiac arrhythmia
B. hyperglycemia
C. methemoglobinemia
D. pneumothorax
E. respiratory distress
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

* ​Correct (A): Excess systemic lidocaine concentrations block cardiac sodium channels, slowing conduction velocity and potentially triggering a lethal cardiac arrhythmia or cardiac arrest.
* ​Incorrect (B): Hyperglycemia is a metabolic side effect tied to glucocorticoid or beta-agonist therapies, not the sodium-channel blockade caused by lidocaine.
* ​Incorrect (C): Methemoglobinemia is a classic toxic hematologic side effect associated with prilocaine or benzocaine metabolism, rather than lidocaine use.
* ​Incorrect (D): Pneumothorax is an anatomical needle-injury complication of intercostal or supraclavicular nerve blocks, not a drug-induced systemic side effect.
* ​Incorrect (E): Respiratory distress can occur secondary to profound central nervous system depression, but direct myocardial depression and arrhythmia remain the standard hallmark cardiac toxicities.
    Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Musculoskeletal, Local Anesthetics

Question: Tetrodotoxin acts on:
A. ganglionic nicotinic receptors
B. muscarinic receptors
C. nerve axons
D. neuromuscular end plates
E. receptor at adrenergic junctions
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

* ​Correct (C): Tetrodotoxin acts on nerve axons by binding specifically to voltage-gated sodium channels, blocking the influx of sodium ions and halting action potential propagation.
* ​Incorrect (A): ganglionic nicotinic receptors are ligand-gated ion channels in autonomic ganglia that are not the molecular target of tetrodotoxin.
* ​Incorrect (B): muscarinic receptors are G-protein coupled receptors that respond to acetylcholine and are not directly bound or affected by tetrodotoxin.
* ​Incorrect (D): neuromuscular end plates express nicotinic receptors and localized ion channels, but the toxin specifically disrupts propagation along the length of nerve axons rather than localized end-plate receptors.
* ​Incorrect (E): receptor at adrenergic junctions are targets for norepinephrine and epinephrine and do not bind or interact with tetrodotoxin.
    Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Musculoskeletal, Local Anesthetics
