 
Question: A patient presents with difficulty rising from a seated position and inability to laterally rotate the thigh at the hip joint. Weakness of which of the following muscles is most likely responsible:
A. gluteus maximus.
B. gluteus medius.
C. gluteus minimus.
D. iliopsoas.
E. tensor fasciae latae.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): The gluteus maximus is the primary, powerful extensor of the hip required to raise the torso from a seated position, and it additionally acts as a potent lateral rotator of the thigh.
Incorrect (B): The gluteus medius functions predominantly as a hip abductor and medial rotator, which directly opposes the clinical presentation.
Incorrect (C): The gluteus minimus acts primarily as a hip abductor and medial rotator of the thigh during the stance phase of gait.
Incorrect (D): The iliopsoas is the chief flexor of the hip joint, meaning its weakness would impair bringing the thigh up toward the torso rather than rising from a chair.
Incorrect (E): The tensor fasciae latae acts to flex, abduct, and medially rotate the thigh, making it incapable of supporting hip extension or lateral rotation.

Question: During walking, one muscle assists in medial rotation of the thigh and contributes to stabilization and locking of the hip joint. Which of the following muscles performs this function:
A. gluteus maximus.
B. gluteus medius.
C. iliopsoas.
D. piriformis.
E. quadratus femoris.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The anterior fibers of the gluteus medius actively assist in the medial rotation of the thigh, while the muscle as a whole stabilizes the pelvis during the single-leg stance phase of gait.
Incorrect (A): The gluteus maximus is a powerful lateral rotator and extensor of the hip, which directly contradicts medial rotation.
Incorrect (C): The iliopsoas functions primarily to flex the hip joint and does not participate in the stabilizing or locking mechanisms associated with walking stance phases.
Incorrect (D): The piriformis acts as a lateral rotator when the hip is extended, opposite to the noted medial rotation.
Incorrect (E): The quadratus femoris is a dedicated lateral rotator of the hip and cannot perform medial rotation.

Question: A patient presents with inability to maintain pelvic stability while standing on one leg. When asked to stand on the affected limb, the pelvis drops on the opposite side. Injury to which nerve is most likely responsible?
A) Common peroneal nerve
B) Inferior gluteal nerve
C) Obturator nerve
D) Sciatic nerve
E) Superior gluteal nerve
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The superior gluteal nerve innervates the gluteus medius and minimus, which are critical hip abductors that stabilize the pelvis; injury leads to a positive Trendelenburg sign where the pelvis drops on the opposite side when standing on the affected limb.
Incorrect (A): The common peroneal nerve innervates the anterior and lateral compartments of the leg, and its injury results in foot drop rather than pelvic instability.
Incorrect (B): The inferior gluteal nerve innervates the gluteus maximus, and a lesion here causes weakness in hip extension (difficulty climbing stairs) rather than a lateral pelvic drop.
Incorrect (C): The obturator nerve innervates the medial compartment of the thigh, causing weakness in thigh adduction rather than a failure of pelvis stabilization during one-legged standing.
Incorrect (D): The sciatic nerve innervates the posterior thigh and all muscles below the knee; while massive, its isolated proximal branches do not selectively mediate the Trendelenburg pelvis drop mechanism without extensive lower limb paralysis.

Question: A deep knife wound in the gluteal region results in a waddling gait due to weakness of hip abductors. Injury to which of the following nerves is most likely responsible?
A) Femoral nerve
B) Inferior gluteal nerve
C) Obturator nerve
D) Sciatic nerve
E) Superior gluteal nerve
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The superior gluteal nerve innervates the primary hip abductors (gluteus medius and minimus); a lesion produces a drop in the contralateral pelvis during gait, resulting in a compensatory waddling gait.
Incorrect (A): The femoral nerve supplies the anterior thigh compartment muscles, causing loss of knee extension rather than a waddling gait from hip abductor failure.
Incorrect (B): The inferior gluteal nerve supplies the gluteus maximus, leading to gluteus maximus lurch (difficulty standing up or climbing stairs) rather than abductor-driven waddling.
Incorrect (C): The obturator nerve supplies the adductors of the thigh, and its damage compromises adduction rather than abduction mechanics.
Incorrect (D): The sciatic nerve injury would cause profound deficits in hamstring function and all leg/foot movements (foot drop), which presents as a steppage gait rather than an isolated waddling gait.

Question: The cutaneous innervation to the upper anterior gluteal region is supplied by:
A. Branch of subcostal nerve.
B. Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve.
C. Perforating cutaneous nerve.
D. Posterior femoral cutaneous nerve.
E. Superior cluneal nerve.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The lateral femoral cutaneous nerve sends its posterior branches backwards to supply the skin of the upper anterior gluteal region.
Incorrect (A): The branch of subcostal nerve supplies skin over the anterior hip and lower lateral abdominal wall, not the upper anterior gluteal quadrant.
Incorrect (C): The perforating cutaneous nerve leaves the lower pelvis to supply skin over the lower medial gluteal region.
Incorrect (D): The posterior femoral cutaneous nerve sends inferior cluneal branches to supply the lower inferior gluteal region skin.
Incorrect (E): The superior cluneal nerve supplies the skin of the upper superior/posterior gluteal region, not the anterior gluteal aspect.
