 
Question: During physical examination of a patient with knee trauma, the physician suspects injury to the medial collateral ligament. Which clinical sign is most likely to be elicited:
A. abnormal lateral rotation during extension.
B. abnormal passive abduction of the extended leg.
C. inability to lock the knee in full extension.
D. positive anterior drawer sign.
E. posterior displacement of the tibia.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Injury to the medial collateral ligament (MCL) compromises medial knee stability, resulting in abnormal passive abduction of the extended leg during a valgus stress test.
Incorrect (A): Eliciting abnormal lateral rotation during extension points toward posterolateral corner instability rather than isolated MCL rupture.
Incorrect (C): An inability to lock the knee in full extension is typically indicative of a mechanical block, such as a torn bucket-handle meniscus or a cruciate ligament remnant.
Incorrect (D): Eliciting a positive anterior drawer sign demonstrates insufficiency of the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL), which prevents forward translation of the tibia.
Incorrect (E): Finding posterior displacement of the tibia under stress confirms a tear of the posterior cruciate ligament (PCL).

Question: During a clinical examination, a patient complains of instability of the knee joint while flexing the leg. The physician suspects injury to the ligament responsible for preventing anterior displacement of the femur on the tibia. Which ligament is most likely damaged:
A. anterior cruciate ligament.
B. fibular collateral ligament.
C. posterior cruciate ligament.
D. tibial collateral ligament.
E. transverse humeral ligament.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Preventing the femur from sliding posteriorly relative to the tibia is equivalent to preventing the tibia from sliding anteriorly relative to the femur; mechanically, the anterior cruciate ligament restricts this translation.
Incorrect (B): The fibular collateral ligament provides lateral stability against varus forces and does not prevent anterior-posterior translational displacement.
Incorrect (C): The posterior cruciate ligament prevents posterior displacement of the tibia relative to the femur (or anterior displacement of the femur on a fixed tibia), which contradicts the specific primary mechanical constraint of the ACL.
Incorrect (D): The tibial collateral ligament reinforces the medial aspect of the knee joint capsule against valgus stressors.
Incorrect (E): The transverse humeral ligament is an anatomical structure located entirely within the shoulder girdle holding the biceps tendon, bearing no relevance to the knee joint.

Question: A patient presents with inability to unlock the knee joint to initiate flexion while walking. Damage to which of the following muscles would most likely produce this condition?
A) Biceps femoris
B) Popliteus
C) Quadratus femoris
D) Rectus femoris
E) Semimembranosus
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The popliteus muscle is anatomically responsible for unlocking the knee joint by laterally rotating the femur on the fixed tibia to initiate flexion from full extension.
Incorrect (A): The biceps femoris acts as a primary flexor of the knee and lateral rotator of the leg when flexed, but it cannot perform the specialized key-in-lock rotational action required to unlock the fully extended joint.
Incorrect (C): The quadratus femoris is a short external rotator of the hip joint located in the gluteal region and has no structural action on the knee.
Incorrect (D): The rectus femoris is a component of the quadriceps that extends the knee joint rather than initiating flexion or unlocking it.
Incorrect (E): The semimembranosus is a medial hamstring that flexes the knee once it is already unlocked, but it does not possess the mechanical vector to initiate unlocking.

Question: During examination of the knee joint, a physician considers the ligaments preventing displacement between femur and tibia. Which ligament prevents posterior displacement of the femur on the tibia?
 
A. Anterior cruciate ligament
B. Lateral collateral ligament
C. Medial collateral ligament
D. Oblique popliteal ligament
E. Posterior cruciate ligament
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The Posterior cruciate ligament tenses during knee flexion to prevent anterior displacement of the tibia on the femur, which is biomechanically equivalent to preventing posterior displacement of the femur on the tibia.
Incorrect (A): Anterior cruciate ligament prevents anterior displacement of the tibia on the femur (or posterior displacement of the femur on the tibia) primarily during extension.
Incorrect (B): Lateral collateral ligament stabilizes the lateral aspect of the knee joint against varus stress and does not prevent anteroposterior displacement.
Incorrect (C): Medial collateral ligament stabilizes the medial aspect of the knee joint against valgus stress and does not prevent anteroposterior displacement.
Incorrect (D): Oblique popliteal ligament reinforces the posterior capsule of the knee joint to resist hyperextension, rather than preventing specific sagittal displacement of the femur.

Question: During dissection of the popliteal fossa, a student observes the structures arranged from superficial to deep. Which structure lies immediately anterior to the popliteal artery?
A. Common fibular nerve
B. Popliteal vein
C. Popliteus muscle
D. Saphenous nerve
E. Tibial nerve
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): In the popliteal fossa, the popliteal vein lies intermediate (immediately superficial/posterior) to the popliteal artery, meaning the artery lies immediately anterior to the vein.
Incorrect (A): The common fibular nerve runs along the lateral border of the fossa and is not intimately arranged in the superficial-to-deep central vascular bundle.
Incorrect (C): The popliteus muscle forms the deep floor of the popliteal fossa, placing it anterior to all the neurovascular structures, not immediately posterior/superficial to the artery.
Incorrect (D): The saphenous nerve exits the adductor canal anteromedially and does not run down the central superficial-deep axis of the popliteal fossa.
Incorrect (E): The tibial nerve is the most superficial structure in the central neurovascular bundle of the popliteal fossa, separated from the artery by the intermediate popliteal vein.

Question: A footballer sustains a direct blow to the posterior knee with hyperflexion. On examination, he cannot flex the knee completely, and lateral rotation of the tibia is impaired. Which muscle is most likely affected?
A. Biceps femoris long head
B. Plantaris
C. Popliteus
D. Semimembranosus
E. Semitendinosus
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The popliteus muscle unlocks the knee joint by initiating flexion and inducing lateral rotation of the tibia relative to the femur when the foot is non-weight-bearing.
Incorrect (A): The biceps femoris long head acts as a knee flexor and lateral rotator of the leg, but it does not play the primary role in initiating flexion or unlocking the joint.
Incorrect (B): The plantaris is a vestigial muscle with a minor role in plantarflexion of the ankle and weak flexion of the knee, but no role in tibial rotation.
Incorrect (D): The semimembranosus is a posterior hamstring muscle that flexes the knee and medially rotates the tibia, contrary to the presentation of impaired lateral rotation.
Incorrect (E): The semitendinosus is a medial hamstring that performs knee flexion and medial rotation of the leg, making its injury inconsistent with lost lateral rotation.
