
Question: Which of the following is not a branch of the brachial artery?
A. Common interosseous artery
B. Inferior ulnar collateral artery
C. Nutrient artery to the humerus
D. Profunda brachii artery
E. Superior ulnar collateral artery
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): Common interosseous artery is a short branch arising from the ulnar artery in the forearm, not from the brachial artery proper.
 * Incorrect (B): Inferior ulnar collateral artery is a direct branch of the distal brachial artery that participates in the elbow joint anastomosis.
 * Incorrect (C): Nutrient artery to the humerus arises mid-arm from the brachial artery to enter the nutrient foramen of the humeral shaft.
 * Incorrect (D): Profunda brachii artery is the first and largest branch of the brachial artery, accompanying the radial nerve in the spiral groove.
 * Incorrect (E): Superior ulnar collateral artery is given off by the brachial artery near the middle of the arm and runs with the ulnar nerve.

Question: The cubital fossa:
A. contains the trunk of the median nerve within its borders.
B. has the tendon of the triceps brachii muscle coursing through it.
C. is a quadrangular space located in front of the elbow.
D. is bounded laterally by the brachioradialis muscle.
E. is bounded medially by the pronator teres muscle.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): contains the trunk of the median nerve within its borders. is highly accurate as the median nerve travels through the fossa medial to the brachial artery.
 * Incorrect (B): has the tendon of the triceps brachii muscle coursing through it. is false; the triceps tendon inserts on the olecranon process on the posterior side of the elbow.
 * Incorrect (C): is a quadrangular space located in front of the elbow. is structurally incorrect because the cubital fossa is anatomically defined as a triangular space.
 * Incorrect (D): is bounded laterally by the brachioradialis muscle. is sub-optimal for the restructured key 'A', as the brachioradialis forms the lateral *border* of the triangle rather than its contents.
 * Incorrect (E): is bounded medially by the pronator teres muscle. is sub-optimal for key alignment as the pronator teres forms the medial *border* of this triangular space.

Question: The following are the branches of the brachial artery except:
A. common interosseous artery.
B. nutrient artery to the humerus.
C. profunda brachii artery.
D. radial collateral artery.
E. superior ulnar collateral artery.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): common interosseous artery. is the correct exception, as it branches directly off the ulnar artery in the forearm rather than arising from the brachial artery.
 * Incorrect (B): nutrient artery to the humerus. is a recognized branch of the brachial artery that enters the bone midway along the shaft.
 * Incorrect (C): profunda brachii artery. is the initial major branch of the brachial artery, tracking posteriorly into the arm.
 * Incorrect (D): radial collateral artery. is given off by the profunda brachii (a direct continuation branch of the brachial system), making it distinct from the common interosseous.
 * Incorrect (E): superior ulnar collateral artery. is a high-yield direct branch arising from the brachial artery that accompanies the ulnar nerve behind the medial epicondyle.

Question: The radial nerve:
A. arises from upper and middle trunks of brachial plexus
B. may be damaged by fractures of neck of humerus
C. supplies sensation to medial side of dorsum of hand
D. supplies the extensor muscles
E. supplies the flexor muscles
**Correct Option: D**
**Explanation:**
 * **Correct (D):** The radial nerve functionally supplies the extensor muscles of both the arm (triceps brachii) and the posterior compartment of the forearm.
 * **Incorrect (A):** The radial nerve arises from the posterior cord, which is formed by the posterior divisions of all three trunks (upper, middle, and lower).
 * **Incorrect (B):** Fractures of the surgical neck of the humerus jeopardize the axillary nerve, whereas the radial nerve is vulnerable at the mid-shaft.
 * **Incorrect (C):** Sensation to the medial side of the dorsum of the hand is mediated by the ulnar nerve, while the radial nerve supplies the lateral side.
 * **Incorrect (E):** The anterior compartment flexor muscles are supplied primarily by the median and ulnar nerves, not the radial nerve.

 * Incorrect (D): The posterior circumflex humeral artery travels through the quadrangular space posteriorly, completely separate from this anterior sulcus.
 * Incorrect (E): The triceps tendon has no anatomical relationship with the anterior humeral sulcus or the posterior circumflex arterial system.

Question: Adduction of wrist joint is function of:
A. extensor carpi radialis longus
B. extensor carpi ulnaris and flexor carpi ulnaris
C. extensor digitorum
D. extensor indicis
E. extensor pollicis brevis
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): Simultaneous contraction of the extensor carpi ulnaris and flexor carpi ulnaris on the medial side cleanly executes adduction (ulnar deviation) of wrist joint.
 * Incorrect (A): The extensor carpi radialis longus acts on the lateral aspect of the joint to cause wrist abduction (radial deviation) and extension.
 * Incorrect (C): The extensor digitorum acts primarily to extend the phalanges of the medial four digits and assists broadly in wrist extension.
 * Incorrect (D): The extensor indicis is a deep muscle dedicated to extending the index finger, providing negligible influence on structural wrist adduction.
 * Incorrect (E): The extensor pollicis brevis controls extension of the thumb phalanx and assists with radial deviation (abduction) at the wrist.

 * **Incorrect (C):** The circumflex scapular artery travels through the upper triangular space to participate in the scapular anastomosis.

 * Incorrect (C): Cutaneous sensation to the medial side of the palm is supplied by the ulnar nerve, not the radial nerve.
