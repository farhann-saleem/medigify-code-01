 
Question: A middle-aged woman presents with a swelling in the groin which is diagnosed as a femoral hernia. Anatomically, this condition can be differentiated from an inguinal hernia because the neck of the femoral hernia lies:
A. above and medial to the pubic tubercle.
B. below and lateral to the pubic tubercle.
C. lateral to the saphenous opening.
D. medial to the femoral vein.
E. within the obturator canal.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): A femoral hernia enters the femoral canal via the femoral ring, which is positioned below and lateral to the pubic tubercle, providing the key clinical landmark to differentiate it from an inguinal hernia.
Incorrect (A): An inguinal hernia neck projects above and medial to the pubic tubercle, making this relationship incorrect for a femoral hernia.
Incorrect (C): The hernia sac projects through and emerges medial to the margins of the saphenous opening, rather than lying lateral to it.
Incorrect (D): While the femoral canal sits medial to the femoral vein, this spatial relationship alone does not serve as the primary surface anatomy standard relative to the pubic tubercle for distinguishing it from an inguinal hernia.
Incorrect (E): A femoral hernia descends through the femoral canal, not the obturator canal, which transmits the obturator neurovascular bundle to the medial compartment.

Question: A patient presents with weakness in extension of the knee joint following trauma to the anterior thigh. On examination, the physician reviews the quadriceps femoris muscles. Which of the following muscles originates above the hip joint:
A. rectus femoris.
B. vastus intermedius.
C. vastus lateralis.
D. vastus medialis.
E. vastus medialis obliquus.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): The rectus femoris is the only component of the quadriceps group that crosses the hip joint, originating via its straight head from the anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS) and its reflected head from the ilium superior to the acetabulum.
Incorrect (B): The vastus intermedius originates from the anterior and lateral surfaces of the femoral shaft, well below the hip joint.
Incorrect (C): The vastus lateralis originates from the greater trochanter and linea aspera of the femur.
Incorrect (D): The vastus medialis originates from the intertrochanteric line and linea aspera of the femoral shaft.
Incorrect (E): The vastus medialis obliquus refers to the distal, horizontal fibers of the vastus medialis and arises entirely from the femur.

Question: Dislocation of the hip joint most commonly occurs due to which mechanism of injury?
A) Abnormal stretch of adductor muscles as limb is forced into abduction
B) Body weight landing heavily on one foot with knee in extension
C) Severe blow on the buttock while the hip joint is fully extended
D) Severe blow on the knee while the hip joint is fully flexed
E) Sudden forceful rotation of the trunk while the foot is planted
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): A severe blow on the knee while the hip joint is fully flexed (such as a dashboard injury in a motor vehicle accident) drives the femoral head posteriorly out of the acetabulum, representing the most common mechanism of hip dislocation.
Incorrect (A): Forcing the limb into abduction with stretched adductor muscles typically results in groin strains or pubic ligament tears, not structural hip dislocations.
Incorrect (B): Landing heavily on an extended knee transmits axial forces that typically result in distal radius, femoral neck, or vertebral fractures rather than a dislocated hip.
Incorrect (C): A blow to the buttock while the hip is fully extended impacts a stable, closed-pack position of the joint capsule and is structurally unlikely to dislodge the femoral head posteriorly.
Incorrect (E): Forceful trunk rotation over a planted foot generally puts torsional stress on the knee ligaments (like the ACL) or creates a spiral long bone fracture rather than isolating a hip dislocation.

Incorrect (D): The posterior thigh muscles are primarily supplied by the perforating branches of the profunda femoris artery, well proximal to the adductor hiatus.
