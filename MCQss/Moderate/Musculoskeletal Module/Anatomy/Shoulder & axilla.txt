
Question: This nerve arises from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus?
A. Axillary nerve
B. Dorsal scapular nerve
C. Musculocutaneous nerve
D. Suprascapular nerve
E. Ulnar nerve
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): Axillary nerve is a terminal branch arising directly from the posterior cord, receiving fibers from the C5 and C6 nerve roots.
 * Incorrect (B): Dorsal scapular nerve arises from the ventral ramus of the C5 root before the formation of the trunks.
 * Incorrect (C): Musculocutaneous nerve is a terminal branch that originates from the lateral cord of the brachial plexus.
 * Incorrect (D): Suprascapular nerve arises from the upper trunk of the brachial plexus, not from any of the cords.
 * Incorrect (E): Ulnar nerve arises as a terminal branch of the medial cord of the brachial plexus.

 * Incorrect (E): The tendon of the subscapularis reinforces the posterior side. is false; the subscapularis muscle blankets the *anterior* aspect of the joint capsule.

 * **Incorrect (E):** The subareolar lymph plexus (of Sappey) is a well-documented anatomical network located beneath the nipple and areola.

Question: The characteristic clinical deformity observed in Erb's paralysis is called:
A. ape hand.
B. claw hand.
C. waiter's tip.
D. winging of scapula.
E. wrist drop.
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
 * **Correct (C):** Injury to the upper trunk of the brachial plexus (C5\text{--}C6) results in Erb's paralysis, presenting as a "waiter's tip" deformity due to loss of abductors and lateral rotators.
 * **Incorrect (A):** Ape hand results from proximal median nerve injury, causing wasting of the thenar eminence.
 * **Incorrect (B):** Claw hand is caused by chronic ulnar nerve injury leading to hyperextension at the metacarpophalangeal joints and flexion at the interphalangeal joints.
 * **Incorrect (D):** Winging of scapula is due to damage to the long thoracic nerve, disabling the serratus anterior muscle.
 * **Incorrect (E):** Wrist drop is caused by radial nerve injury, leading to paralysis of the forearm extensor muscles.
