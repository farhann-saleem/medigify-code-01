Question: A principal difference between spongy and compact bone is the:
A. absolute presence or absence of osseous lamellae
B. chemical composition of the amorphous ground substance
C. relative amount of type I collagen in the matrix
D. spatial ratio of bone matrix to soft tissue spaces
E. structural orientation of individual osteocytes
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Compact bone is primarily dense matrix with minimal space, whereas spongy bone features a high spatial ratio of bone matrix to soft tissue spaces due to its open, honeycomb-like marrow cavities.
Incorrect (A): Absolute presence or absence of osseous lamellae is incorrect because both compact and spongy bone are mature forms composed of organized osseous lamellae.
Incorrect (B): Chemical composition of the amorphous ground substance is identical in both bone types, as both consist of the same proteoglycans, glycoproteins, and hydroxyapatite.
Incorrect (C): Relative amount of type I collagen in the matrix is the same across both types, as type I collagen is the primary organic constituent of all bone matrix.
Incorrect (E): Structural orientation of individual osteocytes is fundamentally similar, as osteocytes in both bone types reside within lacunae aligned parallel to the surrounding lamellae.

Question: During intramembranous ossification, ossification first begins in:
A. dense regular connective tissue
B. fibrocartilaginous callus
C. hyaline cartilage
D. mineralized bone
E. vascular mesenchyme
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Intramembranous ossification occurs directly within a vascular mesenchyme sheet where mesenchymal stem cells condense and differentiate directly into osteoblasts.
Incorrect (A): Dense regular connective tissue forms structures like tendons and ligaments, which do not serve as the embryonic site for intramembranous bone development.
Incorrect (B): Fibrocartilaginous callus is a temporary structure formed during the intermediate healing phase of a bone fracture, not embryonic ossification.
Incorrect (C): Hyaline cartilage acts as the temporary developmental precursor model for endochondral ossification, not intramembranous ossification.
Incorrect (D): Mineralized bone is the final differentiated end product of the ossification process, not the starting tissue where ossification first begins.

Question: The Haversian canals of bone tissue:
A. are highly prominent in spongy bone trabeculae
B. contain active chondrocytes within lacunae
C. form the central structures of a Haversian system
D. line the outermost fibrous boundary of the periosteum
E. run completely perpendicular to the long axis of the bone
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Haversian canals form the central structures of a Haversian system (osteon), serving as the core longitudinal channel containing blood vessels, nerves, and endosteum.
Incorrect (A): Are highly prominent in spongy bone trabeculae is incorrect because spongy bone trabeculae are thin and avascular, absorbing nutrients via diffusion without needing internal Haversian canals.
Incorrect (B): Contain active chondrocytes within lacunae is false because Haversian canals contain blood vessels, nerves, and loose connective tissue, never cartilage cells.
Incorrect (D): Line the outermost fibrous boundary of the periosteum is incorrect as these canals are strictly intraosseous structures bored deep within compact cortical bone matrix.
Incorrect (E): Run completely perpendicular to the long axis of the bone describes Volkmann's canals; Haversian canals run roughly parallel to the long axis.

Question: Growth in the diameter of a long bone is due to:
A. appositional bone deposition by the inner layer of the periosteum
B. appositional bone synthesis by the inner layer of the endosteum
C. endochondral ossification within secondary ossification centers
D. interstitial expansion of cartilage within the epiphyseal plate
E. intramembranous mineral resorption within the medullary cavity
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Growth in the diameter (width) of a long bone is achieved exclusively via appositional bone deposition by osteoblasts residing in the osteogenic inner layer of the periosteum.
Incorrect (B): Appositional bone synthesis by the inner layer of the endosteum typically acts to widen the internal marrow space by resorbing bone rather than driving outward diametric expansion.
Incorrect (C): Endochondral ossification within secondary ossification centers occurs inside the bony epiphyses to develop and shape the joint ends of long bones.
Incorrect (D): Interstitial expansion of cartilage within the epiphyseal plate is the physiological mechanism responsible for the longitudinal growth (lengthening) of bone, not diameter.
Incorrect (E): Intramembranous mineral resorption within the medullary cavity reduces internal bone mass to expand the marrow cavity but does not add to the outer diameter.

Question: "Soft Callus" that is formed after bone fracture is composed of:
A. compact bone
B. elastic cartilage
C. fibrocartilage
D. hyaline cartilage
E. woven bone
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): The intermediate "Soft Callus" formed during the initial weeks of fracture repair is a semi-rigid stabilizing bridge primarily composed of fibrocartilage and dense connective tissue.
Incorrect (A): Compact bone is highly organized, dense mature bone that replaces the temporary calluses during the final, long-term remodeling phase of fracture healing.
Incorrect (B): Elastic cartilage contains elastic fibers and is restricted to areas like the external ear, never forming during skeletal bone fracture repair.
Incorrect (D): Hyaline cartilage may be present in small amounts within the callus, but fibrocartilage is the predominant structural component providing tensile strength across the gap.
Incorrect (E): Woven bone is immature, disorganized bony tissue that characterizes the "hard callus" phase of fracture healing, succeeding the soft callus phase.

Question: What is the function of osteoclasts lying in Howship lacunae after 1 week of bone fracture?
A. Dynamic differentiation of osteoblasts
B. Enzymatic bone resorption
C. Mineralization of osteoid
D. Organic production of osteoid
E. Structural formation of soft callus
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Osteoclasts localized in Howship lacunae perform enzymatic bone resorption to clear away necrotic, splintered bone fragments at the fracture margins.
Incorrect (A): Dynamic differentiation of osteoblasts is mediated by local growth factor signaling cascades, not direct cellular actions or processing by osteoclasts.
Incorrect (C): Mineralization of osteoid is a biochemical process governed by active osteoblasts through alkaline phosphatase expression and matrix vesicle release.
Incorrect (D): Organic production of osteoid is the exclusive functional role of active, non-osteoclastic cells—specifically, healthy functioning osteoblasts.
Incorrect (E): Structural formation of soft callus is driven by fibroblasts and chondrocytes generating a fibrocartilaginous matrix, a process separate from osteoclast activity.

Question: Which component of bone impedes the distribution of nutrients and oxygen to osteocytes:
A. fibrous outer periosteum
B. fluid-filled canaliculi
C. longitudinal Haversian canals
D. mineralized extracellular matrix
E. perforating Volkmann's canals
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The mineralized extracellular matrix is a dense, calcified barrier that blocks free passive diffusion, necessitating a specialized system of canaliculi to nourish resident osteocytes.
Incorrect (A): Fibrous outer periosteum is a supportive connective tissue boundary shell on the exterior of bone that does not directly enclose or isolate internal osteocytes.
Incorrect (B): Fluid-filled canaliculi facilitate rather than impede the delivery of nutrients and oxygen by providing open micro-conduits for diffusion between cell processes.
Incorrect (C): Longitudinal Haversian canals are major vascular thoroughfares containing blood vessels that supply oxygen, actively supporting rather than blocking distribution.
Incorrect (E): Perforating Volkmann's canals carry blood vessels horizontally through bone to supply nutrients, acting as vital delivery pathways.

Question: Osteoarthritis is a chronic condition that commonly occurs during aging; involves gradual loss or changed physical properties of which of the following varieties of cartilage:
A. articular cartilage

Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Osteoarthritis is pathophysiologically defined by the progressive degradation, fibrillation, and focal erosion of the articular cartilage covering load-bearing synovial joint surfaces.
Incorrect (B): Costal cartilage undergoes age-related calcification and rigidity but is not involved in the degenerative joint pathology characteristic of osteoarthritis.
Incorrect (C): Elastic cartilage preserves structural shape in flexible organs and is completely uninvolved in the degenerative processes of diarthrodial joint disease.
Incorrect (D): Fibrous cartilage structures can undergo mechanical tearing or degeneration but are not the primary target tissue defining classical osteoarthritis.
Incorrect (E): While structurally a sub-type of hyaline cartilage, selecting articular cartilage is more pathognomonically precise for the joint localized destruction seen in osteoarthritis.

Question: In which type of cartilage, chondrocytes are arranged in rows:
A. articular cartilage
B. elastic cartilage
C. fibrocartilage
D. hyaline cartilage
E. costal cartilage
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): In fibrocartilage, constraints imposed by the bulky, parallel bundles of dense extracellular collagen fibers force the resident chondrocytes to align in distinct, single-file rows.
Incorrect (A): Articular cartilage organizes cells into deep vertical columns and tangential zones but does not exhibit the long, single-file rows typical of fibrocartilage.
Incorrect (B): Elastic cartilage displays closely packed chondrocytes distributed in small clusters or pairs scattered evenly throughout an intricate elastic meshwork.
Incorrect (D): Hyaline cartilage features cells grouped together loosely in rounded, expanding isogenous clusters rather than linear structural arrays.
Incorrect (E): Costal cartilage contains large, active chondrocytes organized in prominent isogenous groups embedded within a rich, amorphous matrix.
