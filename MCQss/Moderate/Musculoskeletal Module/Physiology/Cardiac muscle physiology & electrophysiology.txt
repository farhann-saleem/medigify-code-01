​Question: The plateau phase of cardiac muscle action potential is mainly due to:
A. Increased chloride permeability
B. Opening of fast Na⁺ channels
C. Prolonged opening of slow Ca²⁺–Na⁺ channels
D. Rapid efflux of K⁺ ions
E. Rapid influx of Cl⁻ ions
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
​Correct (C): Prolonged opening of slow Ca²⁺–Na⁺ channels permits a sustained influx of calcium ions that balances potassium efflux, maintaining the prolonged depolarization state.
​Incorrect (A): Increased chloride permeability causes hyperpolarization or small transient repolarization, not the sustained depolarization of the plateau phase.
​Incorrect (B): Opening of fast Na⁺ channels is responsible for the initial rapid depolarization upstroke (Phase 0), not the plateau phase (Phase 2).
​Incorrect (D): Rapid efflux of K⁺ ions mediates the phase 3 repolarization that brings the action potential to an end, rather than maintaining the plateau.
​Incorrect (E): Rapid influx of Cl⁻ ions would introduce negative charge into the cell, terminating or preventing the plateau phase instead of sustaining it.

​Question: The refractory period of cardiac muscle is long mainly because of:
A. Absence of T-tubules
B. Plateau phase caused by Ca²⁺ influx
C. Rapid Cl⁻ influx
D. Rapid K⁺ efflux
E. Slow Na⁺ channel activation
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
​Correct (B): Plateau phase caused by Ca²⁺ influx prolongs the action potential duration, keeping voltage-gated sodium channels inactivated for an extended period and preventing early re-excitation.
​Incorrect (A): Absence of T-tubules is structurally inaccurate, as ventricular cardiac muscle possesses an extensive T-tubule network, and it has no role in channel refractoriness.
​Incorrect (C): Rapid Cl⁻ influx participates briefly in early transient repolarization (Phase 1) and does not extend the total refractory length.
​Incorrect (D): Rapid K⁺ efflux serves to repolarize the cell, shortening the action potential rather than lengthening its refractory period.
​Incorrect (E): Slow Na⁺ channel activation is factually incorrect; cardiac fast sodium channels activate rapidly during Phase 0 to drive the upstroke.

​Question: Tetanization does not occur in cardiac muscle because:
A. Due to decreased refractory period
B. Due to gap junctions
C. Due to plateau phase
D. Due to prolonged refractory period
E. Due to rapid repolarization
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
​Correct (D): Due to prolonged refractory period, cardiac muscle cannot be re-excited until the mechanical contraction is almost complete, preventing wave summation and tetanic spasm.
​Incorrect (A): Due to decreased refractory period would allow rapid repeated stimulation and increase the risk of tetanization, which is the opposite of cardiac physiology.
​Incorrect (B): Due to gap junctions facilitates electrical syncytium-like spread between adjacent cells but does not dictate the individual cell's mechanical or electrical refractory duration.
​Incorrect (C): Due to plateau phase is the underlying cellular mechanism that generates the long action potential, but it is the resulting prolonged refractory period itself that directly prevents tetanization.
​Incorrect (E): Due to rapid repolarization would shorten the action potential duration, making tetanization more likely rather than preventing it.
