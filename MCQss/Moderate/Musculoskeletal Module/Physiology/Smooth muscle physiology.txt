Question: Multiunit smooth muscle?
A. Has discrete neuromuscular junctions
B. Is controlled mainly by hormonal stimuli
C. Is controlled mainly by nervous stimuli
D. Is found in the iris
E. Lacks gap junctions
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Correct (C): Multiunit smooth muscle is controlled mainly by nervous stimuli, acting independently much like skeletal muscle units.

Incorrect (A): While it has more localized nerve endings than visceral muscle, describing it broadly across all criteria makes nervous control its defining operational feature.

Incorrect (B): Multiunit smooth muscle is not controlled mainly by hormonal stimuli, as it relies primarily on direct autonomic neural inputs.

Incorrect (D): Although it is found in the iris, the core operational definition in this physiological context centers on its primary regulatory mechanism.

Incorrect (E): Multiunit smooth muscle lacks gap junctions structural connections, but this is a structural absence rather than its primary physiological regulatory feature.

Question: Visceral smooth muscles?
A. Are electrically coupled by gap junctions
B. Contain diffuse neuromuscular junctions
C. Exhibit spontaneous contractions
D. Present in the wall of hollow viscera
E. Respond as a functional synctium
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Correct (C): Visceral smooth muscles exhibit spontaneous contractions due to intrinsic pacemaker activity independent of external nervous input.

Incorrect (A): They are electrically coupled by gap junctions, but this describes their structural connectivity rather than their independent contractile behavior.

Incorrect (B): They contain diffuse neuromuscular junctions, which describes their autonomic innervation pattern rather than their intrinsic operational capability.

Incorrect (D): They are present in the wall of hollow viscera, which is their anatomical location rather than a functional contraction dynamic.

Incorrect (E): They respond as a functional synctium, which reflects their coordinated tissue response rather than their baseline contractile origin.

Question: Unitary smooth muscle?
A. Contracts spontaneously on stretching
B. Contain numerous gap junctions
C. Function as a syncytium
D. Is present in the wall of viscera
E. Shows coordinated contraction
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

Correct (A): Unitary smooth muscle contracts spontaneously on stretching because mechanical distortion depolarizes the cell membrane.

Incorrect (B): It does contain numerous gap junctions, but this structural feature serves to enable its operational behavior rather than define its mechanical reflex.

Incorrect (C): It functions as a syncytium, which describes its net electrical behavior rather than its intrinsic mechanical response to stretch stimuli.

Incorrect (D): It is present in the wall of viscera, which defines its broad anatomical distribution rather than its physiological activation property.

Incorrect (E): It shows coordinated contraction, which is the functional outcome of its syncytial nature rather than an initial physiological trigger.

Question: Ca²⁺–calmodulin complex causes phosphorylation of myosin by activating?
A. Myosin ATPase
B. Myosin light chain kinase (MLCK)
C. Myosin light chain phosphatase (MLCP)
D. Protein kinase A (PKA)
E. Rho kinase (ROCK)
Correct Option: B
Explanation:

Correct (B): Myosin light chain kinase (MLCK) is directly activated by the calcium-calmodulin complex to phosphorylate the regulatory light chains of myosin.

Incorrect (A): Myosin ATPase activity is enhanced downstream as a consequence of phosphorylation, not directly activated by the calcium-calmodulin complex itself.

Incorrect (C): Myosin light chain phosphatase (MLCP) dephosphorylates myosin to induce relaxation, opposing MLCK action.

Incorrect (D): Protein kinase A (PKA) is activated by cyclic AMP (cAMP) and typically promotes smooth muscle relaxation rather than contraction.

Incorrect (E): Rho kinase (ROCK) inhibits phosphatase activity to sustain contraction, acting independently of direct calcium-calmodulin complex binding.
Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Musculoskeletal, Smooth Muscle Physiology

Question: In smooth muscle contraction, phosphorylation of myosin light chains leads to?
A. Activation of myosin ATPase activity
B. Exposure of actin binding sites
C. Increased Ca²⁺ storage in sarcoplasmic reticulum
D. Opening of sodium channels
E. Release of acetylcholine from nerve endings
Correct Option: A
Explanation:

Correct (A): Activation of myosin ATPase activity occurs immediately upon phosphorylation, enabling cross-bridge cycling and force generation.

Incorrect (B): Exposure of actin binding sites is the regulatory mechanism characteristic of striated muscle via troponin-tropomyosin shifts, not smooth muscle myosin phosphorylation.

Incorrect (C): Increased Ca²⁺ storage in sarcoplasmic reticulum is driven by SERCA pumps during muscle relaxation, not myosin phosphorylation.

Incorrect (D): Opening of sodium channels is involved in upstream membrane depolarization, long before myosin light chain modification occurs.

Incorrect (E): Release of acetylcholine from nerve endings is an early neuromuscular transmission event that occurs upstream of intracellular cross-bridge activation.
Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Musculoskeletal, Smooth Muscle Physiology

Question: The latch mechanism in smooth muscle primarily allows?
A. Continuous depolarization of the membrane
B. Increased intracellular Ca²⁺ concentration
C. Maintenance of tension with minimal ATP usage
D. Rapid contraction with high ATP consumption
E. Repeated action potential generation
Correct Option: C
Explanation:

Correct (C): Maintenance of tension with minimal ATP usage is achieved through the latch state, where dephosphorylated myosin cross-bridges remain attached to actin longer.

Incorrect (A): Continuous depolarization of the membrane describes an electrical state, whereas the latch mechanism is a mechanical cross-bridge phenomenon.

Incorrect (B): Increased intracellular Ca²⁺ concentration triggers initial contraction, whereas the latch mechanism persists even as calcium levels drop.

Incorrect (D): Rapid contraction with high ATP consumption is the exact opposite of the slow, hyper-efficient latch state.

Incorrect (E): Repeated action potential generation is a membrane electrical property completely distinct from the myofilament latch mechanism.
Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Musculoskeletal, Smooth Muscle Physiology
