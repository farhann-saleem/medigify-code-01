Question
A neuron is perfused with a solution in which the extracellular K+ concentration is increased from 4 mM to 8 mM while other ions remain constant. According to membrane physiology described in Guyton & Hall, what is the most immediate effect on the resting membrane potential:
A. Depolarization due to reduced K+ diffusion gradient.
B. Depolarization due to reversal of the Na+-K+ pump.
C. Hyperpolarization due to activation of Na+-K+ pump.
D. Hyperpolarization due to increased K+ efflux from the cell.
E. No change because Na+ permeability dominates membrane potential.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Increasing extracellular K+ concentration narrows the chemical gradient between the inside and outside of the cell, which limits potassium exit and drives the resting membrane potential toward a more positive value.
Incorrect (B): The phrase "depolarization due to reversal of the Na+-K+ pump" is incorrect because an elevated extracellular potassium level does not cause this primary active transporter to run backward.
Incorrect (C): The phrase "hyperpolarization due to activation of Na+-K+ pump" is incorrect because reducing the potassium concentration gradient causes the cell to become more positive, not more negative.
Incorrect (D): The phrase "hyperpolarization due to increased K+ efflux from the cell" is incorrect because a higher external potassium concentration decreases the driving force for potassium exit, rather than increasing it.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "no change because Na+ permeability dominates membrane potential" is incorrect because potassium permeability, not sodium permeability, dictates the resting state of the membrane.

​Question

A. Resting membrane potential becomes less negative, increasing excitability
B. Resting membrane potential becomes less negative, maintaining excitability
C. Resting membrane potential becomes more negative, decreasing excitability
D. Resting membrane potential becomes more negative, increasing excitability
E. Resting membrane potential remains entirely unchanged, decreasing excitability
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): A drop in extracellular potassium widens the concentration gradient across the membrane, causing more potassium to leak out of the neuron and pulling the resting potential further away from the activation threshold.
Incorrect (A): The phrase "resting membrane potential becomes less negative, increasing excitability" is incorrect because hypokalemia hyperpolarizes the membrane, making it more negative rather than less negative.
Incorrect (B): The phrase "resting membrane potential becomes less negative, maintaining excitability" is incorrect because a lower extracellular potassium concentration drives the potential to a deeper negative state.
Incorrect (D): The phrase "resting membrane potential becomes more negative, increasing excitability" is incorrect because moving the baseline potential further below threshold reduces the likelihood of firing an action potential.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "resting membrane potential remains entirely unchanged, decreasing excitability" is incorrect because the resting potential is highly sensitive to changes in the extracellular potassium concentration.

​Question

A. Ca2+ influx through voltage-gated presynaptic terminals.
B. Cl- influx through ionotropic ligand-gated channels.
C. K+ diffusion through background resting leak channels.
D. Na+ diffusion through voltage-gated sodium channels.
E. Na+-K+ ATPase primary active transport pump activity.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Local anesthetics cross the axonal membrane and bind to the internal pores of voltage-gated sodium channels, preventing the rapid influx of sodium required to initiate and propagate action potentials.
Incorrect (A): The phrase "Ca2+ influx through voltage-gated presynaptic terminals" is incorrect because this process governs neurotransmitter release at the synapse rather than impulse propagation along the axon cylinder.
Incorrect (B): The phrase "Cl- influx through ionotropic ligand-gated channels" is incorrect because it describes inhibitory synaptic transmission mediated by receptors like GABA, which is not the target of local anesthetics.
Incorrect (C): The phrase "K+ diffusion through background resting leak channels" is incorrect because resting potassium pathways remain functional in the presence of standard clinical doses of local anesthetics.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "Na+-K+ ATPase primary active transport pump activity" is incorrect because local anesthetics do not target the metabolic machinery that maintains long-term ion concentration gradients.

B. Na+ equilibrium potential becomes significantly less positive
C. Na+ equilibrium potential becomes significantly more positive
D. Na+ equilibrium potential remains completely unchanged from normal
E. Resting membrane potential becomes entirely independent of Na+

Explanation:

Incorrect (A): The phrase "K+ equilibrium potential becomes significantly more negative" is incorrect because shifting internal sodium parameters does not alter the potassium concentration gradient.
Incorrect (C): The phrase "Na+ equilibrium potential becomes significantly more positive" is incorrect because narrowing the trans-membrane sodium ratio decreases the calculated Nernst potential instead of increasing it.
Incorrect (D): The phrase "Na+ equilibrium potential remains completely unchanged from normal" is incorrect because the Nernst equation is directly dependent on the ratio of internal and external ion values.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "Resting membrane potential becomes entirely independent of Na+" is incorrect because resting voltages are always influenced by the standing concentration gradients of all permeable ions.

B. Marked depolarization of the resting membrane potential
C. Marked hyperpolarization of the resting membrane potential
D. Minimal change in the baseline resting membrane potential

Explanation:

Incorrect (C): The phrase "Marked hyperpolarization of the resting membrane potential" is incorrect because while the shift is technically hyperpolarizing, the low resting conductance of sodium makes this voltage change minuscule rather than marked.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "Sustained oscillation of the resting membrane potential" is incorrect because altering steady-state extracellular ion pools does not introduce the dynamic feedback loops required to generate electrical oscillations.

​Question
A researcher calculates the Nernst potential for K+ at 37°C and obtains a value of approximately –94 mV. Which assumption is essential for this calculation?
A. Active transport mechanisms are entirely absent from the membrane
B. Electrical gradient across the cellular membrane is completely zero
C. Equal permeability exists for both sodium and potassium ions
D. Membrane is selectively permeable only to intracellular potassium ions
E. Temperature of the surrounding fluid remains at absolute zero
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The Nernst equation is valid only when calculating the equilibrium state of a single specific ion across a boundary that allows only that specific ion species to pass.
Incorrect (A): The phrase "Active transport mechanisms are entirely absent from the membrane" is incorrect because active transport systems are necessary to establish the underlying concentration differences that the Nernst equation measures.
Incorrect (B): The phrase "Electrical gradient across the cellular membrane is completely zero" is incorrect because the Nernst value identifies the precise balance point where a non-zero electrical gradient perfectly counteracts a chemical gradient.
Incorrect (C): The phrase "Equal permeability exists for both sodium and potassium ions" is incorrect because introducing a second permeable ion like sodium requires using the multi-ion Goldman equation instead of the single-ion Nernst formula.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "Temperature of the surrounding fluid remains at absolute zero" is incorrect because the equation incorporates absolute temperature as a direct multiplier, which would reduce the final potential to zero millivolts if set to absolute zero.

Incorrect (D): Reduced potassium channel inactivation would prolong the action potential rather than causing the conduction failure typical of demyelination.
Incorrect (E): Reduced Na⁺ channel activation at synapse describes a chemical synaptic transmission failure rather than an axonal conduction defect from peripheral demyelination.

Incorrect (C): Increased Cl⁻ permeability causing depolarization is factually incorrect because chloride influx hyperpolarizes rather than depolarizes the membrane.
Incorrect (E): Persistent Na⁺ channel opening causing depolarization would sustain an excited or depolarized state rather than explaining the reduced excitability of the relative refractory period.
