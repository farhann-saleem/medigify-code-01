​Question
In peripheral nerve injury, Wallerian degeneration occurs distal to the lesion. Which cellular event is characteristic of this process?
A. Degeneration of axon and myelin sheath distal to the injury site
B. Hypertrophy of resting Schwann cells without any secondary axonal loss
C. Immediate structural regeneration of the underlying axonal cytoskeleton
D. Increased primary Na+-K+ pump activity within the damaged nerve segment
E. Proliferation of central oligodendrocytes directly around the lesion site
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Wallerian degeneration is defined by the structural disintegration of the axon core and its surrounding insulation layer downstream from the point of transection due to separation from the metabolic source in the soma.
Incorrect (B): The phrase "Hypertrophy of resting Schwann cells without any secondary axonal loss" is incorrect because the axon segment isolated from the cell body breaks down completely during the degenerative cascade.
Incorrect (C): The phrase "Immediate structural regeneration of the underlying axonal cytoskeleton" is incorrect because the initial response distal to a severe cut is active cytoskeleton breakdown rather than structural repair.
Incorrect (D): The phrase "Increased primary Na+-K+ pump activity within the damaged nerve segment" is incorrect because the isolated distal segment suffers energetic failure and undergoes fragmentation.
Incorrect (E): The phrase "Prolliferation of central oligodendrocytes directly around the lesion site" is incorrect because Wallerian degeneration in peripheral nerves involves Schwann cells, not central nervous system oligodendrocytes.
