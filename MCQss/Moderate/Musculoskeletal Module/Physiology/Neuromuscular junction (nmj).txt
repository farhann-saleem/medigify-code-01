​Question: End-plate potential produced during neuromuscular transmission:
A. follows the all-or-none rule completely
B. has an absolute refractory period
C. is a local potential change due to Na⁺ influx
D. is self-propagated along sarcolemma
E. propagates down the entire T-tubule system
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (C): Is a local potential change due to Na⁺ influx describes the graded, non-propagated depolarization that occurs at the motor end-plate when nicotinic channels open.
 * ​Incorrect (A): follows the all-or-none rule completely is incorrect because the end-plate potential is a graded potential whose amplitude depends entirely on the amount of acetylcholine released.
 * ​Incorrect (B): has an absolute refractory period is incorrect because graded local potentials lack refractory periods and can be temporally or spatially summated.
 * ​Incorrect (D): is self-propagated along sarcolemma is incorrect because it decays electrotonically and requires the threshold to be met to trigger a self-propagating action potential.
 * ​Incorrect (E): propagates down the entire T-tubule system is incorrect because it is the subsequent action potential, not the local end-plate potential, that travels through the T-tubules.

​Question: A toxin blocks release of acetylcholine from the presynaptic motor nerve terminal by cleaving SNARE proteins. Which toxin is most likely responsible:
A. botulinum toxin
B. curare
C. neostigmine
D. tetanospasmin
E. α-bungarotoxin
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): Botulinum toxin cleaves specific core SNARE proteins within the motor nerve terminal, preventing vesicle fusion and leading to flaccid paralysis.
 * ​Incorrect (B): curare is incorrect because it acts postsynaptically by competitively blocking nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the motor end-plate.
 * ​Incorrect (C): neostigmine is incorrect because it acts as an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that increases acetylcholine persistence rather than blocking its release.
 * ​Incorrect (D): tetanospasmin is incorrect because while it cleaves SNARE proteins, it retrogradely travels to the spinal cord to target inhibitory interneurons, not the presynaptic motor terminal.
 * ​Incorrect (E): α-bungarotoxin is incorrect because it binds irreversibly to postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, preventing channel activation.

​Question: In Myasthenia gravis, the amplitude of which potential is most reduced at the neuromuscular junction:
A. action potential
B. end-plate potential
C. miniature end-plate potential
D. resting membrane potential
E. threshold potential
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (B): End-plate potential amplitude is severely decreased because the marked reduction in viable postsynaptic nicotinic receptors prevents sufficient sodium influx during transmission.
 * ​Incorrect (A): action potential is incorrect because action potentials operate on an all-or-none basis and maintain standard amplitude once the required voltage threshold is achieved.
 * ​Incorrect (C): miniature end-plate potential is incorrect because while individual miniature potentials are reduced, the collective end-plate potential elicited by a nerve impulse is the primary deficit underlying weakness.
 * ​Incorrect (D): resting membrane potential is incorrect because the baseline electrogenic resting state of the sarcolemma is preserved and unaffected by the loss of receptor channels.
 * ​Incorrect (E): threshold potential is incorrect because the intrinsic voltage value required to activate fast voltage-gated sodium channels remains constant.

B. increasing acetylcholine synthesis in presynaptic motor neurons
C. increasing sensitivity of the troponin complex to Ca²⁺ ions
D. inhibiting ryanodine receptors within the sarcoplasmic reticulum
E. stimulating Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum directly
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): Decreasing acetylcholine breakdown at the neuromuscular junction allows neurotransmitters to persist longer in the cleft, maximizing the activation of remaining functional receptors.
 * ​Incorrect (B): increasing acetylcholine synthesis in presynaptic motor neurons is incorrect because these agents do not alter the enzymatic production rates of choline acetyltransferase.
 * ​Incorrect (C): increasing sensitivity of the troponin complex to Ca²⁺ ions is incorrect because these drugs act within the synaptic cleft, exerting no direct regulatory influence on myofilament proteins.
 * ​Incorrect (D): inhibiting ryanodine receptors within the sarcoplasmic reticulum is incorrect because anticholinestases do not enter the sarcoplasm to interact with internal calcium stores.
 * ​Incorrect (E): stimulating Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum directly is incorrect because downstream calcium release is only enhanced secondary to improved sarcolemmal depolarization.

C. it releases Ca²⁺ regulating hormones influencing muscle fibers
D. it secretes acetylcholinesterase into the systemic circulation
E. it stimulates neuromuscular transmission via direct innervation
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * ​Correct (A): It is a major site of autoimmune antibody production because thymic myoid cells express nicotinic receptors, triggering the helper T-cell and B-cell mediated production of anti-AChR antibodies.
 * ​Incorrect (B): it produces acetylcholine for systemic neuromuscular transport is incorrect because acetylcholine is synthesized locally inside axon terminals, not distributed via endocrine pathways by the thymus.
 * ​Incorrect (C): it releases Ca²⁺ regulating hormones influencing muscle fibers is incorrect because calcium homeostasis is strictly regulated by the parathyroid glands and thyroid gland (calcitonin).
 * ​Incorrect (D): it secretes acetylcholinesterase into the systemic circulation is incorrect because acetylcholinesterase is synthesized and localized specifically within tissue synaptic clefts, not circulating as a thymic hormone.
 * ​Incorrect (E): it stimulates neuromuscular transmission via direct innervation is incorrect because the thymus does not provide neural tracts or efferent motor fibers to peripheral skeletal muscular units

Question: A characteristic clinical feature of Myasthenia gravis is weakness that:
A. displays no diurnal variation.
B. improves with cooling of muscles.
C. improves with repeated muscle use.
D. resolves completely following rest.
E. worsens with repeated muscle use.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 * Correct (E): Progressive depletion of acetylcholine in the neuromuscular junction during active contraction causes weakness that worsens with repeated muscle use.
 * Incorrect (A): The disease typically displays marked diurnal variation, with symptoms being milder in the morning and more severe in the evening.
 * Incorrect (B): Localized cooling inhibits acetylcholinesterase activity, which actually improves neuromuscular transmission and temporarily improves with cooling of muscles.
 * Incorrect (C): Repeated contraction depletes the limited pool of available neurotransmitter, meaning the weakness worsens, rather than improves with repeated muscle use.
 * Incorrect (D): While rest allows acetylcholine stores to replenish and significantly reduces symptoms, the underlying autoantibody-mediated receptor loss ensures weakness rarely resolves completely following rest.
