Question: During isometric muscle contraction:
A. muscle doesn’t shorten.
B. muscle gets shortened against a fixed load.
C. muscle length changes at a constant speed.
D. shortening of muscle occurs.
E. tension on muscle remains constant.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): During isometric muscle contraction, the external muscle doesn’t shorten because the tension generated matches or is less than an immovable load.
 * Incorrect (B): If a muscle gets shortened against a fixed load, it is undergoing an isotonic concentric contraction.
 * Incorrect (C): A process where muscle length changes at a constant speed is characterized as an isokinetic contraction.
 * Incorrect (D): Shortening of muscle occurs during concentric isotonic contractions, which is explicitly absent in isometric tasks.
 * Incorrect (E): When tension on muscle remains constant while length changes, the contraction is termed isotonic.

Question: Running involves:
A. both isotonic and isometric contractions.
B. eccentric contraction only.
C. isometric contraction only.
D. isotonic contraction only.
E. spontaneous contraction on stretching.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 * Correct (D): Dynamic activities like running primarily involve isotonic contraction only (both concentric and eccentric phases) to move the limbs and propel the body weight across a distance.
 * Incorrect (A): Running relies on dynamic limb movement and joint displacement, which does not rely on both isotonic and isometric contractions simultaneously to perform the gross movement.
 * Incorrect (B): Running incorporates both concentric (shortening) and eccentric (lengthening) phases, making eccentric contraction only incorrect.
 * Incorrect (C): Isometric contraction only would result in static posture maintenance without any joint movement or locomotion.
 * Incorrect (E): Spontaneous contraction on stretching describes a smooth muscle myogenic reflex or a deep tendon reflex, not coordinated voluntary skeletal locomotion.

B. length of sarcomere.

Explanation:

 * Incorrect (D): The rate of calcium release from calsequestrin sets the speed of contraction initiation and onset, not the ongoing mechanical velocity of the contraction cycle.
 * Incorrect (E): The cross-sectional size of muscle fiber determines the absolute maximal force generation capability rather than its shortening velocity.

Question: The H zone disappears during maximal skeletal muscle contraction because:
A. actin filaments shorten.
B. actin filaments slide over myosin filaments.
C. myosin filaments shorten.
D. myosin filaments slide over titin filaments.
E. Z discs break down.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 * Correct (B): The H zone consists exclusively of thick filaments; as maximal contraction occurs, actin filaments slide over myosin filaments into the center of the A band, fully obscuring the H zone.
 * Incorrect (A): According to the sliding filament theory, thin actin filaments slide and overlap but do not structurally shorten.
 * Incorrect (C): Thick myosin filaments remain constant in physical length throughout all phases of muscle contraction and relaxation cycles.
 * Incorrect (D): Contraction is driven by actin-myosin cross-bridge mechanics, not by myosin filaments actively sliding over titin filaments.
 * Incorrect (E): Z discs approach each other to shorten the sarcomere but remain fully intact; they never break down during physiological maximal contraction.

Question: A major factor involved in skeletal muscle fatigue is:

 * Incorrect (B): Intracellular sodium can rise slightly due to high-frequency stimulation, but elevated Na+ is not recognized as a major metabolic or chemical factor in muscle fatigue.
 * Incorrect (C): High blood glucose provides an abundant substrate for energy production, which delays rather than promotes muscle fatigue.
 * Incorrect (E): Muscle fatigue is associated with high intracellular phosphate levels resulting from rapid breakdown of phosphocreatine and ATP, not low intracellular phosphate.
