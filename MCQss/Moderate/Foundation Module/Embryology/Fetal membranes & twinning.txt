Question: During the later stages of pregnancy, maternal blood is separated from fetal blood by the:
A. cytotrophoblast and fetal endothelium.
B. cytotrophoblast only.
C. fetal endothelium only.
D. syncytiotrophoblast and fetal endothelium.
E. syncytiotrophoblast only.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): In late pregnancy, the placental barrier is thinned and mainly consists of syncytiotrophoblast and fetal capillary endothelium.
Incorrect (A): Cytotrophoblast largely disappears from the placental membrane later in gestation.
Incorrect (B): Cytotrophoblast alone cannot separate maternal and fetal blood in late pregnancy.
Incorrect (C): Fetal endothelium alone does not constitute the entire placental barrier.
Incorrect (E): Syncytiotrophoblast alone lacks the fetal endothelial component of the placental membrane.

Question: A 26-year-old pregnant woman experiences repeated episodes of bright red vaginal bleeding at week 28, week 32, and week 34 of pregnancy. The bleeding spontaneously subsided each time. Use of ultrasound shows that the placenta is located in the lower right portion of the uterus over the internal os. What is the diagnosis:
A. complete hydatidiform mole.
B. marginal placenta previa.
C. partial placenta previa.
D. placenta previa.
E. vasa previa.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Placenta previa occurs when the placenta implants over or near the internal cervical os, causing recurrent painless bright red bleeding.
Incorrect (A): Complete hydatidiform mole presents with abnormal trophoblastic proliferation rather than placental implantation over the os.
Incorrect (B): Marginal placenta previa reaches the edge of the os but does not cover it completely.
Incorrect (C): Partial placenta previa only partially covers the os, whereas this stem describes placenta over the os.
Incorrect (E): Vasa previa involves fetal vessels crossing the os and usually causes bleeding after membrane rupture.

Question: A woman gave birth to twin baby boys. One of them was larger in size than the other. Blood groups were different. Examination of placenta showed extensive vascular anastomosis. Clinically this condition is called:
A. conjoined twins.
B. dichorionic twins.
C. fetus papyraceus.
C. monochorionic twin transfusion syndrome.
E. twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome results from placental vascular anastomoses causing unequal blood flow and discordant fetal growth.
Incorrect (A): Conjoined twins are physically fused and unrelated to placental vascular imbalance.
Incorrect (B): Dichorionic twins have separate chorions and typically lack major vascular anastomoses.
Incorrect (C): Fetus papyraceus involves death and compression of one twin rather than circulatory imbalance.
Incorrect (D): Monochorionic twin transfusion syndrome is descriptive but the recognized clinical term is twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome.

Question: A woman gave birth to twin baby boys. One of them was larger in size than the other. Blood groups were different. Examination of placenta showed extensive vascular anastomosis. Clinically this condition is called: Options: a) Twin transfusion syndrome b) Conjoined twins c) Fetus papyraceous d) Fraternal twins Correct Option: Ans. Twin transfusion syndrome Explanation: Occurs in ≈15% of monochorionic, monozygotic pregnancies. In this condition, placental vascular anastomoses develop such that blood transfers from one twin (donor) to the other (recipient). The donor twin develops anemia, hypovolemia, and growth restriction, while the recipient develops polycythemia, hypervolemia, cardiac hypertrophy, and congestive heart failure.
