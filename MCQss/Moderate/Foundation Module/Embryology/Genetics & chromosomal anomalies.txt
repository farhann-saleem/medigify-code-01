Question: A short stature obese girl presented with complaints of seizures and ataxic movements of limbs. What is the most probable diagnosis?
A. Angelman syndrome
B. Cri-du-chat syndrome
C. Miller-Dieker syndrome
D. Prader-Willi syndrome
E. Williams syndrome
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
• Correct (A): Angelman syndrome commonly presents with seizures, ataxic movements of limbs, intellectual disability, and characteristic neurologic dysfunction.
• Incorrect (B): Cri-du-chat syndrome primarily presents with a cat-like cry and microcephaly rather than ataxia with seizures.
• Incorrect (C): Miller-Dieker syndrome is dominated by lissencephaly and severe developmental delay, not obesity.
• Incorrect (D): Prader-Willi syndrome causes obesity and short stature but classically features hyperphagia rather than ataxic limb movements.
• Incorrect (E): Williams syndrome presents with elfin facies and supravalvular aortic stenosis, not seizures with ataxia.

Question: Concentration of alpha fetoprotein less than normal in the amniotic fluid may indicate:
A. colorectal carcinoma
B. down syndrome
C. hepatoblastoma
D. neural tube defect
E. sacrococcygeal teratoma
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
• Correct (B): Low AFP is associated with Down syndrome due to decreased fetal protein production.
• Incorrect (A): Colorectal carcinoma is unrelated to amniotic AFP levels.
• Incorrect (C): Hepatoblastoma increases AFP, not decreases it.
• Incorrect (D): Neural tube defects increase AFP, not decrease it.
• Incorrect (E): Sacrococcygeal teratoma increases AFP due to tumor secretion.
