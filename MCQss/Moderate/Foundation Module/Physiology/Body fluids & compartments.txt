
Question 15
Question: In a hypotonic extracellular solution, red blood cells swell because:
A. Active Na⁺ transport increases
B. K⁺ leaves the cell
C. Na⁺ enters through ion channels
D. Osmotic water enters the cell
E. Water is actively pumped into the cell
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): In a hypotonic solution, osmotic water enters the cell, causing swelling.
Incorrect (A): Increased Na⁺ transport is not the cause.
Incorrect (B): K⁺ loss does not explain osmotic swelling.
Incorrect (C): Na⁺ entry is not the primary mechanism.
Incorrect (E): Water is never actively pumped.

Question 19
Question: During osmotic swelling of a cell, water movement occurs primarily through:
A. Active transport
B. Facilitated diffusion
C. Osmosis through aquaporins and the lipid bilayer
D. Primary active transport
E. Secondary active transport
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Water moves by osmosis through aquaporins and the lipid bilayer during cell swelling.
Incorrect (A): Active transport does not move water.
Incorrect (B): Water movement is classified as osmosis rather than facilitated diffusion.
Incorrect (D): Primary active transport is unrelated to water transport.
Incorrect (E): Secondary active transport does not directly transport water.

Question 20
Question: Which of the following best explains why water moves across a membrane during osmosis?
A. Active transport
B. Bulk flow
C. Diffusion down an osmotic gradient through a semipermeable membrane
D. Facilitated diffusion by ATP-dependent carriers
E. Vesicular transport
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Osmosis is the diffusion down an osmotic gradient through a semipermeable membrane.
Incorrect (A): Water movement during osmosis requires no ATP.
Incorrect (B): Bulk flow is pressure driven, not osmotic.
Incorrect (D): There are no ATP-dependent carriers for osmotic water movement.
Incorrect (E): Vesicular transport does not mediate osmosis.
