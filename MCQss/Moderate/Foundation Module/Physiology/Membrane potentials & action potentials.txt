### Question 2
A researcher measures equilibrium potentials in a neuron. If intracellular K+ is 140 mM and extracellular K+ is 4 mM at 37°C, which statement best explains the significance of the calculated Nernst potential for K+?
A. It indicates the voltage at which voltage-gated Na+ channels open
B. It indicates the voltage at which voltage-gated K+ channels close
C. It is the potential at which electrical and chemical gradients for K+ are equal and opposite
D. It represents the maximum depolarization achievable during a typical neuronal action potential
E. It represents the membrane potential when neuronal Na+ permeability equals K+ permeability
**Correct Option: C**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (C)**: The Nernst potential determines the exact thermodynamic equilibrium point where the electrical driving force balances the chemical concentration force so it is the potential at which electrical and chemical gradients for K+ are equal and opposite.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: The opening threshold of fast ion gates is a structural voltage-sensor attribute, not a value that indicates the voltage at which voltage-gated Na+ channels open.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Deactivation kinetics of potassium gates depend on absolute membrane voltage decay, not a value that indicates the voltage at which voltage-gated K+ channels close.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: The positive peak overshoot of an active impulse approaches the sodium equilibrium potential instead, so it represents the maximum depolarization achievable during a typical neuronal action potential is false.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Equipotent permeability shifts the overall membrane voltage near 0\text{ mV} via Goldman dynamics, so it represents the membrane potential when neuronal Na+ permeability equals K+ permeability is incorrect.

### Question 6
A nerve exposed to a local anesthetic loses the ability to conduct impulses. According to its physiological mechanism, which process is primarily inhibited?
A. Ca2+ influx through presynaptic terminals
B. K+ diffusion through open leak channels
C. K+ efflux through voltage-gated channels
D. Na+ diffusion through voltage-gated channels
E. Na+-K+ ATPase active transport activity
**Correct Option: D**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (D)**: Local anesthetics reversibly bind and occlude the internal pores of active voltage-gated sodium channels, preventing Na+ diffusion through voltage-gated channels and blocking action potential propagation.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: Synaptic transmitter release mechanisms at the terminal bouton depend on Ca2+ influx through presynaptic terminals, which is not the primary site of local anesthetic nerve blocks.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Standard resting background polarization is maintained via K+ diffusion through open leak channels, which remains unaffected by clinical doses of local anesthetics.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Repolarization profiles during an active spike require K+ efflux through voltage-gated channels, which is not the target of local anesthetic blockade.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Long-term ionic gradient maintenance via metabolic fuel usage relies on Na+-K+ ATPase active transport activity, which is not inhibited by these agents.

### Question 8
In an experiment, intracellular Na+ concentration is gradually increased without changing extracellular concentrations. Which of the following will occur according to the Nernst relationship?
A. K+ equilibrium potential becomes more negative
B. Na+ equilibrium potential becomes less positive
C. Na+ equilibrium potential becomes more positive
D. Resting membrane potential becomes independent of Na+
E. Resting membrane potential becomes independent of K+
**Correct Option: B**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (B)**: Increasing intracellular sodium concentration shrinks the concentration ratio (\text{Na}^+*{\text{out}} / \text{Na}^+*{\text{in}}) across the membrane, which mathematically dictates that the Na+ equilibrium potential becomes less positive.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: Modifying sodium concentrations has no chemical impact on potassium ratios, meaning the K+ equilibrium potential becomes more negative is false.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Reducing the transmembrane concentration gradient causes a drop in equilibrium voltage, so the Na+ equilibrium potential becomes more positive is incorrect.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: Membrane voltage profiles still incorporate relative sodium permeabilities via Goldman parameters, so resting membrane potential becomes independent of Na+ is false.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Background potassium conductance continues to exert primary control over the resting state, so resting membrane potential becomes independent of K+ is incorrect.

### Question 14

 * **Incorrect (B)**: Reducing external sodium lowers its inward driving force, which would cause slight hyperpolarization rather than a marked neuronal depolarization.
 * **Incorrect (C)**: Because resting sodium conductance is already extremely low, reducing the sodium gradient produces a negligible shift rather than a marked neuronal hyperpolarization.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: Unchanging potassium dynamics ensure the resting potential remains stable, ruling out a moderate oscillating depolarization.

### Question 20

A. Increased neurotransmitter release at synapsing terminals
B. Increased absolute refractory period of axonal membranes
C. Loss of saltatory conduction along myelinated axons
D. Reduced Na+ channel activation at the postsynaptic membrane
E. Reduced K+ channel activation at the postsynaptic membrane
**Correct Option: C**
#### Explanation:
 * **Correct (C)**: Demyelination forces the action potential to propagate continuously along the axon rather than jumping between nodes, causing a loss of saltatory conduction along myelinated axons that slows or blocks nerve impulses.
 * **Incorrect (A)**: Demyelination disrupts action potential propagation along the axon, which decreases rather than causing an increased neurotransmitter release at synapsing terminals.
 * **Incorrect (B)**: Structural demyelination slows conduction velocity and causes conduction block, rather than causing an increased absolute refractory period of axonal membranes.
 * **Incorrect (D)**: This pathology affects axonal propagation in the nerve fiber, not a reduced Na+ channel activation at the postsynaptic membrane of the neuromuscular junction.
 * **Incorrect (E)**: The symptoms of Guillain-Barré syndrome are caused by axonal conduction failure, not a reduced K+ channel activation at the postsynaptic membrane.

Question 24: A nerve fiber is stimulated repeatedly at very high frequency but fails to generate another action potential immediately after the first. Which physiological period explains this:
A. absolute refractory period
B. accommodation period
C. latent period
D. relative refractory period
E. supranormal period
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
 * Correct (A): The absolute refractory period is the time during which a second action potential cannot be elicited, no matter how strong the stimulus, because voltage-gated Na+ channels are inactivated.
 * Incorrect (B): The accommodation period describes the slow adjustment of the threshold during subthreshold depolarization, not the complete loss of excitability post-excitation.
 * Incorrect (C): The latent period is the brief delay between the application of a stimulus and the first measurable sign of a response.
 * Incorrect (D): The relative refractory period allows a second action potential to be generated, but it requires a stronger-than-normal stimulus due to partial channel recovery.
 * Incorrect (E): The supranormal period is a transient phase of hyper-excitability where a weaker stimulus can trigger an action potential, the exact opposite of failing to fire.

A. increased chloride permeability
B. opening of fast Na+ channels
C. prolonged opening of slow Ca2+–Na+ channels
D. rapid efflux of K+ ions
E. sustained closure of Ca2+ channels
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
 * Correct (C): The plateau phase (Phase 2) is maintained by a balance of currents, sustained primarily by the prolonged opening of slow Ca2+–Na+ channels (L-type calcium channels) allowing calcium influx.
 * Incorrect (A): An increased chloride permeability contributes to the brief, early repolarization notch (Phase 1), not the sustained plateau.
 * Incorrect (B): The opening of fast Na+ channels is responsible for the rapid upstroke (Phase 0) of the cardiac action potential, which precedes the plateau.
 * Incorrect (D): A rapid efflux of K+ ions would cause rapid repolarization of the membrane, which opposes the maintenance of a stable plateau phase.
 * Incorrect (E): A sustained closure of Ca2+ channels would completely abolish the calcium influx needed to offset potassium efflux, preventing a plateau from forming.

A. absence of T-tubules
B. increased K+ efflux

 * Incorrect (B): An increased K+ efflux speeds up repolarization, which would shorten the action potential duration and shorten the refractory period.
 * Incorrect (D): Fast Na+ channels activate instantly during Phase 0; their slow inactivation gate recovery, not slow activation, dictates the refractory period.
 * Incorrect (E): A voltage-gated Cl– influx is a minor, transient event in early repolarization and does not drive the prolonged cardiac refractory state.

Question 35: In Multiple sclerosis, conduction of nerve impulses is slowed primarily because:
A. increased myelin thickness
B. increased Na+ channel density

Correct Option: C
Explanation:

 * Incorrect (B): An increased Na+ channel density occurs downstream as a compensatory mechanism in demyelinated zones to prevent total conduction block, not the cause of the slowing.
 * Incorrect (D): While downstream signaling is altered, conduction along the nerve fiber axon is slowed primarily due to the mechanical loss of myelin insulation rather than reduced synaptic transmission.
 * Incorrect (E): The breakdown of myelin causes current leak that prevents the axon from reaching threshold easily, rather than inducing a state of sustained hyperpolarization of axons.

Question 40: A patient with Multiple sclerosis develops muscle weakness despite normal neuromuscular junction function. The most likely cause is:

D. reduced conduction velocity in motor pathways

Correct Option: D
Explanation:

 * Incorrect (B): The intracellular machinery of the muscle, including the failure of Ca2+ binding to troponin, remains structurally intact and functional in patients with Multiple sclerosis.
 * Incorrect (C): An increased acetylcholine breakdown is a synaptic clearance pathology that does not occur as a consequence of central demyelinating lesions.
 * Incorrect (E): An upregulation of acetylcholinesterase activity is not part of the MS disease process, as the pathological changes are confined to the central oligodendrocytes and myelin sheaths.
