Question: The subcapsular (marginal) sinus of lymph nodes would normally contain:
A. b cells
B. dendritic cells
C. erythrocytes
D. plasma cells
E. reticular cells
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The subcapsular (marginal) sinus receives lymph from afferent vessels, making it the primary site where antigen-presenting dendritic cells enter the lymph node.
Incorrect (A): These are predominantly concentrated within the cortical lymphoid follicles rather than free-floating within the subcapsular space.
Incorrect (C): These are red blood cells restricted to the systemic vasculature and would only appear in the lymph node sinuses under pathological conditions like hemorrhage.
Incorrect (D): These mature antibody-producing cells reside and function in the medullary cords of the lymph node.
Incorrect (E): These cells form the structural meshwork spanning the sinus and parenchymal compartments but do not exist as free-floating elements within the fluid stream.

Question: After their maturation in the thymus and release into the circulation, T lymphocytes migrate preferentially to which of the following sites:
A. cortical lymphoid nodules of lymph nodes
B. germinal centers of splenic white pulp
C. lymphoid nodules of palatine tonsils

Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The paracortex of lymph nodes is specifically designated as the T-cell-dependent zone where mature naive T lymphocytes homing from the blood localize.
Incorrect (A): These microenvironments represent the B-cell-dependent zones of the lymph node where B cells undergo antigen activation.
Incorrect (B): These are sites of active B-cell proliferation, somatic hypermutation, and selection within the splenic tissue.
Incorrect (C): These structures serve as B-cell compartments within the mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue of the oropharynx.
Incorrect (D): This region is populated primarily by plasma cells, macrophages, and B lymphocytes embedded within reticular cords.

Question: In which of the following sites do lymphocytes become immunocompetent:
A. germinal centers of secondary nodules
B. paracortex of peripheral lymph nodes
C. periarteriolar lymphoid sheaths of the spleen
D. thymic cortex of the primary lymphoid organ
E. white pulp of the secondary splenic tissue
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): Initial T-cell selection and maturation into immunocompetent cells take place strictly within the thymic cortex of the primary lymphoid organ.
Incorrect (A): These zones are involved in secondary differentiation, affinity maturation, and memory cell generation after antigen exposure, not initial immunocompetence.
Incorrect (B): This region houses already immunocompetent T cells that are monitoring for antigens within the peripheral lymph node network.
Incorrect (C): This splenic region is a secondary lymphoid zone where mature, fully immunocompetent T cells localize around central arteries.
Incorrect (E): This represents secondary lymphoid tissue where mature, immunocompetent cells mount adaptive immune responses rather than undergo primary development.

Question 6
In which of the following sites do lymphocytes become immunocompetent:
A. germinal center of secondary lymphoid nodules
B. paracortex region of lymph nodes
C. splenic white pulp marginal zone
D. thymic cortex parenchymal layer
E. trabecular sinuses of lymph nodes
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): thymic cortex parenchymal layer is the precise anatomical site where immature T-lymphocyte progenitors undergo positive selection to become functionally immunocompetent.
Incorrect (A): germinal center of secondary lymphoid nodules is a site of mature B-cell proliferation and affinity maturation during an active immune response, not initial immunocompetence.
Incorrect (B): paracortex region of lymph nodes houses already mature, immunocompetent T cells awaiting antigen activation.
Incorrect (C): splenic white pulp marginal zone is a peripheral region where mature lymphocytes encounter blood-borne pathogens.
Incorrect (E): trabecular sinuses of lymph nodes function purely as conduits for the flow of lymph through the node.
