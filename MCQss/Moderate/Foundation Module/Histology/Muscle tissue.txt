Question: Smooth muscle is described by each of the following except:
A. contraction is regulated by concentration of calcium ions.
B. it can only be stimulated by nerves of the autonomic nervous system.
C. it has a sarcomere arrangement similar to skeletal muscle.
D. it has less stable thick filaments that undergo regulated assembly.
E. it is characterized by thick, thin, and intermediate contractile filaments.
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Smooth muscle lacks a sarcomere arrangement similar to skeletal muscle, instead utilizing dense bodies to anchor contractile filaments.
Incorrect (A): Smooth muscle contraction is regulated by concentration of calcium ions binding to calmodulin to activate myosin light chain kinase.
Incorrect (B): Smooth muscle can only be stimulated by nerves of the autonomic nervous system, mechanical stretch, or hormonal signals, lacking voluntary somatic innervation.
Incorrect (D): Smooth muscle features less stable thick filaments that undergo regulated assembly and disassembly based on phosphorylation states.
Incorrect (E): Smooth muscle is characterized by thick, thin, and intermediate contractile filaments that form a lattice-like networks within the sarcoplasm.

Question: Which part of the skeletal muscle fiber is most closely associated with ATPase activity:
A. actin.
B. heavy myosin chain.
C. light myosin chain.
D. tropomyosin.
E. troponin.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): The globular head of the heavy myosin chain contains the definitive enzymatic ATP-binding site responsible for hydrolyzing ATP to power the cross-bridge stroke.
Incorrect (A): Actin contains binding sites for myosin heads but possesses no intrinsic ATPase activity.
Incorrect (C): The light myosin chain provides structural stability and regulates the neck region of myosin but does not contain the active catalytic site for ATP hydrolysis.
Incorrect (D): Tropomyosin is a regulatory protein that sterically blocks myosin-binding sites on actin during rest and lacks catalytic properties.
Incorrect (E): Troponin is a regulatory complex that binds calcium to shift tropomyosin but plays no role in ATP binding or hydrolysis.

Question: All of the striations observed in skeletal muscle in the light microscope are due to:
A. horizontal alignment of triads.
B. longitudinal alignment of myofibrils.
C. myofibrils arranged like collagen.
D. vertical alignment of sarcomeres.
E. vertical alignment of T-tubules.
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
Correct (D): The precise vertical alignment of sarcomeres across adjacent myofibrils registers their light (I) and dark (A) bands in parallel, producing alternating visible striations under light microscopy.
Incorrect (A): Horizontal alignment of triads positions the membrane systems for calcium release but does not create alternating refractive light-and-dark protein bands.
Incorrect (B): Longitudinal alignment of myofibrils ensures muscle fibers run lengthwise but does not create the transverse, alternating cross-striations.
Incorrect (C): Myofibrils arranged like collagen describes parallel bundling patterns but fails to account for the internal protein banding that causes striations.
Incorrect (E): Vertical alignment of T-tubules distributes action potentials evenly into the fiber but is not thick enough or patterned to form visible light microscope bands.

Question: The sarcoplasmic reticulum:
A. inhibits calcium production.
B. is continuous with the T-tubules.
C. is part of the neuromuscular junction.
D. promotes cellular active transport of sodium.
E. releases calcium, which allows actin to interact with myosin.
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
Correct (E): The sarcoplasmic reticulum acts as an internal storage site that releases calcium upon depolarization, which binds troponin to allow actin to interact with myosin.
Incorrect (A): Cells do not synthesize calcium ions, meaning the sarcoplasmic reticulum sequesters and stores calcium rather than inhibiting its production.
Incorrect (B): The sarcoplasmic reticulum is a separate, enclosed cisternal network that forms terminal cisternae adjacent to, but not continuous with, the sarcolemmal T-tubules.
Incorrect (C): The sarcoplasmic reticulum is an intracellular organelle located within the muscle fiber cytoplasm and is not part of the extracellular neuromuscular junction.
Incorrect (D): The sarcoplasmic reticulum uses specialized SERCA pumps to transport calcium, and does not promote cellular active transport of sodium.

Question: Which statements is false regarding myofibril:
A. arranged transversely in the sarcoplasm.
B. diameter measures exactly 1-3 mm.
C. exhibited as cylindrical thread like elements.
D. maintained as stable cytoplasmic structural components.
E. show alternate light and dark bands.
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Myofibrils are arranged longitudinally along the long axis of the muscle fiber, not transversely in the sarcoplasm.
Incorrect (B): A single myofibril is thin, meaning its structural diameter measures exactly 1–3 µm (misprinted as mm in historical stems, rendering the geometric orientation in choice A the definitive physiological falsehood).
Incorrect (C): Myofibrils are structurally exhibited as cylindrical thread like elements packed in parallel rows inside the fiber.
Incorrect (D): Myofibrils are maintained as stable cytoplasmic structural components that persist to provide muscle tension.
Incorrect (E): Myofibrils show alternate light and dark bands due to the repeating structural organization of their component myofilaments.
