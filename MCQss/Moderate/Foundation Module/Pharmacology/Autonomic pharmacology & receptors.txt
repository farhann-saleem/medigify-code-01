Question: Tetrodotoxin acts on: 
Options: 
a) Nerve axon
 b) Receptor at adrenergic junction 
c) Ganglionic nicotinic receptor 
d) Neuromuscular end plates 
Correct Option: Ans. Nerve axon 
Explanation: Tetrodotoxin is a potent neurotoxin that acts specifically on the nerve axon by blocking the voltage-gated sodium channels. Unlike neuromuscular blockers that target the junction or end plate, tetrodotoxin prevents the transmission of action potentials along the entire nerve fiber.

Question: A drug that binds to the inactive state of a receptor & decreases constitutive activity is known as an:
 A. allosteric agonist.
 B. antagonist.
 C. inverse agonist.
 D. partial agonist.
 E. physiological agonist.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
​Correct (C): An inverse agonist binds selectively to the inactive conformation of a receptor, shifting the equilibrium away from the active state to suppress baseline, ligand-independent constitutive activity.
​Incorrect (A): An allosteric agonist binds to a separate, non-orthosteric site on the receptor structure to augment or directly activate downstream signaling cascades rather than silencing baseline activity.
​Incorrect (B): An antagonist blocks or dampens a receptor response by preventing agonist binding without altering the underlying constitutive activity of the receptor system in the absence of an agonist.
​Incorrect (D): A partial agonist binds to and activates the orthosteric site but produces a submaximal biological response even at full receptor saturation, never depressing baseline activity.
​Incorrect (E): A physiological agonist is an endogenous substance that interacts with distinct, non-overlapping receptors to produce an opposing functional change in a target tissue or organ system.

Question: Type of agonist which acts as antagonist in presence of full agonist is known as a:
 A. chemical antagonist.
 B. full agonist.
 C. inverse agonist.
 D. partial agonist.
 E. physiological antagonist.
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
​Correct (D): A partial agonist acts as an antagonist in the presence of a full agonist because it competes for the same receptor binding sites but possesses lower intrinsic efficacy, effectively displacing the more potent agent and lowering the overall response.
​Incorrect (A): A chemical antagonist blocks drug action by interacting directly with the drug molecule in solution to neutralize or sequester it, independent of any receptor binding site.
​Incorrect (B): A full agonist induces the maximal possible biological response from a receptor population, meaning it never dampens or counteracts signaling when combined with other active ligands.
​Incorrect (C): An inverse agonist decreases the baseline constitutive activity of an unoccupied receptor system, operating independently of the competitive displacement kinetics unique to partial agonists.
​Incorrect (E): A physiological antagonist produces a functional block by activating a completely separate receptor network that triggers opposite physiological pathways in the target tissue.
Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Foundation, Autonomic Pharmacology & Receptors

Question: Type of agonist which decreases or abolishes constitutive activity of receptor is an:
 A. allosteric agonist.
 B. full agonist.
 C. inverse agonist.
 D. partial agonist.
 E. physiological agonist.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
​Correct (C): An inverse agonist stabilizes the inactive conformation of a receptor population, which directly decreases or abolishes the baseline constitutive activity observed in unoccupied receptors.
​Incorrect (A): An allosteric agonist interacts with a secondary site to increase receptor signaling, acting as a positive modulator rather than suppressing intrinsic baseline activity.
​Incorrect (B): A full agonist drives the conformational equilibrium completely toward the active state, generating a maximal biological response that elevates signaling above constitutive levels.
​Incorrect (D): A partial agonist acts on the active conformation to produce a submaximal tissue response, failing to reduce baseline signaling below its constitutive threshold.
​Incorrect (E): A physiological agonist stimulates distinct receptor systems to produce a positive functional change, rather than directly suppressing the baseline activity of a target receptor.

Question: Sugammadex is a new drug that reverses the action of rocuronium and certain other non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents. It interacts directly with the rocuronium molecule and not at the receptor. Which term best describes sugammadex:
 A. Chemical antagonist
 B. Noncompetitive antagonist
 C. Partial agonist
 D. Pharmacologic antagonist
 E. Physiologic antagonist
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
​Correct (A): Sugammadex acts as a chemical antagonist because it encapsulates and neutralizes the rocuronium molecule directly in the plasma through chemical binding, completely independent of any cellular receptor interaction.
​Incorrect (B): A noncompetitive antagonist binds to an allosteric or orthosteric receptor site to block agonist signaling, rather than neutralizing the free drug molecule in solution.
​Incorrect (C): A partial agonist binds directly to a receptor’s active site to produce a submaximal biological response, which does not match the receptor-independent neutralizing action seen here.
​Incorrect (D): A pharmacologic antagonist must bind directly to the same receptor structure as the agonist to prevent its activation, which sugammadex does not do.
​Incorrect (E): A physiologic antagonist activates a completely distinct receptor pathway to generate an opposing physiological effect, rather than physically binding to the agonist.
Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Foundation, Autonomic Pharmacology & Receptors

Question: Which of the following statements about receptors is not true:
 A. It determines the quantitative relations between dose or concentration of drug and pharmacologic effects.
 B. It acts as an inert binding site with no downstream signaling properties.
 C. It is responsible for the selectivity of drug action.
 D. It mediates the actions of pharmacological agonists.
 E. It mediates the actions of pharmacological antagonists.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
​Correct (B): This statement is not true because functional receptors are dynamic signaling proteins that convert ligand binding into intracellular biochemical cascades, unlike inert binding sites like plasma albumin.
​Incorrect (A): Receptors directly dictate the quantitative relationship between dose and effect, as the total response is proportional to the number of ligand-receptor complexes formed.
​Incorrect (C): The unique structural configuration of a receptor binding pocket determines the selectivity of drug action, ensuring only specific chemical shapes can fit and activate the pathway.
​Incorrect (D): Receptors are the essential molecular targets that mediate the actions of pharmacological agonists, translating ligand binding into a functional cellular response.
​Incorrect (E): Receptors mediate the actions of pharmacological antagonists by allowing these blockers to bind and prevent native agonists from triggering downstream signaling pathways.
Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Foundation, Autonomic Pharmacology & Receptors

Question: What is meant by constitutive activity of a receptor:
 A. Activity in absence of ligand
 B. Activity in presence of antagonist
 C. Activity in presence of full agonist
 D. Activity in presence of inverse agonist
 E. Activity in presence of partial agonist
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
​Correct (A): Constitutive activity refers to the baseline level of signaling a receptor system produces in the absence of ligand, driven by a fraction of receptors spontaneously adopting the active conformation.
​Incorrect (B): Activity measured in the presence of an antagonist matches this baseline level because neutral antagonists occupy the site without changing the underlying equilibrium of the unoccupied receptor.
​Incorrect (C): Activity in the presence of a full agonist represents the maximal stimulated signaling state of the receptor system, which is significantly higher than baseline constitutive activity.
​Incorrect (D): Activity in the presence of an inverse agonist drops below the baseline constitutive level because the drug actively forces the receptor population into its inactive conformation.
​Incorrect (E): Activity in the presence of a partial agonist reflects a submaximal stimulated signaling state, rather than the true baseline activity of the unoccupied receptor.
 Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Foundation, Autonomic Pharmacology & Receptors

Question: Partial agonist produces less than full effect because:
 A. it binds only to activated state of receptor.
 B. it binds only to non-activated state of receptor.
 C. it binds to activated & non-activated states of receptor.
 D. it exhibits only constitutive activity.
 E. it has low affinity for receptor.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
​Correct (C): A partial agonist produces less than full effect because it binds to activated & non-activated states of receptor, keeping a portion of the receptor population locked in the inactive conformation and limiting overall signaling efficacy.
​Incorrect (A): If a ligand bound only to the activated conformation, it would function as a full agonist by shifting the entire receptor pool into the active state to produce a maximal biological response.
​Incorrect (B): Binding only to the non-activated state describes the mechanism of an inverse agonist, which suppresses baseline signaling rather than partially activating the pathway.
​Incorrect (D): Constitutive activity is the baseline signaling of an unoccupied receptor; a partial agonist actively modifies this baseline by triggering a weak, submaximal response.
​Incorrect (E): Receptors can bind partial agonists with high affinity; submaximal efficacy is caused by a lower ability to induce the fully active receptor conformation, not a weak binding strength.
 Tags: Moderate, Pharmacology, Foundation, Autonomic Pharmacology & Receptors
