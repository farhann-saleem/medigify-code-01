A. are formed in the intervertebral foramen. 
B. comprise 25 pairs. 
C. distribute to the lower limb through dorsal rami. 
D. have dorsal and ventral rami with a peripheral ganglion on the dorsal ramus. 
E. contain purely sensory fibers throughout their entire course. 
Correct Option: A 
Explanation:
* Correct (A): Spinal nerves are formed in the intervertebral foramen by the union of the anterior (ventral) and posterior (dorsal) nerve roots.
* Incorrect (B): They comprise 31 pairs of nerves rather than 25 pairs.
* Incorrect (C): They distribute to the lower limb exclusively through ventral rami that contribute to the lumbar and sacral plexuses.
* Incorrect (D): They split into rami after formation, and the peripheral ganglion (spinal ganglion) is located on the dorsal root, not the dorsal ramus.
* Incorrect (E): They contain purely mixed fibers (both sensory and motor) immediately after the junction of the roots.

Question: Regarding the sympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system: 
A. acetylcholine is released by some sympathetic postganglionic fibers. 
B. most sympathetic preganglionic nerve fibers secrete noradrenaline. 
C. sympathetic postganglionic cell bodies are found in spinal segments T1–L2. 
D. the sympathetic chain extends from the thoracic to the lumbar region only. 
E. all preganglionic fibers bypass the sympathetic trunk. 
Correct Option: A 
Explanation:
* Correct (A): Acetylcholine is released by some sympathetic postganglionic fibers, specifically those innervating eccrine sweat glands.
* Incorrect (B): Most sympathetic preganglionic nerve fibers secrete acetylcholine, not noradrenaline, at their ganglionic synapses.
* Incorrect (C): Sympathetic postganglionic cell bodies are found in paravertebral and prevertebral ganglia, whereas sympathetic preganglionic cell bodies reside in spinal segments T1–L2.
* Incorrect (D): The sympathetic chain extends from the cervical region down to the coccygeal region, far exceeding just the thoracic and lumbar regions.
* Incorrect (E): Many preganglionic fibers synapse within the paravertebral ganglia of the sympathetic trunk instead of bypassing it.

Question: Which of the following statements regarding postganglionic fibers of parasympathetic ganglia is INCORRECT? 
A. From the ciliary ganglion they supply the constrictor pupillae muscle. 
B. From the otic ganglion they supply the minor salivary glands. 
C. From the pterygopalatine ganglion they supply the lacrimal gland. 
D. From the submandibular ganglion they supply the sublingual gland. 
E. From the submandibular ganglion they supply the submandibular gland. 
Correct Option: B 
Explanation:
* Correct (B): Postganglionic fibers from the otic ganglion supply the parotid salivary gland, whereas minor salivary glands are primarily supplied by postganglionic fibers from the pterygopalatine and submandibular ganglia.
* Incorrect (A): Postganglionic fibers from the ciliary ganglion correctly supply the constrictor pupillae muscle and the ciliary muscle.
* Incorrect (C): Postganglionic fibers from the pterygopalatine ganglion correctly travel via branches of the maxillary and ophthalmic nerves to supply the lacrimal gland.
* Incorrect (D): Postganglionic fibers from the submandibular ganglion correctly project forward to supply the adjacent sublingual gland.
* Incorrect (E): Postganglionic fibers from the submandibular ganglion correctly supply the submandibular gland directly.

Question: Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding spinal nerves? 
A. Dorsal rami supply the posterior part of the body. 
B. Thoracic spinal nerves are typical spinal nerves. 
C. Ventral rami supply the anterolateral body wall. 
D. Ventral rami except T1–T12 form plexuses. E. Ventral rami except T6–L3 form plexuses. Correct Option: E 
Explanation:
* Correct (E): The statement that ventral rami except T6–L3 form plexuses is false because the actual segment range spared from plexus formation is T1–T12 (or T2–T11), which run directly as intercostal nerves.
* Incorrect (A): Dorsal rami correctly supply the intrinsic muscles of the back and the overlying skin on the posterior part of the body.
* Incorrect (B): Thoracic spinal nerves are correctly considered typical spinal nerves because they maintain a segmental distribution without joining major somatic plexuses.
* Incorrect (C): Ventral rami correctly supply the skin and muscles of the anterolateral body wall and limbs.
* Incorrect (D): Ventral rami except T1–T12 correctly form complex nerve networks known as plexuses (cervical, brachial, lumbar, and sacral).

Question: Collateral sprouting occurs in:
A. damaged axonal shafts
B. damaged neuronal somas
C. intact glial processes
D. retracting growth cones
E. undamaged neighboring axons
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
* Correct (E): Collateral sprouting is a compensatory process where undamaged neighboring axons extend new terminal branches to reinnervate synaptic sites vacated by denervation.
* Incorrect (A): Damaged axonal shafts undergo Wallerian degeneration distally and regenerative sprouting proximally, which is distinct from collateral sprouting.
* Incorrect (B): Damaged neuronal somas undergo chromatolysis and metabolic shifts toward repair rather than direct collateral branch emission.
* Incorrect (C): Intact glial processes undergo reactive gliosis or hypertrophy following injury instead of extending neuronal collateral sprouts.
* Incorrect (D): Retracting growth cones represent a failure or cessation of axon guidance and elongation, opposite to active collateral sprouting.

Question: Major impediment to regeneration in CNS is:
A. astrocytic scar formation
B. axonal process fragmentation
C. collagenous matrix deposition
D. microglial phagocytic activation
E. oligodendrocyte debris clearance Correct Option: A
Explanation:
* Correct (A): Astrocytic scar formation produces a dense physical barrier and secretes inhibitory chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans that robustly arrest axon regeneration in the CNS.
* Incorrect (B): Axonal process fragmentation is a generic initial destructive consequence of injury (Wallerian degeneration) rather than the primary mechanism blocking subsequent repair.
* Incorrect (C): Collagenous matrix deposition is characteristic of fibrotic scar tissue in peripheral injuries and is not the defining impediment within the central neural parenchyma.
* Incorrect (D): Microglial phagocytic activation helps clear debris, which theoretically supports regeneration rather than acting as its primary impediment.
* Incorrect (E): Poor or delayed oligodendrocyte debris clearance leaves inhibitory myelin molecules behind, meaning the lack of swift clearance—rather than active clearance itself—hinders regeneration.
