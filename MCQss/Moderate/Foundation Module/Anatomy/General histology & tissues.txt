Question: Fenestrated capillaries are found in following places EXCEPT: 
A. cerebral cortex 
B. ciliary body of eye 
C. endocrine glands 
D. intestinal mucosa 
E. renal glomerulus 
Correct Option: A 
Explanation:
* Correct (A): The cerebral cortex contains continuous capillaries with sealed tight junctions that structurally constitute the blood-brain barrier, making it the exception.
* Incorrect (B): The ciliary body of eye utilizes fenestrated capillaries to facilitate the rapid filtration of fluid required to produce aqueous humor.
* Incorrect (C): Endocrine glands require fenestrated capillaries to allow large peptide hormones to directly and rapidly enter the systemic bloodstream.
* Incorrect (D): The intestinal mucosa contains fenestrated capillaries designed for the rapid absorption of digested nutrients from the gut lumen.
* Incorrect (E): The renal glomerulus is fundamentally composed of specialized fenestrated capillaries that allow high-volume plasma ultrafiltration.

Question: The thoroughfare channel ends at the: 
A. lymphatic vessel 
B. metarteriole 
C. muscular arteriole 
D. true capillary bed 
E. venule 
Correct Option: E 
Explanation:
* Correct (E): The thoroughfare channel represents the distal, non-contractile segment of a vascular shunt that empties directly into the systemic venule.
* Incorrect (A): A lymphatic vessel belongs to an entirely separate drainage system and does not serve as the termination point for structural blood capillary shunts.
* Incorrect (B): A metarteriole is the proximal, contractile segment of the shunt pathway that precedes and gives rise to the thoroughfare channel.
* Incorrect (C): A muscular arteriole is an upstream resistance vessel that branches into metarterioles, far before the thoroughfare channel begins.
* Incorrect (D): The true capillary bed branches laterally away from the metarteriole and thoroughfare channel complex rather than acting as its terminal endpoint.

Question: Multiple sclerosis is a disease characterized by preferential damage to myelin that becomes detached from axons in the central nervous system. Which associated cells are involved? 
A. Astrocytes 
B. Microglia 
C. Oligodendrocytes 
D. Satellite cells 
E. Schwann cells 
Correct Option: C 
Explanation:
* Correct (C): Oligodendrocytes are the cells responsible for synthesizing and maintaining myelin within the central nervous system, making them the primary target of autoimmune destruction in multiple sclerosis.
* Incorrect (A): Astrocytes provide structural and metabolic support to central neurons but do not form the central myelin sheaths.
* Incorrect (B): Microglia act as the resident macrophages of the central nervous system and clear debris during demyelination, but they are not the cells that produce myelin.
* Incorrect (D): Satellite cells encapsulate neuron cell bodies within peripheral sensory and autonomic ganglia.
* Incorrect (E): Schwann cells form myelin sheaths exclusively in the peripheral nervous system, which is spared in multiple sclerosis.

Question: Each of the following is found within the cytoplasm of a nerve cell body EXCEPT: A. axoplasmic vesicles. 
B. lysosomes. 
C. microtubules. 
D. mitochondria. 
E. nissl granules. 
Correct Option: A 
Explanation:
* Correct (A): Axoplasmic vesicles are cargo transport structures or synaptic vesicles concentrated within the axon and axon terminals, rather than being structural components of the nerve cell body per se.
* Incorrect (B): Lysosomes are hydrolytic organelles normally scattered within the cytoplasm of a nerve cell body to manage cellular recycling.
* Incorrect (C): Microtubules form the structural cytoskeletal framework running through the cytoplasm of a nerve cell body to facilitate transport.
* Incorrect (D): Mitochondria are highly abundant within the cytoplasm of a nerve cell body to fulfill the high metabolic energy demands of the neuron.
* Incorrect (E): Nissl granules are dense aggregations of rough endoplasmic reticulum and ribosomes prominent within the cytoplasm of a nerve cell body.

Question: The highest concentration of microtubules occur in which part of neuron:
A. apical dendrite
B. initial segment of axon
C. Nissl granules
D. Node of Ranvier
E. synaptic terminal knob
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
* Correct (B): The initial segment of the axon possesses a dense, highly concentrated bundle of parallel microtubules linked together by molecular cross-bridges to facilitate polarized intracellular transport.
* Incorrect (A): An apical dendrite contains microtubules, but their density and structural arrangement are significantly lower than those found tightly packed in the axon hillock and initial segment.
* Incorrect (C): Nissl granules are aggregates of rough endoplasmic reticulum and free ribosomes, lacking concentrated structural microtubule networks.
* Incorrect (D): The Node of Ranvier is specialized for a high concentration of voltage-gated sodium channels rather than an enriched intracellular concentration of microtubules.
* Incorrect (E): The synaptic terminal knob is predominantly filled with synaptic vesicles and mitochondria rather than a high concentration of structural microtubules.

Question: The node of Ranvier is a region which may be best described as:
A. an area of direct synaptic contact
B. an area of primary sensory input
C. a functional neuromuscular motor endplate D. a naked segment between myelin sheaths E. a region where Schwann cells are in juxtaposition
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
* Correct (E): The Node of Ranvier is accurately described ultrastructurally as the specialized junctional gap where the outer cytoplasmic loops of terminating, adjacent Schwann cells meet in close juxtaposition.
* Incorrect (A): The node is optimized for saltatory action potential propagation and is not an anatomical site dedicated to direct synaptic contact.
* Incorrect (B): The node propagates signals down an axon and does not act as a specialized receptor organelle for primary sensory input.
* Incorrect (C): A neuromuscular motor endplate is a distinct, specialized chemical synapse between a motor neuron and skeletal muscle fiber, not an axonal node.
* Incorrect (D): While myelin is absent, the segment is not entirely "naked" because it is covered by the interdigitating microvilli of the flanking Schwann cells and basal lamina.
Tags: Moderate, Anatomy, Foundation Module, General Histology & Tissues
