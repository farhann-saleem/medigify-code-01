
Question: Oesophageal varices are caused by which one of the following: Options: a) Portal hypertension b) Pulmonary hypertension c) Systemic hypertension d) Enlarged liver pressing on portal vein Correct Option: Ans. Portal hypertension Explanation: Portal hypertension causes blood to back up into portosystemic anastomoses. In the lower esophagus, the high-pressure portal blood diverts into the submucosal veins (left gastric vein to esophageal veins), causing them to dilate into oesophageal varices that are prone to life-threatening rupture. Question: A 25 year old young man presented in emergency with blunt trauma abdomen. He is in shock with blood pressure 70/40; pulse 124/min. On examination, abdomen is tense & tender more on left upper abdomen. Which solid organ is most likely to be injured: Options: a) Liver b) Spleen c) Diaphragm d) Kidney Correct Option: Ans. Spleen Explanation: In cases of blunt trauma to the left upper abdomen, the spleen is the most likely solid organ to rupture. Because its "red pulp" is essentially a blood-filled sponge enclosed in a thin capsule, injury leads to massive internal bleeding, rapid onset of shock, and abdominal tenderness.
