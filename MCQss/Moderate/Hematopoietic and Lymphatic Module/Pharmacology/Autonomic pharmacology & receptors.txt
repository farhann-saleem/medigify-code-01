Question: An elderly man is brought to the emergency room after ingesting a large quantity of prazosin tablets (alpha1 antagonist). Which symptom is most likely to be seen in this patient:
  A. bradycardia.
  B. bronchoconstriction.
  C. decreased gastrointestinal motility.
  D. dilation of blood vessels.
  E. decreased glandular secretion.
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (D): Prazosin blocks alpha1 receptors on vascular smooth muscle, producing dilation of blood vessels.
 •	Incorrect (A): Alpha1 blockade commonly causes reflex tachycardia rather than bradycardia.
 •	Incorrect (B): Bronchoconstriction is not a characteristic effect of alpha1 receptor blockade.
 •	Incorrect (C): Decreased gastrointestinal motility is not the principal consequence of alpha1 antagonism.
 •	Incorrect (E): Glandular secretion is not primarily controlled by alpha1 receptors.

Question: Botulinum toxin impairs all types of cholinergic transmission. If a child presents with ingestion of botulinum toxin, which sign or symptom will confirm the diagnosis of botulinum poisoning:
  A. bronchospasm.
  B. cycloplegia (loss of accommodating power of eye).
  C. diarrhea.
  D. skeletal muscle spasm.
  E. urinary frequency.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Botulinum toxin blocks acetylcholine release, causing cycloplegia (loss of accommodating power of eye) due to parasympathetic paralysis.
 •	Incorrect (A): Cholinergic blockade causes bronchodilation rather than bronchospasm.
 •	Incorrect (C): Botulinum poisoning produces constipation, not diarrhea.
 •	Incorrect (D): Botulinum toxin causes flaccid paralysis rather than skeletal muscle spasm.
 •	Incorrect (E): Urinary retention is expected instead of urinary frequency.

Question: Which physiological change is expected when the sympathetic system is inhibited using a pharmacological agent:
  A. constriction of blood vessels.
  B. decrease in fluid secretions.
  C. increase in blood pressure.
  D. increase in heart rate.
  E. reduction in heart rate.
 Correct Option: E
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (E): Inhibition of sympathetic activity removes cardiac adrenergic stimulation, leading to a reduction in heart rate.
 •	Incorrect (A): Sympathetic inhibition tends to promote vasodilation rather than constriction of blood vessels.
 •	Incorrect (B): Fluid secretions are generally not reduced by sympathetic inhibition.
 •	Incorrect (C): Blood pressure typically falls rather than increases when sympathetic tone is blocked.
 •	Incorrect (D): Sympathetic inhibition decreases rather than increases heart rate.

Question: Which of the following neurotransmitters is released at the SA node of the heart in response to increased heart rate:
  A. acetylcholine.
  B. adrenaline.
  C. dopamine.
  D. noradrenaline.
  E. serotonin.
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (D): Sympathetic nerve endings release noradrenaline at the SA node, increasing the rate of spontaneous depolarization and heart rate.
 •	Incorrect (A): Acetylcholine decreases SA node firing and slows heart rate.
 •	Incorrect (B): Adrenaline is primarily released from the adrenal medulla rather than directly from cardiac sympathetic nerve terminals.
 •	Incorrect (C): Dopamine is not the principal neurotransmitter regulating SA node rate.
 •	Incorrect (E): Serotonin does not serve as the primary autonomic neurotransmitter at the SA node.
