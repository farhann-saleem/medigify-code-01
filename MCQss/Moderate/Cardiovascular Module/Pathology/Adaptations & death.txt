Question: Two days after myocardial infarction, histology of the heart shows:
  A. collagen deposition.
  B. granulation tissue formation.
  C. mature fibrous scar.
  D. necrotic myocardium with neutrophilic infiltration.
  E. organization with macrophage predominance.
 Correct Option: D
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (D): At 1–3 days after myocardial infarction, coagulative necrosis is accompanied by prominent neutrophilic infiltration of the necrotic myocardium.
 •	Incorrect (A): Collagen deposition begins later during the healing phase, not within the first 2 days.
 •	Incorrect (B): Granulation tissue develops approximately 1–2 weeks after infarction.
 •	Incorrect (C): Mature fibrous scar formation occurs several weeks to months after myocardial infarction.
 •	Incorrect (E): Macrophage predominance is characteristic of the 3–7 day period following infarction.

Question: Which of the following organelles is first affected in myocardial infarction:
 A. endoplasmic reticulum
 B. lysosomes
 C. mitochondria
 D. nucleus
 E. ribosomes
 Correct Option: E
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (E): Ribosomal detachment from RER occurs early due to ATP depletion, halting protein synthesis.
 •	Incorrect (A): Endoplasmic reticulum changes occur later in cell injury progression.
 •	Incorrect (B): Lysosomal rupture is a later irreversible injury event.
 •	Incorrect (C): Mitochondrial dysfunction occurs early but ribosomal detachment is the earliest structural change.
 •	Incorrect (D): Nuclear changes represent irreversible injury stages.
 Tags: Moderate, Pathology, Cell Injury, Adaptations & Death
