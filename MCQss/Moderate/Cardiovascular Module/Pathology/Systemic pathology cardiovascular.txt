Question: After how much time of myocardial infarction do neutrophils appear:
 A. 4 hours.
 B. 12 hours.
 C. 24–48 hours.
 D. 72 hours.
 E. 7 days.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Neutrophilic infiltration becomes prominent within 24–48 hours after myocardial infarction.
 •	Incorrect (A): Four hours is too early for significant neutrophil accumulation.
 •	Incorrect (B): Twelve hours may show early changes but not peak neutrophilic infiltration.
 •	Incorrect (D): By 72 hours macrophages begin replacing neutrophils.
 •	Incorrect (E): One week is characterized by granulation tissue formation.

Question: Which of the following features is exclusive to left heart failure:
 A. ascites.
 B. feet edema.
 C. heart failure cells.
 D. hepatomegaly.
 E. nutmeg liver.
 Correct Option: C
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Heart failure cells are hemosiderin-laden macrophages formed due to pulmonary congestion in left heart failure.
 •	Incorrect (A): Ascites is typically associated with right-sided failure.
 •	Incorrect (B): Feet edema is a manifestation of systemic venous congestion.
 •	Incorrect (D): Hepatomegaly occurs in right heart failure.
 •	Incorrect (E): Nutmeg liver results from chronic hepatic venous congestion.
 Tags: Moderate, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Systemic Pathology: Cardiovascular

Question: What is the cause of sudden cardiac death in weight lifters:
 A. dilated cardiomyopathy.
 B. myocardial hypertrophy.
 C. myocardial infarction.
 D. mural thrombus.
 E. unstable angina.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Myocardial hypertrophy predisposes to fatal arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death during exertion.
 •	Incorrect (A): Dilated cardiomyopathy is not the classic lesion in weight lifters.
 •	Incorrect (C): Myocardial infarction is less typical in young athletes.
 •	Incorrect (D): Mural thrombus causes embolic complications rather than sudden death.
 •	Incorrect (E): Unstable angina does not directly explain sudden cardiac death.
 Tags: Moderate, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Systemic Pathology: Cardiovascular

Question: A 60-year-old man presents with crushing substernal chest pain radiating to the neck and arm with elevated troponin I levels. If he dies the next day, which histologic feature would most likely be found in the myocardium:
 A. coagulative necrosis with neutrophil infiltration
 B. early interstitial edema and wavy fibers
 C. fibrotic tissue replacing infarcted tissue
 D. granulation tissue with neovascularization
 E. hemorrhagic necrosis
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (A): After 24 hours, myocardial infarction shows coagulative necrosis with prominent neutrophilic infiltration.
 •	Incorrect (B): Wavy fibers and edema occur within the first few hours.
 •	Incorrect (C): Fibrosis develops weeks after infarction during healing.
 •	Incorrect (D): Granulation tissue appears after several days, not within 24 hours.
 •	Incorrect (E): Hemorrhagic necrosis is not typical of typical MI histology.
 Tags: Moderate, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Systemic Pathology: Cardiovascular

Question: A 41-year-old woman presents with severe headache, visual disturbances, and blood pressure of 220/150 mmHg. These findings are most likely associated with:
  A. atherosclerosis
  B. arteriosclerosis obliterans
  C. hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis
  D. hyaline arteriolosclerosis
  E. medial calcific sclerosis
  Correct Option: C
  Explanation:
 •	Correct (C): Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis is seen in malignant hypertension with “onion-skin” thickening.
 •	Incorrect (A): Atherosclerosis affects large arteries, not malignant HTN arterioles.
 •	Incorrect (B): Arteriosclerosis obliterans is peripheral artery disease.
 •	Incorrect (D): Hyaline arteriolosclerosis is seen in benign hypertension/diabetes.
 •	Incorrect (E): Medial calcific sclerosis is Mönckeberg sclerosis without luminal narrowing.
 Tags: Moderate, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Systemic Pathology: Cardiovascular

Question: The most frequent cause of cor pulmonale with isolated right-sided heart failure is:
  A. congenital heart disease.
  B. disease of the lungs or pulmonary vessels.
  C. left-sided heart failure.
  D. pulmonary infundibular or valvular stenosis.
  E. systemic hypertension.
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (B): Cor pulmonale results from pulmonary hypertension secondary to disease of the lungs or pulmonary vessels, leading to right ventricular hypertrophy and failure.
 •	Incorrect (A): Congenital heart disease may cause right-sided failure but is not the most frequent cause of cor pulmonale.
 •	Incorrect (C): Left-sided heart failure causes secondary right-sided failure rather than isolated cor pulmonale.
 •	Incorrect (D): Pulmonary infundibular or valvular stenosis causes right ventricular overload but is not the commonest cause of cor pulmonale.
 •	Incorrect (E): Systemic hypertension primarily affects the left ventricle.
 Tags: Moderate, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Systemic Pathology: Cardiovascular

Question: The most common cause of death immediately following acute myocardial infarction is:
  A. arrhythmia.
  B. cardiogenic shock.
  C. congestive heart failure.
  D. left ventricular free wall rupture.
  E. papillary muscle rupture.
 Correct Option: A
 Explanation:
 •	Correct (A): Fatal ventricular arrhythmias, especially ventricular fibrillation, are the leading cause of death during the first hours after acute myocardial infarction.
 •	Incorrect (B): Cardiogenic shock is a major complication but is less common as the immediate cause of death.
 •	Incorrect (C): Congestive heart failure generally develops as a later complication.
 •	Incorrect (D): Left ventricular free wall rupture typically occurs several days after infarction.
 •	Incorrect (E): Papillary muscle rupture is a mechanical complication occurring days after myocardial infarction.
 Tags: Moderate, Pathology, Cardiovascular, Systemic Pathology: Cardiovascular
