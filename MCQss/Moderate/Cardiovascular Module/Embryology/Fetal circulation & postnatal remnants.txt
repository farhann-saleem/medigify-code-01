Question: The smooth muscle of which fetal vessel is most sensitive to bradykinin released by inflating lungs in neonates?
A. Aorta
B. Ductus arteriosus
C. Ductus venosus
D. Umbilical artery
E. Umbilical vein
Correct Option: B
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (B): During neonatal lung inflation, the sudden increase in systemic oxygen levels triggers the local release of bradykinin, which acts as a powerful contractile stimulus on the specialized smooth muscle of the ductus arteriosus to initiate functional closure.
Incorrect (A): The smooth muscle within the elastic walls of the aorta is structural and not designed to undergo active vaso-occlusion or spastic closure in response to bradykinin.
Incorrect (C): The ductus venosus closes postnatally via endothelial changes and lacks the specialized, highly reactive smooth muscle apparatus responsive to lung-inflation bradykinin.
Incorrect (D): The umbilical artery constricts at birth primarily due to mechanical stretching, trauma, and direct exposure to high oxygen tensions, rather than lung-derived bradykinin.
Incorrect (E): The umbilical vein collapses passive-mechanically following placental separation and clamping, lacking sensitive vaso-occlusive smooth muscle rings.

Question: In fetal circulation, the highest oxygen content is present in the:
A. Ductus arteriosus
B. Ductus venosus
C. Inferior vena cava
D. Right atrium
E. Umbilical arteries
Correct Option: B
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (B): Purely oxygenated blood from the placenta travels through the umbilical vein and passes directly into the ductus venosus, bypassing the liver with minimal mixing, thus maintaining the highest oxygen saturation (~80%) in the fetal body.
Incorrect (A): The ductus arteriosus carries poorly oxygenated blood mixed from the superior vena cava and right ventricle back to the descending aorta, making its oxygen content quite low.
Incorrect (C): The inferior vena cava carries a mixture of highly oxygenated blood from the ductus venosus and deoxygenated blood from the lower limbs, resulting in a diluted oxygen content compared to the ductus venosus.
Incorrect (D): The right atrium receives a streams-mixed blend of oxygenated inferior vena cava blood and deoxygenated superior vena cava blood, lowering its total oxygen tension.
Incorrect (E): The umbilical arteries carry depleted, deoxygenated systemic blood from the fetal internal iliac arteries back to the placenta for reoxygenation.

Question: Administration of cyclooxygenase inhibitors after 34 weeks of gestation may cause premature closure of the:
A. Ductus arteriosus
B. Ductus venosus
C. Foramen ovale
D. Sinus venosus
E. Umbilical circulation
Correct Option: A
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (A): Cyclooxygenase inhibitors block prostaglandin synthesis; removing this relaxant tone causes the muscular walls of the fetal ductus arteriosus to constrict and close prematurely in utero.
Incorrect (B): The ductus venosus is not structurally sustained by prostaglandin pathways in the same way, meaning it does not constrict or close prematurely in response to cyclooxygenase inhibitors.
Incorrect (C): The foramen ovale is an anatomical flap-valve held open by a left-to-right kinetic pressure differential, not by active prostaglandin-mediated smooth muscle relaxation.
Incorrect (D): The sinus venosus is an early embryonic chamber absorbed into the right atrium during the first trimester, long before 34 weeks of gestation.
Incorrect (E): The umbilical circulation relies on low-resistance placental pathways and mechanical blood flow vectors rather than localized prostaglandin E2 dilation to prevent structural vessel closure.

Question: A congenital anomaly caused by failure of involution of a fetal vessel connecting pulmonary and systemic circulations involves the:
A. Ductus arteriosus
B. Ductus venosus
C. First aortic arch artery
D. Left vitelline artery
E. Right superior cardinal vein
Correct Option: A
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (A): Patent ductus arteriosus occurs when the fetal shunt connecting the left pulmonary artery (pulmonary) to the descending aorta (systemic) fails to involute postnatally.
Incorrect (B): The ductus venosus is an intrahepatic fetal shunt that links the umbilical vein to the inferior vena cava, bypasses the liver, and does not connect the pulmonary and systemic vascular beds.
Incorrect (C): The first aortic arch artery involutes early during normal embryonic development to form parts of the maxillary artery, which does not bridge the pulmonary and systemic trunks.
Incorrect (D): The left vitelline artery is an early embryonic vessel that distributes blood to the primitive midgut and yolk sac, eventually forming mesenteric branches.
Incorrect (E): The right superior cardinal vein is a systemic venous structure that forms the normal adult superior vena cava rather than an abnormal persistent pulmonary-systemic bridge.

Question: Functional closure of the ductus arteriosus after birth is primarily triggered by:
A. Increased atrial natriuretic peptide
B. Increased circulating catecholamines
C. Increased nitric oxide
D. Increased oxygen tension
E. Reduced prostaglandin E2
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 
Correct (D): The immediate functional closure of the ductus arteriosus after birth is primarily triggered by Increased oxygen tension resulting from the newborn's first breaths, which induces smooth muscle contraction.
Incorrect (A): Increased atrial natriuretic peptide regulates fluid volume and vascular resistance but does not mediate ductal closure.
Incorrect (B): Increased circulating catecholamines rise during birth stress but are not the primary direct chemical trigger for ductal constriction.
Incorrect (C): Increased nitric oxide is a potent vasodilator that would maintain patency rather than promote closure.
Incorrect (E): Reduced prostaglandin E2 contributes significantly to subsequent anatomical closure, but the immediate functional trigger is the sudden rise in neonatal oxygenation.
