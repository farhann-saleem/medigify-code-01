Question: Dextrocardia results from bending of the embryonic heart tube:
A. Cranially.
B. Dorsally.
C. To the left.
D. To the right.
E. Ventrally.
Correct Option: C
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (C): Normal cardiac looping occurs when the straight embryonic heart tube bends to the right; if it erroneously loops to the left, the heart is transposed into the right hemithorax, causing dextrocardia.
Incorrect (A): Bending cranially is a normal part of the vertical folding process that repositions the cardiogenic field into the thoracic region, not a lateral axis defect.
Incorrect (B): Bending dorsally happens normally during cardiac looping to bring the primitive atrium posterior to the ventricular segments.
Incorrect (D): Bending to the right is the standard, wild-type directional movement (d-looping) that establishes normal cardiac chambers and left-sided apex positioning.
Incorrect (E): Bending ventrally co-occurs with normal looping as the bulbus cordis moves down and forward, which does not result in a reversed right-sided heart apex.

Question: After cephalocaudal folding of the embryo, the septum transversum lies in which relation to the heart?
A. Caudal to heart
B. Cranial to heart
C. Dorsal to heart
D. Lateral to heart
E. Ventral to heart
Correct Option: A
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (A): Cephalocaudal folding rotates the cardiogenic region 180 degrees, swinging the formerly cranialmost septum transversum down so that it lies caudal to the heart, where it forms the diaphragm.
Incorrect (B): Prior to embryonic folding, the septum transversum is located cranial to the heart tube and the cardiogenic area; folding completely reverses this relationship.
Incorrect (C): The septum transversum does not rotate behind the heart tube along the dorsal axis; the dorsal space is occupied by the foregut and dorsal aortae.
Incorrect (D): The folding occurs in the sagittal plane, moving the septum transversum along a vertical arc rather than positioning it lateral to the cardiac tube.
Incorrect (E): The septum transversum is positioned inferiorly, while the ventral surface of the heart faces the newly formed anterior body wall.

Question: Transposition of the great vessels results from:
A. Abnormal migration of neural crest cells
B. Abnormal incorporation of the sinus venosus
C. Defective endocardial cushion formation
D. Failure of septation between atria
E. Failure of the aorticopulmonary septum to spiral
Correct Option: E
Explanation:
 
Correct (E): Transposition of the great vessels is caused directly by the Failure of the aorticopulmonary septum to spiral during conotruncal septation, leaving the aorta arising from the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk from the left ventricle.
Incorrect (A): Abnormal migration of neural crest cells typically results in a complete absence of septation or severe conotruncal defects like truncus arteriosus, rather than isolated straight septation.
Incorrect (B): Abnormal incorporation of the sinus venosus causes defects in the smooth walls of the atria or anomalous venous return.
Incorrect (C): Defective endocardial cushion formation results in atrioventricular septal defects, not errors in great vessel alignment.
Incorrect (D): Failure of septation between atria produces atrial septal defects and does not impact the outflow tract geometry.

Question: Persistent truncus arteriosus is commonly associated with:
A. Atrioventricular septal defect
B. Membranous ventricular septal defect
C. Ostium primum ASD
D. Patent ductus arteriosus
E. Patent foramen ovale
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
 
Correct (B): Because the aorticopulmonary septum participates in closing the upper interventricular space, a Membranous ventricular septal defect is an obligatory structural component of a persistent truncus arteriosus.
Incorrect (A): Atrioventricular septal defect arises from endocardial cushion failures and is not a default structural defect tied to truncus arteriosus.
Incorrect (C): Ostium primum ASD is a lower atrial septal anomaly linked to endocardial cushion defects and is not an inherent part of truncus arteriosus.
Incorrect (D): Patent ductus arteriosus is an isolated vascular patency defect that is not embryologically required or uniformly associated with truncus arteriosus.
Incorrect (E): Patent foramen ovale is an failure of atrial septal fusion post-birth and is independent of truncal septum formation.

Question: A “boot-shaped heart” on chest X-ray is characteristic of:
A. Coarctation of aorta
B. Hypoplastic left heart syndrome
C. Tetralogy of Fallot
D. Transposition of great vessels
E. Truncus arteriosus
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
 
Correct (C): The structural combination of right ventricular hypertrophy lifting the apex and a small, concaved pulmonary segment produces the classic “boot-shaped heart” (coeur en sabot) on chest X-ray in Tetralogy of Fallot.
Incorrect (A): Coarctation of aorta typically displays a "3 sign" on chest X-ray along with rib notching due to collateral intercostal circulation.
Incorrect (B): Hypoplastic left heart syndrome displays generalized cardiomegaly and pulmonary venous congestion rather than a boot shape.
Incorrect (D): Transposition of great vessels classically displays an "egg-on-a-string" appearance due to a narrowed superior mediastinum.
Incorrect (E): Truncus arteriosus causes cardiomegaly with increased pulmonary vascular markings but does not elevate the cardiac apex into a boot configuration.

Question: During resegmentation of the conotruncal region, failure of normal spiraling of the aorticopulmonary septum while septation itself occurs most likely produces:
A. Double outlet right ventricle
B. Persistent truncus arteriosus
C. Tetralogy of Fallot
D. Transposition of the great arteries
E. Tricuspid atresia
Correct Option: D
Explanation:
 
Correct (D): When septation proceeds normally but suffers from a complete failure of normal spiraling of the aorticopulmonary septum, the vessels form in a parallel fashion, resulting in Transposition of the great arteries.
Incorrect (A): Double outlet right ventricle occurs when both great arteries connect embryologically to the right ventricle due to improper conotruncal shifting.
Incorrect (B): Persistent truncus arteriosus occurs when there is a total failure of the septum to form, rather than a failure of its spiral rotation.
Incorrect (C): Tetralogy of Fallot is caused by an unequal anterior displacement (skewed partitioning) of the aorticopulmonary septum.
Incorrect (E): Tricuspid atresia results from unequal division or fusion failure of the right atrioventricular endocardial cushions.

Question: During development of pulmonary veins, failure of incorporation of the common pulmonary vein into the left atrium may result in:
A. coarctation of aorta
B. ebstein anomaly
C. patent ductus arteriosus
D. persistent left superior vena cava
E. total anomalous pulmonary venous return
Correct Option: E
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (E): Failure of incorporation of the common pulmonary vein into the left atrium prevents normal connection, causing the pulmonary veins to drain abnormally into systemic veins, resulting in total anomalous pulmonary venous return.
Incorrect (A): Coarctation of aorta is caused by abnormal smooth muscle constriction near the ductus arteriosus or altered intrauterine hemodynamics through the left outflow tract, unrelated to pulmonary vein incorporation.
Incorrect (B): Ebstein anomaly is characterized by the downward displacement of the tricuspid valve leaflets into the right ventricle, which arises from defective delamination of the tricuspid valve tissue.
Incorrect (C): Patent ductus arteriosus results from a failure of the muscular closure of the distal portion of the left sixth aortic arch after birth, completely independent of pulmonary venous formation.
Incorrect (D): A persistent left superior vena cava results from the failure of the left anterior cardinal vein to obliterate, leading to an accessory systemic vein draining into the coronary sinus.

Question: During cardiac looping, the bulbus cordis moves ventrally, caudally, and to the right, while the primitive atrium moves:
A. caudally and ventrally
B. cranially, dorsally, and to the left
C. dorsally and caudally
D. laterally to the right
E. ventrally and cranially
Correct Option: B
 
Explanation:
 
Correct (B): During cardiac looping, the bulboventricular segment bends rightward while the primitive atrium moves cranially, dorsally, and to the left, establishing the definitive spatial relationships of the cardiac chambers.
Incorrect (A): Moving caudally and ventrally describes the spatial trajectory of the arterial bulbus cordis and ventricle segments, which is the exact opposite of the atrial displacement.
Incorrect (C): An exclusive dorsal and caudal shift would misplace the primitive atrium inferiorly, failing to position the venous inflow tract superior to the developing ventricles.
Incorrect (D): Moving laterally to the right is the pathway taken by the bulbus cordis; the atrium must shift to the left to mirror this movement and form the loop.
Incorrect (E): A ventral and cranial shift would place the atrium anterior to the bulbus cordis, preventing the normal anteroposterior configuration where the ventricles sit anterior to the atria.
