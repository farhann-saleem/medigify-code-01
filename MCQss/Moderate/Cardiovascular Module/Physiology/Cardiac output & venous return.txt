Question: What distinguishes decompensated heart failure from compensated heart failure?
 A. Complete cessation of cardiac electrical activity
 B. Progressive fluid retention with worsening cardiac overload
 C. Permanent normalization of cardiac output
 D. Sudden rise in systemic arterial pressure
 E. Complete reversal of ventricular remodeling
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 • Correct (B): Decompensation occurs when compensatory mechanisms fail, leading to progressive fluid retention and worsening congestion.
 • Incorrect (A): Electrical cessation is cardiac arrest, not heart failure progression.
 • Incorrect (C): Cardiac output is not normalized in decompensation.
 • Incorrect (D): Blood pressure typically falls or becomes unstable, not persistently elevated.
 • Incorrect (E): Ventricular remodeling does not fully reverse in decompensation.

Question: What is the primary factor that regulates cardiac output under normal, resting conditions?
A. Aortic blood pressure
B. Venous return (amount of blood flowing into heart from tissues)
C. Heart rate
D. Myocardial contractility
E. Systemic vascular resistance
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Venous return determines end-diastolic volume, driving stroke volume via the Frank-Starling mechanism and thereby primarily setting cardiac output at rest.
Incorrect (A): Aortic blood pressure reflects afterload and is a consequence/modulator of cardiac output, not its primary resting regulator.
Incorrect (C): Heart rate contributes to cardiac output but is normally tightly regulated at rest and is secondary to venous return effects on stroke volume.
Incorrect (D): Myocardial contractility changes stroke volume but is not the dominant determinant under normal resting physiological conditions.
Incorrect (E): Systemic vascular resistance mainly influences afterload and arterial pressure rather than directly regulating resting cardiac output.
