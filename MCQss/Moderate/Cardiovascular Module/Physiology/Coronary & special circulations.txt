Question: Tetralogy of Fallot is characterized by which combination and primary consequence?
 A. ASD, mitral stenosis, LVH, pulmonary hypertension
 B. VSD, pulmonary stenosis, overriding aorta, RVH with right-to-left shunt
 C. PDA, coarctation, LV hypertrophy, systemic hypertension
 D. Tricuspid atresia, coronary aneurysm, high output state
 E. Pulmonary atresia, ASD, LV dilation, left-to-right shunt
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 • Correct (B): TOF consists of VSD, pulmonary stenosis, overriding aorta, RV hypertrophy causing right-to-left shunt and cyanosis.
 • Incorrect (A): Not components of TOF.
 • Incorrect (C): Describes ductal/aortic anomalies, not TOF.
 • Incorrect (D): Not a recognized TOF pattern.
 • Incorrect (E): TOF is not a left-to-right shunt condition.

Question: In chronic ischemic heart disease, narrowings develop in the coronary arteries over several months. Despite this, resting myocardial blood flow remains normal. What long-term local mechanism accounts for this adaptation?
A. Downregulation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase leading to baseline vasoconstriction
B. Development of collateral circulation via angiogenesis stimulated by VEGF in response to hypoxia
C. Obliteration of coronary lymphatic drainage improving perfusion pressure
D. Spastic constriction of downstream capillaries reducing metabolic demand
E. Persistent endothelin-1 dominance causing uniform epicardial vasoconstriction
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Chronic hypoxia induces VEGF-driven angiogenesis, producing collateral vessels that preserve resting myocardial perfusion despite stenosis.
Incorrect (A): eNOS downregulation would worsen perfusion rather than preserve normal flow.
Incorrect (C): Lymphatic changes do not compensate for coronary arterial narrowing.
Incorrect (D): Capillary spasm and metabolic suppression are not adaptive long-term perfusion mechanisms.
Incorrect (E): Endothelin-1 dominance would increase ischemia rather than normalize flow.
