Question: A harsh crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur at the second right intercostal space indicates:
 A. Mitral stenosis
 B. Aortic stenosis
 C. Aortic regurgitation
 D. Tricuspid regurgitation
 E. Pulmonary stenosis
 Correct Option: B
 Explanation:
 • Correct (B): Aortic stenosis produces a systolic ejection murmur at the right second intercostal space radiating to carotids.
 • Incorrect (A): Mitral stenosis causes diastolic rumbling murmur at apex.
 • Incorrect (C): Aortic regurgitation causes diastolic murmur.
 • Incorrect (D): Tricuspid regurgitation is best heard at lower left sternal border.
 • Incorrect (E): Pulmonary stenosis is best heard at left second intercostal space.
 Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Cardiovascular System, Cardiac Cycle & Heart Sounds

Question: A continuous machinery-like murmur heard throughout both systole and diastole in a newborn child is diagnostic of:
A. Coarctation of the aorta.
B. Patent ductus arteriosus.
C. Tetralogy of Fallot.
D. Transposition of the great vessels.
E. Ventricular septal defect.
Correct Option: B
Explanation:
Correct (B): Patent ductus arteriosus produces continuous flow between the aorta and pulmonary artery, creating a machinery-like murmur throughout systole and diastole.
Incorrect (A): Coarctation of the aorta does not produce the classic continuous machinery murmur.
Incorrect (C): Tetralogy of Fallot is typically associated with a systolic murmur from right ventricular outflow obstruction.
Incorrect (D): Transposition of the great vessels is defined by abnormal vessel connections rather than a machinery murmur.
Incorrect (E): Ventricular septal defect usually causes a holosystolic murmur.
Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Cardiovascular System, Cardiac Cycle & Heart Sounds

Question: During the "Isovolumic Contraction" phase of the cardiac cycle?
A. All heart valves are closed
B. All heart valves are open
C. The A-V valves are closed and semilunar valves are open
D. The A-V valves are open and semilunar valves are closed
E. The semilunar valves are open and A-V valves are open
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): During isovolumic contraction, ventricular pressure rises rapidly while both A-V and
semilunar valves remain closed, so ventricular volume does not change.
Incorrect (B): All heart valves are never open during this phase.
Incorrect (C): Semilunar valves do not open until ventricular pressure exceeds arterial pressure.
Incorrect (D): A-V valves close at the start of ventricular systole.
Incorrect (E): Simultaneous opening of semilunar and A-V valves does not occur physiologically.
Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Cardiovascular, Cardiac Cycle & Heart Sounds

Question: A "Physiological Gallop" or S3 heart sound is most likely heard during:
A. Atrial contraction
B. Early rapid ventricular filling

D. Late systolic ejection

Explanation:

Incorrect (D): S3 occurs in early diastole, not systolic ejection.
Incorrect (E): Ventricular isovolumic relaxation precedes rapid filling.
