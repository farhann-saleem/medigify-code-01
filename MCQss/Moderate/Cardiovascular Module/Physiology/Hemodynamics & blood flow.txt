Question: If a vessel's radius is reduced to exactly one-third (1/3) of its original size while the driving pressure gradient remains perfectly constant, what will be the new blood flow rate relative to the original flow?
A. 1/27 of original flow
B. 1/3 of original flow
C. 1/81 of original flow
D. 1/9 of original flow
E. 1/16 of original flow
Correct Option: C
Explanation:
Correct (C): Poiseuille’s law (r⁴ dependence) makes flow (1/3)⁴ = 1/81.
Incorrect (A): 1/27 incorrectly uses cubic scaling instead of fourth power.
Incorrect (B): Linear scaling ignores Poiseuille’s law.
Incorrect (D): 1/9 underestimates radius exponent effect.
Incorrect (E): 1/16 has no relation to r⁴ physiology.

Question: Three vascular beds are arranged in a parallel circuit with individual resistances of R₁ = 3, R₂ = 3, and R₃ = 3 mmHg·min/L. What is the total vascular resistance (R_total) of this integrated circuit?
A. 0.33 mmHg·min/L
B. 1 mmHg·min/L
C. 3 mmHg·min/L
D. 6 mmHg·min/L

Explanation:
Correct (B): Equal parallel resistances give R_total = 1/(1/3+1/3+1/3)=1.
Incorrect (A): 0.33 incorrectly divides instead of summing reciprocals.
Incorrect (C): Ignores parallel reduction effect.

Incorrect (E): Misapplies direct summation.

Question: When a sudden volume of blood is infused into a vein, the pressure initially rises sharply but gradually declines back toward normal over several minutes. This characteristic property of veins is known as:

C. immediate passive recoil without time dependence.
D. inertial resistance to acute volume loading.
E. myogenic autoregulatory adaptation of venules.

Explanation:
Correct (B): Stress-relaxation (delayed compliance) allows pressure normalization after stretch.
Incorrect (A): Elastic recoil is immediate, not time-dependent decline.
Incorrect (C): Ignores delayed pressure adaptation.
Incorrect (D): Inertia is not the governing vascular property.
Incorrect (E): Myogenic response applies to arterioles, not veins.

Question: According to the myogenic theory of autoregulation, what happens at the cellular level when a rise in arterial pressure stretches an arteriole wall?
A. Stretch-activated calcium channels open, causing calcium influx and smooth muscle contraction
B. Potassium channels open, causing smooth muscle hyperpolarization and relaxation
C. Sodium-calcium exchanger is inhibited, reducing intracellular calcium levels
D. Nitric oxide production increases in endothelial cells causing vasodilation
E. Voltage-gated chloride channels open leading to smooth muscle hyperpolarization
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Increased transmural pressure stretches vascular smooth muscle, opening stretch-activated calcium channels, increasing intracellular Ca²⁺ and causing myogenic contraction.
Incorrect (B): Potassium channel opening causes hyperpolarization, but this is not the primary stretch-response mechanism in myogenic autoregulation.
Incorrect (C): Sodium-calcium exchanger inhibition is not the initiating event in pressure-induced smooth muscle contraction.
Incorrect (D): Nitric oxide production is an endothelial metabolic vasodilatory pathway, not the myogenic stretch reflex.
Incorrect (E): Voltage-gated chloride channels are not the key mediators of pressure-induced arteriolar smooth muscle contraction.
Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Cardiovascular System, Hemodynamics & Blood Flow

Question: A patient is taking a drug that selectively blocks soluble guanylyl cyclase. Which local vascular control mechanism will be impaired by this medication?

Explanation:

Incorrect (A): Endothelin-ETA receptor signaling produces vasoconstriction and does not depend on guanylyl cyclase.
Incorrect (C): eNOS-mediated NO synthesis is upstream and not directly dependent on soluble guanylyl cyclase.
Incorrect (D): EDHF-mediated vasodilation acts via K⁺ channel activation, independent of cGMP.
Incorrect (E): Prostacyclin acts via cAMP signaling, not the NO–cGMP pathway.

Question: An experimental setup monitors blood flow to a skeletal muscle group. The arterial pressure is increased from 90 mmHg to 140 mmHg. Within 1 minute, the flow increases temporarily but returns to near baseline via metabolic autoregulation. Describe the metabolic steps that restore normal flow.
A. Increased pressure delivers excess oxygen and washes out local vasodilator metabolites causing arteriolar constriction
B. Increased pressure induces rapid angiogenesis within seconds due to hypoxia signaling
C. Elevated pressure permanently downregulates alpha-adrenergic receptors in vascular smooth muscle
D. Tissue cells undergo metabolic shutdown reducing oxygen demand to baseline levels
E. Activation of endothelial hyperpolarizing factor maintains sustained vasodilation despite washout
Correct Option: A
Explanation:
Correct (A): Increased perfusion washes out local vasodilator metabolites (e.g., adenosine, CO₂, H⁺), leading to arteriolar constriction and return of flow toward baseline.
Incorrect (B): Angiogenesis is a long-term hypoxia-driven process, not a rapid autoregulatory response.
Incorrect (C): α-adrenergic receptor downregulation is not the acute mechanism of metabolic autoregulation.
Incorrect (D): Metabolic demand does not acutely shut down to normalize flow in seconds.
Incorrect (E): Endothelial hyperpolarizing factor promotes vasodilation, which would oppose restoration to baseline flow.
Tags: Moderate, Physiology, Cardiovascular System, Hemodynamics & Blood Flow
